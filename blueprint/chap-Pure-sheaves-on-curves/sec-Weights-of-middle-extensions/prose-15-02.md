Let $j$ be the inclusion of a dense open in a smooth curve, and let
$\mathcal F$ be a lisse sheaf on the open. The Fourier argument needs an
extension of $\mathcal F$ to the whole curve that loses nothing at the
boundary points. The ordinary direct image $j_*\mathcal F$ is such an
extension, whereas extension by zero loses something. For example, for the constant
sheaf on $\mathbf G_m\subset\mathbf A^1$, $j_!\overline{\mathbf Q}_\ell$ has stalk $0$ at the
origin, while the constant sheaf on $\mathbf A^1$ is the natural
extension. In general, by Lemma [lemma-boundary-stalk], $j_*\mathcal F$ recovers a lisse
extension whenever one exists, and its stalk at a boundary point is the
space of inertia invariants.

If $\mathcal F$ is punctually of weights $\le a$, then so is
$j_*\mathcal F$, at the boundary points as well
(Proposition [proposition-weights-j-boundary-points]). In
Chapter [chap-The-Deligne-Laumon-theorem-for-curves] this bounds the weights of
the boundary terms in the Fourier transform of a middle extension.
