The three clauses of [def-weil-exceptional-adjoint-admissibility] separate
existence of an adjoint from constructible and finite-Tor preservation. The
comparison-class construction supplies the bounded instances used here; it
does not supply the unrestricted all-object datum of
[def-weil-ambient-exceptional-adjoint]. Smooth preservation is the smooth
admissibility corollary; the finite-stage geometric assembly and its scope
are the preservation-class theorem. General Verdier biduality at finite
coefficients is a separate geometric theorem, proved by constructible
preservation, geometric generic restriction, and dimension induction. The
finite-stage boundary comparison is then a consequence. Integral duality and
the constructible exceptional adjunction, and their field transports, are
stated in the coefficient chapter. These results concern the specified
constructible categories; the unrestricted unbounded ambient adjoint remains
a separate assertion.

The open-immersion and finite-etale cases, including uniform coefficient
comparison, are proved without admissibility; the finite closed subset of a
smooth curve is treated separately, as are the independent closed-support
construction and its site comparison. The necessary direct-image bounds for
the closed-support step are made explicit there; the general geometric
verification is not supplied by calling a morphism admissible. In particular,
the upper shift $c_f^+$ cannot be omitted: an explicit computation exhibits
positive cohomological degrees.
