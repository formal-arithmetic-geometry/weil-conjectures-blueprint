The construction of $f^!$ needs finiteness properties of $\Dctf(X,\Lambda)$,
the first being that proper direct image preserves it
(Proposition [sk-thm-sfb-proper-base-change], the main result of this
section). The section also provides what the compactness argument of
Section “Construction of the exceptional inverse image” needs for field coefficients (continuity of
Ext and descent to a finite $E$), and the global lattices on normal schemes
that identify lisse sheaves with representations in
Section “Rank-one sheaves on curves”. For finite
commutative $\ell$-primary rings, including $\mathcal O_E/\pi_E^n$, these
follow from classical results on the étale site together with finite Tor
amplitude (Proposition [sk-prop-ctf-finite-proper]).

In this section we reduce
the cases $\Lambda=\mathcal O_E$, $E$ and $\overline{\mathbf Q}_\ell$ to these finite levels.
For these rings $\Dctf=\Dbc$, and we write $\Dbc$ when discussing
cohomology sheaves, truncations and lattices. The difficulty is that a
rational coefficient sheaf has no reduction modulo $\pi_E$; we must first
find an integral object inside it. This is possible locally: an object of
$\Dbc(X,\overline{\mathbf Q}_\ell)$ is defined over a finite extension $E$ of $\Ql$, an object
of $\Dbc(X,E)$ is built by finitely many cones from extensions by zero of lisse
sheaves on locally closed strata, and after refining the stratum such a
lisse sheaf contains a lattice. The reductions modulo $\pi_E^n$ of the
extension by zero of that lattice are then classical torsion sheaves.
