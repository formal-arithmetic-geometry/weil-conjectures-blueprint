The equivalence is the projectivity theorem for compact Hausdorff
spaces; see [Stacks, Tag 08YN](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/08YN). To lift a map $S\to Y$ along a surjection
$X\to Y$, one pulls the surjection back to $S$ and chooses a minimal
closed subspace of the fibre product that still surjects onto $S$.
Extremal disconnectedness forces this irreducible surjection to be a
homeomorphism, and its inverse gives the lift. The converse is proved in
[Stacks, Tag 08YN](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/08YN). For the cover, write $T^{\delta}$ for the underlying set of $T$ with
the discrete topology and take $S=\beta(T^{\delta})$, its Stone–Čech
compactification. The Stone–Čech compactification of a discrete
space is extremally disconnected, and the identity $T^{\delta}\to T$
extends uniquely to a continuous surjection $\beta(T^{\delta})\to T$; see
[Stacks, Tag 090D](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/090D). Finally, an extremally disconnected compact Hausdorff
space is totally disconnected, since the closure of an open set is
clopen, and hence it is profinite.
