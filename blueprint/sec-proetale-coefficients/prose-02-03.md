In (2), flatness of the diagonal does the work of unramifiedness. Flatness of $R\to B$ alone would not suffice. The
converse of the second implication in (1) fails in general: a weakly
étale map need not be a filtered colimit of étale maps.

It does become ind-étale after a faithfully flat refinement
(Theorem [sk-thm-pe-indetale-refinement] below, and [Stacks, Tag 097Z](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/097Z)).
Geometrically, a weakly étale scheme over $X$ need not be a limit of
étale $X$-schemes, but it is covered, in the pro-étale topology, by
one that is.
