A finitely presented étale $\Omega$-algebra is a finite product of
copies of $\Omega$. Hence filtered colimits of such algebras correspond,
under $\operatorname{Spec}$, to inverse limits of finite sets. Faithfully
flat covering maps become surjections of profinite sets, and by
quasi-compactness every cover can be refined by a finite one. This proves
the equivalence of sites. Since the affine pro-étale objects form a
basis, sheaves are determined by their values on them, so the two topoi
agree. The last assertion is the definition of the condensed set of a
topological ring $R$ (Section [sec-n-coefficients]): the condensed ring
$\underline R$ has as sections over a profinite set $S$ the continuous maps
$S\to R$. If $R$ is discrete, these are the locally constant functions,
which are the sections of the classical constant sheaf.
