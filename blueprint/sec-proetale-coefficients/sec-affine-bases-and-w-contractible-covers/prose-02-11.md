We now combine the two theorems just proved: the refinement theorem
replaces an arbitrary object of $X_{\mathrm{pro}\acute{e}t}$ by pro-étale affines, and
the existence of w-contractible covers replaces those by affines over which
taking sections is exact. Hence, to compute with sheaves on $X_{\mathrm{pro}\acute{e}t}$,
it suffices to evaluate on such affines.

The two steps play different roles. Pro-étale affines
$U=\varprojlim_iU_i$ are the objects on which sheaves pulled back from
$X_{\acute{e}t}$ can be evaluated by a colimit, and the comparison theorems of
Section [sec-pe-comparison] are proved on them. The w-contractible
affines are the objects on which every epimorphism of sheaves is
surjective on sections, and the homological results of the next three
subsections are proved on them. The point of
Theorem [sk-thm-pe-weakly-contractible-basis] is that the splitting of
ring maps in Proposition [sk-prop-pe-wc-rings] becomes a statement about
sheaves: a finite cover of a w-contractible affine has a section, and
such a section amounts to a decomposition of the affine into finitely
many clopen pieces, each mapping into one member of the cover. Local
lifts along an epimorphism can then be glued over these pieces without
any further refinement.
