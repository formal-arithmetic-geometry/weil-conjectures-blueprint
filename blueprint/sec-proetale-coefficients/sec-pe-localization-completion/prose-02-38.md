For arguments with closed supports we will use the local cohomology functor

$$
R\underline\Gamma_ZK
=i^*\fib(K\longrightarrow Rj_*j^*K)
\in D(Z_{\mathrm{pro}\acute{e}t},i^*\mathcal R).
$$

As a functor $D(X_{\mathrm{pro}\acute{e}t},\mathcal R)\to D(Z_{\mathrm{pro}\acute{e}t},i^*\mathcal R)$ it is right
adjoint to the exact fully faithful functor
$i_*:D(Z_{\mathrm{pro}\acute{e}t},i^*\mathcal R)\to D(X_{\mathrm{pro}\acute{e}t},\mathcal R)$. Indeed, $j^*i_*M=0$
implies $\operatorname{Hom}(i_*M,Rj_*j^*K)=0$, so maps $i_*M\to K$ are
maps into the fibre, and the fibre is $i_*$ of
$R\underline\Gamma_ZK$ because its restriction to $U$ vanishes. Since
$R\underline\Gamma_Z$ is built from $i^*$, $j^*$ and $Rj_*$ alone,
it is available before $i^!$ is constructed by Brown representability.
Once $i^!$ exists, $i^!$ agrees with $R\underline\Gamma_Z$ on the
localizing category on which $i^!$ is defined.

We treat integral coefficients through their reductions modulo
powers of $\pi_E$. This is possible for complexes that are recovered
from their reductions, the derived complete ones. Derived completeness
is the analogue for complexes of the condition $M=\varprojlim_nM/\pi_E^nM$
on a module. For example, $\mathcal O_{E,X}$ itself is derived complete,
while $E_X$ is not, since all its reductions vanish.
