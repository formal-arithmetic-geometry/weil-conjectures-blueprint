Put $A=H^0(X,\mathcal O_X)$. The map $k\to A$ is injective, $X$ being
nonempty. Since $X$ is proper over $k$, every global function is integral
over $k$ (indeed $A$ is finite-dimensional over $k$, by finiteness of
coherent cohomology on proper schemes). Let $a\in A$. The subalgebra
$k[a]\subseteq A$ is then a finite-dimensional $k$-algebra. It is
reduced, as a subring of the reduced ring $A$, and it has no idempotents
other than $0$ and $1$, because an idempotent of $A$ cuts $X$ into two
disjoint open and closed pieces and $X$ is connected. A finite reduced
algebra over a field is a finite product of finite field extensions, and
without nontrivial idempotents it is a single finite extension of $k$, hence
equal to $k$ as $k$ is algebraically closed. So $a\in k$, and $k\to A$ is
bijective. (Reducedness is needed: the proper connected curve
$\mathbf P^1_k\times_k\operatorname{Spec}k[\varepsilon]/(\varepsilon^2)$ has
global functions $k[\varepsilon]/(\varepsilon^2)$.)
