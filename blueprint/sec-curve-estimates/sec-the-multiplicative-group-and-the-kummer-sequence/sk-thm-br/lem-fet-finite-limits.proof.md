Step 1: finite limits. With $\mathrm{F\acute Et}(Y)$ as in
[def-galois-categories] and the scheme-theoretic fibre products of
[def-weil-pbc-scheme-conventions], the final object is $Y$, and fibre
products of finite etale $Y$-schemes are finite etale over $Y$, since
finite etale morphisms are stable under base change and composition and
a morphism between finite etale $Y$-schemes is itself finite etale.
Hence $\mathrm{F\acute Et}(Y)$ is closed under finite limits in
$Y$-schemes.
Step 2: finite coproducts. The empty scheme and disjoint unions of
finite etale $Y$-schemes are finite etale over $Y$, and they are the
initial object and the coproducts in $Y$-schemes.
Step 3: quotients by finite groups. Let a finite group $G$ act on $P$ by
$Y$-automorphisms. Over an affine open $V=\operatorname{Spec}R$ of $Y$ the
preimage of $V$ in $P$ is $\operatorname{Spec}A$ with $A$ finite etale
over $R$, and $\operatorname{Spec}A^G$ is finite etale over $R$, its
formation commuting with flat base change, in particular with passing to
smaller affine opens. These affine quotients glue to a finite etale
$Y$-scheme $P/G$, and the universal property of $A^G$ among
$G$-invariant maps gives the universal property of the quotient.
Step 4: monomorphisms. A monomorphism $X\to Z$ in
$\mathrm{F\acute Et}(Y)$ is a monomorphism of schemes (its diagonal is an
isomorphism, fibre products being computed in schemes), and a flat
monomorphism locally of finite presentation is an open immersion. It is
also finite, hence closed, so its image is open and closed, and $Z$ is
the disjoint union of $X$ and the complementary open and closed
subscheme.
