Let $C$ be a smooth curve over an algebraically closed field $k$, and
let $\nu_C^*\mathbf G_m$ be the
pullback of the multiplicative group $\mathbf G_m$ of $C_{\acute{e}t}$ to $C_{\mathrm{pro}\acute{e}t}$. For $n$ invertible
in $k$ we use the Kummer sequence to reduce the cohomology of $\mu_n$ to
that of $\nu_C^*\mathbf G_m$, which we compute first.
In degree one, $H^1(C,\mathbf G_m)$ is the Picard group, as in the
Zariski topology. The substance of Lemma [sk-thm-br] is the
vanishing in degrees at least two, and in particular of the Brauer
group. Over $\mathbf C$ this matches the topology of a Riemann surface,
whose integral cohomology vanishes above degree two and whose $H^2$ is
accounted for by the degree of line bundles. Algebraically the vanishing
comes from Tsen's theorem: forms of low degree over the function field
$k(C)$ have nontrivial zeros, so $k(C)$ has no nontrivial central
division algebras, and the Galois cohomology of $k(C)$ with values in
the multiplicative group vanishes in positive degrees. The divisor
sequence then transfers this vanishing from the generic point to $C$.
Over a finite field Tsen's theorem fails: the function field of a
curve has nonzero Brauer group, by class field theory, and this is one
reason why we work over an algebraically closed field.
With Lemma [sk-thm-br] and the Jacobian, the Kummer sequence gives
the cohomology of $\mu_n$ on a proper curve
(Proposition [sk-thm-curveestimate]).
