Proper base change identifies the stalks of a proper direct image with
the cohomology of the geometric fibres: for $f:X\to Y$ proper and a
torsion sheaf $F$ on $X$, the stalk of $R^qf_*F$ at a geometric point
$\bar y$ is $H^q(X_{\bar y},F)$. More generally, pulling a proper family
back to a new base and taking its cohomology can be done in either order.
This is the étale analogue of the corresponding theorem for proper maps
of locally compact spaces. The base change formula for compactly
supported cohomology, and with it the six-functor formalism below,
depends on it. The proof reduces to the projective line over a henselian
local ring, where we compare the cohomology of $\mathbf P^1$ with that
of its closed fibre. The general case is reduced to this one by passing
to projective spaces and by Chow's lemma (Section “Arbitrary proper morphisms”).

For torsion coefficients, the theorem concerns classical pro-étale
complexes, and we prove it by calculations on the étale site, along the
lines of (BP). The comparison theorems of
Section “The pro-étale site and its comparison theorems” relate these calculations to the
pro-étale site, and the adic versions then follow by completion and
change of coefficients. The resulting
Theorem [sk-thm-properbasechange], Corollary [sk-cor-pbc-torsion-stalk] and
Proposition [sk-prop-pbc-complete] show that $Rf_!$ does not depend on
the compactification (Section “Construction of the exceptional inverse image”), and they compute the
stalks of $Rf_!$ in the trace formula (Section “The Grothendieck–Lefschetz trace formula”).
