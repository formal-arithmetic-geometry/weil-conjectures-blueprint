Let $f:X\to S$ be a separated morphism of finite type which is everywhere
quasi-finite [pqf-def-quasifinite], with $S$ quasi-compact and
quasi-separated. Then there exist a scheme $T$, a quasi-compact open
immersion $j:X\hookrightarrow T$ and a finite morphism $p:T\to S$ with
$f=p\circ j$. If $f$ is locally of finite presentation, one may choose $T$
finite and of finite presentation over $S$ (the classical
finite-presentation refinement of the theorem).

*Stacks Project:* the Zariski main theorem, More on Morphisms, Lemma
37.43.3, [Tag 05K0](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/05K0), with the
finite-presentation form [Tag
0F2N](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0F2N); the
normalization-of-the-target-in-the-source constructions used in the proof
are the quasi-finite-locus theorem [Tag
03GW](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/03GW) and [Tag
02LR](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/02LR).

## Proof

*Step 1: the canonical branch (recorded input).* The normalization of $S$
in $X$ (the relative integral-closure construction of the recorded input
Tag 03GW, cf. also the quasi-finite-locus statement OBL-PQF-06,
[pqf-lem-qf-locus-open]) is a scheme $N_f$ with a morphism
$$
 X\xrightarrow{\ \iota_f\ }N_f\xrightarrow{\ \nu_f\ }S,
 \qquad \nu_f\circ\iota_f=f,
$$
where $\nu_f$ is *integral* and, since $f$ is everywhere quasi-finite
[pqf-def-quasifinite], $\iota_f$ is a *quasi-compact open immersion* (the
classical form of the theorem, Tag 02LR). Write
$\mathcal A_f=\nu_{f*}\mathcal O_{N_f}$, an $\mathcal O_S$-algebra whose
fibres are the integral closures mentioned there; locally on an affine
$\operatorname{Spec}A\subseteq S$ the algebra
$\mathcal A_f(\operatorname{Spec}A)$ is the integral closure of $A$ in
$\Gamma(f^{-1}(\operatorname{Spec}A),\mathcal O_X)$.

*Step 2: the finite approximation tail.* On every affine
$\operatorname{Spec}A\subseteq S$, the ring $B=\mathcal A_f(A)$ is the
filtered union of its finite $A$-subalgebras: each $b\in B$ is integral
over $A$, so the subalgebra $A[b]$ is a finite $A$-module (generated by
$1,b,\dots,b^{n-1}$ for the minimal degree of a monic equation), finite
lists of integral elements generate finite subalgebras (the same argument,
iterated), and the union of the finite subalgebras generated by finite
lists of elements of $B$ is $B$ and is filtered by inclusion of the
generating lists. Globally, since $S$ is quasi-compact, $\mathcal A_f$ is
the filtered union of its finite quasi-coherent
$\mathcal O_S$-subalgebras $\mathcal A_\lambda$, with
$T_\lambda=\operatorname{Spec}_S\mathcal A_\lambda$ finite over $S$ by the
scheme conventions (affine and finite $\mathcal O_S$-module); the
transition maps of the system $\{T_\lambda\}$ are affine, and
$N_f=\varprojlim_\lambda T_\lambda$.

*Step 3: the descended stage.* By [thm-pqf-immersion-descent] there are an
index $\lambda_0$ and a factorization
$$
 X\xrightarrow{\ j\ }T\xrightarrow{\ i\ }T_{\lambda_0}
$$
with $j$ a quasi-compact *open* immersion and $i$ a *closed* immersion. The
closed immersion $i$ is finite, hence a finite morphism in the sense of the
scheme conventions, and a closed subscheme of a finite $S$-scheme is finite
over it: $T\to T_{\lambda_0}$ is an affine $S$-morphism with finite module
of sections (closed immersion), and finiteness is transitive (finite over
finite is finite: the module of sections over the composite affine is
generated by the products of the two finite generating lists). Therefore
$p=T\to T_{\lambda_0}\to S$ is finite, and pasting the canonical equality
$\nu_f\circ\iota_f=f$ with the stage projections gives $p\circ j=f$: the
projections of the limit $N_f=\varprojlim_\lambda T_\lambda$ are
compatible, and the interface identifies the descended factorization with
the one of Step 1. (For $f$ locally of finite presentation the
factorization may be chosen with $T$ finite and of finite presentation over
$S$: this is the finite-presentation refinement of the theorem, Stacks
Project Tag 0F2N, proved in the last section of this chapter
[pqf-thm-zmt-fp], namely Tag 09YY applied to the finite scheme $T$ of this
step; its proof replaces the presentation of the normalization by an
approximating directed system of finite finitely presented $S$-schemes
rather than descending the immersion to a stage of the presentation used
here, so that it is not a consequence of the interface.)

*Audit note (PQF-02, closed).* Steps 1 and 2 are proved in this blueprint:
Step 1 records the relative normalization input, and the
finite-approximation tail of Step 2 is proved. Step 3 formerly deduced from
the limit lemma (Tag 01ZM) that the composite $X\to N_f\to T_{\lambda_0}$
is a quasi-compact immersion. That deduction is invalid: the limit lemma
descends the *open* subscheme $\iota_f(X)$ to an open $U$ of a stage, which
exhibits $X$ as the fibre product $N_f\times_{T_{\lambda_0}}U$, and the
projection of that fibre product onto $U$ is the base change of the
integral morphism $\nu_f$, hence finite and surjective of the same degree,
and not an immersion as soon as one fibre of $\nu_f$ over $U$ has more than
one point. The immersion statement was therefore isolated as the interface
[thm-pqf-immersion-descent], which is now a theorem.
