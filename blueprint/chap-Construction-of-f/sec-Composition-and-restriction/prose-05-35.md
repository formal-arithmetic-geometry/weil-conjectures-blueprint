Brown representability gives $f^!$ one morphism at a time. To
compute it in Section [chap-Constructibility-of-f], however, we factor $f$
locally. The link is uniqueness of adjoints. An isomorphism between
composites of left adjoints, such as $Rg_!\circ Rf_!\simeq R(gf)_!$ or
$Rf_!=Rp_*\circ j_!$, gives an isomorphism between the right
adjoints, composed in the opposite order. Since $j^!=j^*$ for an open
immersion $j$, it follows that $f^!$ is local on $X$ and on $Y$. For example, a smooth $f$ is
locally the composite of an étale map and a projection
$\mathbf A^n_Y\to Y$. By composition and locality, $f^!$ is then
determined by its values on these two kinds of maps.

Base change for $f^!$ does not follow in this way. Passing to right
adjoints in the base change isomorphism $v^*Rf_!\simeq Rf'_!u^*$ relates
$f^!Rv_*$ and $Ru_*f'^!$. These involve direct images, not the
inverse images $u^*f^!$ and $f'^!v^*$ that one wants to compare. We prove
base change for smooth morphisms in
Proposition [proposition-exceptional-inverse-image-smooth-morphism]. That
$f^!$ preserves $D^b_{\mathrm{ctf}}$ is proved in
Theorems [theorem-f-shriek-finite-coefficients] and [theorem-f-shriek-ell-adic-coefficients].
