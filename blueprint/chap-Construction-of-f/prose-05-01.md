Verdier duality, Poincaré duality and the trace formula all rest on a
right adjoint $f^!$ of $Rf_!$. Indeed, Verdier duality (Section [chap-Verdier-and-Poincare-duality])
is duality with respect to the dualizing complex $a_X^!\Lambda$, where
$a_X:X\to\operatorname{Spec}k$ is the structure map, and Poincaré duality
follows from it once smooth purity
(Theorem [theorem-smooth-purity-all-admissible-coefficients]) computes
$f^!$ for smooth morphisms. Similarly, the local terms of the trace formula
(Section [chap-The-trace-formula]) are built from $f^!$ for the two
projections of a correspondence.

The functor $Rf_!$ itself is easy to
write down. A separated morphism of finite type $f:X\to Y$ has a
compactification $f=p\circ j$, with $j:X\hookrightarrow\overline X$ an
open immersion and $p:\overline X\to Y$ proper
(Theorem [theorem-nagata-s-compactification-theorem]), and we put
$Rf_!=Rp_*\circ j_!$. This definition stays within constructible complexes because, for every admissible $\Lambda$, the functor $Rp_*$ maps $D^b_{\mathrm{ctf}}(\overline X,\Lambda)$ into $D^b_{\mathrm{ctf}}(Y,\Lambda)$. The resulting functor satisfies proper base
change and the projection formula
(Proposition [proposition-proper-direct-image-constructible-complexes]).

The right adjoint of $Rf_!$ is another matter. The factor $j_!$ has the right
adjoint $j^*$, but for $Rp_*$ with $p$ proper no formula for a right
adjoint is available. Even where a formula exists, it is not computed
pointwise: for a closed immersion $i$ the functor $i^!$ takes sections with
support in the closed subscheme, so the value of $i^!K$ at a point depends
on $K$ near the point, not only on its stalk there.

Grothendieck and Hartshorne built the coherent $f^!$ from local formulas
and then had to glue them. Neeman noticed, instead, that $D_{\mathrm{qc}}(X)$
is generated by compact perfect complexes and that $Rf_*$ preserves direct
sums, so that Brown representability produces $f^!$ with no formula. We follow his approach, with $D^b_{\mathrm{ctf}}$ in place of the perfect complexes.

The theorem we use is the following. Let $\mathcal T$ be a triangulated
category with arbitrary direct sums, and suppose that $\mathcal T$ is
compactly generated: there is a set $\mathcal G$ of compact objects $G$
(that is, $\Hom(G,-)$ commutes with direct sums) such that $K=0$ whenever
$\Hom(G[n],K)=0$ for all $G\in\mathcal G$ and $n\in\mathbf Z$. Then every
exact functor out of $\mathcal T$ that preserves direct sums has an exact
right adjoint [Stacks, [0A8F](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0A8F), [0A8G](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0A8G)]. The category $D^b_{\mathrm{ctf}}(X,\Lambda)$ is not closed
under infinite direct sums. We therefore apply the theorem to the localizing
category $\Loc(D^b_{\mathrm{ctf}}(X,\Lambda))$ that it generates
(Section [sec-Existence-and-finiteness-of-f]). The result is
Theorem [theorem-existence-exceptional-inverse-image]: for every
admissible $\Lambda$ and every separated morphism of finite type $f:X\to Y$
there is an exact functor

$$
f^!:\Loc(D^b_{\mathrm{ctf}}(Y,\Lambda))\to\Loc(D^b_{\mathrm{ctf}}(X,\Lambda)),
\qquad
\Hom(Rf_!M,N)\simeq\Hom(M,f^!N).
$$

For finite coefficients nothing is lost
by passing to the localizing category, since
$\Loc(D^b_{\mathrm{ctf}}(X,A))$ is equivalent to the classical derived category
$D(X_{\acute{e}t},A)$ (Proposition [proposition-comparison-etale-derived-category]).

Everything therefore rests on the two hypotheses of the theorem: that
the objects of $D^b_{\mathrm{ctf}}(X,\Lambda)$ are compact in $\Loc(D^b_{\mathrm{ctf}}(X,\Lambda))$,
and that $Rp_*$ preserves direct sums for proper $p$. Neither is formal,
because the summands of a direct sum may live in degrees going off to
$-\infty$. If the Ext groups out of a sheaf do not vanish in high degrees,
compactness fails even for the constant sheaf. Here is an example, outside
our conventions because $\mathbf R$ is neither finite nor algebraically
closed. Take $\ell=2$ and $X=\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf R$. Then sheaves on $X_{\acute{e}t}$ are modules over
$\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbf C/\mathbf R)=\mathbf Z/2$, and
$\Ext^n(\mathbf F_2,\mathbf F_2)=H^n(\mathbf Z/2,\mathbf F_2)=\mathbf F_2$
for all $n\geq0$. Since products of modules are exact and the complexes
$\mathbf F_2[n]$ live in distinct degrees, the sum
$\bigoplus_{n\geq0}\mathbf F_2[n]$ in $D(X_{\acute{e}t},\mathbf F_2)$ is also the
product. Hence

$$
\Hom\Bigl(\mathbf F_2,\bigoplus_{n\ge0}\mathbf F_2[n]\Bigr)
=\prod_{n\ge0}\mathbf F_2
\ne\bigoplus_{n\ge0}\mathbf F_2
=\bigoplus_{n\ge0}\Hom(\mathbf F_2,\mathbf F_2[n]).
$$

By our conventions (Section [sec-Conventions]) the base field is finite or
algebraically closed, so its
$\ell$-cohomological dimension is finite
(Lemma [lemma-cohomological-dimension-base-field]). For finite
coefficients this rules out such examples. In that case both hypotheses
reduce to the fact that classical étale cohomology commutes with direct
sums of unbounded complexes (Section [sec-Compactness-for-finite-coefficients]).
For $\Lambda=E$ or $\overline{\mathbf Q}_\ell$ a bound on the base is not enough, and we
prove instead a bound on $\Ext$ that depends only on the source
(Lemma [lemma-uniform-ext-bound]).
