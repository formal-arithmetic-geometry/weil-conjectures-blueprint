From now on, $\Lambda$ is any admissible coefficient ring.
Lemmas [lemma-uniform-bound-proper-direct-image] and [lemma-proper-direct-image-unbounded-complexes]
treat $E$ and $\overline{\mathbf Q}_\ell$. We use them only through the fact that $Rp_*$ preserves direct
sums for proper $p$. For finite and integral rings this fact has its own
proof, recalled in the next proof.

Proper base change shows, by one and the same argument, that $Rp_*\circ j_!$
does not depend on the compactification and is compatible with
composition and base change. The comparison maps are maps out of an
extension by zero $j_!K$. They are therefore determined by their restriction to
$X$, where they are the identity. On the closed complement of $X$,
proper base change shows that both sides vanish, and so the map is an
isomorphism. For instance, if $h:Z'\to Z$ is proper and
$h^{-1}(X)=X$, then $Rh_*j'_!K$ restricted to $Z\setminus X$ is the direct
image of the restriction of $j'_!K$ to $Z'\setminus X$, which is zero.
Since proper base change is only known on $D^b_{\mathrm{ctf}}$, we first show that all
the functors involved preserve direct sums, so that it suffices to check
isomorphisms on $D^b_{\mathrm{ctf}}$ (Lemma [lemma-checking-isomorphisms-constructible-complexes]).

That both factors of $Rp_*\circ j_!$ preserve the localizing
categories comes from the definition of $\Loc(D^b_{\mathrm{ctf}}(X,\Lambda))$ in the following
form: if the objects of $\Loc(D^b_{\mathrm{ctf}}(X,\Lambda))$ with some property form
a triangulated subcategory that is closed under direct sums and contains
$D^b_{\mathrm{ctf}}(X,\Lambda)$, then every object of $\Loc(D^b_{\mathrm{ctf}}(X,\Lambda))$ has the
property.
