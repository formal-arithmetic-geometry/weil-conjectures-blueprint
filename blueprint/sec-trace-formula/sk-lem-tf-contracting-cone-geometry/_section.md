Here $k$ is algebraically closed, a correspondence $c=(c_1,c_2):C\to X\times X$ is a pair
of morphisms of separated finite-type $k$-schemes, and for a morphism $f$ and an ideal $I$
the ideal $f^\cdot I\,\mathcal O_C$ is generated by the inverse image of $I$. For a closed
subscheme $Z\subset X$ with ideal $I$, $N_ZX=\operatorname{Spec}_Z\bigoplus_{r\geq0}I^r/I^{r+1}$
is its normal cone, with zero section $Z\subset N_ZX$.

Let $c=(c_1,c_2):C\to X\times X$ be a correspondence, let
$Z\hookrightarrow X$ have ideal $I$,
and put

$$
 J=c_1^\cdot I\,\mathcal O_C,\qquad
 L=c_2^\cdot I\,\mathcal O_C,\qquad E=V(J).
$$

Assume $L\subset J$ and $L^n\subset J^{n+1}$ for some $n\geq1$.
Over the trait $T=\operatorname{Spec}k[t]_{(t)}$, whose strict
henselization is the trait $S$ used for nearby cycles, the two legs
lift to the deformations to the normal cones,

$$
 \widetilde c_i:\mathcal D_E C\longrightarrow\mathcal D_ZX,
 \qquad
 \mathcal D_ZX=
 \operatorname{Spec}_{X_T}\mathcal O_{X_T}[I/t],
$$

where $\mathcal D_ZX$ is written over $T$ and $\mathcal D_EC$ is
defined in the same way from $J$. Their special fibres
$\gamma_i:N_EC\to N_ZX$ satisfy:

1. **(1)** $\gamma_1^{-1}(Z)=E$ scheme-theoretically, where $Z$ and $E$
are the zero sections of the two normal cones;
2. **(2)** the image of $\gamma_2$ is set-theoretically contained in the
zero section $Z\subset N_ZX$.
