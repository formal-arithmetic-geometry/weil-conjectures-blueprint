On $F$ the two legs agree, so both $J$ and $L$ restrict to $H$, and restricting
$L^n\subset J^{n+1}$ gives $H^n\subset H^{n+1}$. The reverse inclusion always holds, so the
coherent ideal $M=H^n$ satisfies $M=HM$.

At a point of $V(H)$, the stalk of $H$ lies in the maximal ideal of the local ring, and
Nakayama's lemma gives $M=0$ in the local ring; since $M$ is coherent, $M$ vanishes on a
neighbourhood of the point. At a point outside $V(H)$, $H$ and hence $M$ is the unit ideal.
So $V(H)=V(M)$ is open as well as closed. It is nonempty because $a(F)$ meets $Z$, and $F$ is
connected, so $V(H)=F$. Then $M=0$ near every point, that is, $H^n=0$. Hence
$a^{-1}(Z)=V(H)$ has the same reduction as $F$.
