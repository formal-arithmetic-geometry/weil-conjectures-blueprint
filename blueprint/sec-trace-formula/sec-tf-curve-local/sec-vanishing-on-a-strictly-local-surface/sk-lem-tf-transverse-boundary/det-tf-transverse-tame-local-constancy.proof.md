*Reduction to one divisor.* On the open subscheme $Z-X$, which contains
$Y-z$, the immersion $i$ restricts to the identity of $(Z-X)-Y$, so
$i_!E$ restricts to $E$ there and $M|_{Z-X}=Rj'_*E$ with
$j':(Z-X)-Y\hookrightarrow Z-X$. Only the regular divisor $Y$ remains.

*Tameness.* Wild inertia is pro-$p$ and $\ell\ne p$, so monodromy
through a finite $\ell$-group is tame along $Y$. By Abhyankar's lemma
along the regular divisor $Y$
([Stacks, Tag 0EYG](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0EYG)),
étale locally on $Y$ each cohomology sheaf of $E$ is trivialized by a
Kummer cover extracting a root of a local parameter of $Y$, followed by
an unramified cover.

*Stalks.* Consequently, at every geometric point of $Y-z$ the stalk of
$Rj'_*$ of a cohomology sheaf of $E$ is the continuous cohomology of
the same tame inertia representation. The pro-$\ell$ part of tame
inertia is $\mathbf Z_\ell(1)$; its cohomology on a finite
$\ell$-primary module is computed by the two-term complex given by a
topological generator minus one, and the other prime-to-$p$ factors of
tame inertia have exact invariants on these coefficients, their orders
being prime to $\ell$. The local parameter and the unramified cover can
be chosen on an étale neighbourhood, so this description, including the
specialization maps between the stalks, is constant on that
neighbourhood: the specialization maps along $Y-z$ are isomorphisms and
the cohomology sheaves of $Rj'_*$ of each cohomology sheaf of $E$ are
finite locally constant on $Y-z$.

*Complexes.* For the bounded complex $E$, the hypercohomology spectral
sequence from the cohomology sheaves of $E$ is bounded and compatible
with specialization, so the same holds for $M|_{Y-z}$.
