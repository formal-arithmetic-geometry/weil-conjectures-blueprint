*(1).* The strict localization $T$ is the limit of the affine étale
neighbourhoods $(V,v)$ of $t$, and $D$, $H$ descend to closed curves on
some such neighbourhood. Blowing up commutes with flat base change, so
$\widetilde T$ is the limit of the blowups $\mathrm{Bl}_vV$, which are
smooth surfaces, with transition maps that are base changes of the
affine transition maps of the $V$; the open subscheme $Z$ is the limit
of the corresponding open subschemes. The exceptional divisor $P$ is a
projective line, the projectivized tangent plane at $t$. The strict
transform $D'$ meets $P$ in the finitely many tangent directions of the
branches of $D$, so $Y=P-D'$ is a projective line with finitely many
points removed: it is a regular, connected divisor on $Z$. Since $H$ is
regular at $t$, its strict transform $H'\to H$ is the blowup of a
Cartier divisor on a regular curve, hence an isomorphism, so $H'$ is
regular; it meets $P$ transversely in the single point $z$ given by
$T_tH$. The hypothesis $C_tD\cap T_tH=\{0\}$ says $z\notin D'$, so
$z\in Y$, and $X=H'\cap Z$ is a regular divisor on $Z$ with
$X\cap Y=\{z\}$, the intersection being transverse.

*(2).* The blowup is an isomorphism over $T-t$. Since $t\in D$ and
$t\in H$, one has $f^{-1}(D)=D'\cup P$ and $f^{-1}(H)=H'\cup P$ as sets.
Hence $Z-Y=\widetilde T-(D'\cup P)=f^{-1}(T-D)\simeq T-D$, and likewise
$Z-(X\cup Y)=\widetilde T-(D'\cup H'\cup P)\simeq T-(D\cup H)$. The
open immersions correspond under these isomorphisms.

*(3).* By (2), the pair $(Z-Y,\,Z-(X\cup Y))$ is isomorphic to
$(T-D,\,T-(D\cup H))$, carrying $E'$ to $E$ and $i_!E'$ to $i_!E$; this
gives the identification of cohomology. Being finite locally constant
with a given monodromy group is preserved under isomorphism.
