Since $\Phi_X$ is the base change of a morphism of $X_0$, it maps
$U=U_0\times_{k_0}k$ into itself for every open $U_0\subset X_0$, and the
restriction to $U$ of the fixed scheme of $\Phi_X^m$ is the fixed scheme of
$\Phi_U^m$. As $X_0$ is quasi-compact, we may therefore assume that $X_0$
is a closed subscheme of $\mathbf A^r_{\mathbf F_q}$, with coordinates
$t_1,\ldots,t_r$. Since the equations of $X_0$ have coefficients in $\mathbf F_q$,
we have $(\Phi_X^m)^\#t_i=t_i^{q^m}$, so the fixed scheme, the pullback of
the diagonal along the graph, is cut out by the defining equations
together with $t_i^{q^m}-t_i=0$. Since each
polynomial $t^{q^m}-t$ is separable, the algebra
$k[t_1,\ldots,t_r]/(t_i^{q^m}-t_i)_i$ is a product of copies of $k$
indexed by $\mathbf F_{q^m}^r$. Its quotient by the defining equations
retains exactly the factors at the rational solutions of those
equations, which gives the assertion. The argument uses only the
equations of $X_0$, so $X_0$ need not be reduced.
