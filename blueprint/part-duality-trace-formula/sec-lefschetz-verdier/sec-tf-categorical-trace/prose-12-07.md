Both sides of the trace formula are traces of endomorphisms of perfect
$A$-complexes, namely of $R\Gamma_c(X,K)$ on the global side and of the
stalks of $K$ on the local side.

Over a field the trace of an endomorphism of a complex is the
alternating sum of its traces on cohomology. Over $A=\mathbf Z/\ell^2$
the cohomology modules need not be free, so they have no trace, and the
two endomorphisms of $[A\xrightarrow{\ell}A]$ in
Example [sk-ex-tf-trace-example] below agree on cohomology but have
traces $0$ and $\ell$. The trace must therefore be defined on the
complex. On a bounded complex of finite projective modules the
alternating sum of the traces of the components works, and the
definition below expresses it through evaluation and coevaluation, so
that it depends only on the morphism in $D(A)$. In this form it is
compatible with every change of rings (Proposition [sk-p-trace]),
which is how the trace formula passes from $\mathcal O_E/\pi_E^n$ to
$\mathcal O_E$ and $E$ (Section [sec-tf-coefficient-transport]), and on the
point it is the local term (Lemma [sk-p-pairing]).
