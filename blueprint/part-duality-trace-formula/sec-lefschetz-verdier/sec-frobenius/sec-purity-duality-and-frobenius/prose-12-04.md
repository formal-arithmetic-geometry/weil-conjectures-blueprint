For a smooth $X_0$ of pure dimension $d$ and $K$ locally perfect, so
that $\mathbb D_XK\simeq K^\vee(d)[2d]$, cup product pairs $H^i_c(X,K)$
with $H^{2d-i}(X,K^\vee)$ and takes values in $H^{2d}_c(X,\Lambda)$. To
use this pairing for the Weil conjectures we need to know how $F$ acts
on both sides. The target is not the trivial Weil complex. It is
$\Lambda(-d)$, as we saw above for $\mathbf P^1$, and if we forgot the twist we
would predict the relation $\alpha\leftrightarrow1/\alpha$ between
eigenvalues in complementary degrees instead of
$\alpha\leftrightarrow q^d/\alpha$. Poincaré duality was built from
the purity isomorphism $\gamma_f$ and the trace map, so it suffices to
check that these two maps commute with pullback along Frobenius. This
is a statement about natural transformations, and the only point that
is not transport of structure is the identification of Tate twists,
where we use that pullback along $\operatorname{Fr}_T$ respects Kummer
classes (Proposition [sk-prop-prop-sp-frobenius-purity]). The compatibility
of the duality pairing with Weil structures then follows formally
(Theorem [sk-thm-frobenius]).

Let $f$ be obtained from a smooth morphism $f_0:X_0\to Y_0$ over
$k_0=\mathbf F_q$ by extension to $k$, and let $\operatorname{Fr}_X$,
$\operatorname{Fr}_Y$ be the geometric Frobenius maps above. The isomorphism
$\gamma_f$ of (10.1) and the trace map commute with pullback by Frobenius as natural
transformations, before any Weil structure is chosen.
