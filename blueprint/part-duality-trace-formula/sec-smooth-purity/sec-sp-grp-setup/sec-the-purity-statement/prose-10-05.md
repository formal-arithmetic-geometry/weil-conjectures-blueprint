For étale $f$ we have $d=0$, and the theorem says that
$f^{!}\simeq f^{*}$ for every admissible $\Lambda$. For a smooth curve $C$
with structure map $a:C\to\operatorname{Spec}k$, it gives
$a^!\mathbf Z_{\ell,\operatorname{Spec}k}\simeq\mathbf Z_{\ell,C}(1)[2]$,
a single sheaf in degree $-2$.

For smooth $f$ the whole functor $f^!$ is determined by its value on
the constant sheaf, through the map
$f^*K\otimes^{\mathbf L}f^!\Lambda_Y\to f^!K$ of
(10.9). This needs smoothness. Let $i:y\to C$ be the
inclusion of a rational point of a smooth curve, with open complement
$j:U\to C$. The analogous map $i^*K\otimes^{\mathbf L}i^!\Lambda_C\to i^!K$
has source zero for $K=j_!\Lambda_U$, since the stalk of $j_!\Lambda_U$
at $y$ vanishes, whereas the localization triangle gives
$i^!j_!\Lambda_U\simeq(i^*Rj_*\Lambda_U)[-1]\neq0$. We give $f^!\Lambda_Y$ a
name rather than working with $\Lambda_X(d)[2d]$ directly, because the
two are not yet known to be isomorphic: $f^!\Lambda_Y$ is attached to
$f$ without choices and satisfies base change
(10.11), and proving the theorem amounts to
constructing an isomorphism with $\Lambda_X(d)[2d]$ that does not
depend on charts. It
plays the role of the shifted orientation sheaf $\mathrm{or}_{X/Y}[m]$,
and for the structure map of a smooth scheme it is the dualizing
complex of Poincaré duality (Corollary [sk-cor-sp-all-coefficients]
and Theorem [sk-thm-sf-identification]).
