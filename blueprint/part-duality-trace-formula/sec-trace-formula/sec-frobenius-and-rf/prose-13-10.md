The trace formula with finite coefficients is proved by induction on the
number of affine coordinates. We project a closed subscheme of
$\mathbf A^d$ to $\mathbf A^{d-1}$, apply the induction hypothesis to
$Rf_!K$, and identify each stalk of $Rf_!K$ at a rational point with
the cohomology of the fibre, a closed subscheme of $\mathbf A^1$ over a
larger finite field. For this, $Rf_!K$ must carry a Frobenius
correspondence whose operator on $R\Gamma_c$ is that of $\psi$ and whose
stalk maps are the Frobenius operators on the cohomology of the fibres.
The square formed by $f$ and the two Frobenii commutes, but it is
usually not cartesian. Already for $f:\mathbf A^1\to s$ the Frobenius
of $s$ is the identity, so the fibre product is $\mathbf A^1$ itself,
while $\Phi_{\mathbf A^1}$ has degree $q$. The relative Frobenius
measures the difference. It is a universal homeomorphism, so pullback
along it is an equivalence on étale sheaves, and the comparison it
provides is an isomorphism. The next lemma describes the induced
Frobenius correspondence on $Rf_!K$ and its operators; part (4) is the
identity behind Corollary [sk-cor-tf-trace-function-pushforward]. The same
base change arguments give additivity along an open subscheme and its
closed complement (Lemma [sk-i-global-additivity]), which we
use to pass from affine to general schemes.
