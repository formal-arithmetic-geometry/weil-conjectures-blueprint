For $f=a_X$, the exchange formulas become dualities between
compactly supported and ordinary cohomology (compare (PDBP)).
Over an algebraically closed field the target of $a_X$ is the category
of perfect $\Lambda$-complexes, where $\mathbb D_s$ is
$R\!\operatorname{Hom}_\Lambda(-,\Lambda)$, and the first two Verdier exchange formulas
read as two statements about cohomology
(Theorem [sk-thm-sf-global-duality]). This is global Verdier duality, and
it holds for every $X$ of finite type, smooth or not. For
$X=\mathbf A^1_k$ and $K=\Lambda$ it already contains a familiar
computation: $\omega_{\mathbf A^1}=\Lambda(1)[2]$, so
$R\Gamma(\mathbf A^1,\omega)=\Lambda(1)[2]$ is the dual of
$R\Gamma_c(\mathbf A^1,\Lambda)=\Lambda(-1)[-2]$.

The point of the subsection is the case of smooth $X$ of pure
dimension $d$. There $\omega_X\simeq\Lambda_X(d)[2d]$, for locally
perfect $K$ we have $\mathbb D_XK\simeq K^\vee(d)[2d]$ with $K^\vee$ the
ordinary dual, and the counit of
$Ra_{X!}\dashv a_X^!$ becomes the trace map of Definition [sk-def-sp-trace].
Global duality then becomes the Poincaré pairing
$R\Gamma_c(X,K)\otimes^{\mathbf L}R\Gamma(X,K^\vee(d))[2d]\to\Lambda$ of
Theorem [sk-thm-sf-identification], the form of Weil's axiom (c) that is
used with Frobenius in Theorem [sk-thm-frobenius]. Over a field it gives
perfect pairings degree by degree (Corollary [sk-thm-derived-duality]). Over
$\mathcal O_E$ it does not, and Remark [sk-rem-pd-integral-corrections]
says what survives on cohomology groups.
