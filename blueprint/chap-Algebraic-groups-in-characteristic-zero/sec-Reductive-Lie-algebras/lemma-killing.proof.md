We first use Cartan's criterion to show that the kernel of $B$ is a
solvable ideal, hence zero; nondegeneracy then identifies the orthogonal
of the derived algebra with the center. By invariance of $B$, that is,
$B([x,y],z)=B(x,[y,z])$, the kernel $\mathfrak k$ of $B$
is an ideal. For $x\in[\mathfrak k,\mathfrak k]$ and $y\in\mathfrak k$,
the operators $\operatorname{ad}x$ and $\operatorname{ad}y$ map
$\mathfrak l$ into $\mathfrak k$, so the trace of
$\operatorname{ad}x\operatorname{ad}y$ may be computed on $\mathfrak k$.
Since $x\in\mathfrak k$, we obtain

$$
\operatorname{tr}_{\mathfrak k}
(\operatorname{ad}(x)|_{\mathfrak k}
\operatorname{ad}(y)|_{\mathfrak k})=B(x,y)=0.
$$

By Cartan's criterion (Lemma [lemma-cartan-s-criterion]), the
image of $\mathfrak k$ in $\operatorname{End}(\mathfrak k)$ is
solvable. The kernel of $\mathfrak k\to\operatorname{End}(\mathfrak k)$
is the center of $\mathfrak k$, which is abelian. So $\mathfrak k$
is a solvable ideal of $\mathfrak l$. Since $\operatorname{rad}\mathfrak l=0$,
we get $\mathfrak k=0$, that is, $B$ is nondegenerate. Invariance and nondegeneracy then give

$$
[\mathfrak l,\mathfrak l]^\perp=Z(\mathfrak l).
$$

The center is a solvable ideal, hence zero, and a subspace with zero
orthogonal complement is all of $\mathfrak l$.
