For $\mathfrak{gl}_n$ the decomposition into scalars and trace-zero
matrices, $\mathfrak{gl}_n=\Omega\cdot1\oplus\mathfrak{sl}_n$, has the form
$\mathfrak l=Z(\mathfrak l)+[\mathfrak l,\mathfrak l]$. We prove this
decomposition for every Lie algebra with a faithful semisimple representation
(Proposition [proposition-lie-algebras-faithful-semisimple-representation]),
as Lemma [lemma-identity-component] requires. The proof has two
steps. First, the radical acts by scalars on each simple summand. To see
this, we take a common eigenvector given by Lie's theorem. A trace
computation, in which we divide by the dimension of a subspace, shows
that its eigenspace is stable under all of $\mathfrak l$. Since the
representation is faithful, the radical is then central.
Second, the quotient by the center has zero radical. For such a Lie
algebra the Killing form is nondegenerate by Cartan's criterion. This
forces the algebra to equal its derived algebra
(Lemma [lemma-killing]). The upper triangular
algebra $\mathfrak b$ of the previous subsection shows that the
hypothesis of semisimplicity cannot be dropped.
