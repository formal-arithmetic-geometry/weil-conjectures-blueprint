See [humphreys72, §4.3]. It suffices to show that every element $X$
of $[\mathfrak a,\mathfrak a]$ is nilpotent. Then
$[\mathfrak a,\mathfrak a]$ is solvable by
Lemma [lemma-engel-s-theorem], and hence so is $\mathfrak a$.
To see that $X$ is nilpotent, one pairs $X$ with operators that are
polynomials in its semisimple part. Let $\lambda_i$ be the eigenvalues
of the semisimple part, and let $f$ be a $\mathbf Q$-linear functional
on their $\mathbf Q$-span with values in $\mathbf Q$. The hypothesis
gives $\sum_i\lambda_i f(\lambda_i)=0$. Applying $f$ to this identity
gives $\sum_i f(\lambda_i)^2=0$. This is a vanishing sum of squares of
rational numbers, so $f$ vanishes on all $\lambda_i$, and $f=0$. As
$f$ was arbitrary, all $\lambda_i$ are zero. This step is where we
use characteristic zero.
