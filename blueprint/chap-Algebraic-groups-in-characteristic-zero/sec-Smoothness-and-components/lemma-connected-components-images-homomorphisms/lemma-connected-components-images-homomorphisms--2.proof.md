For the proper-source statement, factor $f$ through its
scheme-theoretic image as $G\xrightarrow{f'}Z\xrightarrow{j}G'$.
The map $f'$ is proper and dominant, so its image is closed and
dense, hence all of $Z$. The closed immersion $j$ makes $Z$ locally
of finite type over $k$.

The map $f'$ is scheme-theoretically dominant. Since every scheme
over $k$ is flat over $k$, the same is true of $f'\times f'$.
The homomorphism identities for $f$ show that the multiplication and
inverse of $G'$, restricted to $Z$, factor through $j$: vanishing
after a scheme-theoretically dominant map gives the required
containment of ideal sheaves. The unit factors through $j$ as well.
The group axioms descend through the monomorphism $j$, giving the
group scheme structure and its homomorphism to $G'$.

For the constructible-subgroup statement, $Y$ contains a nonempty
open subset $U$ of its irreducible closure $T$. The closure $T$ is
stable under inversion and translation by its rational points, by
continuity and the subgroup law on $S$. For $x\in T(k)$, both $U$
and $xU^{-1}$ are nonempty open subsets of $T$. They meet by
irreducibility, and their intersection contains a closed point
$z$, which is rational since $k$ is algebraically closed. Thus
$z$ and $z^{-1}x$ belong to $Y(k)$ and hence to $S$, giving
$x=z(z^{-1}x)\in S$.
