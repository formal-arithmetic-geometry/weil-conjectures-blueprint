For $X_0=\mathbf P^1_{\mathbf F_q}$ we have $d=1$, $b_0=b_2=1$, $b_1=0$, so
$\chi=2$, and $D=\det(F_0)\det(F_2)=1\cdot q=q$, so that $\varepsilon=1$. The
corollary predicts $Z(\mathbf P^1,1/(qT))=qT^2Z(\mathbf P^1,T)$. Directly,

$$
\frac1{(1-(qT)^{-1})(1-T^{-1})}
=\frac{qT^2}{(qT-1)(T-1)}
=\frac{qT^2}{(1-T)(1-qT)}.
$$

Here the factor $T^{\chi}$ is not trivial, unlike for the elliptic curve, and
the constant $q^{d\chi/2}=q$ comes from the twist $(-1)$ on $H^2$.
