Both complexes have cohomology $\mathbf F_\ell$ in degree zero,
$\mathbf F_\ell(-1)$ in degree one, and zero in all other degrees.
For the surface this follows from localization, the acyclicity of the
strictly local schemes $S,D_2$, and the purity identity
$i_{D_2}^!\mathbf F_\ell=\mathbf F_\ell(-1)[-2]$. This identity, in turn, comes by transitivity from the dualizing complexes of the smooth schemes $S$ and $D_2$.

For the punctured trait $D_1-z$, vanishing in degrees $\geq2$ follows from
Proposition [proposition-cohomology-smooth-affine-curve], by passing to the
filtered limit of punctured neighbourhoods that are curves. In degree one the
Kummer sequence gives $H^1(-,\mu_\ell)=k'^*/k'^{*\ell}$, where $k'$ is the
fraction field. Moreover, the units of the strictly henselian trait are $\ell$-divisible.
Hence the valuation induces $k'^*/k'^{*\ell}\simeq\mathbf Z/\ell$, and the
class of a uniformizer is a generator.

To see that the restriction map itself, and not only the two sides, is an
isomorphism, we find a generator of $H^1$ on each side. The restriction is clearly the identity in degree zero. In degree one, let $g$ be a local equation of $D_2$. Since
the regular local ring of $S$ is factorial, $\operatorname{Pic}(S-D_2)=0$
and the units on $S-D_2$ are the units of $S$ times the powers of $g$.
As the units of $S$ are $\ell$-divisible, the Kummer sequence shows that
$H^1(S-D_2,\mu_\ell)\simeq\mathbf Z/\ell$, generated by the class of $g$.
Since $D_1$ and $D_2$ meet transversely, $g$ restricts to a uniformizer
of $D_1$, whose class generates $H^1(D_1-z,\mu_\ell)$. So the
restriction is an isomorphism in degree one as well, and since both
complexes vanish in the other degrees, it is an isomorphism.
