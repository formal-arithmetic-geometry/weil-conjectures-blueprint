*Step 0: reduction to an integral $X$.* Since $X$ is smooth over $k$, its
local rings are regular ([lem-sc-smooth-regular]), hence domains, so
distinct irreducible components of $X$ do not meet and the connected
components of $X$ are integral. As $X$ is quasi-compact (proper over $k$)
and locally Noetherian, there are finitely many of them,
$X=X_1\sqcup\dots\sqcup X_m$, each open and closed, integral, smooth of
relative dimension $1$ and proper over $k$, and
$H^i(X,\mathcal{F})=\bigoplus_jH^i(X_j,\mathcal{F}|_{X_j})$ with
$\mathcal F|_{X_j}$ quasi-coherent. Hence we may and do assume that $X$ is
integral.

*Step 1: a finite morphism to $\mathbf P^1_k$.* Let $k(X)$ be the function
field of $X$ ([def-sc-function-field]). It contains an element
transcendental over $k$. Otherwise, for a nonempty affine open
$\operatorname{Spec}(A)\subseteq X$, the domain $A\subseteq k(X)$ would be a
finitely generated $k$-algebra generated by algebraic elements, hence a
finite-dimensional $k$-algebra which is a domain, hence a field; then
$\operatorname{Spec}(A)$ would be a single point. But a nonempty open
subscheme of the integral one-dimensional scheme $X$ is one-dimensional
([def-pc-dimension]), a contradiction. Choose $f\in k(X)$ transcendental
over $k$. By [lem-sc-morphism-from-function] the morphism
$\pi=\pi_f:X\to\mathbf P^1_k$ is finite, in particular affine.

*Step 2: transfer to $\mathbf P^1_k$.* Since $\pi$ is affine and
$\mathcal F$ quasi-coherent, [lem-pc-affine-morphism-hi] gives
$R^q\pi_*\mathcal F=0$ for $q>0$ and $\pi_*\mathcal F$ quasi-coherent. The
Leray spectral sequence ([pqf-lem-leray])
$E_2^{p,q}=H^p(\mathbf P^1_k,R^q\pi_*\mathcal F)\Rightarrow H^{p+q}(X,\mathcal F)$
is therefore concentrated on the row $q=0$, so it degenerates and

$$
H^i(X,\mathcal{F})\cong H^i(\mathbf P^1_k,\pi_*\mathcal{F})\qquad\text{for all }i\ge0 .
$$

By [lem-sc-p1-hi-vanishing] the right-hand side vanishes for $i\ge2$, hence
$H^i(X,\mathcal{F})=0$ for all $i\ge2$.
