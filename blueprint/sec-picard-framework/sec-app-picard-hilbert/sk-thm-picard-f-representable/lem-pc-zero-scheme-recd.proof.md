$\mathcal{M}:=\mathcal{L}\otimes f_T^*\mathcal{N}^{-1}$ is an invertible module on $X_T$ and
$s\in H^0(X_T,\mathcal{M})$. By
[def-pc-zero-scheme] the ideal sheaf
$\mathcal{I}_{Z(s)}=\operatorname{im}(s^\vee)$ is locally generated by one
element, i.e. $D=Z(s)$ is a locally principal closed subscheme ([lem-pc-zero-scheme-properties](1)) of
$X_T$. Also $X_T\to T$ is smooth (base change of the smooth structure
morphism, [lem-pc-proper-base-change]), hence flat
([lem-pc-smooth-flat]), of finite presentation, and of relative
dimension $1$ (fibres are base changes of $X$ by fields, of dimension $1$
by [lem-pc-dim-base-change]).

We verify that every fibre $D_t\subset X_t$ ($t\in T$,
$X_t=X_{\kappa(t)}$) is an effective Cartier divisor on $X_t$; then
$D$ is a relative effective Cartier divisor by [lem-pc-recd-locally-principal],
and the first claim follows.
Let $\kappa=\kappa(t)$ and pass to the fibre. Since $F$ is a subfunctor of
$\operatorname{Pic}_{X/k,\sigma}$ ([lem-pc-f-open-subfunctor]), the pullback
$\mathcal{L}_t$ of $\mathcal L$ to $X_t$ lies in $F(\operatorname{Spec}\kappa)$:
$Rf_{\kappa,*}\mathcal{L}_t$ is an invertible $\mathcal{O}_\kappa$-module, i.e. a
$1$-dimensional $\kappa$-vector space, placed in degree $0$. On the point
$\operatorname{Spec}\kappa$ the higher direct images are the cohomology groups,
so $\dim_\kappa H^0(X_t,\mathcal{L}_t)=1$ and $H^i(X_t,\mathcal{L}_t)=0$ for
all $i\ge1$.
Consider the identical analysis for $\mathcal{O}$. First
$H^0(X,\mathcal O_X)=k$: it is a finite $k$-algebra since $X$ is proper
([lem-pc-coherent-hi]), and by flat base change
([lem-pc-flat-base-change-cohom]) its base change to an algebraic closure
$\bar k$ is $H^0(X_{\bar k},\mathcal O)$, a finite $\bar k$-algebra which is a
domain because $X_{\bar k}$ is integral, hence equal to $\bar k$. So $X_t$ is
proper and smooth of relative dimension $1$ over $\kappa$ with
$H^0(X_t,\mathcal{O})=H^0(X,\mathcal{O})\otimes_k\kappa=\kappa$
([lem-pc-flat-base-change-cohom]) and
$\dim_\kappa H^1(X_t,\mathcal{O})=\dim_\kappa(H^1(X,\mathcal{O})\otimes_k\kappa)=g$
([lem-pc-flat-base-change-cohom], the genus being
$\dim_kH^1(X,\mathcal{O})$, [def-pc-genus]); by
[lem-pc-vanishing-above-dim] the higher cohomology of both vanishes, so [cor-picard-degree-g] applies.
Therefore

$$
\deg(\mathcal{L}_t)=\chi(X_t,\mathcal{L}_t)-\chi(X_t,\mathcal{O}_t)=1-(1-g)=g
$$

([def-pc-degree-line-bundle]; $\chi$ is defined by
[def-pc-euler-characteristic]; the finite-dimensionality and the
finiteness of the sum are [lem-pc-coherent-hi]).

The restriction $s_t\in H^0(X_t,\mathcal{M}_t)$ of $s$, with
$\mathcal{M}_t=\mathcal{L}_t\otimes f_t^*\mathcal{N}_t^{-1}$, is non-zero. To
see this, choose an affine open $V=\operatorname{Spec}(A)\ni t$ of $T$ over
which $\mathcal N$ is trivial, $\mathcal N|_V\cong\mathcal O_V$, and a finite
cover $\mathcal U$ of $X_V$ by affine opens ($X_V\to V$ is proper, hence
separated and quasi-compact). Over $V$ one has $f_{V,*}\mathcal L\cong\mathcal O_V$
and $R^qf_{V,*}\mathcal L=0$ for $q>0$, so by [lem-pc-cech-resolution] the Cech
complex $\check C^\bullet(\mathcal U,\mathcal L)$ has cohomology $A$ in degree
$0$, generated by a section $\ell\in H^0(X_V,\mathcal L)$, and $0$ elsewhere;
it is quasi-isomorphic to $A[0]$. Over $V$ the section $s$ is $\ell$ times the
pullback of a generator of $\mathcal N^{-1}|_V$, since the adjunction map
$f_V^*\mathcal N|_V\to\mathcal L|_{X_V}$ sends the generator $1$ to $\ell$. By
[lem-pc-rf-base-change-flat-module], applied to the affine bases $V$ and
$\operatorname{Spec}\kappa$ and to $\mathcal L$, which is flat over $V$, the
base-change map
$A[0]\otimes_A\kappa=\check C^\bullet(\mathcal U,\mathcal L)\otimes^{\mathbf L}_A\kappa\to\check C^\bullet(\mathcal U_t,\mathcal L_t)$,
induced by restricting sections to $X_t$, is an isomorphism in $D(\kappa)$.
On $H^0$ this says that the restriction $\ell|_{X_t}$ is a basis of the
one-dimensional space $H^0(X_t,\mathcal L_t)$; in particular $\ell|_{X_t}\neq0$
and hence $s_t\neq0$. Since $X_t$ is integral
($X$ being geometrically integral), a non-zero
global section $s_t$ of an invertible module has non-zero stalk
$s_{t,x}\neq0$ in $\mathcal{M}_{t,x}$ for every $x\in X_t$ (the restriction map
$\mathcal{M}_{t,x}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{M}_{t,\eta}$ to the generic stalk is injective
by integrality, and $s_{t,\eta}\neq0$ since $s_t\neq0$). Hence the ideal
$\mathcal{I}_{Z(s_t),x}$ is generated by a non-zero element of the local ring
$\mathcal{O}_{X_t,x}$, which is regular of dimension $1$
([lem-sc-smooth-regular]), hence a DVR
([lem-sc-regular-dim1-dvr]); in a DVR a non-zero element generates
an invertible ideal. Therefore $\mathcal{I}_{Z(s_t)}$ is invertible: $D_t$ is an
effective Cartier divisor.

Thus $D$ is a relative effective Cartier divisor on $X_T/T$ by
[lem-pc-recd-locally-principal]. Since $X_T\to T$ is smooth of
relative dimension $1$ and proper (base change of proper,
[lem-pc-proper-base-change]), [lem-pc-cartier-criterion] gives
(2)$\Rightarrow$(1): $D\to T$ is finite locally free. Its degree: the
degree of the fibre divisor $D_t$ equals the degree of its associated
module,

$$
\deg(D_t)=\deg(\mathcal{O}_{X_t}(D_t))=\deg(\mathcal{M}_t)
=\deg(\mathcal{L}_t\otimes f_t^*\mathcal{N}_t^{-1})=\deg(\mathcal{L}_t)=g ,
$$

by [lem-pc-degree-base-change] (the module $f_t^*\mathcal{N}_t^{-1}$ is a
pullback from a point, hence of degree $0$) and the computation above.
Since $D\to T$ is finite locally free and the fibre degree at $t$ equals
$\dim_{\kappa(t)}\Gamma(D_t,\mathcal{O}_{D_t})=\deg(D_t)=g$, the morphism
$D\to T$ is finite locally free of degree $g$
([def-pc-finite-locally-free]), as claimed.
