Let $C$ be a smooth affine curve over algebraically closed $k$, and let $n$
be prime to $\operatorname{char}k$. Let $\overline C$ be the
compactification and $S=\overline C\setminus C$ its (nonempty finite)
complement; fix $P_0\in S$. Then
$$
\operatorname{Pic}(C)\cong J_{\overline C}(k)/\Lambda,
\qquad
\Lambda:=\langle[P]-[P_0]:P\in S\rangle\subseteq J_{\overline C}(k),
$$
where $J_{\overline C}(k)=\operatorname{Pic}^0(\overline C)$ is the
canonical identification of the Jacobian [def-sc-jacobian] of $\overline C$
([thm-sc-jacobian-representability]), so $\operatorname{Pic}(C)$ is a
quotient of the divisible group $J_{\overline C}(k)$ (there is no degree map
on $\operatorname{Pic}(C)$ itself). In particular $\operatorname{Pic}(C)$ is
$n$-divisible and $\operatorname{Pic}(C)[n]$ is finite.

## Proof

By the compactification lemma there is a proper smooth curve $\overline C$
with $C=\overline C\setminus S$, and $S$ is finite and nonempty: if
$S=\emptyset$ then $C=\overline C$ would be proper and affine, hence finite
of dimension zero, contradicting $\dim C=1$.

The Picard group of a smooth curve is its divisor class group, and the
restriction map
$$
\operatorname{Cl}(\overline C)\to\operatorname{Cl}(C),\qquad D\mapsto D|_C,
$$
is surjective (a divisor of $C$ is a divisor of $\overline C$), with kernel
exactly the subgroup generated by the classes $[P]$, $P\in S$: indeed
$D|_C$ is principal iff $D|_C=\operatorname{div}(f)$ for
$f\in k(C)^*=k(\overline C)^*$, iff $D-\operatorname{div}(f)$ is supported
on $S$. Hence
$$
\bigoplus_{P\in S}\mathbf Z[P]\longrightarrow\operatorname{Pic}(\overline C)
\longrightarrow\operatorname{Pic}(C)\longrightarrow0
$$
is exact. By the decomposition lemma,
$\operatorname{Pic}(\overline C)=\mathbf Z\oplus\operatorname{Pic}^0(\overline C)$
with the $\mathbf Z$-factor generated by $[P_0]$; under this splitting
$[P]\mapsto(1,[P]-[P_0])$, and since $P_0\in S$ the subgroup
$\langle[P]:P\in S\rangle$ contains $(1,0)$, so the quotient is
$$
\operatorname{Pic}(C)\cong J_{\overline C}(k)/\Lambda,\qquad
\Lambda=\langle[P]-[P_0]:P\in S\rangle
$$
([def-sc-jacobian], [thm-sc-jacobian-representability]).

The group $J_{\overline C}(k)$ is $n$-divisible (an isogeny is surjective on
points), hence so is its quotient $\operatorname{Pic}(C)$. Apply
[lem-sc-divisible-quotient-torsion] with $G=J_{\overline C}(k)$. The
subgroup $\Lambda$ is generated by the finite set $\{[P]-[P_0]:P\in S\}$, so
$\Lambda/n\Lambda$ is finite: it is a quotient of
$(\mathbf Z/n\mathbf Z)^{|S|}$. Also $G[n]=J_{\overline C}[n](k)$, and this
is finite because the $n$-torsion subgroup scheme is finite étale. The exact
sequence therefore expresses $\operatorname{Pic}(C)[n]$ as an extension of
two finite groups, proving finiteness.
