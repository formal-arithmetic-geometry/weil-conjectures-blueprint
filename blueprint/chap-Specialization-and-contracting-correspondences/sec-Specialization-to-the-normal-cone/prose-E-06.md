Theorem [theorem-verdier-s-theorem-specialization-zero] asserts that $\tau_{X,Z}$ is an
isomorphism, that is, that the cofibre

$$
C_{X,Z}(L)=\operatorname{cofib}\bigl(
\tau_{X,Z}(L)\bigr)
$$

vanishes. As for $C_Y$, we compute the cofibre in the stable
$\infty$-category, so that $C_{X,Z}$ is an exact functor. The proof has three steps.  First, the base
change maps of the next lemma reduce us to the case $i^*L=0$ and, after
a blow-up, to the case of an effective Cartier divisor.  Then, for a
principal divisor in a normal scheme, an explicit contraction
of the affine line shows that $C_{X,Z}(L)$ vanishes at the generic points of
$Z$.  Finally, after an embedding into affine space, projection along a line and
induction on the dimension show that the remaining support is empty.
