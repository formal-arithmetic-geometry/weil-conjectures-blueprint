As in Proposition [proposition-specialization-trace-class], write $F=\Fix(c)$ for
the fixed scheme, and let $d=c_1|_F=c_2|_F:F\to X$ be the common map.
We will show that a connected component of $F$ that meets $Z$ lies in
$Z$ set-theoretically, and that the deformed fixed scheme is constant up
to nilpotents. Both follow from the hypothesis $I_2^n\subset I_1^{n+1}$,
because the two legs agree on $F$.

The deformation of $F$ to the normal cone of $d^{-1}(Z)$ embeds in the
fixed scheme of the deformed correspondence. Put $I_F=d^{-1}(I)\mathcal O_F$, so
that $d^{-1}(Z)=V(I_F)$. Since $d=c_1|_F$, the ideal $I_F^r$ is the
image of $I_1^r$ in $\mathcal O_F$ for every $r$. Hence the closed
immersion $F\hookrightarrow C$ induces a surjection of deformation
algebras

$$
\mathcal O_{C_T}[I_1/t]\twoheadrightarrow
\mathcal O_{F_T}[I_F/t],
$$

that is, a closed immersion
$\mathcal D_{d^{-1}(Z)}F\hookrightarrow\mathcal D_{Z'}C$. Its two
composites with $\widetilde c_1$ and $\widetilde c_2$ are the maps
induced by $c_1|_F$ and $c_2|_F$, which are equal. So it factors through
the fixed scheme $\widetilde F=\Fix(\widetilde c)$ of
$\widetilde c=(\widetilde c_1,\widetilde c_2)$. We write

$$
\iota_F:\mathcal D_{d^{-1}(Z)}F\longrightarrow\widetilde F
\tag{E.20}
$$

for the resulting closed immersion.
