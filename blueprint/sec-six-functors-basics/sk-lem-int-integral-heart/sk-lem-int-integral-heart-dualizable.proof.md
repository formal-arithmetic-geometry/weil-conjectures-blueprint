Locally on $S$, $C$ is $N\otimes_{\mathcal O_E}\mathcal O_{E,S}$ for a finitely
generated $\mathcal O_E$-module $N$, and since $\mathcal O_E$ is a discrete
valuation ring, $N$ has a free resolution
$F=[\mathcal O_E^a\xrightarrow{\phi}\mathcal O_E^b]$ in degrees $-1,0$. As
$\underline{\mathcal O_E}\to\mathcal O_{E,S}$ is flat, $C$ is locally
quasi-isomorphic to the perfect complex
$F_S=F\otimes_{\mathcal O_E}\mathcal O_{E,S}$. A perfect complex is dualizable,
with dual the termwise dual complex
$F_S^\vee=[\mathcal O_{E,S}^b\xrightarrow{\phi^T}\mathcal O_{E,S}^a]$ in degrees
$0,1$, and the evaluation $F_S^\vee\otimes F_S\to\mathcal O_{E,S}$ and
coevaluation $\mathcal O_{E,S}\to F_S\otimes F_S^\vee$ are given by the standard
formulas. These local evaluation and coevaluation maps are the canonical maps
attached to $C^\vee=R\mathcal{H}om(C,\mathcal O_{E,S})$, so they glue; the
triangle identities hold locally, hence globally. The Tor amplitude of a
complex of flat modules in degrees $[m,n]$ lies in $[m,n]$, which gives
$[-1,0]$ for $C$ and $[0,1]$ for $C^\vee$. For $N=\mathcal O_E/\pi_E$ the
resolution is $[\mathcal O_E\xrightarrow{\pi_E}\mathcal O_E]$, so
$M\otimes^{\mathbf L}C$ is the cone of $\pi_E$ on $M$ (degrees $[-1,0]$ for a
module $M$), and the dual complex
$[\mathcal O_E\xrightarrow{\pi_E}\mathcal O_E]$ in degrees $0,1$ is
$\mathcal O_E/\pi_E[-1]$.
