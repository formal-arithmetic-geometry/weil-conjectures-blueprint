Étale sheaves on $\Spec K$ are the same as discrete modules over the absolute
Galois group, and étale cohomology is Galois cohomology (Stacks Project, Tag
03QQ). For an algebraically closed field the Galois group is trivial, so
$\operatorname{cd}_\ell(K)=0$ ([lem-sf-separably-closed-torsion-cd]). Let $K$ be
finite with $q$ elements. Its finite extensions are the fields
$\mathbf F_{q^n}$ of roots of $T^{q^n}-T$, with cyclic Galois group generated by
Frobenius of order exactly $n$ (a smaller order $d$ would give $q^n$ roots of
$T^{q^d}-T$). Hence the absolute Galois group is $\widehat{\mathbf Z}$
([det-base-field-cd-galois-group]). We show
$H^s(\widehat{\mathbf Z},M)=0$ for $s\geq2$ and every torsion discrete module
$M$; this gives $\operatorname{cd}_\ell(K)\leq1$
([det-base-field-cd-finite-field]).

*Continuous cochains compute derived functors.* For a profinite group $G$ and a
discrete $G$-module $M$, continuous inhomogeneous cochains form a
$\delta$-functor: a continuous cochain has finite image, so it lifts through a
surjection of modules by lifting its finitely many values. It is effaceable.
Embed $M$ in the coinduced module $I=\operatorname{Map}_{\mathrm{cont}}(G,M)$ by
$m\mapsto(g\mapsto gm)$, with $(hf)(g)=f(gh)$; this action is discrete because a
locally constant function on $G$ is invariant under an open subgroup. In
homogeneous form, $I$-valued equivariant cochains $F$ correspond to continuous
functions $f\colon G^{r+1}\to M$ via $F(g_0,\ldots,g_r)(t)=f(tg_0,\ldots,tg_r)$,
with the alternating-sum differential, and inserting $1$ as a first coordinate
is a contracting homotopy in positive degrees. By Stacks Project, Tag 010T,
continuous cochain cohomology is therefore the right derived functor of
invariants ([det-base-field-cd-cochains]).

*Reduction to finite cyclic groups.* A torsion discrete
$\widehat{\mathbf Z}$-module is the union of its finite stable submodules (the
orbit of an element is finite, and its elements have a common finite order), and
continuous cochains commute with this union since they have finite image. So let
$M$ be finite, with action through $C_m=\mathbf Z/m$. A continuous function on a
power of $\widehat{\mathbf Z}$ factors through a finite quotient, so the cochain
complex of $\widehat{\mathbf Z}$ is the colimit, under inflation, of those of
$C_{m'}$ for multiples $m'$ of $m$.

*Inflation kills high degrees.* For $C_m=\langle\sigma\rangle$, the periodic free
resolution of $\mathbf Z$ over $\mathbf Z[C_m]$ has differentials alternating
between $\sigma-1$ and $N_m=1+\sigma+\cdots+\sigma^{m-1}$; it is exact, because
the kernel of $\sigma-1$ is spanned by $N_m$ and the kernel of $N_m$ consists of
the elements with coefficient sum zero, which form the image of $\sigma-1$. For
$C_{rm}\to C_m$, the quotient map in degrees $0,1$ and $r^{\lfloor s/2\rfloor}$
times the quotient map in degree $s$ is a chain map, because
$N_{rm}\mapsto rN_m$. It lifts the identity of $\mathbf Z$, so it computes
inflation (two lifts are homotopic, by lifting recursively through the exact
free resolution). On cohomology with values in $M$, inflation in degree $s$ is
therefore multiplication by $r^{\lfloor s/2\rfloor}$, which is zero for $s\geq2$
when the exponent of $M$ divides $r$. Hence the colimit vanishes in degrees
$s\geq2$ ([det-base-field-cd-inflation]).
