The hypotheses on $\mathcal C$ make the K-flat complexes a left derivability structure
for the quasi-isomorphisms. Every complex has a K-flat resolution: the functorial flat
resolutions resolve each term, and the resulting double complex is totalized using the
countable coproducts. The exactness of $\mathbf Z$-indexed colimits and their
compatibility with $\otimes$ make this totalization K-flat and quasi-isomorphic to the
given complex. Any two resolutions are compared through a third one. By hypothesis, $F$
sends quasi-isomorphisms between K-flat complexes to quasi-isomorphisms. So
$Q\circ F$, restricted to K-flat complexes, inverts the quasi-isomorphisms and descends to
$D(\mathcal C)$; this descended functor is $LF$. The general theory of derivability
structures then shows that $LF$, with the counit induced by the resolutions, is the total
left derived functor. It also shows that a functor with a counit is the left derived
functor exactly when its counit is invertible on the objects of the derivability
structure, i.e. on the K-flat complexes.
