An exact functor preserves cohomology, hence quasi-isomorphisms, so $D(F)$ is defined and
$Q\circ F\cong D(F)\circ Q$. A functor that already inverts the quasi-isomorphisms is its
own derived functor: $D(F)$ with this isomorphism as unit satisfies the universal property
of [def-sfb-right-derived-functor]. Right derived functors are unique up to unique
isomorphism compatible with the units, which gives $RF\cong D(F)$.
