The multiplication $R\times R\to R$ is $\mathbf Z$-bilinear by the distributive laws, so $\mu_R$
exists and is unique. The map $\eta_R$ is additive.

1. Since $R\otimes_{\mathbf Z}R\otimes_{\mathbf Z}R$ is generated by pure tensors, it suffices to
   check associativity on $a\otimes b\otimes c$, where both sides give $(ab)c=a(bc)$. Under the
   left unitor $\mathbf Z\otimes_{\mathbf Z}R\cong R$, $n\otimes b\mapsto nb$, the composite
   $\mu_R(\eta_R\otimes\mathrm{id})$ sends $n\otimes b$ to $(n\cdot1)b=nb$; so it is $\lambda_R$.
   The right unit law is the same computation on $a\otimes n$.
2. If $f$ is a ring homomorphism, then $f\mu_R(a\otimes b)=f(ab)=f(a)f(b)=\mu_{R'}(f\otimes f)(a\otimes b)$
   on generators, and $f(\eta_R(n))=f(n\cdot1)=n\cdot1=\eta_{R'}(n)$. Functoriality is clear since
   the underlying maps are the same.
3. $\mu_R\beta_{R,R}(a\otimes b)=\mu_R(b\otimes a)=ba=ab$ on generators.
