Finite Tor amplitude is a condition on the whole complex, not on its
cohomology sheaves. The category
$D^b_{\mathrm{ctf}}$ is thick but in general not stable under standard truncations.
For example, for $A=\mathbf Z/\ell^2$ the complex $[A\xrightarrow{\ell}A]$ is
perfect, whereas its nonzero cohomology modules are $\mathbf F_\ell$,
which are not perfect. For this reason, the compactness proofs for
finite $A$ work with bounded flat constructible models, and standard
truncations are taken in $\Dbc$.

The right adjoint $f^!$ is constructed by Brown representability. This
theorem says that in a triangulated category with arbitrary direct sums,
generated by a set of compact objects, every exact functor that
preserves direct sums has a right adjoint. Here an object $C$ is
*compact* if $\operatorname{Hom}(C,-)$ commutes with direct sums. In the derived category of vector spaces
over a field, for example, the compact objects are the complexes with
finite-dimensional total cohomology.
