The lattice arguments below need the discrete valuation ring
$\mathcal O_E$ with its uniformizer $\pi_E$, and the valuation ring of
$\overline{\mathbf Q}_\ell$ is not of this kind. Since $\overline{\mathbf Q}_\ell$ is the filtered union of the
finite extensions $E$ of $\Ql$, we show instead that every object and every
morphism of $\Dbc(X,\overline{\mathbf Q}_\ell)$ is defined over some finite $E$. For $C\in D(X_{\mathrm{pro}\acute{e}t},E)$ and a finite extension $E'/E$, the
extensions of scalars are the complexes $C_{E'}=E'_X\otimes_{E_X}C$ and
$C_{\overline{\mathbf Q}_\ell}=\QbarX\otimes_{E_X}C$. They need no deriving, since
$E'_X$ and $\QbarX$ are flat over $E_X$.

The classical counterpart is the fact that a continuous representation of
a profinite group on $\overline{\mathbf Q}_\ell^r$, with the $\ell$-adic topology, takes
values in $\GL_r(E)$ for some finite $E/\Ql$; its proof uses the Baire
category theorem. We need less. A constructible complex, or a morphism
between two of them, is described by finitely many matrices with entries
in $\QbarX$, and on a quasi-compact scheme finitely many such entries
already lie in a single $E_X$. The lemma is used whenever a statement for
$\overline{\mathbf Q}_\ell$ is reduced to one for a finite $E$, as in
Lemma [sk-lem-integral-models](4) and in the uniform Ext bound
(Lemma [sk-lem-uniform-constructible-ext]).
