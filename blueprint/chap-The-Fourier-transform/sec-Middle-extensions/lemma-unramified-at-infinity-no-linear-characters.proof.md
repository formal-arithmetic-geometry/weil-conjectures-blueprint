For $a\ne0$ the sheaf $\mathcal L_{\psi(ax)}$ is ramified at infinity,
and the trivial character is excluded because $M$ is irreducible and
not geometrically constant, its geometric restriction being semisimple. For the first
point, write $t=1/x$ and express the character by
the Artin–Schreier equation $u^p-u=c/t$ with $c\ne0$. Let $v$ be the
normalized valuation of the field generated by a root $u$ over the
completion $k((t))$ at infinity, and let $e$ be the ramification
index, so that $v(t)=e$ and $e\le p$. Since $v(c/t)=-e<0$, we must
have $v(u)<0$, so that $v(u^p)=pv(u)<v(u)$ and
$v(u^p-u)=pv(u)$. Now $pv(u)=-e$ forces $p\mid e$, and so
$e=p$: the cover is totally ramified. Inertia at
infinity therefore maps onto the Galois group $\mathbf F_p$ of this cover, on
which $\psi$ is nontrivial. Since $M$ is unramified at infinity, this
proves the first point, because a character that is nontrivial on
inertia cannot be a constituent of a representation on which
inertia acts trivially.

For the trivial character, we apply
Lemma [lemma-restriction-normal-subgroup] to the geometric
fundamental group, which is normal in the Weil group: the restriction
of $M$ to it is semisimple, and so a
trivial geometric constituent gives nonzero geometric invariants.
Since this space of invariants is Weil-stable, the irreducibility of
$M$ shows that $M$ is geometrically constant.
