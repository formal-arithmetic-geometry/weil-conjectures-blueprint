Let $j:U_0\hookrightarrow\mathbf A^1_{k_0}$ be a dense open, and let
$M$ be an irreducible lisse Weil sheaf on $U_0$. The argument of
Chapter [chap-The-Deligne-Laumon-theorem-for-curves] needs the transform of the middle
extension of $M$ to be again a middle extension, that is, to come from a
lisse sheaf $M'$ on a dense open of the dual line. This holds unless $M$ has a geometric constituent of the form
$\mathcal L_{\psi(ax)}$, and the hypothesis of the next proposition excludes
these. They behave differently: for $a\ne0$ the sheaf $\mathcal L_{\psi(ax)}$ has no cohomology
with compact support on the line by
Lemma [lemma-integral-linear-character], and its transform is a
skyscraper rather than a lisse sheaf of weight one. Excluding them, however, costs
nothing in the application, because a sheaf unramified at infinity has no such
constituent other than the constant one, and constant constituents
contribute nothing, since $H^1_c(\mathbf A^1,\overline{\mathbf Q}_\ell)=0$.
