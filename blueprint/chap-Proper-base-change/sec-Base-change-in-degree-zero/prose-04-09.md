For a proper morphism, the underived base change map is an isomorphism
for every étale sheaf of sets, with no torsion hypothesis. The reason is
that, for a proper scheme over a henselian pair, restriction of sections to
the closed fibre is bijective
(Theorem [theorem-comparison-over-henselian-pair-degree]).

Recall that a pair $(R,I)$, with $I$
an ideal of the ring $R$, is *henselian* if $I$ is contained in the Jacobson radical of $R$
and Hensel's lemma holds for it: every factorization modulo $I$ of a
monic polynomial into coprime monic factors lifts to a factorization in
$R$ [Stacks, [09XE](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/09XE)]. A local ring with its maximal ideal
is a henselian pair exactly when it is henselian. For example, a complete
local ring is henselian.
