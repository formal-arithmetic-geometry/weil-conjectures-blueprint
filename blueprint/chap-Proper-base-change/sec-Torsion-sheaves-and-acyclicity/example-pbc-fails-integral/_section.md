Let $R$ be a strictly henselian discrete valuation
ring with uniformizer $t$ and algebraically closed residue field of
characteristic $\neq2$, and let $X\subset\mathbf P^2_R$ be the closure of
the affine curve $y^2=x^3-x^2+t$. Then $X$ is regular (at the origin of
the closed fibre the equation has the linear term $t$), while its closed
fibre $X_s$ is the nodal cubic $y^2=x^2(x-1)$. Let
$f:X\to\operatorname{Spec}R$ be the structure map. Since $R$ is strictly
henselian, the stalk of $R^1f_*\mathbf Z$ at the closed point is
$H^1(X,\mathbf Z)$.

This group is zero. Indeed, $X$ is normal and connected, so
$\mathbf Z$-torsors on $X$ correspond to continuous homomorphisms
$\pi_1(X)\to\mathbf Z$, and the image of such a homomorphism is a compact
subgroup of $\mathbf Z$, hence zero. On the closed fibre, however,
$H^1(X_s,\mathbf Z)=\mathbf Z$, generated by the loop through the node.
This means that the base change map from the stalk of $R^1f_*\mathbf Z$
at the closed point to $H^1(X_s,\mathbf Z)$ is not surjective.

With $\mathbf Z/n$ in place of $\mathbf Z$, the class of the loop is
realized by the cyclic étale cover of $X_s$ of degree $n$ obtained by
unwinding the node, and because $R$ is henselian, this cover lifts to $X$.
This is the phenomenon the torsion hypothesis captures.
