If $\Lambda$ is torsion as an abelian group, it is
killed by the additive order of $1$, so every sheaf of $\Lambda$-modules
is torsion. The two descriptions of torsion in
Definition [definition-torsion-sheaves] agree. Indeed, the colimit is
taken in sheaves, and geometric stalks commute with colimits and finite
limits and detect isomorphisms, so the equivalence reduces to the case
of abelian groups.

Torsion is a local condition on sections: every section is killed by an
integer after passing to an étale covering. However, the integer may vary
between members of the covering. For example, the
constant sheaf $\mathbf Q/\mathbf Z$ on a nonempty scheme is torsion, but on the
disjoint union of countably many copies of the scheme its sections
include $(1/2^n)_{n\geq1}$, which has infinite order.
