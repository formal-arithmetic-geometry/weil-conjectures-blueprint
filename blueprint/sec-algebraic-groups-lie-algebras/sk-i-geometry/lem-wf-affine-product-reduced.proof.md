The algebra $A\otimes_kB$ is generated by the tensors of generators of
$A$ and of $B$, so it is finitely generated. A $k$-algebra homomorphism
$A\otimes_kB\to k$ is the same as a pair of $k$-algebra homomorphisms
$A\to k$, $B\to k$, so by part (1) of
[lem-wf-affine-nullstellensatz-vanishing] the $k$-points of $X\times Y$
are the pairs $(x,y)$, and the value of $a\otimes b$ at $(x,y)$ is
$a(x)b(y)$.

Let $0\neq t\in A\otimes_kB$. Write $t=\sum_{i=1}^ra_i\otimes b_i$ with
$b_1,\ldots,b_r$ linearly independent over $k$; then some $a_i\neq0$.
By the vanishing principle for the reduced algebra $A$ there is a
$k$-point $x$ with $a_i(x)\neq0$. Applying evaluation at $x$ in the first
factor gives $\sum_ia_i(x)b_i\in B$, which is nonzero by linear
independence. By the vanishing principle for $B$ there is a $k$-point
$y$ where it does not vanish, i.e.
$t(x,y)=\sum_ia_i(x)b_i(y)\neq0$.

So a nonzero element of $A\otimes_kB$ has a nonzero value at some
$k$-point. If $t$ were nilpotent, $t^n=0$, then $t(x,y)^n=0$ in the
field $k$ for all $(x,y)$, so $t$ would vanish at every $k$-point and
hence $t=0$. Thus $A\otimes_kB$ is reduced, and the argument just given
is precisely the vanishing principle on $X\times Y$. For the last
sentence: two regular maps $\varphi,\psi:X\times Y\to Z$ into an affine
variety agree if $\varphi^*g-\psi^*g$ vanishes at all $k$-points for
every $g\in k[Z]$, that is, if $\varphi(x,y)=\psi(x,y)$ for all
$k$-points; and this may be checked by fixing $x$ and varying $y$, then
varying $x$.
