Since the stabilizer of a subspace is closed, the $\rho(W)$-stable
subspaces are precisely the $G$-stable subspaces. Hence $V$ is semisimple
as a $G(\overline{\mathbf Q}_\ell)$-module and, by [sk-i-restriction],
also as a $G^\circ(\overline{\mathbf Q}_\ell)$-module. By
[sk-p-comparison], it is then a faithful semisimple module for
$\mathfrak g=\Lie(G^\circ)$, and [sk-p-lie] shows that

$$
\mathfrak g=Z(\mathfrak g)+[\mathfrak g,\mathfrak g].
$$

Put $T=Z(G^\circ)^\circ$ and $\mathfrak h=\Lie(H^\circ)$. By the
comparison between groups and Lie algebras in [sk-p-comparison],
$\Lie(T)=Z(\mathfrak g)$. Since the commutator morphism takes values in
$H$, its mixed term over the dual numbers shows that
$[\mathfrak g,\mathfrak g]\subseteq\mathfrak h$. Consequently

$$
\mathfrak g=\Lie(T)+\mathfrak h.
$$

The multiplication $T\times H^\circ\to G^\circ$ is a homomorphism because
$T$ is central. By [sk-p-geometry], its image is a closed connected
subgroup, and its Lie algebra contains both summands on the right.
Therefore the image has the same Lie algebra as $G^\circ$, and it equals
$G^\circ$, again by that lemma.

The general version in the complement is the same argument, the two
hypotheses replacing the commutator and semisimplicity statements of
[sk-i-restriction]; surjectivity on underlying spaces follows because the
closed image contains all closed points.
