By the theorem, $H^{2d}(X,\href{@definition-tate-twists}{\Lambda_X(d)})$ is free of rank one. It is
generated by the class of a point, which we define by the counit of the
[exceptional adjunction](@theorem-existence-exceptional-inverse-image) for the inclusion of the point. Let
$x:\operatorname{Spec}k\to X$ be a closed point. Using
$\href{@theorem-existence-exceptional-inverse-image}{x^!}\href{@definition-verdier-dual-evaluation}{\omega_X}=\href{@theorem-existence-exceptional-inverse-image}{(a_Xx)^!\Lambda_s}=\Lambda$ and
$\href{@definition-verdier-dual-evaluation}{\omega_X}\simeq\href{@definition-tate-twists}{\Lambda_X(d)}[2d]$, the counit $x_*\href{@theorem-existence-exceptional-inverse-image}{x^!}\href{@definition-verdier-dual-evaluation}{\omega_X}\to\href{@definition-verdier-dual-evaluation}{\omega_X}$
induces a map
$\Lambda\to R\Gamma(X,\href{@definition-verdier-dual-evaluation}{\omega_X})=R\Gamma(X,\href{@definition-tate-twists}{\Lambda_X(d)})[2d]$, and we let
$[x]\in H^{2d}(X,\href{@definition-tate-twists}{\Lambda_X(d)})$ be the image of $1$.
