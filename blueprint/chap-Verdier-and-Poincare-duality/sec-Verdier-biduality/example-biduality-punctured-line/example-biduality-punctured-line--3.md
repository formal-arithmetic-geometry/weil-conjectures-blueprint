Hence the cone has $\Lambda(1)$ in degree $-2$ and $\Lambda$ in degree $-1$.

Dualizing once more, biduality predicts $\mathbb D_X\mathbb D_X(j_!\Lambda_U)
\simeq j_!\Lambda_U$, with zero stalk at $0$. In other words, the second dual
cancels the stalk that the first created. Here this follows from the two easy
cases. The map $\delta$ is a morphism of triangles, and it is an isomorphism on
$\Lambda_X$ and on $i_*\Lambda$. By the five lemma it is an isomorphism on
$j_!\Lambda_U$. The argument used that $\Lambda_U$ extends to the locally constant
sheaf $\Lambda_X$. A locally constant $L$ on $U$ with nontrivial monodromy around
$0$ has no such extension, and that is the case which needs the henselian
disc.
