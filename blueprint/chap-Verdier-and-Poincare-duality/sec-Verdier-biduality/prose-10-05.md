On a smooth curve $C$ over an algebraically closed field, with
$\omega_C=\Lambda_C(1)[2]$, biduality is easy to see for the two basic kinds of sheaves. Indeed, for a locally constant sheaf $L$ of free
$\Lambda$-modules the dual is $L^\vee(1)[2]$, and dualizing twice gives
back $L$. For a skyscraper $i_*M$ at a closed point $i:x\to C$, the
exchange formula $\mathbb D_Ci_*\simeq i_*\mathbb D_x$ for the proper
map $i$ shows that the dual is $i_*\mathbb D_xM$, so biduality reduces
to biduality on the point. In general, a constructible sheaf is an extension of $i_*M$ by $j_!L$, where
now $i$ is the inclusion of a finite set of closed points and $L$ is
locally constant on its complement $j:U\hookrightarrow C$. The open piece is the difficult one: we have $\mathbb D_C(j_!L)\simeq Rj_*\mathbb D_UL$, and to dualize $Rj_*$ we would need
the second exchange formula. That formula, however, depends on biduality. In dimension one this can still be checked by hand, using the cohomology of the henselian punctured disc, whereas in higher dimension the direct images
$Rj_*$ along the boundary of a stratum have no such description.
