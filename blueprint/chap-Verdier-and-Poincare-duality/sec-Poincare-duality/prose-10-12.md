Over a finite base field $k$ the same argument gives us isomorphisms
$Ra_{X*}\href{@definition-verdier-dual-evaluation}{\mathbb D_XK}\simeq\href{@definition-verdier-dual-evaluation}{\mathbb D_s}\href{@proposition-construction-independence-rf-shriek}{Ra_{X!}K}$ and
$\href{@proposition-construction-independence-rf-shriek}{Ra_{X!}}\href{@definition-verdier-dual-evaluation}{\mathbb D_XK}\simeq\href{@definition-verdier-dual-evaluation}{\mathbb D_s}Ra_{X*}K$ in $D(\href{@definition-small-pro-etale-site}{s_{\mathrm{pro}\acute{e}t}},\Lambda)$,
with $\href{@definition-verdier-dual-evaluation}{\mathbb D_s}=\cHom_s(-,\Lambda_s)$. These are the first two formulas
of Corollary [corollary-verdier-exchange-formulas] for $f=a_X$. After
base change to an algebraic closure they become the isomorphisms of the
theorem.

The first isomorphism of the theorem is the adjoint of a pairing. We give a
name to pairings whose adjoint is an isomorphism.
