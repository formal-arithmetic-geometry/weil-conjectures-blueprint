On the geometric point, constructible complexes are perfect complexes,
and duality is the duality of finite projective modules. This is the
case $\dim X=0$ of biduality, where the induction in the proof of
Theorem [theorem-verdier-biduality] ends. Over $\mathbf Z/\ell^n$
perfectness is a real condition. For instance, the module $\mathbf Z/\ell$
has infinite projective dimension over $\mathbf Z/\ell^2$. This is why we
require the coefficients to have finite Tor dimension.
Lemma [lemma-perfect-complexes-geometric-point] shows that on $D^b_{\mathrm{ctf}}$
nothing else can go wrong.

After defining the dualizing complex $\omega_X$,
the Verdier dual $\mathbb D_X$ and the double-dual map
$\delta_K:K\to\mathbb D_X\mathbb D_XK$, we prove the two exchange
formulas that need only adjunctions
(Lemma [lemma-internal-adjunction-two-exchange-formulas]). They say
that $\mathbb D$ turns $Rf_!$ into $Rf_*$
and $f^*$ into $f^!$. For $f=a_X$ and $K=\Lambda_X$ the first says that
$R\Gamma(X,\omega_X)$ is the dual of $R\Gamma_c(X,\Lambda)$, as for the
homology of a manifold. The other half, with the roles of $!$ and $*$
reversed, needs biduality.
