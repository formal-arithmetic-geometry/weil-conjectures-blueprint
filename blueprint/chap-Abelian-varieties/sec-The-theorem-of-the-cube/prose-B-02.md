The theorem of the cube says that an invertible sheaf $\mathcal L$ on
$X\times Y\times Z$, with $X,Y$ complete varieties and $Z$ connected,
that is trivial on
the three slices through a point $(x,y,z)$ is trivial. To prove it, we
view $\mathcal L$ as a family of invertible sheaves on $X\times Y$
parametrized by $Z$. Following [Stacks, [0BEZ](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0BEZ)], we then consider the set of
points of $Z$ over which the sheaf is trivial. This set is closed, by
semicontinuity. It is also open, by a computation of $H^0$ and $H^1$
near such a point. Since it contains $z$ and $Z$ is connected, it is
all of $Z$. The openness uses the triviality on the two slices
$x\times Y\times Z$ and $X\times y\times Z$, and with only two factors
it fails.
