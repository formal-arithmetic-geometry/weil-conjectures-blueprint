Before purity is known, the eigenvalues of a lisse sheaf $\mathcal V$
of rank $r$ at a closed point are not controlled individually, but their
product is: it is the eigenvalue of $\det\mathcal V$, a sheaf of rank
one, whose weight Corollary [sk-cor-wrp-rankone-pure] gives. Dividing by
$r$ gives the average weight of the eigenvalues, and this is the only
candidate for a weight of $\mathcal V$: if $\mathcal V$ is pure of
weight $w$, then $\det\mathcal V$ is pure of weight $rw$, and the number
defined below is $w$. For a sheaf that is not pure it is only an
average. On $\overline{\mathbf Q}_\ell\oplus \overline{\mathbf Q}_\ell(-1)$ the determinant is $\overline{\mathbf Q}_\ell(-1)$, of
weight $2$, so the number is $1$, the weight of no eigenvalue. For this
reason the later results apply it to irreducible constituents:
Proposition [sk-p-real-rankin] bounds the eigenvalues of a real
sheaf by the largest determinant weight of its constituents, and
Theorem [sk-p-real-purity] shows that each irreducible constituent is
pure of weight equal to its determinant weight.
