*Euler characteristics and degrees.* The Zariski-cover representability
criterion [lem-pc-schemes-criterion] is the tool that will turn the
subfunctor decomposition into a scheme. Additivity of $\chi$
[lem-pc-euler-additive] gives the behaviour of the degree under base change
and tensoring with an effective divisor [lem-pc-degree-base-change]; very
ampleness descends along field extensions [lem-pc-very-ample-descends], and
on a smooth projective curve ampleness is exactly positivity of the degree
[lem-pc-ample-positive-degree].

*Divisors and Hilbert schemes.* Local equations cut out effective Cartier
divisors [lem-pc-recd-locally-principal], these pull back
[lem-pc-recd-pullback], and a Cartier criterion [lem-pc-cartier-criterion]
identifies them. The Hilbert functor of relative effective Cartier divisors
is a sheaf [lem-pc-hilb-sheaf], is representable in the affine-space model
[lem-pc-hilb-representable], carries an addition law [lem-pc-hilb-addition]
and is smooth [lem-pc-hilb-smooth]; descent along flat sources
[lem-pc-flat-source-descent] then yields the Hilbert scheme of a curve
[thm-pc-hilb-curves], with its universal divisor
[lem-pc-universal-divisor]. The fpqc descent statement
[lem-pc-fpqc-descent] is the last representability input.
