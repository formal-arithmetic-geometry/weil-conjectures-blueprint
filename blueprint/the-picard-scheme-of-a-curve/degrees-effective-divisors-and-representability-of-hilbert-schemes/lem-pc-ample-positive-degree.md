Let $k$ be a field, $X$ a smooth projective curve [def-pc-curve] over $k$
and $\mathcal{L}$ an invertible $\mathcal{O}_X$-module. Then $\mathcal{L}$
is ample if and only if $\deg(\mathcal{L})>0$.

## Proof

*($\Rightarrow$).* If $\mathcal{L}$ is ample then
$\mathcal{L}^{\otimes m}$ is very ample for some $m\ge1$; fix such an $m$
and an immersion $i:X\to\mathbf P^N_k$ with
$\mathcal{L}^{\otimes m}=i^*\mathcal{O}(1)$. Since $X$ is proper, the
immersion is a closed immersion (the image of the proper $X$ is closed, and
an immersion with closed image is a closed immersion; Schemes, Lemma
26.10.4, [Tag 01IQ](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/01IQ)).

Choose $x_0\in X$ and a section
$s\in H^0(X,\mathcal{L}^{\otimes m})$ with $s(x_0)\neq0$ (the base locus of
a very ample module is empty: the linear system
$\mathbf P(H^0(X,\mathcal{L}^{\otimes m}))$ gives the embedding, so for
every point there is a section not vanishing at it). The zero scheme
$D=Z(s)$ is an effective Cartier divisor (finite closed subscheme of the
smooth curve; the local description of [def-pc-zero-scheme] and the DVR
structure of [rem-pc-regularity-sc]: the ideal of $D$ at each of its points
is generated by a nonzero element of the DVR $\mathcal{O}_{X,x}$, hence
invertible) and finite ($X_s$ is a dense open of the curve, so $D$ is a
closed subscheme of dimension $0$ and of finite type, hence finite).

Moreover $D\neq\emptyset$: if $D=\emptyset$, then $s$ is a nowhere
vanishing section, so $\mathcal{L}^{\otimes m}\cong\mathcal{O}_X$; but the
trivial module is not ample on a curve — the affine open sets $X_s$ for the
constant section $1$ give $X\subseteq$ one affine piece, so $X$ would be
affine, and a proper affine scheme over $k$ is finite over $k$, whereas the
curve is one-dimensional. Hence $\deg D\ge1$. From the exact sequence
$0\to\mathcal{O}_X\to\mathcal{L}^{\otimes m}\to\mathcal Q\to0$ with
$\mathcal Q$ supported on $D$ and the computation
$\dim_kH^0(X,\mathcal Q)=\deg D$ of [lem-pc-degree-base-change](2), the
additivity of $\chi$ gives
$$
\chi(\mathcal{L}^{\otimes m})=\chi(\mathcal{O}_X)+\deg D,\qquad
\text{so}\qquad
m\deg(\mathcal{L})=\deg(\mathcal{L}^{\otimes m})=\deg D\ge1,
$$
and $\deg\mathcal{L}>0$.

*($\Leftarrow$).* Assume $\deg\mathcal{L}>0$. By
[lem-pc-degree-base-change](1), $\deg(\mathcal{L}_{\bar k})=\deg\mathcal{L}>0$,
and $X_{\bar k}$ is a smooth projective curve over the algebraically closed
field $\bar k$ (smooth and proper: [lem-pc-proper-base-change]; a curve:
[lem-pc-dim-base-change]; integral by [lem-pc-h0-geom-connected] and the
regularity-and-reducedness argument of [prop-pc-XK-integral], which applies
verbatim: $H^0(X_{\bar k},\mathcal{O})=H^0(X,\mathcal{O})\otimes_k\bar k$ is
a field by [lem-pc-h0-proper-variety](1), so $X_{\bar k}$ is connected,
normal and reduced, hence integral).

By the corresponding lemma of the curves chapter, applied to the smooth
projective curve $X_{\bar k}$ of genus $g$ (finite by
[lem-pc-genus-well-defined], invariant under base change by
[lem-pc-genus-base-change]), the module $\mathcal{L}_{\bar k}$ is ample.
Then $\mathcal{L}$ is ample: some power $\mathcal{L}_{\bar k}^{\otimes m}$
is very ample, i.e. the base change of $\mathcal{L}^{\otimes m}$ to
$\bar k$ is very ample; by [lem-pc-very-ample-descends]
$\mathcal{L}^{\otimes m}$ is very ample over $k$, hence $\mathcal{L}$ is
ample.
