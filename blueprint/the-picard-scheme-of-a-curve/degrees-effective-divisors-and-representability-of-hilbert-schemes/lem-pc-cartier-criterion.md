(Picard Schemes of Curves, Lemma 44.3.1, [Tag
0B9D](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0B9D).) Let $X\to S$ be a smooth
morphism of relative dimension $1$ and let $D\subset X$ be a closed
subscheme. Consider:

1. $D\to S$ is finite locally free;
2. $D$ is a relative effective Cartier divisor on $X/S$;
3. $D\to S$ is locally quasi-finite, flat and locally of finite
   presentation;
4. $D\to S$ is locally quasi-finite and flat.

Then $(1)\Rightarrow(2)\Leftrightarrow(3)\Rightarrow(4)$. If $S$ is locally
Noetherian the last arrow is an if-and-only-if; if $X\to S$ is proper, the
first arrow is an if-and-only-if.

## Proof

*$(3)\Rightarrow(2)$.* This is the "in particular" of
[lem-pc-recd-locally-principal]: $D$ is locally principal (the fibres are
effective Cartier divisors and $D$ is flat and locally quasi-finite), and
$D\to X$ is of finite presentation because $D\to S$ is — the closed
immersion $D\to X$ has a finite type ideal sheaf, since $D\to S$ locally of
finite presentation and $X\to S$ of finite presentation force the ideal to
be finitely generated (the quotient $B/J$ of a finitely presented $B$ over
$A$ which is itself finitely presented over $A$ has finitely generated
kernel $J$). So [lem-pc-recd-locally-principal] applies and $D$ is a
relative effective Cartier divisor.

*$(2)\Rightarrow(3)$.* $D\to S$ is flat (part of
[def-pc-relative-effective-cartier]) and locally of finite presentation (as
$X\to S$ is of finite presentation and $D$ is an effective Cartier divisor:
locally $D=\operatorname{Spec}(B/(f))$ with $B$ finitely presented over $A$).
The fibres $D_s$ are effective Cartier divisors on the smooth
one-dimensional $X_s$, hence finite unions of closed points with their
Artinian structures (the local theory of [rem-pc-regularity-sc]: on a
smooth curve the effective Cartier divisors are the finite unions of closed
points); hence $D_s$ is finite, so $D\to S$ is locally quasi-finite (a
morphism is locally quasi-finite iff its fibres are discrete).

*$(1)\Rightarrow(2)$.* $D\to S$ finite locally free means flat and locally
of finite presentation ([def-pc-finite-locally-free]), and $D\to X$ is of
finite presentation as above. For a fibre: $D_s$ is finite flat of rank $d$
over $\kappa(s)$, and at a point $x\in D_s$ the local ring
$\mathcal{O}_{X_s,x}$ is a regular local ring of dimension one, hence a
discrete valuation ring; the stalk of the ideal of $D_s$ at $x$ is an ideal
of the DVR with nonzero quotient (the quotient $\mathcal{O}_{D_s,x}$ is a
finite flat module over the field, hence of finite length), so it is the
$n$-th power of the maximal ideal, a principal ideal generated by a
nonzerodivisor: $D_s$ is an effective Cartier divisor at $x$, i.e.
everywhere. Now [lem-pc-recd-locally-principal] applies: $D$ is a relative
effective Cartier divisor.

*$(3)\Rightarrow(4)$.* Clear.

*$(4)\Rightarrow(3)$ for locally Noetherian $S$.* A locally quasi-finite
morphism into a locally Noetherian scheme is locally of finite presentation
(Morphisms, Lemma 29.22.9, [Tag
01TX](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/01TX)), so (4) implies (3) in
this case.

*$(1)\Leftrightarrow(2)$ for proper $X\to S$.* If (1) holds, (2) holds a
fortiori. Conversely let (2) hold; then $D\to S$ is locally quasi-finite
(by $(2)\Rightarrow(3)$) and flat; $D\to S$ is proper: $D\to X$ is a closed
immersion (hence proper) and $X\to S$ is proper, and compositions of proper
morphisms are proper. A proper locally quasi-finite morphism is finite
(More on Morphisms, Lemma 37.44.1, [Tag
02LS](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/02LS)); a finite flat and
finitely presented morphism is finite locally free (Morphisms, Lemma
29.49.2, [Tag 02KB](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/02KB)): $D\to S$
is finite locally free.
