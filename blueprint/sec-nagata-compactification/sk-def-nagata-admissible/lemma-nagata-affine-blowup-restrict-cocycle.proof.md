Applying $\psi\varphi$ to a coefficient is the same as applying $\varphi$
and then $\psi$, so the graded ring homomorphisms of
[def-nagata-rees-localization-map] satisfy
$\rho_{\psi\varphi}=\rho_\psi\circ\rho_\varphi\colon
\mathcal R_A(I)\to\mathcal R_C(K)$.

The morphism of Proj schemes induced by such a homomorphism $\rho$ is
described chart by chart: for $a\in I$, it maps the standard open
$D_+(\rho(at))$ of the target Proj to $D_+(at)$ by $\operatorname{Spec}$ of
the induced map of degree-zero homogeneous localizations
$\mathcal R_A(I)_{(at),0}\to\mathcal R_C(K)_{(\rho(at)),0}$. Since $K$ is
generated by the image of $I$, the elements $\rho(at)$, $a\in I$, generate an ideal
containing the irrelevant ideal of $\mathcal R_C(K)$, so these standard
opens cover $\operatorname{Bl}_K(\operatorname{Spec}C)$ and the morphism is
everywhere defined. The induced maps of homogeneous localizations are
compatible with composition, so on each standard open
$D_+(\rho_{\psi\varphi}(at))$ the morphisms $r_{\psi\varphi}$ and
$r_\varphi\circ r_\psi$ are $\operatorname{Spec}$ of the same ring map.
As these opens cover $\operatorname{Bl}_K(\operatorname{Spec}C)$, the two
morphisms are equal. In the same way $\rho_{\mathrm{id}_A}$ is the identity
of $\mathcal R_A(I)$, so on every chart $r_{\mathrm{id}_A}$ is
$\operatorname{Spec}$ of the identity, and $r_{\mathrm{id}_A}$ is the
identity.
