Let $X$ be qcqs, let $U\subseteq X$ be a dense quasi-compact open and let
$\pi\colon\widetilde X=\operatorname{Bl}_{\mathcal I}(X)\to X$ be a
$U$-admissible blow-up. Then $U$, viewed as an open subscheme of
$\widetilde X$ via [thm-nagata-global-blowup-complement], is
topologically dense in $\widetilde X$. If moreover
$V(\mathcal I)=X\setminus U$, then $U$ is scheme-theoretically dense in
$\widetilde X$.

## Proof

Write $\mathcal I'=\mathcal I\cdot\mathcal O_{\widetilde X}$, a finite type
invertible ideal by [thm-nagata-blowup-principalizes-center]. Its
complement $\widetilde X\setminus V(\mathcal I')$ is a quasi-compact open
subscheme, and $\pi$ restricts over it to an isomorphism onto
$X\setminus V(\mathcal I)$ by [thm-nagata-global-blowup-complement].
An invertible ideal is locally generated by a nonzerodivisor, so
$\mathcal O_{\widetilde X}\to j_*\mathcal O_{\widetilde X\setminus V(\mathcal I')}$ is injective for the inclusion $j$ of that complement:
the complement is scheme-theoretically dense, hence topologically dense.
Since $\mathcal I$ is the unit ideal on $U$ we have
$U\subseteq X\setminus V(\mathcal I)$, and $U$ is dense there because it is
dense in $X$. Density is transitive along the two displayed inclusions, so
$U$ is dense in $\widetilde X$.

If $V(\mathcal I)=X\setminus U$ then $X\setminus V(\mathcal I)=U$, so the
displayed scheme-theoretically dense complement *is* $U$.
