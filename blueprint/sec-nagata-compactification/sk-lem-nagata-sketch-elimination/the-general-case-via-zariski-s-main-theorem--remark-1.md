The case $U=V$ is the only one used in the applications, but the proof does
not go through if $U=V$ is imposed throughout. The extra flexibility is
needed in the general case of the proof of
[thm-nagata-conrad-elimination]: there one restricts to
$X_i=\overline{U_i}$ for a piece $U_i=f^{-1}(Y_i)$ of $U$, and even when
$U=V$ to begin with, the open $V_i=X_i\cap V$ can be strictly larger than
$U_i$. This is why
[lemma-nagata-conrad-quasi-affine-general] (Case 3) is stated for
$U\subseteq V$ and not only for $U=V$, and why
[lemma-nagata-conrad-disjointness-blowup] is needed to reduce Case 3 to
Case 2.
