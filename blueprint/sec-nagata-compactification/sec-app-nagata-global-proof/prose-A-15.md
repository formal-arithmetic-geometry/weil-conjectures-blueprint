Over a Noetherian base, the theorem follows from
Proposition [sk-thm-nagata-sketch-two-open] by induction over an affine cover
whose members overlap densely. Each affine piece is compactified by its
closure in a projective space, and the proposition then compactifies the
union of the first $r$ pieces with the next one. The proposition
requires the overlap to be dense, because its valuative criterion only
tests maps from spectra of valuation rings whose generic point maps
into the overlap. We therefore
choose the cover so that its total intersection contains all generic
points of $X$ (Lemma [sk-lem-nagata-sketch-affine-cover]).

Over a general qcqs base we use Noetherian approximation: a morphism of
finite presentation is the base change of one over a Noetherian scheme,
and compactifications are stable under base change. A morphism that is
only of finite type need not descend in this way, so we first embed $X$
as a closed subscheme of a scheme of finite presentation over $S$ and
take the closure of $X$ in a compactification of the latter.
