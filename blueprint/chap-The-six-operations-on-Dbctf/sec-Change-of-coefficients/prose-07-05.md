The map for $f^!$ is defined in the same way, as the transpose of a
counit, once we know that (5) is an isomorphism; see
(7.1) below. We now show, one
operation at a time, that these maps are isomorphisms on $D^b_{\mathrm{ctf}}$.

In the lemmas below it suffices to treat the maps in items (1)–(3) of the
list of coefficient maps, that is, all maps except composites. Indeed,
extension of scalars along a composite of two maps of the list is the
composite of the two extensions. Each map in
Definition [definition-change-of-coefficients-maps] for the composite
is the composite of the maps for the two factors. For (1)–(3) this holds by the coherence of the associativity isomorphisms, and for (4)–(6) it holds because the adjoint transpose of a composite of such maps is the composite
of the transposes.
