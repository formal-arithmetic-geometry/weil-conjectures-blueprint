*Shared context.* The general assertions in [proposition-isomorphisms-reduce-finite-coefficients--1], [proposition-isomorphisms-reduce-finite-coefficients--3] and [proposition-isomorphisms-reduce-finite-coefficients--4] retain the functors $F_\Lambda,G_\Lambda$ and natural transformations $\theta_\Lambda$ of [proposition-isomorphisms-reduce-finite-coefficients]. For the last two assertions assume the stated scalar-extension identifications for $F$ and $G$, carrying $\theta_\Lambda(K)\otimes\Lambda'$ to $\theta_{\Lambda'}(K\otimes\Lambda')$, and assume that $\theta_A(K)$ is an isomorphism for every finite admissible $A$ and every $K\in\Dctf(X,A)$.

The integral criterion [proposition-isomorphisms-reduce-finite-coefficients--2] is stated independently, with the hypotheses of the mainline Lean theorem. Applied to $\theta_{\mathcal O_E}(K)$ and reduction modulo $\ell$, it supplies the integral step under these compatibility assumptions. Lean uses this criterion for specific maps; the general assertions for arbitrary $F,G,\theta$ are retained as informal consequences.
