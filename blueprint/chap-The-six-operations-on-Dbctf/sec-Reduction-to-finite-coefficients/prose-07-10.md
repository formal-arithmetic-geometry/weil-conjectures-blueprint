Many later theorems say that a canonical map is an isomorphism. We prove them with finite coefficients and then extend them to all admissible rings in
three steps. First, for $\mathcal O_E$ we reduce modulo $\pi_E$ and use derived Nakayama. This step needs derived completeness, since $E$ itself is a
nonzero $\mathcal O_E$-module with $E/\pi_E E=0$. Then for $E$ we invert $\ell$ on integral models, and for $\overline{\mathbf Q}_\ell$ we descend to a finite extension. The following proposition makes this argument once and for all. It only asks that the functors $F$, $G$ and the map $\theta$ of the
statement commute with change of coefficients,
and by Proposition [proposition-six-operations-change-coefficients]
this holds for everything built from the six operations.
