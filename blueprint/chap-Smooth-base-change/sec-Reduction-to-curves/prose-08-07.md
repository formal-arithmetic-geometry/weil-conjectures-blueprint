Recall that $\mathrm{BC}(g,n,r)$ asserts base change in degrees $\leq r$,
for $\mathbf Z/n$-coefficients, along every base change $Y'\times_YT\to T$
of $g$. Assume now that $q\geq1$ and that $\mathrm{BC}(g,n,q-1)$ holds. If
$\mathrm{BC}(g,n,q)$ fails, the next four lemmas, all on the étale
sites, move the failure to one with constant coefficients and direct image from
the spectrum of an algebraically closed field, in the form of
Lemma [lemma-reduction-curve-over-normal-base].

We argue by contradiction rather than by dévissage, because a nonzero
cokernel of the base change map can be transported along an embedding of
coefficient sheaves $M\hookrightarrow N$, whereas an isomorphism cannot.
