The points of $\operatorname{Spec}A^w$ are pairs $(x,y)$ of primes of $A$
with $y\subset x$; their local rings are $A_y$. Its connected components are
the fibres over $x$, each isomorphic to $\operatorname{Spec}A_x$. Its closed
points $(x,x)$ form a closed subset identified with $\operatorname{Spec}A$ in
the patch topology. In particular, $A\to A^w$ is faithfully flat and
$\operatorname{Spec}A^w$ is w-local [def-weil-pbc-w-local].

## Proof

A prime of the colimit in [def-weil-pbc-w-localization] chooses one part in
each finite constructible partition. These choices form an ultrafilter of
constructible subsets and therefore give a unique patch point $x$, by
compactness in [lem-weil-pbc-w-local-partition]. Its contraction $y$ to $A$
avoids every $f\notin x$: use the partition by $D(f),V(f)$, and invert $f$ on
the first part. Thus $y\subset x$. Conversely such a pair specifies the prime
at every stage, so gives a unique prime of the colimit. The local ring is the
colimit of the stage local rings $A_y$, whose transition maps are identities;
it is $A_y$.

The idempotents of the constructible partitions give a continuous map to the
patch space of $x$'s. The fibre over $x$ is obtained by localizing at all the
idempotents selecting parts containing $x$. In this localization every
element is a fraction from $A$ with denominator outside $x$, and every such
fraction occurs. Equality of two fractions is likewise detected after one
further localization. Its ring is therefore $A_x$. These fibres are
connected, while different fibres are separated by those idempotents, so they
are the components.

A point $(x,y)$ is closed precisely when $y=x$. The complement of these
points is open: if $f\in x\setminus y$, the intersection of the idempotent
part labelled $V(f)$ with the ordinary basic open $D(f)$ contains $(x,y)$ and
no diagonal pair. The topology on diagonal pairs is generated by
constructible subsets of $\operatorname{Spec}A$, so is the patch topology.
This proves w-locality in [def-weil-pbc-w-local]. Finally the map is flat by
[lem-weil-pbc-ind-etale-stability], and every prime $x$ is the image of
$(x,x)$. Flatness and surjectivity on spectra give faithful flatness.
