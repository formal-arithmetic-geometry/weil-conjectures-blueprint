For a proper morphism, the underived base change map is an isomorphism
for every étale sheaf of sets. The reason is that, for a proper scheme
over a henselian pair, restriction of sections to the closed fibre is
bijective (Theorem [sk-thm-pbc-henselian-h0]).

Recall that a pair $(R,I)$, with $I$
an ideal of the ring $R$, is *henselian* if $I$ is contained in the Jacobson radical of $R$
and Hensel's lemma holds for it: every factorization modulo $I$ of a
monic polynomial into coprime monic factors lifts to a factorization in
$R$ [Stacks, Tag 09XE](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/09XE). A local ring with its maximal ideal
is a henselian pair exactly when it is henselian, and a complete local
ring is henselian.

The property defined next is formulated so that it can be proved for
$\mathbf P^1$ and then transported. It quantifies over all base changes
$g$, which makes it stable under composition and lets it descend along
proper surjections (Lemma [sk-lem-pbc-composition-cancellation]). It
concerns bounded below complexes, because the proof applies the
degree-zero case to injective resolutions; unbounded complexes are
treated later with bounds on cohomological dimension. And it concerns
torsion sheaves, because for the constant sheaf $\mathbf Z$ the
statement is false. Let $R$ be a strictly henselian discrete valuation
ring with uniformizer $t$ and algebraically closed residue field of
characteristic $\neq2$, and let $X\subset\mathbf P^2_R$ be the closure of
the affine curve $y^2=x^3-x^2+t$. Then $X$ is regular (at the origin of
the closed fibre the equation has the linear term $t$), and its closed
fibre $X_s$ is the nodal cubic $y^2=x^2(x-1)$. Let
$f:X\to\operatorname{Spec}R$ be the structure map. Since $R$ is strictly
henselian, the stalk of $R^1f_*\mathbf Z$ at the closed point is
$H^1(X,\mathbf Z)=\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathrm{cont}}(\pi_1(X),\mathbf Z)=0$,
as $X$ is normal and connected, whereas $H^1(X_s,\mathbf Z)=\mathbf Z$,
generated by the loop through the node.
