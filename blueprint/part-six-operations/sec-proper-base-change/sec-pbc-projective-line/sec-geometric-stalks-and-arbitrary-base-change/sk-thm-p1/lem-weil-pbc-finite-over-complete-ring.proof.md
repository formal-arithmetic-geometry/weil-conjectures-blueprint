Notation for all steps: $A$, $I$, $M$ satisfy (1)-(3); $x_1,\dots,x_r\in M$ are elements whose images generate $M/IM$ over $A/I$ (they exist by (3); $r=0$ is allowed), and $N=\sum_iAx_i\subseteq M$. (Generation modulo every power of $I$.) For every $k\ge0$ and every $y\in I^kM$ there are $c_1,\dots,c_r\in I^k$ with $y-\sum_ic_ix_i\in I^{k+1}M$ (with $I^0=A$). Proof: for $k=0$, the image of $y$ in $M/IM$ is $\sum_i\bar a_i\bar x_i$ with $\bar a_i\in A/I$; lifting $\bar a_i$ to $a_i\in A$ gives $y-\sum_ia_ix_i\in IM$. For $k\ge1$, write $y=\sum_jt_jm_j$ (finite sum) with $t_j\in I^k$, $m_j\in M$; by the case $k=0$, $m_j=\sum_ia_{ji}x_i+m_j'$ with $a_{ji}\in A$, $m_j'\in IM$. Then $y=\sum_ic_ix_i+\sum_jt_jm_j'$ with $c_i=\sum_jt_ja_{ji}\in I^k$ and $t_jm_j'\in I^k\cdot IM=I^{k+1}M$. (In other words $I^kM=I^kN+I^{k+1}M$.)

(Successive approximation.) Fix $m\in M$. Define inductively $m_0=m$ and, for $k\ge0$ with $m_k\in I^kM$ already defined, choose by Step 1 elements $c^{(k)}_1,\dots,c^{(k)}_r\in I^k$ with $m_{k+1}:=m_k-\sum_ic^{(k)}_ix_i\in I^{k+1}M$. Put $s^{(n)}_i=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}c^{(k)}_i\in A$ for $n\ge0$. By induction on $n$ (the case $n=0$ being $m=m_0$, and $m_n=\sum_ic^{(n)}_ix_i+m_{n+1}$ giving the step), $m=\sum_is^{(n)}_ix_i+m_n$, hence $m-\sum_is^{(n)}_ix_i\in I^nM$ for every $n\ge0$. Moreover, for $n'\ge n$, $s^{(n')}_i-s^{(n)}_i=\sum_{k=n}^{n'-1}c^{(k)}_i\in I^n$, since $c^{(k)}_i\in I^k\subseteq I^n$ for $k\ge n$.

(The limits of the coefficients exist in $A$.) For each $i$, the classes $(s^{(n)}_i\bmod I^n)_{n\ge1}$ form a compatible sequence, since $s^{(n+1)}_i\equiv s^{(n)}_i\pmod{I^n}$ (Step 2), i.e. an element of the completion $\varprojlim_nA/I^n$. By (1) the canonical map $A\to\varprojlim_nA/I^n$, $a\mapsto(a\bmod I^n)_n$, is bijective; by its surjectivity there is $a_i\in A$ with $a_i\equiv s^{(n)}_i\pmod{I^n}$ for every $n\ge1$.

(Conclusion.) For every $n\ge1$, $m-\sum_ia_ix_i=\bigl(m-\sum_is^{(n)}_ix_i\bigr)+\sum_i\bigl(s^{(n)}_i-a_i\bigr)x_i$, where the first summand lies in $I^nM$ by Step 2 and the second lies in $I^nM$ since $s^{(n)}_i-a_i\in I^n$ by Step 3. Hence $m-\sum_ia_ix_i\in\bigcap_{n\ge1}I^nM=0$ by (2), so $m=\sum_ia_ix_i\in N$. As $m\in M$ was arbitrary, $M=N$ is generated over $A$ by $x_1,\dots,x_r$, in particular finitely generated.
