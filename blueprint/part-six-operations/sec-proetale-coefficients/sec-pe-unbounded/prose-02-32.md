Proper base change only needs the comparison for bounded-below complexes,
but Brown representability takes place in the unbounded derived category, and
there we need the following extension. A morphism from a sheaf into an
unbounded complex $K$, or a cohomology sheaf of $Rg_*K$, involves in
principle all the cohomology sheaves of $K$ at once. It depends on only
finitely many of them if Ext groups and cohomology of sheaves vanish
above a fixed degree, uniformly in the sheaf, and the smallest such degree is the
cohomological dimension defined below. Without such a bound even the
constant sheaf can fail to be compact. For $\ell=2$ and
$S=\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf R$, étale cohomology is the cohomology of
$\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbf C/\mathbf R)=\mathbf Z/2$, and
$H^q(\mathbf Z/2,\mathbf F_2)=\mathbf F_2$ for every $q\geq0$, so that
$\operatorname{cd}_2(S)=\infty$; in the opening of
Section [sec-six-functors-basics] we will see that, as a consequence,
$\Hom(\mathbf F_2,-)$ does not commute with the direct sum
$\bigoplus_{n\geq0}\mathbf F_2[n]$. Over a field of finite cohomological
dimension, the étale cohomology of schemes of finite type is uniformly
bounded, so every computation in a fixed degree can be carried out on a
bounded-below truncation. This is how the bounds enter
Proposition [sk-prop-pe-unbounded-classical] below and, later, the proofs of
compactness and of the preservation of direct sums by $Rp_*$
(Lemmas [sk-lem-uniform-constructible-ext]
and [sk-lem-proper-heart-bound]).
