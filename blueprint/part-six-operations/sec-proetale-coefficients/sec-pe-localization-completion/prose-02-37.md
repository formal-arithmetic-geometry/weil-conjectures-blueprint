The integral coefficient sheaves are inverse limits, so to restrict
integral complexes to the strata of a stratification, and to extend them
by zero, we need the functors $j_!$, $j^*$, $i^*$ and $i_*$ to commute with limits. On
the pro-étale site this holds, even for inverse image along a closed
immersion. From this we deduce the localization triangles
and construct the local cohomology functor, which let us argue with
closed supports before the exceptional inverse image and proper
base change are available.

That $i^*$ commutes with limits is special to the pro-étale site. On
$X_{\acute{e}t}$ the stalk of $i^*F$ at a point of $Z$ is the stalk of $F$, a
colimit, and colimits do not commute with infinite products. On
$X_{\mathrm{pro}\acute{e}t}$ the value of $i^*M$ on a weakly contractible affine over
$Z$ is the value of $M$ on one affine object of $X_{\mathrm{pro}\acute{e}t}$, henselian
along $Z$, which is a limit-preserving operation
(Proposition [sk-prop-sf-open-closed-triangle]). For $\mathcal O_{E,X}=\varprojlim(\mathcal O_E/\pi_E^n)_X$
this means that $i^*$ applied to the limit is the limit of the
restrictions $(\mathcal O_E/\pi_E^n)_Z$, and the same holds for any
integral complex given as a derived limit of its reductions.
The second half of the subsection explains when a complex over
$\mathcal O_E$ is determined by its reductions modulo $\pi_E^n$; with the
cohomology of these reductions computed on the étale site, this is how
integral statements are reduced to finite coefficients throughout the
notes.
