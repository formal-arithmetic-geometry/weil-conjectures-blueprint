With torsion coefficients the étale site does everything we want: SGA 4
proves the finiteness and base change theorems for sheaves of
$\mathbf Z/\ell^n$-modules (SGA4), and these are the results on which
the geometry of these notes rests. The trouble begins when we pass to the
limit over $n$. As recalled in Section [sec-n-pro-etale-why], an $\ell$-adic
sheaf is classically a compatible system $(F_n)$ rather than a sheaf, and
the reason is that inverse limits on the étale site lose information.
The smallest example shows how. Take $X=\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf F_q$
with $\ell\nmid q$. Sheaves on $X_{\acute{e}t}$ are discrete modules over
$\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbf F}_q/\mathbf F_q)=\widehat{\mathbf Z}$,
and the limit of the constant sheaves $\mathbf Z/\ell^n$ is $\Zl$ with the
discrete topology. A continuous homomorphism from $\widehat{\mathbf Z}$ to a
discrete torsion-free group has finite, hence zero, image, and therefore

$$
H^1(X_{\acute{e}t},\varprojlim_n\mathbf Z/\ell^n)=0,
\qquad\text{whereas}\qquad
\varprojlim_nH^1(X_{\acute{e}t},\mathbf Z/\ell^n)=\Zl .
$$

The second group is the expected answer: $\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf F_q$
behaves like a circle whose fundamental group is generated by Frobenius.
The first is wrong because the étale topology only sees the discrete
group $\Zl$, and the sheaf it produces has forgotten that $\Zl$ is
profinite. Jannsen (1988) repaired this by defining continuous étale cohomology
as a derived functor on inverse systems, and Ekedahl built the derived
category of $\ell$-adic systems by hand (Eke). The pro-étale
site of Bhatt and Scholze (BS) repairs it at the level of the site.

We need a site with three properties. Inverse limits of sheaves along
towers of surjections should be well behaved, so that $\Zl$ can be a sheaf
of rings whose cohomology is the derived limit of the torsion cohomology.
The topology should remember the topology of $\Zl$. And sheaves coming from
the étale site should keep their étale cohomology, so that the
classical torsion theorems remain available. The objects of $X_{\mathrm{pro}\acute{e}t}$
are the weakly étale $X$-schemes, among them all cofiltered limits of
affine étale ones, and the coverings are fpqc coverings
(Definition [sk-def-pe-site]). Over a separably closed field the affine
pro-étale objects are the spectra of rings of locally constant
functions on profinite sets, so the pro-étale topos of a geometric point
is, up to the set-theoretic cutoff, the topos of condensed sets (Proposition [sk-prop-pe-condensed-point]). This is where
the topology of the coefficients enters: the sheaf $\Zl$ on a point has as
sections over a profinite set $S$ all continuous maps $S\to\Zl$, not only
the locally constant ones. The following comparison may help to keep the
two sites apart.

| | on $X_{\acute{e}t}$ | on $X_{\mathrm{pro}\acute{e}t}$ |
|---|---|---|
| objects | étale $X$-schemes | weakly étale $X$-schemes |
| local objects | strictly henselian local rings | w-contractible rings |
| topos of a geometric point | sets | condensed sets |
| the sheaf $\Zl$ on a point | $\Zl$ with discrete topology | continuous maps to $\Zl$ |

The first property comes from the w-contractible rings. Roughly, such a
ring is a family of strictly henselian local rings parametrized by an
extremally disconnected profinite set: it is w-local, its local rings at
closed points are strictly henselian, and its $\pi_0$ is extremally
disconnected. Every faithfully flat ind-étale cover of it
splits (Proposition [sk-prop-pe-wc-rings]), and every object of $X_{\mathrm{pro}\acute{e}t}$
is covered by spectra of such rings
(Theorem [sk-thm-pe-weakly-contractible-basis]). On these affines taking
sections is exact, so they play the role that geometric points play for
exactness, and any surjectivity statement may be checked on them without
refining covers. It follows that the pro-étale topos is replete
(Proposition [sk-prop-pe-replete]): the limit of a tower of epimorphisms maps
onto each term. Products are exact (Proposition [sk-prop-pe-products]), every
complex is the derived limit of its truncations
(Proposition [sk-prop-pe-left-complete]), and for a tower of
surjections the cohomology of $\varprojlim_nF_n$ is the derived limit of
the cohomology of the $F_n$ (Proposition [sk-prop-pe-continuous-cohomology]).
Over $\mathbf F_q$ we recover $H^1=\Zl$
(Example [sk-rem-pe-finite-field-adic]). The construction of w-contractible
covers is indirect. We first make the local rings strictly henselian by
simultaneous strict localization, and then replace the profinite space of
components by an extremally disconnected one mapping onto it, using
Gleason's theorem that such spaces are projective among compact Hausdorff
spaces (Proposition [sk-prop-pe-stonean] and Theorem [sk-thm-pe-wc-cover]). Since
the objects of the site are weakly étale rather than limits of étale
schemes, we also need Olivier's theorem that a weakly étale local map out
of a strictly henselian local ring is an isomorphism; it shows that a
weakly étale scheme is covered by a limit of étale schemes
(Theorem [sk-thm-pe-indetale-refinement]).

The third property is the comparison with the étale site. A sheaf on
$X_{\mathrm{pro}\acute{e}t}$ is called classical if its sections on a limit
$\varprojlim_iU_i$ of affine étale schemes are the colimit of its
sections on the $U_i$. The pullback $\nu_X^*$ from $X_{\acute{e}t}$ identifies
étale sheaves with classical sheaves (Theorem [sk-thm-pe-classical-sheaves]),
it preserves cohomology of bounded below complexes
(Theorem [sk-prop-sf-comparison]), it commutes with direct images
along quasi-compact quasi-separated morphisms
(Theorem [sk-thm-pe-direct-image-comparison]), and it identifies
constructible complexes with finite coefficients on the two sites
(Theorem [sk-thm-pe-constructible-comparison]). Note that the coefficient
sheaf $\Zl$ itself is not classical
(Example [sk-rem-pe-nonclassical-example]): its section given by the identity
of the profinite set $\Zl$ comes from no finite stage. So the comparison
theorems apply to the reductions $\mathbf Z/\ell^n$ and not to $\Zl$, and
$\ell$-adic statements are reached from torsion ones through derived
completion (Section [sec-pe-localization-completion]), never by comparison
alone. Moreover, although weakly contractible objects have no
higher cohomology, this says nothing about the cohomological dimension of
other objects. For instance $\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf R$ is covered by
weakly contractible objects (Proposition [sk-prop-pe-hypercover]), and yet
$H^i(\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf R,\mathbf F_2)$, the group cohomology of
$\mathbf Z/2$ with coefficients in $\mathbf F_2$, is nonzero for every
$i\geq0$. For unbounded complexes the comparison
therefore needs the finite cohomological dimension of schemes of finite
type over $k$ (Proposition [sk-prop-pe-unbounded-classical]).

Later sections use these results as follows. The comparison of direct
images transports proper base change from the étale site
(Section [sec-proper-base-change]), and the unbounded comparison with
finite coefficients is what Brown representability needs
(Section [sec-six-functors-basics]). The localization sequences and derived
completion of Section [sec-pe-localization-completion] let us restrict integral
complexes to strata and recover them from their reductions, which is the
basis of the constructibility criterion of Section [sec-coefficients-constructible]. Our
account follows (BS) and the pro-étale chapter of the Stacks
Project (Stacks).

Throughout this section, schemes are arbitrary unless a result states a
finiteness hypothesis, because objects of $X_{\mathrm{pro}\acute{e}t}$ are in general
neither Noetherian nor of finite type, even when $X$ is a variety. The
standing assumption that schemes are of finite type over $k$ resumes in
Section [sec-coefficients-constructible].
