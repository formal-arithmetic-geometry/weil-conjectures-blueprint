The étale topos of $\Omega$ is the topos of sets, whereas by the
proposition its pro-étale topos is the topos of condensed sets. Its final
object is weakly contractible, so taking global sections is exact, but a
condensed sheaf is not determined by its global sections. Geometric
stalks still suffice for classical sheaves. A general sheaf of modules on
the pro-étale site has to be evaluated on all weakly contractible affines
(Corollary [sk-cor-pe-sections]). The completed coefficient sheaf
$\mathbf Z_{\ell,X}$ of Example [sk-rem-pe-nonclassical-example] is of this
general kind, since its sections on a profinite set $S$ are all continuous
functions $S\to\mathbf Z_\ell$.

The sheaf $\mathbf Z_{\ell,X}$ is the inverse limit of the classical sheaves
$(\mathbf Z/\ell^n)_X$ (Lemma [sk-prop-sfb-qlbar-colimit-x]). The
cohomology of an inverse limit with epimorphic transition maps is the
derived limit of the cohomology of the terms; for classical terms this is continuous
étale cohomology.
