The targets in the compactness statement are direct sums of objects of
$\Loc(\Dbc(X,\Lambda))$, and their cohomology sheaves are no longer
constructible. They are, however, filtered colimits of constructible
sheaves, and both continuity and the uniform bound for Ext groups extend
to such colimits. Recall that the ind-category
$\operatorname{Ind}(\mathcal C)$ of a small category $\mathcal C$ has as
objects the filtered diagrams in $\mathcal C$ (“formal filtered colimits”), with
$\Hom(\varinjlim_\alpha C_\alpha,\varinjlim_\beta D_\beta) =\varprojlim_\alpha\varinjlim_\beta\Hom(C_\alpha,D_\beta)$. The
proposition says that for constructible sheaves the formal and the actual
colimits agree.

Already the sheaf $\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbf N}\Lambda_X$ is not constructible,
since its stalks are not finitely generated, so the cohomology sheaves of
objects of $\Loc(\Dbc(X,\Lambda))$ leave $\Cons(X,\Lambda)$ at once. What
the dévissage arguments need from a class of sheaves is that it be
abelian, closed under extensions and direct sums, and that $\Ext^q(C,-)$
behave on it as on constructible sheaves. The proposition gives the
first three properties for $\IndCons(X,\Lambda)$, and the corollary
after it gives the last.
