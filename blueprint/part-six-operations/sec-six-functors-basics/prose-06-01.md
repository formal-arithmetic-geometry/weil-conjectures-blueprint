At this point we know proper direct image well: for every admissible
$\Lambda$ and proper $p:X\to Y$ the functor $Rp_*$ maps $D^b_{\mathrm{ctf}}(X,\Lambda)$
into $D^b_{\mathrm{ctf}}(Y,\Lambda)$ and satisfies proper base change and the projection
formula (Proposition [sk-thm-sfb-proper-base-change]). Since every
separated morphism of finite type has a compactification $f=p\circ j$ with
$j$ an open immersion and $p$ proper (Theorem [sk-thm-nagata-compactification]),
this suggests putting $Rf_!=Rp_*\circ j_!$. What we really want is the
right adjoint $f^!$ of $Rf_!$. Its value $a_X^!\Lambda$ on the structure map
$a_X:X\to\operatorname{Spec}k$ is the dualizing complex of Verdier duality
(Section [sec-poincare-duality]), smooth purity
(Theorem [sk-thm-purity-transport]) computes it for smooth morphisms, and the
local terms of the trace formula (Section [sec-trace-formula]) are
built from $f^!$ for the two projections of a correspondence.

For locally compact spaces Verdier constructed the right adjoint of
$f_!$ from soft resolutions, and in coherent duality Grothendieck and
Hartshorne defined $f^!$ by local formulas and then had to show that they
glue. Neeman observed that, because $D_{\mathrm{qc}}(X)$ is generated by
compact perfect complexes and $Rf_*$ preserves direct sums, Brown
representability gives the adjoint with no formula at all. We follow
Neeman's route. For torsion
coefficients on the étale site, $f^!$ was constructed by Deligne in
SGA 4, Exposé XVIII (SGA4).

The theorem we use is the following. Let $\mathcal T$ be a triangulated
category with arbitrary direct sums, and suppose that $\mathcal T$ is
compactly generated: there is a set $\mathcal G$ of compact objects $G$
(that is, $\Hom(G,-)$ commutes with direct sums) such that $K=0$ whenever
$\Hom(G[n],K)=0$ for all $G\in\mathcal G$ and $n\in\mathbf Z$. Then every
exact functor out of $\mathcal T$ that preserves direct sums has an exact
right adjoint [Stacks, Tag 0A8F](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0A8F), [Stacks, Tag 0A8G](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0A8G). The category $D^b_{\mathrm{ctf}}(X,\Lambda)$ is not closed
under infinite direct sums, so we apply the theorem to the localizing
category $\Loc(D^b_{\mathrm{ctf}}(X,\Lambda))$ that it generates, and the result is
Theorem [sk-prop-sf-existence]: for every admissible $\Lambda$ and every
separated morphism of finite type $f:X\to Y$, there is an exact functor

$$
f^!:\Loc(D^b_{\mathrm{ctf}}(Y,\Lambda))\to\Loc(D^b_{\mathrm{ctf}}(X,\Lambda)),
\qquad
\Hom(Rf_!M,N)\simeq\Hom(M,f^!N).
$$

This proves parts (1)–(3) of Theorem [sk-thm-existence]. For finite
coefficients nothing is lost by passing to the localizing category, since
$\Loc(D^b_{\mathrm{ctf}}(X,A))$ is equivalent to the classical derived category
$D(X_{\acute{e}t},A)$ (Proposition [sk-prop-ctf-finite-comparison]). For a
compactification $f=p\circ j$ the formula $f^!\simeq j^*p^!$ is then a
consequence (Proposition [sk-lem-sf-mate]).

Two hypotheses have to be checked: the objects of $D^b_{\mathrm{ctf}}(X,\Lambda)$ are
compact in $\Loc(D^b_{\mathrm{ctf}}(X,\Lambda))$, and $Rp_*$ preserves direct sums for
proper $p$. Both fail without a uniform bound on cohomological degrees,
because the summands of a direct sum may live in degrees going off to
$-\infty$. Compactness is a real condition even for the
constant sheaf. Take $\ell=2$ and $X=\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf R$, which
lies outside our conventions. Sheaves on $X_{\acute{e}t}$ are modules over
$\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbf C/\mathbf R)=\mathbf Z/2$, and
$\Ext^n(\mathbf F_2,\mathbf F_2)=H^n(\mathbf Z/2,\mathbf F_2)=\mathbf F_2$
for all $n\geq0$. Products of modules are exact, and the complexes
$\mathbf F_2[n]$ live in distinct degrees, so the sum
$\bigoplus_{n\geq0}\mathbf F_2[n]$ in $D(X_{\acute{e}t},\mathbf F_2)$ is also the
product. Hence

$$
\Hom\Bigl(\mathbf F_2,\bigoplus_{n\ge0}\mathbf F_2[n]\Bigr)
=\prod_{n\ge0}\mathbf F_2
\ne\bigoplus_{n\ge0}\mathbf F_2
=\bigoplus_{n\ge0}\Hom(\mathbf F_2,\mathbf F_2[n]).
$$

Our base field is finite or algebraically closed, so its
$\ell$-cohomological dimension is finite (Lemma [sk-lem-base-field-cd]),
and this rules out such examples. For $\Lambda=E$ or $\overline{\mathbf Q}_\ell$, what we
prove is a bound depending only on the source: for every constructible
sheaf $C$ there is $d_C$ with
$\Ext^q(C,N)=0$ for $q>d_C$ and all constructible $N$
(Lemma [sk-lem-uniform-constructible-ext]). It follows that maps from
$C$ into an unbounded complex only see $d_C+1$ of its cohomology sheaves
(Lemma [sk-lem-finite-window]), and compactness follows
(Theorem [sk-thm-constructible-compactness]). For a proper $p$ whose
fibres have dimension at most $d$, the classical torsion bound on the
degrees of $R^qp_*$ is $2d$; we only prove the bound $2d+1$, which
suffices, and $\mathcal H^q(Rp_*K)$ then depends only on the cohomology of $K$ in degrees
$[q-2d-1,q]$ (Lemmas [sk-lem-proper-heart-bound]
and [sk-lem-proper-unbounded-window]).

For $E$ and $\overline{\mathbf Q}_\ell$ these arguments are dévissages along cohomology
sheaves, among complexes whose cohomology sheaves are filtered colimits of
constructible sheaves, with a reduction to lattices and torsion
coefficients. For finite $A$ both facts
reduce to classical étale cohomology, which commutes with direct sums of
unbounded complexes (Section [sec-ctf-finite-foundations]). For $\mathcal O_E$
they are proved in Section [sec-constructibility-upper-shriek]
(Proposition [sk-prop-int-compactness]), by reduction to torsion coefficients and
without using $f^!$. Proper base change then shows that $Rp_*\circ j_!$
does not depend on the compactification
(Proposition [sk-prop-sf-comp-coherence]) and is compatible with
composition and base change (Proposition [sk-thm-sf-comp-composition]).
Passing to right adjoints, $f^!$ is compatible with composition, equals
$j^*$ for an open immersion $j$, and is local on $X$ and on $Y$
(Proposition [sk-lem-sf-mate]), which is what we need to
compute it in the next section.

Brown representability says nothing about the values of $f^!$. In
particular it does not tell us that $f^!$ maps $D^b_{\mathrm{ctf}}$ into $D^b_{\mathrm{ctf}}$; that
is part (4) of Theorem [sk-thm-existence] and the subject of
Section [sec-constructibility-upper-shriek]. Base change for $f^!$ does not
follow by passing to adjoints either, and we prove it only for smooth
morphisms (Proposition [sk-prop-cohsm]).
