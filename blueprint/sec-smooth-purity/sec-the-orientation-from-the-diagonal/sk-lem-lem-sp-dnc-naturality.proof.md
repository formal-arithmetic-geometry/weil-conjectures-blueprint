The geometric identifications follow from the coordinate description of
the deformation space. Every comparison of dualizing complexes is then
the identity at $t=1$, and so the uniqueness in Lemma [sk-thm-dnc]
identifies it with the comparison of that lemma.

*The geometry.* Since $g$ is smooth and $s$ is a section,
$N_s\simeq s^*T_{V/S}$. Formation of relative differentials for a
smooth map commutes with arbitrary base change, and an étale map
induces an isomorphism on relative tangent bundles. This proves both
assertions about normal bundles.

For deformation spaces, we use the cartesian étale coordinate chart
$U\to\mathbf A^d_S$ of the proof of Lemma [sk-thm-dnc]. There the
deformation is

$$
\mathcal D_{s(S)}U
\simeq U\times_{\mathbf A^d_S}\mathbf A^d_{\mathbf A^1_S},
\qquad x_i=t u_i,
$$

because this is the chart of the blowup along $(t,x_1,\ldots,x_d)$ on
which the pullback of the ideal is generated by $t$. This description
commutes with every change of base. Away from the centre, the
deformation is $(V\setminus s(S))\times\mathbf G_m$, which also
commutes with base change. After passing to an étale cover of $S$ if necessary, these
opens cover the deformation space, and their identifications
glue to the canonical one. For an étale map of pairs as in (2), we
obtain the étale map of deformation spaces by flat
base change for the blowup and for the removal of the strict transform,
and the coordinate
description identifies its special fibre with the map of normal
bundles.

*The comparison of dualizing complexes.* Write

$$
\chi_{g,s}:\widetilde s^*\omega_{\widetilde g}\xrightarrow{\sim}a^*s^*\omega_g
$$

for the isomorphism of Lemma [sk-thm-dnc] that is the identity at
$t=1$, so that $\alpha_{g,s}$ is the inverse of its restriction to
$t=0$. For (1), we apply Proposition [sk-prop-cohsm](3) to $g$,
$\pi$ and $\widetilde g$. These exceptional base change isomorphisms
commute with restriction to $t=1$ and $t=0$, because they are adjoint
transposes of base change for compactly supported direct image applied to the
same counit, and this base change respects composition of cartesian
squares. Transported through these isomorphisms, the pullback of $\chi_{g,s}$
is a comparison for $(g',s')$ that is the identity at $t=1$, and hence it equals
$\chi_{g',s'}$ by uniqueness. Restricting to $t=0$ and inverting
proves (1).

For (2), composing $Re_!\dashv e^*$ with $Rg_!\dashv g^!$ gives
$e^*\omega_g\simeq\omega_{g'}$, and likewise for the induced étale
map of deformation spaces. On the fibres $t=1$ and $t=0$ these
identifications are the ones for the original pairs and for the normal
bundles, since the counit of a composite adjunction is the composite of
the counits. They transport $\chi_{g,s}$ to a comparison that is the
identity at $t=1$, which is $\chi_{g',s'}$ by uniqueness.

Finally, the geometric identifications respect composition, and so do
the identifications obtained from adjunctions, as we see by transposing
composite counits. Therefore each composite in the coherence assertions is a
comparison that is the identity at $t=1$, and uniqueness shows that any two of
them agree.
