*Proposition (sketch).*

In the conventions of [sk-def-purity-conv], for $Y$ separated finite type
and the zero section $s:Y\hookrightarrow\Aaf^1_Y$, the complex
$Rs^{!}\Lambda_{\Aaf^1_Y}(1)[2]$ is concentrated in degree zero with lisse
rank-one $\mathcal H^0$, and
$\mathcal H^0\cong H^2_{s(Y)}(\Aaf^1_Y,\Lambda(1))$ with normalized
generator $1$ at every geometric point. The boundary of $\kappa(t)$ is
the *fundamental class (orientation)*

$$
\mathrm{pur}_s:\Lambda_Y\longrightarrow Rs^{!}\Lambda_{\Aaf^1_Y}(1)[2],
$$

the unique morphism with this normalization (sections of a lisse
sheaf are determined by their stalks).

## Proof

The closed–open pair $(s,j)$ gives the localization triangle
$Rs_*Rs^{!}F\to F\to Rj_*j^*F\xrightarrow{+1}$ on $\Aaf^1_Y$. Apply
$Rp_*$ and use $ps=\mathrm{id}$, so that $Rp_*Rs_*Rs^{!}F=Rs^{!}F$:

$$
Rs^{!}\Lambda(1)\longrightarrow Rp_*\Lambda(1)
\longrightarrow R\pi_*\Lambda(1)\xrightarrow{\ +1\ }.
$$

By [sk-prop-a1-kummer] (a) the middle term is $\Lambda_Y(1)$ in degree zero;
by (b) and (c) the right-hand term has $\mathcal H^0=\Lambda_Y(1)$,
$\mathcal H^1=\Lambda_Y\cdot\kappa(t)$ and nothing else. The degree-zero
map is $p^*$, an isomorphism by (b), so the long exact sequence forces
$\mathcal H^m(Rs^{!}\Lambda(1))=0$ for $m\ne2$ and
$\mathcal H^2(Rs^{!}\Lambda(1))\simeq\mathcal H^1(R\pi_*\Lambda(1)) \simeq\Lambda_Y$, generated by the boundary $\partial\kappa(t)$.
Thus $Rs^{!}\Lambda(1)[2]$ sits in degree zero with $\mathcal H^0$ lisse of
rank one, identified with $H^2_{s(Y)}(\Aaf^1_Y,\Lambda(1))$ by the same
triangle. Base-change compatibility in [sk-prop-a1-kummer] normalizes the
generator to $1$ at every geometric point. Finally a morphism
$\Lambda_Y\to Rs^{!}\Lambda(1)[2]$ is a global section of that lisse sheaf,
hence determined by its stalks; so $\mathrm{pur}_s$ is the unique morphism
with the stated normalization.

Uses: [sk-def-purity-conv], [sk-prop-a1-kummer]
