Compare $J'$-transposes, i.e. compose with $Rf_{!,\Lambda'}$ and then
$\varepsilon^{J'}(K_{\Lambda'})$. For the right-hand side, the counit
compatibility of $\gamma'$ gives
$\varepsilon^{J'}\circ Rf_{!,\Lambda'}(\gamma')=\varepsilon_{f,\Lambda'}$, the
definition of $c_f$ gives
$\varepsilon_{f,\Lambda'}(K_{\Lambda'})\circ Rf_{!,\Lambda'}(c_f(K))=(\varepsilon_{f,\Lambda}(K))_{\Lambda'}\circ b(f^!_\Lambda K)^{-1}$,
naturality of $b$ moves $Rf_{!,\Lambda'}((\gamma^{-1})_{\Lambda'})$ to
$(Rf_{!,\Lambda}\gamma^{-1})_{\Lambda'}$, and the counit compatibility of $\gamma$
gives $\varepsilon_{f,\Lambda}(K)\circ Rf_{!,\Lambda}(\gamma(K)^{-1})=\varepsilon^J(K)$.
So the transpose of the right-hand side is
$(\varepsilon^J(K))_{\Lambda'}\circ b(JK)^{-1}$, which is by definition the
transpose of $c_J(K)$; hence the two sides are equal. As $\gamma,\gamma'$ are
isomorphisms, $c_J$ is an isomorphism iff $c_f$ is. For a chain
$\Lambda\to\Lambda'\to\Lambda''$, conjugating the composition identity for $c_f$
by the $\gamma$'s gives that for $c_J$, the middle factors $\gamma'$ and
$\gamma'^{-1}$ cancelling.
