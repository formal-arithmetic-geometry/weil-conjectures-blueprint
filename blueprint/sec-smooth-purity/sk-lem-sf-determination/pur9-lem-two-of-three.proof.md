$\Phi$ and $\Psi$ carry the triangle to distinguished triangles, and by naturality and the shift compatibility of $\alpha$ the maps $(\alpha_X,\alpha_Y,\alpha_W)$ form a morphism of triangles. For every object $V$ of $\mathscr T'$, $\operatorname{Hom}(V,-)$ carries both triangles to long exact sequences, and $\alpha$ induces a map between them in which four of every five consecutive terms are bijections (using $\alpha_{X[1]}$ and $\alpha_{Y[1]}$, invertible by Lemma [pur9-lem-shift-invertibility], when $W$ is the unknown, and similarly otherwise). The five lemma makes the fifth a bijection for all $V$, so the Yoneda lemma makes the remaining component an isomorphism.
