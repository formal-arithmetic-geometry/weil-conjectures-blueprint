By [def-pur-td-datum], $\epsilon$ and $\eta$ are morphisms of
the ordinary functors $Rf_!$ and $G_L$, and the two assumed
equations are precisely the two triangular identities of an
adjunction. Thus $G_L$ is right adjoint to $Rf_!$ on the finite-Tor
categories. The functor $Rf^!$ of [def-weil-exceptional-inverse-image] is
another right adjoint there: its adjunction holds for $A\in
D^b_{ctf}(X,\Lambda)$ and $K\in D^b_{ctf}(Y,\Lambda)$ (at a finite stage
these are bounded-below objects of étale origin), and it carries
$D^b_{ctf}(Y,\Lambda)$ into $D^b_{ctf}(X,\Lambda)$; no ambient datum of
[def-weil-ambient-exceptional-adjoint] is used. The uniqueness of right
adjoints on these categories gives the displayed identification, compatible
with the counits by construction. Taking the value at $\Lambda_Y$ gives
$\omega_f\cong L$, and hence the identification
$Rf^!\cong Lf^*(-)\otimes^L L$.
Consequently, on $D^b_{ctf}(Y,\Lambda)$, the identification
exhibits $Rf^!$ as an exact functor carrying finite-Tor
complexes with cohomology in an interval $[a,b]$ into those with
cohomology in $[a+p,b+q]$, where $[p,q]$ is a cohomological interval
for $L$: the analogue of clause (2) of
[def-weil-exceptional-adjoint-admissibility] holds for $Rf^!$ on
finite-Tor objects whenever such a trace–diagonal datum and such an $L$
exist. No clause of that definition is assumed in the argument.
