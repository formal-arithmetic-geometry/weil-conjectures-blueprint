A morphism $f:X\to Y$ of schemes is
smooth if and only if it is flat,
locally of finite presentation, and every fibre $X_y$ is smooth over
$\kappa(y)$.
(*Stacks Project: a smooth morphism is locally of finite
presentation and flat by
[Lemma 29.35.8](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/01VE) and
[Lemma 29.35.9](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/01VF), its
fibres are smooth by base change,
[Lemma 29.35.5](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/01VB), and
conversely flatness, local finite presentation and smooth fibres give
smoothness by
[Lemma 29.35.3](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/01V8); the two
definitions of smoothness are identified by
[Lemma 37.11.7](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/02H6). For
morphisms of finite type the criterion is the Jacobian criterion,
[Lemma 29.35.14](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/01V9).*)

## Proof

This is the recorded classical input named in the statement: smooth morphisms are flat and locally of finite presentation (Stacks Project, Lemmas 29.35.8 and 29.35.9, [Tag 01VE](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/01VE), [Tag 01VF](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/01VF)), have smooth fibres by base change (Lemma 29.35.5, [Tag 01VB](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/01VB)), and conversely flatness with local finite presentation and smooth fibres gives smoothness (Lemma 29.35.3, [Tag 01V8](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/01V8)), the two definitions of smoothness being identified by Lemma 37.11.7 ([Tag 02H6](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/02H6)); for morphisms of finite type this is the Jacobian criterion, Lemma 29.35.14 ([Tag 01V9](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/01V9)). The blueprint uses the criterion, which is why it is recorded here; no separate proof is given.
