In a cartesian square with $u:X'\to X$, $f':X'\to Y'$, $f:X\to Y$ and $g:Y'\to Y$, smooth base
change assumes $g$ smooth and
allows any $f$ in the standing range. Proper base change assumes $f$
proper and allows any $g$. Smooth purity, in the next section, gives a
third exchange isomorphism $u^*f^!\simeq f'^!g^*$, for the exceptional
inverse image, when $f$ is smooth and $g$ is arbitrary.

Without properness of $f$, the stalk of $Rf_*K$ at a geometric point
can differ from the cohomology of the geometric fibre. Over an
algebraically closed $k$, take
$j:\mathbf A^1_k\setminus\{0\}\hookrightarrow\mathbf A^1_k$
and $K=\Fell$. The classical stalk of $j_*\Fell$ at $0$ is $\Fell$,
whereas the fibre of $j$ over $0$ is empty. Theorem [sk-thm-sbc-coefficients]
says nothing here, because the point immersion
$\{0\}\hookrightarrow\mathbf A^1_k$ is not smooth.
