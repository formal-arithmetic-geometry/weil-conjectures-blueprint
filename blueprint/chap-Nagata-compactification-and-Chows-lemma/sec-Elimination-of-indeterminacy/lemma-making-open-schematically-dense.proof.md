We blow up an ideal that cuts out the complement of $U$. Its pullback
is then locally generated by a nonzerodivisor $a$, and an open whose
complement is cut out by a nonzerodivisor is schematically dense,
because a section killed by a power of $a$ is zero.

Choose a quasi-coherent ideal $\mathcal I$ of finite type with
$V(\mathcal I)=T\setminus U$. Such an ideal exists because $U$ is a
quasi-compact open in a qcqs scheme [Stacks, [01PH](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/01PH)]. We blow up this
ideal, and the extended ideal defines the exceptional Cartier divisor.
On the affine charts its local equation $a$ is a nonzerodivisor. Hence
each chart ring injects into its localization at $a$. The complement of
the divisor is the inverse image of $U$, and it is therefore
schematically dense.
