We quote this from [Stacks, [0F3V](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0F3V)] and sketch the argument, which
works with closed subschemes of $T$ rather than closed subsets of $V$. Let
$\mathcal I_1,\mathcal I_2$ be ideals of finite type on $T$ cutting out
closed subschemes $Z_1,Z_2$ that are disjoint over $V$ and restrict
to $F_1,F_2$ there. Producing such ideals on all of $T$ is where
constructibility and finite presentation are needed. Since
$\mathcal I_1+\mathcal I_2$ is the unit ideal over $V$, its blow-up
is $V$-admissible. On each affine chart the extended ideal is
generated by a single element of $\mathcal I_1$ or of $\mathcal I_2$.
On a chart where it is generated by $f\in\mathcal I_1$, the strict
transform of $Z_1$ is obtained from its inverse image by dividing out
the $f$-power torsion. Since $f$ vanishes on $Z_1$, every element of
the ring of the inverse image is killed by $f$. Hence the strict
transform of $Z_1$ does not meet that chart. The same holds for
$Z_2$ on the other charts, and therefore the strict transforms of $Z_1$
and $Z_2$ are disjoint [Stacks, [080P](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/080P)].
In the Noetherian applications below, every closed subset is
constructible and its reduced ideal is of finite type.
