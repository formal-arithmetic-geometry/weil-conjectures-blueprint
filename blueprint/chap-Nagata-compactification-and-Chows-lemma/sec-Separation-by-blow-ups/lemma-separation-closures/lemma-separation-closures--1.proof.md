Choose finite-type quasi-coherent ideals $\mathcal I_1,\mathcal I_2$
on $T$ whose vanishing sets restrict to $F_1,F_2$ on $V$.
For each $i$, one first cuts out $F_i$ by a finite-type ideal on $V$,
using the quasi-compactness of $V\setminus F_i$, and then extends this
ideal to $T$. This is
`AlgebraicGeometry.Scheme.IdealSheafData.exists_finiteType_preimage_coe_support_eq`.
Write $Z_i$ for the closed subscheme cut out by $\mathcal I_i$.
Since $F_1$ and $F_2$ are disjoint, $Z_1\cap Z_2$ misses $V$.
Thus the blow-up $b:T'\to T$ of $\mathcal I_1+\mathcal I_2$ is
$V$-admissible; this is `AlgebraicGeometry.separationBlowup`.

The strict transforms $Z_1',Z_2'$ of [definition-strict-transform],
formed away from the center, are disjoint. Indeed, the blow-up is
covered by charts coming from local generators of $\mathcal I_1$
or $\mathcal I_2$. On a chart coming from $\mathcal I_i$, the extended
center is generated by a section of $\mathcal I_i$, so $Z_i'$ is empty
on that chart. This gives
`AlgebraicGeometry.disjoint_range_strictTransformMap_sup`.

Each $b^{-1}(j(F_i))$ lies in $Z_i'$: it lies over $V$, which misses
the center, and maps into $Z_i$. Since the image of $Z_i'$ in $T'$ is
closed, it also contains $\overline{b^{-1}(j(F_i))}$. This is
`AlgebraicGeometry.IsAdmissible.closure_preimage_subset_range_strictTransformMap`.
The disjointness of the strict transforms therefore gives the required
disjointness of the closures.
