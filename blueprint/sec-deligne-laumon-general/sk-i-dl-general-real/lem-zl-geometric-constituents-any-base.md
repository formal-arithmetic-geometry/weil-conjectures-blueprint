Let $k$ be an algebraically closed field, let $\rho:W\to\operatorname{GL}_k(V)$
be a finite-dimensional representation of a group $W$, let
$\Pi\subseteq W$ be a normal subgroup and $F\in W$. Then there are
$m\geq1$ and a finite chain of subspaces
$0=U_0\subsetneq U_1\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq U_n=V$, each stable under
$\Pi$ and under $\rho(F^m)$, such that every successive quotient
$U_{i+1}/U_i$ is irreducible under $\Pi$.

## Proof

Induction on $\dim V$; for $V=0$ take $n=0$ and $m=1$. Let $V\ne0$ and
write $T=\rho(F)$. Choose a nonzero $\Pi$-stable subspace $U$ of minimal
dimension; it is $\Pi$-irreducible. Since $\Pi$ is normal, each
$T^jU$ ($j\in\mathbf Z$) is again $\Pi$-stable and $\Pi$-irreducible:
$\rho(g)T^jU=T^j\rho(F^{-j}gF^j)U=T^jU$. Their sum $S$ is stable under
$\Pi$ and $T$, and is $\Pi$-semisimple, being a sum of irreducible
$\Pi$-subspaces. Conjugation by $F$ permutes the isomorphism classes of
irreducible $\Pi$-representations, so $T$ permutes the finitely many
$\Pi$-isotypic components of $S$. Choose $m_1\ge1$ and an isotypic
component $I\neq0$ with $T^{m_1}I=I$.

Write $I\simeq S_1\otimes M$ with $\Pi$ acting irreducibly on $S_1$ and
trivially on $M$. The conjugate of $S_1$ by $F^{m_1}$ lies in the same
isotypic component, so there is an invertible $\tau$ on $S_1$ with
$\tau\rho(g)=\rho(F^{m_1}gF^{-m_1})\tau$ for $g\in\Pi$. Then
$T^{m_1}(\tau^{-1}\otimes1)$ commutes with $\Pi$ on $I$, and Schur's lemma
[lem-wf-schur-scalar] identifies the action of $F^{m_1}$ on $I$ with
$\tau\otimes B$ for an invertible operator $B$ on $M$. The operator $B$
has an eigenline $\langle v\rangle$, as $k$ is algebraically closed; the
subspace $U_1=S_1\otimes v$ is $\Pi$-irreducible and $T^{m_1}$-stable.

Apply the induction hypothesis to the representation of $W$ on $V/U_1$:
it gives $m_2\ge1$ and a chain of subspaces of $V/U_1$, stable under
$\Pi$ and $\rho(F^{m_2})$, with $\Pi$-irreducible quotients. Put
$m=m_1m_2$ and take $U_1$ followed by the preimages of that chain.
Every member is stable under $\Pi$ and under $\rho(F^m)$, a power of
both $\rho(F^{m_1})$ and $\rho(F^{m_2})$; the successive quotients are
$U_1$ and the quotients of the chain in $V/U_1$, all $\Pi$-irreducible.

In the application, $X_0$ is smooth and geometrically connected over a
finite field, $\mathcal G$ is a lisse $\Qlb$-Weil sheaf on $X_0$, $V$ its
representation of the Weil group $W$ of [def-wf-weil-group] (via
[def-wf-fundamental-representation]), $\Pi=\pi_1(X)$, and $F$ a
Frobenius lift. The subgroup generated by $\Pi$ and $F^m$ is the Weil
group after a degree-$m$ constant extension, by the group calculation of
[lem-wf-weil-group-constant-extension]. So after this constant extension
$\mathcal G$ has a finite filtration by lisse Weil subsheaves with
geometrically irreducible nonzero quotients.
These subrepresentations are objects of the finite-stage coefficient
category.
Indeed, start with a finite coefficient field defining the original
representation and its Frobenius map, as in
[def-weil-pbc-qbar-category]. Choose bases for the finitely many
subspaces in the resulting filtration. Their finitely many coordinates
belong to one finite extension $E'$ of that field. The subspaces
they span over $E'$ are stable: this can be checked after the faithful
scalar extension $E'\subset\Qlb$, where stability has already been
proved. Their subspace and quotient representations are continuous
for the geometric fundamental group and inherit the $F^m$-action.
Thus they give lisse Weil subsheaves and quotients at the finite stage
$E'$, which also justifies applying the finite matrix witnesses to
these constituents.
This argument concerns the extension $\Pi\rtimes\mathbf Z$ only
and does not use a rank-one weight theorem on $X_0$.
