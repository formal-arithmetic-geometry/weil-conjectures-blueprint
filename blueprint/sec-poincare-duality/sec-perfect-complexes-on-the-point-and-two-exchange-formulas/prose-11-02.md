All duality statements are eventually reduced to the geometric point,
where constructible complexes are perfect complexes and duality is the
duality of finite projective modules. This is the case $\dim X=0$ of
biduality, and it is also where the induction in the proof of
Theorem [sk-thm-pd-verdier-duality] ends. Over $\mathbf Z/\ell^n$ perfectness is a real
condition, since a module such as $\mathbf Z/\ell$ has infinite projective
dimension over $\mathbf Z/\ell^2$, and this is why the coefficients are
required to have finite Tor dimension. Lemma [sk-lem-finite] says
that on $D^b_{\mathrm{ctf}}$ nothing else can go wrong.

With the point understood, we define the dualizing complex $\omega_X$,
the Verdier dual $\mathbb D_X$ and the double-dual map
$\delta_K:K\to\mathbb D_X\mathbb D_XK$. Then we prove the half of the
exchange formalism that needs only adjunctions
(Lemma [sk-thm-sf-tensor-hom]): $\mathbb D$ turns $Rf_!$ into $Rf_*$
and $f^*$ into $f^!$. For $f=a_X$ and $K=\Lambda_X$ the first says that
$R\Gamma(X,\omega_X)$ is the dual of $R\Gamma_c(X,\Lambda)$, as for the
homology of a manifold, and for $K=\Lambda_Y$ the second is
$f^!\omega_Y=\omega_X$. The other half, with the roles of $!$ and $*$
reversed, needs biduality.
