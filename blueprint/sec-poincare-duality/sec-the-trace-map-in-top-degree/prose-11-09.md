For constant coefficients on a smooth, proper and connected $X$, Poincaré
duality in degree zero says that $H^{2d}(X,\Lambda_X(d))$ is free of
rank one, generated by the class of a point. This is the normalization
used in the trace formula and in point counting.

For a compact connected oriented manifold this is the statement that
integration $H^n(M)\to\mathbf R$ is an isomorphism sending a point to
$1$. Here it has an arithmetic meaning as well. Geometric Frobenius
acts on $\Lambda(-d)$ by $q^d$, so once the trace map is known to
commute with Frobenius (Theorem [sk-thm-frobenius]), the theorem below
says that Frobenius acts on $H^{2d}$ by $q^d$ and on $H^0$ trivially.
This gives the factors $1-T$ and $1-q^dT$ of the zeta function, and for
$X=\mathbf P^1$ the traces $1$ and $q$ in degrees $0$ and $2$ add up to
the $q+1$ rational points. The proof starts from Poincaré duality in
degree zero with coefficients $\mathbf Z/\ell^n$; what has to be added
is the vanishing outside $[0,2d]$, the passage from $\mathbf Z/\ell^n$
to general coefficients, where
derived duals of cohomology groups are not their ordinary duals, and the
value of the trace on the class of a point. The theorem combines with
Corollary [sk-thm-derived-duality] to give the cup product pairing
(Corollary [sk-cor-pd-cup-product]).
