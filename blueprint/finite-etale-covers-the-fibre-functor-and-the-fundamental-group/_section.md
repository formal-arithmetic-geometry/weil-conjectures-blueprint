The Galois-theoretic dictionary that the rest of the blueprint uses. Three
places invoke it without proof and without citation: the definition of the
fundamental representation of lisse sheaves in the monodromy chapter
("the standard equivalence of lisse sheaves on a connected scheme with
continuous representations of its etale fundamental group"); the proof of
the finite-constant $H^1$ lemma there ("the defining finite-etale
fibre-functor equivalence"); and the proof of the lisse-trivialization
lemma of the smooth base change chapter ("open subgroups correspond to
finite etale coverings"). The chapter closes the gap [rem-galois-uses].

The development follows SGA 1, Exposé V, §§4–6 and Stacks Project, Chapter
58, §§58.3 and 58.5, with the proofs written out rather than cited: the
abstract Galois-category machinery, the existence of Galois objects (proved
by the $S_n$-action on the $n$-fold fibre power, which replaces the "Galois
closure" as a black box), the transitivity of the action of
$\operatorname{Aut}(F)$ on fibres, the abstract equivalence theorem, the
concrete facts that finite etale covers form a Galois category (finite
limits and colimits, connected objects are connected schemes, $F$ reflects
isomorphisms), and finally the equivalence with finite continuous
$\pi_1$-sets together with the three corollaries used downstream.

The inputs used and not reproved are elementary and are named in the status
notes: Nakayama's lemma and the structure of finite etale algebras over a
separably closed field (for the etale-local triviality of finite etale
covers), gluing of schemes along open immersions, and the limit arguments
spreading finitely presented data out over an etale neighbourhood.
