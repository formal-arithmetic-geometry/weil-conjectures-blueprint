Since the transform is an exact equivalence of perverse hearts, it
sends simple objects to simple objects, and
Lemma [sk-lem-wft-curve-simples] lists these. The only question is when
the transform of a middle extension is punctual, and the hypothesis
of the next proposition excludes this. Inversion answers it. The
inverse transform of a skyscraper at $a$ is a linear character sheaf
$\mathcal L_{\psi(-ax)}$, shifted by one and twisted, so a middle
extension with punctual transform is, geometrically, a sum of linear
characters. These
are exactly the sheaves for which the Gauss-sum picture breaks down:
for $a\ne0$ the sheaf $\mathcal L_{\psi(ax)}$ has no cohomology with
compact support on the line (Lemma [sk-lem-wft-character-integral]), and
its transform is a skyscraper rather than a lisse sheaf of weight one. Excluding them
costs nothing for the application, since a sheaf unramified at
infinity has no such constituent other than the constant one, and
constant constituents contribute nothing, since
$H^1_c(\mathbf A^1,\overline{\mathbf Q}_\ell)=0$.
The outcome is the sheaf $M'$ on a dense open of the dual line that
Section [sec-deligne-laumon-a1] studies.

Let $j:U_0\hookrightarrow\mathbf A^1_{k_0}$ be a dense open, and let
$M$ be an irreducible lisse Weil sheaf on $U_0$.
