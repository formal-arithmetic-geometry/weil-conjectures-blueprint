By Proposition [sk-p-dl-a1-perverse], the transform of the simple
object $(j_*M)[1]$ is simple, and so by
Lemma [sk-lem-wft-curve-simples] it is either punctual or of the
displayed form. Restricting the kernel to $\{a\}\times\mathbf A^1$, we
see that the inverse transform of a geometric skyscraper at $a$ is
$\mathcal L_{\psi(-ax)}(1)[1]$. Hence, if the transform were punctual,
geometric base change and Fourier inversion would make the restriction
of $M$ a sum of these linear characters, which the hypothesis
excludes. To see that the open and the sheaf are defined over $k_0$,
we choose a Weil-stable open on which the constructible transform is
lisse.

*Unramified sheaves.* For the last assertion, we first show that
$\mathcal L_{\psi(ax)}$ with $a\ne0$ is ramified at infinity. Write $t=1/x$ and express the character by
the Artin–Schreier equation $u^p-u=c/t$ with $c\ne0$. Let $v$ be the
normalized valuation of the field generated by a root $u$ over the
completion $k((t))$ at infinity, and let $e$ be the ramification
index, so that $v(t)=e$ and $e\le p$. Since $v(c/t)=-e<0$, we must
have $v(u)<0$, hence $v(u^p)=pv(u)<v(u)$ and
$v(u^p-u)=pv(u)$. It follows that $pv(u)=-e$, so $p\mid e$ and
$e=p$, that is, the cover is totally ramified. Hence inertia at
infinity maps onto the Galois group $\mathbf F_p$ of this cover, on
which $\psi$ is nontrivial. The character is therefore nontrivial on
inertia, and it cannot be a constituent of a representation on which
inertia acts trivially. It remains to
exclude the trivial character. By
Lemma [sk-i-restriction], applied to the normal geometric
subgroup of the Weil group, the restriction of $M$ to the geometric
fundamental group is semisimple. A trivial geometric constituent
therefore gives nonzero geometric invariants. This invariant space is
Weil-stable, so by irreducibility $M$ is geometrically constant.
