These are notes for a course on the proof of the Weil conjectures, for
students who have had a first course in algebraic geometry. We construct
$\ell$-adic cohomology with its six operations, prove the
Grothendieck–Lefschetz trace formula and Poincaré duality, and deduce
that the zeta function of a variety over a finite field is rational and,
for smooth proper varieties, satisfies a functional equation. We then
prove Deligne's theorem that the cohomology of a smooth proper variety is
pure, following Laumon's proof by the Fourier transform. From this we
obtain the Riemann hypothesis. We also see that the characteristic
polynomials of Frobenius have integer coefficients independent of $\ell$.
To treat finite, $\ell$-adic and rational coefficients at once, we work
on the pro-étale site with condensed coefficients. Passing from one
coefficient ring to another is then a comparison theorem rather than a
construction by limits.
