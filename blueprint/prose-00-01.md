These are notes for a course on the proof of the Weil conjectures, for
students who have had a first course in algebraic geometry. We construct
$\ell$-adic cohomology with its six operations, prove the
Grothendieck–Lefschetz trace formula and Poincaré duality, and deduce
that the zeta function of a variety over a finite field is rational and,
for smooth proper varieties, satisfies a functional equation. We then
prove Deligne's theorem that the cohomology of a smooth proper variety is
pure, following Laumon's proof by the Fourier transform. This gives the
Riemann hypothesis, and shows that the characteristic polynomials of
Frobenius have integer coefficients independent of $\ell$. To treat
finite, $\ell$-adic and rational coefficients at once, we work on the
pro-étale site with condensed coefficients; the passage between
coefficient rings then becomes a comparison theorem instead of a
construction by limits.

The notes are adapted from the blueprint of a Lean formalization of the
Weil conjectures [blue]; we follow the constructions of
the Lean project at the revision cited in [lean], and
include arguments not yet formalized there.
