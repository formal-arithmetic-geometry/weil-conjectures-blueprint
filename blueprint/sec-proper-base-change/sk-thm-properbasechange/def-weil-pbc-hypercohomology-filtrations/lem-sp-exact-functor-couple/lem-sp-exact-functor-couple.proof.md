**Step 1.** **Part 1: $\Phi C$ is an exact couple.** By [def-sp-exact-couple] (Stacks Definition 12.21.1, Tag 011Q, bigraded as in Remark 12.21.5, Tag 0AMJ) an exact couple is the datum of the maps $\alpha$, $f$, $g$ of bidegrees $(-1,+1)$, $(+2,-1)$, $(0,0)$ such that at every bidegree the three-term sequences $E^{p-2,q+1}\xrightarrow{f}D^{p,q}\xrightarrow{\alpha}D^{p-1,q+1}$, $D^{p,q}\xrightarrow{g}E^{p,q}\xrightarrow{f}D^{p+2,q-1}$ and $D^{p+1,q-1}\xrightarrow{\alpha}D^{p,q}\xrightarrow{g}E^{p,q}$ are exact (kernel equals image; consecutive composites zero). Applying $\Phi$ gives maps of the same bidegrees between the objects $\Phi D^{p,q}$, $\Phi E^{p,q}$; $\Phi$ is additive, so it sends the zero composites to zero, and by [lem-sp-exact-functor-preserves-homology](c) it carries each of these exact sequences to an exact sequence. So $\Phi C$ satisfies the three exactness conditions at every bidegree.

**Step 2.** **One derived step commutes with $\Phi$.** Let $C=(D,E,\alpha,f,g)$ be any exact couple in $\mathcal A$ and $C'=(D',E',\alpha',f',g')$ its derived couple [def-sp-exact-couple] (Stacks Lemma 12.21.2, Tag 011R), an exact couple by [lem-sp-derived-couple-exact]; by Part 1, $\Phi(C')$ and $(\Phi C)'$ are exact couples in $\mathcal B$. We show that (identity, $\theta$) is an isomorphism of exact couples $\Phi(C')\to(\Phi C)'$ for an isomorphism $\theta$ on $E$-terms, and that $\Phi(D')=(\Phi D)'$ as subobjects. (i) $D$-terms: $D'^{p,q}=\operatorname{im}(\alpha:D^{p+1,q-1}\to D^{p,q})$, and by [lem-sp-exact-functor-preserves-homology](b) $\Phi(D'^{p,q})=\operatorname{im}(\Phi\alpha)=(\Phi D)'^{p,q}$ as subobjects of $\Phi D^{p,q}$, the epimorphism $\Phi(\bar\alpha):\Phi D^{p+1,q-1}\twoheadrightarrow\Phi D'^{p,q}$ being the corestriction $\overline{\Phi\alpha}$ of $\Phi\alpha$. (ii) $E$-terms: with $d=g f$, $\Phi(d)=\Phi g\circ\Phi f=d^\Phi$ is the differential of $\Phi C$, and [lem-sp-exact-functor-preserves-homology](c) applied to $E^{p-2,q+1}\xrightarrow{d}E^{p,q}\xrightarrow{d}E^{p+2,q-1}$ gives $\Phi(\ker d)=\ker d^\Phi$ as subobjects and an isomorphism $\theta:\Phi(E'^{p,q})=\Phi(\ker d/\operatorname{im}d)\xrightarrow{\sim}\ker d^\Phi/\operatorname{im}d^\Phi=(\Phi E)'^{p,q}$ with $\theta\circ\Phi(\pi)=\pi^\Phi$ on $\Phi(\ker d)=\ker d^\Phi$, $\pi,\pi^\Phi$ the projections to cohomology. (iii) $\alpha'$ is the restriction of $\alpha$ to $D'$, i.e. the unique map with $\iota\circ\alpha'=\alpha\circ\iota$ for the inclusions $\iota$ ($\iota$ mono); applying $\Phi$, $\Phi(\iota)\circ\Phi(\alpha')=\Phi\alpha\circ\Phi(\iota)$ and $\Phi(\iota)$ is the inclusion of $(\Phi D)'$ (by (i)), so $\Phi(\alpha')=(\Phi\alpha)'$. (iv) $f'$ is characterized by $\iota\circ f'\circ\pi=f\circ j$, with $j:\ker d\hookrightarrow E$ ($\pi$ epi, $\iota$ mono); applying $\Phi$ and using (i), (ii): $\iota^\Phi\circ\Phi(f')\circ\theta^{-1}\circ\pi^\Phi=\iota^\Phi\circ\Phi(f')\circ\Phi(\pi)=\Phi f\circ j^\Phi$, which is the characterizing identity of $(\Phi f)'$; hence $\Phi(f')\circ\theta^{-1}=(\Phi f)'$. (v) $g'$ is characterized by $g'\circ\bar\alpha=\pi\circ\hat g$, where $\hat g:D^{p+1,q-1}\to\ker d\subseteq E^{p+1,q-1}$ is the factorization of $g$ ($d g=g f g=0$) and $\bar\alpha$ is epi ([lem-sp-derived-couple-exact]); applying $\Phi$: $\theta\circ\Phi(g')\circ\overline{\Phi\alpha}=\theta\circ\Phi(\pi)\circ\Phi(\hat g)=\pi^\Phi\circ\widehat{\Phi g}$, the characterizing identity of $(\Phi g)'$, and $\overline{\Phi\alpha}$ is epi, so $\theta\circ\Phi(g')=(\Phi g)'$. By (iii)-(v), (identity on $D'$, $\theta$ on $E'$) commutes with all three structure maps, so it is an isomorphism of exact couples $\Phi(C')\to(\Phi C)'$ (Stacks Definition 12.21.1(2), Tag 011Q).

**Step 3.** **Part 2: induction on $r$.** For $r=2$, $\Phi(C_2)=\Phi C=(\Phi C)_2$, $\phi_2=\mathrm{id}$. Suppose (identity, $\phi_r$) is an isomorphism of exact couples $\Phi(C_r)\to(\Phi C)_r$ with $\Phi(D_r)=(\Phi D)_r$ as subobjects of $\Phi D$ ($C_r$ is an exact couple by [lem-sp-derived-couple-exact], so the previous step applies to it). A morphism of exact couples induces a morphism of the derived couples, functorially ([lem-sp-couple-morphism]; Stacks Lemma 12.21.2, Tag 011R): on $D'$ the restriction, on $E'$ the map on cohomology; so the isomorphism (identity, $\phi_r$) and its inverse induce mutually inverse morphisms, and (identity, $\bar\phi_r$) is an isomorphism $\Phi(C_r)'\to((\Phi C)_r)'=(\Phi C)_{r+1}$, $\bar\phi_r$ the map induced by $\phi_r$ on cohomology of $\Phi(d_r)$ and $d_r^\Phi$. Composing with the isomorphism $(\mathrm{id},\theta):\Phi(C_{r+1})=\Phi(C_r')\to\Phi(C_r)'$ of the previous step, applied to $C_r$, gives the isomorphism (identity, $\phi_{r+1}=\bar\phi_r\circ\theta$) of exact couples $\Phi(C_{r+1})\to(\Phi C)_{r+1}$, and $\Phi(D_{r+1})=(\Phi(D_r))'=((\Phi D)_r)'=(\Phi D)_{r+1}$ as subobjects. Since $d_r=g_rf_r$ and $d_r^\Phi=g_r^\Phi f_r^\Phi$ and $\Phi$ is a functor, compatibility of $\phi_r$ with the structure maps gives $\phi_r\circ\Phi(d_r)=d_r^\Phi\circ\phi_r$. This proves Part 2 for all $r\geq2$ (Stacks Definition 12.21.3, Tag 011S, for the pages of the spectral sequence of a couple).

**Step 4.** **Part 3: hypotheses and $E_\infty$.** $\Phi$ is additive, so $\Phi(0)=0$: the vanishing $D^{p,q}=0$ ($q<a$) and $E^{p,q}=0$ (unless $p\geq0$, $q\geq a$) give the same vanishing for $\Phi D$, $\Phi E=(\Phi E)_2$, so $\Phi C$ satisfies the hypotheses of [thm-sp-half-plane-convergence] with the same $a$, and that theorem gives for both couples the same bound $r>\max(q-a+1,p)$ beyond which $E_r^{p,q}$ is stable, the identification $E_{r+1}^{p,q}=\ker d_r/\operatorname{im}d_r=E_r^{p,q}/0=E_r^{p,q}$ coming from the vanishing of the incoming and outgoing $d_r$ (both have a source or a target outside the region). For such $r$, $\Phi(d_r)=0$ and $d_r^\Phi=0$ at $(p,q)$, so $\theta$ is the identity of $\Phi(E_r^{p,q})$ and the map $\bar\phi_r$ induced on cohomology is $\phi_r$ itself (Step 3): $\phi_{r+1}^{p,q}=\phi_r^{p,q}$ under the identifications. Since $E_\infty^{p,q}$ is the stable value $E_r^{p,q}$ ([thm-sp-half-plane-convergence], [def-sp-convergence]: $Z_r$, $B_r$ are constant from that $r$ on), $\phi_\infty^{p,q}:=\phi_r^{p,q}$ for any such $r$ is well defined.

**Step 5.** **Part 3: abutment and filtration.** The abutment of $\Phi C$ in [thm-sp-half-plane-convergence] is $(\Phi D)^{0,n}=\Phi A^n$, and its filtration is $F^p\Phi A^n=\operatorname{im}((\Phi\alpha)^p:\Phi D^{p,n-p}\to\Phi A^n)$ for $p\geq0$ and $\Phi A^n$ for $p\leq0$. Since $(\Phi\alpha)^p=\Phi(\alpha^p)$, [lem-sp-exact-functor-preserves-homology](b) gives $F^p\Phi A^n=\Phi(F^pA^n)$ as subobjects of $\Phi A^n$; for $p\leq0$ both sides are $\Phi A^n$. Monotonicity, finiteness ($F^p=0$ for $p>n-a$ since then $D^{p,n-p}=0$) and exhaustiveness ($F^0=A^n$) carry over because $\Phi$ preserves inclusions of subobjects and $\Phi(0)=0$ ([lem-sp-exact-functor-preserves-homology](a)); these are the properties of [def-sp-filtration] (Stacks Definition 12.19.1, Tag 0121). By [lem-sp-exact-functor-preserves-homology](a) applied to $F^{p+1}A^n\subseteq F^pA^n$, $\kappa:\Phi(F^pA^n/F^{p+1}A^n)\cong\Phi F^pA^n/\Phi F^{p+1}A^n=\operatorname{gr}^p\Phi A^n$, compatible with the projections from $\Phi F^pA^n$.

**Step 6.** **Part 3: $\psi_r$ is well defined and is carried by $\Phi$.** Let $C$ satisfy the hypotheses, $n=p+q$, $r\geq\max(p+3,q-a+2)$, $u=p-r+2\leq-1$, $v=q+r-2$. (1) For $w\leq-1$, $\alpha:D^{w+1,n-w-1}\to D^{w,n-w}$ is an isomorphism: its kernel is $\operatorname{im}(f:E^{w-1,n-w}\to D^{w+1,n-w-1})=0$ and its cokernel injects through $g$ into $E^{w,n-w}=0$ ([def-sp-exact-couple]; the argument of Step 1 of [thm-sp-half-plane-convergence]); so $\lambda_w=\alpha^{-w}$ is an isomorphism for $w\leq0$, with $\alpha\circ\lambda_{w+1}=\lambda_w$. (2) By [lem-sp-derived-couple-exact], $D_r^{u,v}=\operatorname{im}(\alpha^{r-2}:D^{p,q}\to D^{u,v})$ and $\alpha^{r-2}=\lambda_u\circ\alpha^p$ there, so $D_r^{u,v}=\lambda_u(F^pA^n)$; likewise $D_r^{u+1,v-1}=\lambda_{u+1}(F^{p+1}A^n)$ ($u+1\leq0$), and $\alpha_r$, the restriction of $\alpha$, has image $\lambda_u(F^{p+1}A^n)$. The map $f_r:E_r^{p,q}\to D_r^{p+2,q-1}=\operatorname{im}(\alpha^{r-2}:D^{p+r,q-r+1}\to D^{p+2,q-1})$ is zero because $q-r+1<a$. Exactness of the couple $C_r$ ([lem-sp-derived-couple-exact]) at $D_r^{u,v}$ and at $E_r^{p,q}$ ($g_r$ of bidegree $(r-2,2-r)$ maps $D_r^{u,v}$ to $E_r^{p,q}$) gives the exact sequence $D_r^{u+1,v-1}\xrightarrow{\alpha_r}D_r^{u,v}\xrightarrow{g_r}E_r^{p,q}\to0$, hence $\psi_r:\operatorname{gr}^pA^n\xrightarrow{\lambda_u}D_r^{u,v}/\alpha_r(D_r^{u+1,v-1})\xrightarrow{\bar g_r}E_r^{p,q}$, an isomorphism; $r>\max(q-a+1,p)$, so $E_r^{p,q}=E_\infty^{p,q}$. This is the identification $E_r^{p,q}=G_qA^{p+q}/G_{q-1}A^{p+q}$ of Step 2 of the proof of [thm-sp-half-plane-convergence] (with $G_q=F^p$, $G_{q-1}=F^{p+1}$). (3) Apply $\Phi$. By Part 2 (identity, $\phi_r$) is an isomorphism $\Phi(C_r)\to(\Phi C)_r$ with $\Phi(D_r)=(\Phi D)_r$, so $g_r^\Phi=\phi_r\circ\Phi(g_r)$ and $\alpha_r^\Phi=\Phi(\alpha_r)$; $\lambda_w^\Phi=(\Phi\alpha)^{-w}=\Phi(\lambda_w)$ and $\Phi(\lambda_u(F^pA^n))=\lambda_u^\Phi(F^p\Phi A^n)$ by Step 5. Let $\rho:F^pA^n\twoheadrightarrow\operatorname{gr}^pA^n$ and $\rho^\Phi:F^p\Phi A^n\twoheadrightarrow\operatorname{gr}^p\Phi A^n$ be the projections, so $\kappa\circ\Phi(\rho)=\rho^\Phi$ (Step 5) and $\psi_r\circ\rho=g_r\circ\lambda_u|_{F^pA^n}$. Then $\phi_r\circ\Phi(\psi_r)\circ\Phi(\rho)=\phi_r\circ\Phi(g_r)\circ\Phi(\lambda_u)|_{\Phi F^pA^n}=g_r^\Phi\circ\lambda_u^\Phi|_{F^p\Phi A^n}=\psi_r^\Phi\circ\rho^\Phi=\psi_r^\Phi\circ\kappa\circ\Phi(\rho)$, and $\Phi(\rho)$ is an epimorphism ((a)), so $\phi_r\circ\Phi(\psi_r)=\psi_r^\Phi\circ\kappa$.
