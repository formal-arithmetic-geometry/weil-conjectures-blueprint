**Step 1.** **Step 1 (comparison of resolutions; $\sigma$ on a K-injective complex).** Notation as in [prop-weil-pbc-derived-inverse-image-adjunction]: every $K\in\Dplus(U_{\Et})$ comes with a bounded below K-injective complex $I(K)$ and an isomorphism $\rho_K:K\xrightarrow{\sim}QI(K)$ (the class of the resolution), $Ru_*K=Q(u_*I(K))$, and for $a:K\to K'$, $Ru_*(a)=Q(u_*\tilde a)$ with $\tilde a$ the unique homotopy class with $Q(\tilde a)\rho_K=\rho_{K'}a$ [def-weil-pbc-direct-image], [lem-weil-pbc-derived-direct-image-triangulated]. (i) *Comparisons.* If $t:N\to J$ and $t':N\to J'$ are quasi-isomorphisms into K-injective complexes, then by [lem-weil-pbc-kinjective-hom-derived] there is a unique homotopy class $c:J\to J'$ with $Q(c)=Q(t')Q(t)^{-1}$, i.e. with $ct\simeq t'$ as maps in $D$ (and then in $K$, again by that lemma if $N$ is K-injective; in general $ct$ and $t'$ have the same class in $D$); $c$ is a homotopy equivalence (its inverse class is the comparison the other way); comparisons compose, and the comparison of $t[1 ],t'[1 ]$ is $c[1 ]$ (uniqueness, $Q(c[1 ])=Q(c)[1 ]$). (ii) *Shifts.* $J[1 ]$ is K-injective and $t[1 ]$ is a K-injective resolution of $N[1 ]$ [lem-weil-pbc-kinjective-shift]; the localization $Q$ commutes with $[1 ]$, $Q(N[1 ])=Q(N)[1 ]$, $Q(f[1 ])=Q(f)[1 ]$ [def-weil-abstract-derived-category]; and the termwise functors $u_*,u^{-1}$ commute with $[1 ]$ on the nose: $u_*(J[1 ])=(u_*J)[1 ]$, $u_*(f[1 ])=u_*(f)[1 ]$, since $u_*(-d)=-u_*(d)$ by additivity, and they carry homotopic maps to homotopic maps [lem-weil-pbc-termwise-adjunction-homotopy] (b). By definition [lem-weil-pbc-derived-direct-image-triangulated], $\sigma_K=Q(u_*c_K):Ru_*(K[1 ])=Q(u_*I(K[1 ]))\to Q(u_*(I(K)[1 ]))=(Ru_*K)[1 ]$, where $c_K:I(K[1 ])\to I(K)[1 ]$ is the comparison of the resolutions $\rho_{K[1 ]}$ and $\rho_K[1 ]$ of $K[1 ]$. (iii) *$\sigma$ on a K-injective complex.* Let $I$ be a bounded below K-injective complex and $K=QI$; let $h_I:I\to I(K)$ be the homotopy equivalence with $Q(h_I)=\rho_K$ and $\lambda_I=Q(u_*h_I):Q(u_*I)\xrightarrow{\sim}Ru_*(QI)$ the identification under which '$u_*I$ represents $Ru_*I$' [prop-weil-pbc-derived-inverse-image-adjunction] (step 'Property (1)' there). Then $I[1 ]$ is bounded below and K-injective, and
$$\sigma_{QI}\circ\lambda_{I[1 ]}=\lambda_I[1 ]\qquad(\ast)$$
as maps $Q(u_*(I[1 ]))=Q(u_*I)[1 ]\to(Ru_*QI)[1 ]$: indeed $c_K\circ h_{I[1 ]}$ and $h_I[1 ]$ are both maps $I[1 ]\to I(K)[1 ]$ between K-injective complexes whose classes in $D$ agree after the identifications with $K[1 ]$ ($Q(c_K)Q(h_{I[1 ]})=Q(c_K)\rho_{K[1 ]}=\rho_K[1 ]=Q(h_I)[1 ]$), so they are homotopic by [lem-weil-pbc-kinjective-hom-derived]; applying $u_*$ and $Q$ gives $(\ast)$. In words: on K-injective complexes, with $u_*I$ representing $Ru_*I$, the isomorphism $\sigma$ is the identity $u_*(I[1 ])=(u_*I)[1 ]$.

**Step 2.** **Step 2 (part (a) on a K-injective complex).** Let $I$ be a bounded below K-injective complex on $U_{\Et}$, $K=QI$. By property (1) of [prop-weil-pbc-derived-inverse-image-adjunction], read through the identifications $\lambda_I$ of Step 1 and $\varphi$ of [def-weil-pbc-derived-inverse-image] (under which $u^{-1}Q(u_*I)$ is represented by the termwise complex $u^{-1}u_*I$),
$$\varepsilon^D_{u,QI}\circ u^{-1}(\lambda_I)=Q(\varepsilon_{u,I}),\qquad\varepsilon_{u,I}=(\varepsilon_{u,I^r})_r:u^{-1}u_*I\to I,\qquad(1')$$
and the same for the bounded below K-injective complex $I[1 ]$ [lem-weil-pbc-kinjective-shift]. The termwise counit of $I[1 ]$ has components $\varepsilon_{u,(I[1 ])^r}=\varepsilon_{u,I^{r+1}}$, which are the components of the shifted morphism $\varepsilon_{u,I}[1 ]$; and $u^{-1}u_*(I[1 ])=(u^{-1}u_*I)[1 ]$ as complexes (termwise additive functors, Step 1 (ii)). So $\varepsilon_{u,I[1 ]}=\varepsilon_{u,I}[1 ]$ as morphisms of complexes. The isomorphism $\iota_{u_*I}:u^{-1}((u_*I)[1 ])\to(u^{-1}u_*I)[1 ]$ is the identity of this complex [def-weil-pbc-derived-inverse-image]. Now compute the right-hand side of (a) at $K=QI$, precomposed with the isomorphism $u^{-1}(\lambda_{I[1 ]})$:
$$\varepsilon^D_{u,QI}[1 ]\circ\iota_{Ru_*QI}\circ u^{-1}(\sigma_{QI})\circ u^{-1}(\lambda_{I[1 ]})\overset{(\ast)}=\varepsilon^D_{u,QI}[1 ]\circ\iota_{Ru_*QI}\circ u^{-1}(\lambda_I[1 ])=\varepsilon^D_{u,QI}[1 ]\circ u^{-1}(\lambda_I)[1 ]\circ\iota_{Q(u_*I)}=Q(\varepsilon_{u,I})[1 ]\circ\mathrm{id}=Q(\varepsilon_{u,I[1 ]}),$$
using naturality of $\iota$ for $\lambda_I$, then $(1')$ shifted. By $(1')$ for $I[1 ]$, $Q(\varepsilon_{u,I[1 ]})=\varepsilon^D_{u,QI[1 ]}\circ u^{-1}(\lambda_{I[1 ]})$, and $QI[1 ]=K[1 ]$. Cancelling the isomorphism $u^{-1}(\lambda_{I[1 ]})$ gives (a) at $K=QI$. (The counit at a K-injective complex is the termwise sheaf counit, which commutes termwise with the shift.)

**Step 3.** **Step 3 (part (a) in general).** Let $K\in\Dplus(U_{\Et})$. Its chosen resolution gives a bounded below K-injective complex $I=I(K)$ and an isomorphism $\alpha=\rho_K:K\xrightarrow{\sim}QI$ [def-weil-pbc-direct-image], [prop-weil-pbc-derived-inverse-image-adjunction] (such a representative exists by [lem-weil-pbc-dplus-injective-representative]: every object of $\Dplus$ is isomorphic to $Q$ of a bounded below complex of injectives, which is K-injective). Both sides of (a) are the components at $K$ of natural transformations $u^{-1}Ru_*\circ[1 ]\to[1 ]$: the left side is $\varepsilon^D_u$ whiskered by $[1 ]$, the right side the composite of $u^{-1}(\sigma)$, $\iota Ru_*$ and $\varepsilon^D_u[1 ]$, each natural ($\sigma$ by [lem-weil-pbc-derived-direct-image-triangulated], $\iota$ by [def-weil-pbc-derived-inverse-image], $\varepsilon^D_u$ as the counit of an adjunction [prop-weil-pbc-derived-inverse-image-adjunction]). For natural transformations $\tau,\tau'$ and an isomorphism $\alpha$, $\tau_K=(\alpha[1 ])^{-1}\circ\tau_{QI}\circ u^{-1}Ru_*(\alpha[1 ])$ and likewise for $\tau'$; since $\tau_{QI}=\tau'_{QI}$ by Step 2, $\tau_K=\tau'_K$. This proves (a).

**Step 4.** **Step 4 (part (b) on a bounded below complex).** Let $M$ be a bounded below complex on $V_{\Et}$ and $j:u^{-1}M\to J$ a K-injective resolution with $J$ bounded below. By property (2) of [prop-weil-pbc-derived-inverse-image-adjunction] (the identification '$Ru_*u^{-1}M=u_*J$' being $\mu_j=Q(u_*c_j)$, $c_j:J\to I(u^{-1}QM)$ the comparison of Step 1 (i) of $j$ with the chosen resolution, read through $\varphi_M$ of [def-weil-pbc-derived-inverse-image]),
$$\eta^D_{u,QM}=\mu_j\circ Q\bigl(u_*(j)\circ\eta_{u,M}\bigr),\qquad\eta_{u,M}=(\eta_{u,M^r})_r:M\to u_*u^{-1}M.\qquad(2')$$
Shift: $u^{-1}(M[1 ])=(u^{-1}M)[1 ]$ as complexes and $\iota_M$ is its identity [def-weil-pbc-derived-inverse-image]; $j[1 ]:(u^{-1}M)[1 ]\to J[1 ]$ is a K-injective resolution with $J[1 ]$ bounded below [lem-weil-pbc-kinjective-shift]; the termwise unit of $M[1 ]$ has components $\eta_{u,M^{r+1}}$, so $\eta_{u,M[1 ]}=\eta_{u,M}[1 ]$, and $u_*(j[1 ])=u_*(j)[1 ]$ (Step 1 (ii)). Hence by $(2')$ for $M[1 ]$ and $j[1 ]$: $\eta^D_{u,QM[1 ]}=\mu_{j[1 ]}\circ Q(u_*(j)\circ\eta_{u,M})[1 ]$. Next, $Ru_*(\iota_M)\circ\mu_{j[1 ]}$ is $Q(u_*)$ of the comparison $J[1 ]\to I((u^{-1}M)[1 ])$ of $j[1 ]$ with the chosen resolution ($Ru_*$ of a morphism is $u_*$ of the comparison, and comparisons compose, Step 1 (i)); and $\sigma_{u^{-1}QM}=Q(u_*c)$ with $c:I((u^{-1}M)[1 ])\to I(u^{-1}M)[1 ]$ the comparison of the resolutions of $(u^{-1}M)[1 ]$ (Step 1 (ii)). The composite $J[1 ]\to I(u^{-1}M)[1 ]$ is a comparison of $j[1 ]$ with $\rho[1 ]$, so it is homotopic to $c_j[1 ]$ (Step 1 (i), uniqueness); therefore
$$\sigma_{u^{-1}QM}\circ Ru_*(\iota_M)\circ\mu_{j[1 ]}=Q(u_*(c_j[1 ]))=\mu_j[1 ].$$
Composing: $\sigma_{u^{-1}QM}\circ Ru_*(\iota_M)\circ\eta^D_{u,QM[1 ]}=\mu_j[1 ]\circ Q(u_*(j)\circ\eta_{u,M})[1 ]=\eta^D_{u,QM}[1 ]$ by $(2')$ and functoriality of $[1 ]$. This is (b) at $QM$. (The unit is the termwise sheaf unit followed by $u_*$ of the resolution $u^{-1}M\to J$; both commute with the shift termwise, and $\sigma$ is the comparison of resolutions.)

**Step 5.** **Step 5 (part (b) in general).** Every $X\in\Dplus(V_{\Et})$ is isomorphic to $QM$ for a bounded below complex $M$ (even one of injectives) by [lem-weil-pbc-dplus-injective-representative] ($\Ab(V_{\Et})$ has enough injectives, [lem-weil-pbc-module-sheaves-enough-injectives]). Both sides of (b) are components at $X$ of natural transformations from the functor $X\mapsto X[1 ]$ to the functor $X\mapsto(Ru_*u^{-1}X)[1 ]$: the left side composes $\eta^D_u$ at $X[1 ]$, $Ru_*(\iota_X)$ and $\sigma_{u^{-1}X}$, all natural ([prop-weil-pbc-derived-inverse-image-adjunction], [def-weil-pbc-derived-inverse-image], [lem-weil-pbc-derived-direct-image-triangulated]); the right side is $\eta^D_u$ shifted. As in Step 3, equality at $QM$ (Step 4) transports along an isomorphism $X\cong QM$ to equality at $X$. This proves (b).

**Step 6.** **Step 6 (the composite isomorphisms, and iteration).** The shift-commutation isomorphism of the composite $Ru_*u^{-1}$ is $\chi_M=\sigma_{u^{-1}M}\circ Ru_*(\iota_M)$ and that of $u^{-1}Ru_*$ is $\chi'_K=\iota_{Ru_*K}\circ u^{-1}(\sigma_K)$ [lem-weil-pbc-triangulated-functor-composite], [lem-weil-pbc-commshift-nattrans-comp-whisker], the identity functor carrying the identity; so (a) and (b) say that $\varepsilon^D_u$ and $\eta^D_u$ are compatible with the shift in the sense of [lem-weil-pbc-commshift-nattrans-comp-whisker]. For $n\in\mathbf Z$ the iterated isomorphisms are $\iota^{(0)}=\sigma^{(0)}=\mathrm{id}$, $\sigma^{(n+1)}_K=\sigma^{(n)}_K[1 ]\circ\sigma_{K[n ]}$ for $n\ge0$, and $\sigma^{(-m)}_K=((\sigma^{(m)}_{K[-m ]})^{-1})[-m ]$, likewise for $\iota,\chi,\chi'$; by naturality of $\sigma$ and $\iota$ and induction, $\chi^{(n)}_M=\sigma^{(n)}_{u^{-1}M}\circ Ru_*(\iota^{(n)}_M)$ and $\chi'^{(n)}_K=\iota^{(n)}_{Ru_*K}\circ u^{-1}(\sigma^{(n)}_K)$ (the composite structure for all $n$, [lem-weil-pbc-commshift-nattrans-comp-whisker]). For a natural transformation $\tau:F_1\to F_2$ with $\psi_X\circ\tau_{X[1 ]}=\tau_X[1 ]\circ\varphi_X$, induction gives $\psi^{(n+1)}_X\circ\tau_{X[n+1 ]}=\psi^{(n)}_X[1 ]\circ\psi_{X[n ]}\circ\tau_{X[n ][1 ]}=\psi^{(n)}_X[1 ]\circ\tau_{X[n ]}[1 ]\circ\varphi_{X[n ]}=(\tau_X[n ]\circ\varphi^{(n)}_X)[1 ]\circ\varphi_{X[n ]}=\tau_X[n+1 ]\circ\varphi^{(n+1)}_X$, and for $n=-m<0$ the case $m$ at $X[-m ]$, $\psi^{(m)}_{X[-m ]}\circ\tau_X=\tau_{X[-m ]}[m ]\circ\varphi^{(m)}_{X[-m ]}$, rearranges to $(\psi^{(m)}_{X[-m ]})^{-1}\circ\tau_{X[-m ]}[m ]=\tau_X\circ(\varphi^{(m)}_{X[-m ]})^{-1}$, and applying $[-m ]$ (with $[m ][-m ]=\mathrm{id}$ on complexes) gives the case $-m$. Applied to $\tau=\eta^D_u$ ($\varphi=\mathrm{id}$, $\psi=\chi$) and $\tau=\varepsilon^D_u$ ($\varphi=\chi'$, $\psi=\mathrm{id}$) this is the last sentence of the statement.

**Step 7.** **Step 7 (the abstract reading: $\sigma$ is the shift structure induced by the adjunction).** By [lem-weil-pbc-adjunction-shift-compatibility-unique] (ii), for the adjunction $u^{-1}\dashv Ru_*$ and the shift isomorphism $\iota$ of $u^{-1}$ there is at most one shift isomorphism of $Ru_*$ compatible with the unit, and by (iii) there is one (Mathlib's `Adjunction.rightAdjointCommShift`), for which the adjunction is compatible with the shifts (`Adjunction.CommShift`). Part (b) (Step 5) says that $\sigma$ is compatible with the unit; hence $\sigma$ is that induced structure, $\sigma_K^{-1}=Ru_*(\varepsilon^D_{u,K}[1 ])\circ Ru_*(\iota_{Ru_*K})\circ\eta^D_{u,(Ru_*K)[1 ]}$, which is the isomorphism of Stacks 0A8D (the adjoint of $\varepsilon[1 ]\circ\xi_{G(A)}$). By (i), part (a) also follows from part (b), which gives a second proof of (a) independent of Steps 2-3. For a formalization this means that the content of the node is that the resolution-comparison isomorphism $\sigma$ (in Lean the shift structure of `rightDerivedFunctorPlus` transported from $K^+(\mathrm{Inj})$) is compatible with the unit, i.e. Step 4; (a) and the iteration then come from Mathlib.
