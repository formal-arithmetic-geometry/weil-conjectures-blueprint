**Step 1.** **Step 1 ((a) for $\tau_{\leq q}$).** Let $s:K\to L$ be a quasi-isomorphism. The morphism $\tau_{\leq q}s$ is the restriction of $s$ to the subcomplexes ($s^r$ for $r<q$, the map $\Ker d^q_K\to\Ker d^q_L$ induced by $s^q$, $0$ for $r>q$), so $c_{q,L}\circ\tau_{\leq q}s=s\circ c_{q,K}$ for the inclusions $c_q:\tau_{\leq q}\to\mathrm{id}$, and $H^r(c_{q,L})\circ H^r(\tau_{\leq q}s)=H^r(s)\circ H^r(c_{q,K})$ by functoriality of $H^r$. For $r\le q$, $H^r(c_{q,K})$ and $H^r(c_{q,L})$ are isomorphisms [lem-weil-pbc-truncation-cohomology] and $H^r(s)$ is one by hypothesis, so $H^r(\tau_{\leq q}s)=H^r(c_{q,L})^{-1}H^r(s)H^r(c_{q,K})$ is an isomorphism. For $r>q$, $H^r(\tau_{\leq q}K)=H^r(\tau_{\leq q}L)=0$ [lem-weil-pbc-truncation-cohomology], and a morphism between zero objects is an isomorphism. So $\tau_{\leq q}s$ is a quasi-isomorphism; the same with $q-1$ for $\tau_{\leq q-1}s$.

**Step 2.** **Step 2 ((a) for $H^q(-)[-q ]$).** For a quasi-isomorphism $s:K\to L$, $H^q(s)[-q ]:H^q(K)[-q ]\to H^q(L)[-q ]$ is the morphism of complexes whose component in degree $q$ is $H^q(s)$ and whose other components are maps between zero objects. $H^q(s)$ is an isomorphism by hypothesis, so every component is an isomorphism, and the componentwise inverses (they commute with the zero differentials) form an inverse morphism of complexes: $H^q(s)[-q ]$ is an isomorphism of complexes, hence a quasi-isomorphism, since each functor $H^r$ sends isomorphisms to isomorphisms [def-weil-abstract-derived-category].

**Step 3.** **Step 3 (naturality on complexes).** $i_q:\tau_{\leq q-1}\to\tau_{\leq q}$ and $j_q:\tau_{\leq q}\to H^q(-)[-q ]$ are natural transformations of functors on cochain complexes: for $\phi:K\to L$, $(\tau_{\leq q-1}\phi,\tau_{\leq q}\phi)$ is part of a morphism of short exact sequences, so $\tau_{\leq q}\phi\circ i_{q,K}=i_{q,L}\circ\tau_{\leq q-1}\phi$, and $j_q=\bar j_q\circ p_q$ with $H^q(\phi)[-q ]\circ\bar j_{q,K}=\bar j_{q,L}\circ M_q(\phi)$ and $M_q(\phi)\circ p_{q,K}=p_{q,L}\circ\tau_{\leq q}\phi$, whence $H^q(\phi)[-q ]\circ j_{q,K}=j_{q,L}\circ\tau_{\leq q}\phi$ [lem-weil-pbc-truncation-step-ses] (c). (Degreewise: in degree $q$ the projection $\Ker d^q_K\to H^q(K)$ followed by $H^q(\phi)$ is the class of $\phi^q$ restricted to cycles.)

**Step 4.** **Step 4 ((b) the functors).** $Q$ sends quasi-isomorphisms to isomorphisms [def-weil-abstract-derived-category], so by Steps 1 and 2 the functors $Q\circ\tau_{\leq q}$, $Q\circ\tau_{\leq q-1}$ and $Q\circ H^q(-)[-q ]:\mathrm{CoCh}(\mathcal A)\to D(\mathcal A)$ invert the quasi-isomorphisms. Since $Q$ is the localization at the quasi-isomorphisms [def-weil-abstract-derived-category], part (1) of [lem-weil-pbc-localization-universal-property] gives functors $\tau_{\leq q},\tau_{\leq q-1},H^q(-)[-q ]:D(\mathcal A)\to D(\mathcal A)$ with natural isomorphisms $\phi:\tau_{\leq q}\circ Q\xrightarrow{\sim}Q\circ\tau_{\leq q}$ etc., and by part (2) these functors are unique up to unique isomorphism compatible with the $\phi$'s.

**Step 5.** **Step 5 ((b) the transformations).** Whiskering the natural transformations of Step 3 with $Q$ gives natural transformations $Q i_q:Q\circ\tau_{\leq q-1}\to Q\circ\tau_{\leq q}$ and $Q j_q:Q\circ\tau_{\leq q}\to Q\circ H^q(-)[-q ]$ between functors inverting quasi-isomorphisms. By part (2) of [lem-weil-pbc-localization-universal-property], applied with the functors and isomorphisms $\phi$ of Step 4, there are unique natural transformations $i_q:\tau_{\leq q-1}\to\tau_{\leq q}$ and $j_q:\tau_{\leq q}\to H^q(-)[-q ]$ of functors on $D(\mathcal A)$ with $i_{q,QK}=\phi^{-1}\circ Q(i_{q,K})\circ\phi$ and $j_{q,QK}=\phi^{-1}\circ Q(j_{q,K})\circ\phi$ for every complex $K$; this is the statement that their composites with $Q$ are $Q(i_q)$, $Q(j_q)$ up to the $\phi$'s.

**Step 6.** **Step 6 (the consequences for a morphism $f$).** For a morphism $f:K\to L$ of $D(\mathcal A)$ put $\tau_{\leq q}f$, $\tau_{\leq q-1}f$, $H^q(f)[-q ]$ for the images of $f$ under the functors of Step 4. The two identities $\tau_{\leq q}f\circ i_{q,K}=i_{q,L}\circ\tau_{\leq q-1}f$ and $H^q(f)[-q ]\circ j_{q,K}=j_{q,L}\circ\tau_{\leq q}f$ are the naturality squares of the transformations of Step 5 at $f$. If $f=Q(\phi)$, naturality of the isomorphism $\phi:\tau_{\leq q}\circ Q\xrightarrow{\sim}Q\circ\tau_{\leq q}$ of Step 4 at $\phi$ gives $\tau_{\leq q}(Q\phi)=\phi_L^{-1}\circ Q(\tau_{\leq q}\phi)\circ\phi_K$, and likewise for $\tau_{\leq q-1}$ and $H^q(-)[-q ]$.
