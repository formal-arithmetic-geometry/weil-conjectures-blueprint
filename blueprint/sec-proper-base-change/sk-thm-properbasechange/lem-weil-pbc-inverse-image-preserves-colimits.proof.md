**Step 1.** By [def-weil-pbc-inverse-image] the inverse image $u^{-1}:\Ab(V_{\Et})\to\Ab(U_{\Et})$ is the left adjoint of the direct image $u_*:\Ab(U_{\Et})\to\Ab(V_{\Et})$ of [def-weil-pbc-direct-image]: there are bijections $\operatorname{Hom}_{\Ab(U_{\Et})}(u^{-1}G,F)\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{\Ab(V_{\Et})}(G,u_*F)$ natural in $G\in\Ab(V_{\Et})$ and $F\in\Ab(U_{\Et})$ [def-weil-pbc-abelian-etale-sheaves], with unit $\eta_u$ and counit $\varepsilon_u$. This is an instance of the definition.

**Step 2.** Apply [lem-weil-pbc-left-adjoint-preserves-colimits] with $\mathcal C=\Ab(V_{\Et})$, $\mathcal D=\Ab(U_{\Et})$, $L=u^{-1}$, $R=u_*$ (step 1) and the diagram $M=F:I\to\Ab(V_{\Et})$ with its colimit $(\varinjlim_iF_i,(\iota_i)_i)$: the cocone $(u^{-1}(\varinjlim_iF_i),(u^{-1}\iota_i)_i)$ is a colimit of $i\mapsto u^{-1}F_i$. The canonical morphism $c:\varinjlim_iu^{-1}F_i\to u^{-1}(\varinjlim_iF_i)$ is by definition [def-weil-pbc-category-colimit] the unique morphism with $c\circ\iota'_i=u^{-1}\iota_i$ for all $i$, $\iota'_i$ the coprojections of the chosen colimit of $u^{-1}\circ F$. Since the target cocone is also a colimit, its universal property gives a unique $d$ with $d\circ u^{-1}\iota_i=\iota'_i$; then $d\circ c$ and $c\circ d$ are compatible with the coprojections, hence equal to the identities by the uniqueness clauses of the two colimits. So $c$ is an isomorphism, for every small $I$ and every $F$: $u^{-1}$ preserves all small colimits.
