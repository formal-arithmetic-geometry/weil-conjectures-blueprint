**Step 1.** **Step 1 (the inclusion $i_q$).** By the definition of $\tau_{\leq q}$ applied to $q$ and to $q-1$: $(\tau_{\leq q-1}K)^r=K^r$ for $r\leq q-2$, $=\Ker d^{q-1}$ for $r=q-1$, $=0$ for $r\geq q$; and $(\tau_{\leq q}K)^r=K^r$ for $r\leq q-1$, $=\Ker d^q$ for $r=q$, $=0$ for $r>q$. Define $i_q^r$ as the identity of $K^r$ for $r\leq q-2$, the inclusion $\Ker d^{q-1}\hookrightarrow K^{q-1}$ for $r=q-1$, and $0$ for $r\geq q$. Both complexes are subcomplexes of $K$ and $i_q$ commutes with these inclusions into $K$ in every degree, so $i_q$ commutes with the differentials (they are restrictions of those of $K$): $\tau_{\leq q-1}K$ is a subcomplex of $\tau_{\leq q}K$. Each $i_q^r$ is a monomorphism of $\mathcal A$.

**Step 2.** **Step 2 (the complex $M_q(K)$).** Let $\pi:K^{q-1}\to K^{q-1}/\Ker d^{q-1}$ be the cokernel of $\Ker d^{q-1}\hookrightarrow K^{q-1}$. Since $d^{q-1}$ vanishes on $\Ker d^{q-1}$, $d^{q-1}=\overline{d}\circ\pi$ for a unique $\overline d:K^{q-1}/\Ker d^{q-1}\to K^q$, which is a monomorphism by [lem-weil-pbc-coimage-mono]. Since $d^q\circ\overline d\circ\pi=d^qd^{q-1}=0$ and $\pi$ is an epimorphism, $d^q\circ\overline d=0$, so $\overline d$ factors as $\Ker d^q\hookrightarrow K^q$ composed with a unique $\delta:K^{q-1}/\Ker d^{q-1}\to\Ker d^q$; $\delta$ is a monomorphism because its composite with the inclusion is the monomorphism $\overline d$. Let $M_q(K)$ be the complex with $M_q(K)^{q-1}=K^{q-1}/\Ker d^{q-1}$, $M_q(K)^q=\Ker d^q$, differential $\delta$ from degree $q-1$ to $q$, and $0$ in all other degrees and differentials; it is a complex since at most one differential is nonzero, so all composites of two differentials are $0$. Moreover the image of $\delta$ in $\Ker d^q$ is $\Im d^{q-1}$ (viewed in $\Ker d^q$): $\pi$ is an epimorphism, so $\delta$ and $\delta\circ\pi$ (the corestriction of $d^{q-1}$ to $\Ker d^q$) have the same image.

**Step 3.** **Step 3 (the morphism $p_q$).** Define $p_q:\tau_{\leq q}K\to M_q(K)$ by $p_q^{q-1}=\pi$, $p_q^q=\mathrm{id}_{\Ker d^q}$, and $p_q^r=0$ for $r\neq q-1,q$. It is a morphism of complexes: the only square whose two paths are not both through a zero object is the one from degree $q-1$ to degree $q$, where the differential of $\tau_{\leq q}K$ is the corestriction $d^{q-1}:K^{q-1}\to\Ker d^q$, and $\delta\circ\pi$ equals that corestriction by Step 2, i.e. $\delta\circ p_q^{q-1}=p_q^q\circ d^{q-1}$. Also $p_q\circ i_q=0$: in degree $q-1$ it is $\pi\circ(\Ker d^{q-1}\hookrightarrow K^{q-1})=0$, in degree $q$ the source $(\tau_{\leq q-1}K)^q$ is $0$, in degrees $\leq q-2$ and $>q$ the target is $0$.

**Step 4.** **Step 4 (clause (a): short exactness and the cokernel).** In each degree $r$ the sequence $0\to(\tau_{\leq q-1}K)^r\xrightarrow{i_q^r}(\tau_{\leq q}K)^r\xrightarrow{p_q^r}M_q(K)^r\to0$ is short exact in $\mathcal A$: for $r\leq q-2$ it is $0\to K^r\xrightarrow{\mathrm{id}}K^r\to0\to0$; for $r=q-1$ it is $0\to\Ker d^{q-1}\to K^{q-1}\xrightarrow{\pi}K^{q-1}/\Ker d^{q-1}\to0$, short exact since $\pi$ is the cokernel of a monomorphism (abelian category [def-sp-abelian]); for $r=q$ it is $0\to0\to\Ker d^q\xrightarrow{\mathrm{id}}\Ker d^q\to0$; for $r>q$ all terms are $0$. By [lem-weil-pbc-cochain-complexes-degreewise-exact] (2), $0\to\tau_{\leq q-1}K\xrightarrow{i_q}\tau_{\leq q}K\xrightarrow{p_q}M_q(K)\to0$ is a short exact sequence of complexes; in particular $i_q$ is a monomorphism, and by (3) $p_q$ is an epimorphism and a cokernel of $i_q$, so $M_q(K)=\tau_{\leq q}K/\tau_{\leq q-1}K$ with $p_q$ the quotient map, and its terms are the degreewise quotients computed above.

**Step 5.** **Step 5 (clause (b): the factorization $j_q=\bar j_q\circ p_q$).** Let $\rho:\Ker d^q\to H^q(K)=\Ker d^q/\Im d^{q-1}$ be the projection [def-weil-abstract-derived-category]. Define $\bar j_q:M_q(K)\to H^q(K)[-q ]$ by $\bar j_q^q=\rho$ and $\bar j_q^r=0$ for $r\neq q$. It is a morphism of complexes: the only square to check is from degree $q-1$ to $q$, where it says $\rho\circ\delta=0$, which holds because the image of $\delta$ is $\Im d^{q-1}$ (Step 2) and $\rho$ kills $\Im d^{q-1}$; the square from $q$ to $q+1$ has target $0$. Degreewise $\bar j_q^q\circ p_q^q=\rho\circ\mathrm{id}=\rho=j_q^q$ and both sides are $0$ in the other degrees (the target $H^q(K)[-q ]$ is $0$ there), so $\bar j_q\circ p_q=j_q$. The factorization is unique because $p_q$ is an epimorphism of complexes (Step 4).

**Step 6.** **Step 6 (clause (b): $\bar j_q$ is a quasi-isomorphism).** Cohomology is $H^r=\Ker d^r/\Im d^{r-1}$ [def-weil-abstract-derived-category]. For $M_q(K)$: if $r\notin\{q-1,q\}$ then $M_q(K)^r=0$ and $H^r(M_q(K))=0$; $H^{q-1}(M_q(K))=\Ker\delta/\Im(0)=0$ because $\delta$ is a monomorphism (Step 2); $H^q(M_q(K))=\Ker(\Ker d^q\to0)/\Im\delta=\Ker d^q/\Im d^{q-1}=H^q(K)$, using $\Im\delta=\Im d^{q-1}$ (Step 2). For $H^q(K)[-q ]$: $H^q=\Ker(H^q(K)\to0)/\Im(0\to H^q(K))=H^q(K)$ and $H^r=0$ for $r\neq q$. Hence $H^r(\bar j_q)$ is a map between zero objects for $r\neq q$, and $H^q(\bar j_q)$ is the map $\Ker d^q/\Im d^{q-1}\to H^q(K)$ induced by $\bar j_q^q=\rho$, which is the identity of $H^q(K)$. So $H^r(\bar j_q)$ is an isomorphism for every $r$, i.e. $\bar j_q$ is a quasi-isomorphism.

**Step 7.** **Step 7 (clause (c): naturality).** Let $f:K\to L$ be a morphism of complexes. Since $d_L^rf^r=f^{r+1}d_K^r$, $f^r$ maps $\Ker d_K^r$ into $\Ker d_L^r$ and $\Im d_K^{r-1}$ into $\Im d_L^{r-1}$. So $\tau_{\leq q}f$ ($f^r$ for $r<q$, the restriction $\Ker d_K^q\to\Ker d_L^q$ for $r=q$, $0$ for $r>q$) and likewise $\tau_{\leq q-1}f$ are morphisms of complexes (restrictions of $f$ to subcomplexes), and $\tau_{\leq q}f\circ i_q=i_q\circ\tau_{\leq q-1}f$ since both are restrictions of $f$. Define $M_q(f)$ in degree $q-1$ as the map $K^{q-1}/\Ker d_K^{q-1}\to L^{q-1}/\Ker d_L^{q-1}$ induced by $f^{q-1}$ (it maps the kernel into the kernel), in degree $q$ as the restriction $\Ker d_K^q\to\Ker d_L^q$, and $0$ elsewhere. Then $M_q(f)\circ p_q=p_q\circ\tau_{\leq q}f$ degreewise by construction, and $M_q(f)$ commutes with $\delta$ because, after composing with the epimorphism $\pi_K$, both sides are the corestriction of $d_L^{q-1}f^{q-1}=f^qd_K^{q-1}$. So $(\tau_{\leq q-1}f,\tau_{\leq q}f,M_q(f))$ is a morphism of short exact sequences. Finally, in degree $q$, $H^q(f)\circ\rho_K$ and $\rho_L\circ(f^q|_{\Ker d^q_K})$ agree by the definition of $H^q(f)$ as the map induced by $f^q$ on $\Ker/\Im$, and both sides are $0$ in other degrees; hence $H^q(f)[-q ]\circ\bar j_q=\bar j_q\circ M_q(f)$.
