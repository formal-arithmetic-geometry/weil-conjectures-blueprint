**Step 1.** **Step 0 (element calculus).** Elements: as in [lem-sp-abelian-refinements], an element of $X$ is a morphism $a:T\to X$ from any object $T$ (the test objects of Stacks Lemma 12.21.4, Tag 011T), and "up to refinement" means after precomposing with an epimorphism $\rho:T'\twoheadrightarrow T$; elements of $X$ with a common $T$ form the abelian group $\operatorname{Hom}(T,X)$, so differences are literal. No embedding theorem (Freyd-Mitchell) is used. An element $a$ of $X$ *lies in* a subobject $S\overset{i}\hookrightarrow X$ if $a=i\circ b$ for some (unique, $i$ mono) $b:T\to S$. Facts used below: (F0) if $\rho$ is an epimorphism and $a\circ\rho$ lies in $S$, then $a$ lies in $S$: in an abelian category $i$ is a kernel [def-sp-abelian], namely of its cokernel $c:X\to X/S$, and $c\circ a\circ\rho=0$ gives $c\circ a=0$ as $\rho$ is epi. (F1) $a$ lies in $\ker u$ iff $u\circ a=0$ (universal property of the kernel). (F2) $a$ lies in $\operatorname{im}u$ ($u:Z\to X$) iff there are an epimorphism $\rho$ and $z$ with $a\circ\rho=u\circ z$: ($\Rightarrow$) [lem-sp-abelian-refinements](a) for the epimorphism $Z\twoheadrightarrow\operatorname{im}u$; ($\Leftarrow$) $u\circ z$ lies in $\operatorname{im}u$, then (F0). (F3) for $u:X\to Y$ and $S\subseteq Y$, $a$ lies in $u^{-1}(S)=X\times_YS$ iff $u\circ a$ lies in $S$ (universal property of the fibre product). (F4) $S\subseteq S'$ iff the arrow of $S$ (an element of $X$) lies in $S'$ (definition of the order on subobjects); hence two subobjects in which the same elements lie are equal. (F5) for $v:Y\to Z$ and $S\subseteq Y$, $z$ lies in $v(S)=\operatorname{im}(S\hookrightarrow Y\xrightarrow{v}Z)$ iff up to refinement $z\circ\rho=v\circ y$ with $y$ lying in $S$ (F2). (F6) if $c$ is a cokernel of $u:Z\to X$, then $c\circ a=0$ iff up to refinement $a\circ\rho=u\circ z$ ([lem-sp-abelian-refinements](c)). (F7) a three-term complex is exact iff every element killed by the second arrow lifts along the first up to refinement ([lem-sp-abelian-refinements](b)); an epimorphism is surjective up to refinement ((a)); and two morphisms $u,v:X\to Y$ with $u\circ a\circ\rho=v\circ a\circ\rho$ for $a=\mathrm{id}_X$ and an epi $\rho$ are equal. Compositions of epimorphisms are epimorphisms, so refinements may be repeated.

**Step 2.** **Step 1 ($d\circ d=0$).** Exactness implies that the three sequences are complexes: $\alpha\circ f=0$, $f\circ g=0$, $g\circ\alpha=0$ (image contained in kernel). Hence $d\circ d=g\circ(f\circ g)\circ f=0$, for $d:E^{p,q}\to E^{p+s+2,q-s-1}$ followed by $d:E^{p+s+2,q-s-1}\to E^{p+2s+4,q-2s-2}$. So $\operatorname{im}d\subseteq\ker d$ and $E'$ is defined; let $\hat d:E^{p-s-2,q+s+1}\to\ker(d:E^{p,q}\to\cdot)$ be the factorization of $d$, so $\iota_{\ker d}\circ\hat d=d$; since $E^{p-s-2,q+s+1}\twoheadrightarrow\operatorname{im}d\hookrightarrow\ker d$, $\pi:\ker d\to E'^{p,q}$ is a cokernel of $\hat d$. (For $s=0$ this is the remark $d\circ d=0$ of Stacks Lemma 12.21.2(1); for general $s$ the computation of Remark 12.21.5.)

**Step 3.** **Step 2 (claim 1, kernel of $d$).** Using elements (Step 0). Let $a:T\to E^{p,q}$. By (F1) $a$ lies in $\ker d$ iff $g\circ(f\circ a)=0$ iff $f\circ a$ lies in $\ker(g:D^{p+2,q-1}\to E^{p+s+2,q-s-1})$ (F1) iff $a$ lies in $f^{-1}(\ker g)$ (F3). By (F4) $\ker d=f^{-1}(\ker g)$. Exactness of $D^{p+3,q-2}\xrightarrow{\alpha}D^{p+2,q-1}\xrightarrow{g}E^{p+s+2,q-s-1}$ gives $\ker g=\operatorname{im}\alpha$, so $\ker d=f^{-1}(\operatorname{im}\alpha)$.

**Step 4.** **Step 3 (claim 1, image of $d$).** Using elements. Let $a:T\to E^{p,q}$. By (F2), $a$ lies in $\operatorname{im}(d:E^{p-s-2,q+s+1}\to E^{p,q})$ iff up to refinement $a\circ\rho=g\circ(f\circ x)$ for some $x:T'\to E^{p-s-2,q+s+1}$. By (F5) and (F2), $a$ lies in $g(\operatorname{im}f)$ (with $f:E^{p-s-2,q+s+1}\to D^{p-s,q+s}$) iff up to refinement $a\circ\rho=g\circ y$ with $y$ lying in $\operatorname{im}f$, i.e. (F2, a further refinement $\rho'$) $y\circ\rho'=f\circ x$; then $a\circ\rho\circ\rho'=g\circ f\circ x$. Conversely from $a\circ\rho=g\circ(f\circ x)$ take $y=f\circ x$. So the same elements lie in both subobjects, and $\operatorname{im}d=g(\operatorname{im}f)$ by (F4). Exactness of $E^{p-s-2,q+s+1}\xrightarrow{f}D^{p-s,q+s}\xrightarrow{\alpha}D^{p-s-1,q+s+1}$ gives $\operatorname{im}f=\ker\alpha$, so $\operatorname{im}d=g(\ker\alpha)$.

**Step 5.** **Step 4 ($\alpha'$ well defined).** The morphism $\alpha\circ\iota:D'^{p,q}\to D^{p-1,q+1}$ equals $\alpha\circ(\iota)$ with $\iota$ an element of $D^{p,q}$, so it lies in $\operatorname{im}(\alpha:D^{p,q}\to D^{p-1,q+1})=D'^{p-1,q+1}$ (F2 with $\rho=\mathrm{id}$): there is a unique $\alpha':D'^{p,q}\to D'^{p-1,q+1}$ with $\iota\circ\alpha'=\alpha\circ\iota$ (unique as $\iota$ is mono). It has bidegree $(-1,+1)$. Moreover $\alpha'\circ\bar\alpha=\bar\alpha\circ\alpha$ as maps $D^{p+1,q-1}\to D'^{p-1,q+1}$ (here $\alpha:D^{p+1,q-1}\to D^{p,q}$ and the second $\bar\alpha:D^{p,q}\twoheadrightarrow D'^{p-1,q+1}$): both give $\alpha\circ\alpha$ after $\iota$, which is mono.

**Step 6.** **Step 5 ($f'$ well defined).** Let $j:\ker(d)\hookrightarrow E^{p,q}$. By Step 2, $j$ lies in $\ker d=f^{-1}(\operatorname{im}\alpha)$, so by (F3) $f\circ j$ lies in $\operatorname{im}(\alpha:D^{p+3,q-2}\to D^{p+2,q-1})=D'^{p+2,q-1}$: $f\circ j=\iota\circ\hat f$ for a unique $\hat f:\ker d\to D'^{p+2,q-1}$. Then $\iota\circ\hat f\circ\hat d=f\circ j\circ\hat d=f\circ d=(f\circ g)\circ f=0$ (Step 1), and $\iota$ is mono, so $\hat f\circ\hat d=0$. Since $\pi$ is a cokernel of $\hat d$ (Step 1), $\hat f=f'\circ\pi$ for a unique $f':E'^{p,q}\to D'^{p+2,q-1}$, of bidegree $(+2,-1)$; $\iota\circ f'\circ\pi=f\circ j$.

**Step 7.** **Step 6 ($g'$ well defined).** Let $g:D^{p+1,q-1}\to E^{p+s+1,q-s-1}$. Since $d\circ g=g\circ(f\circ g)=0$, $g=j\circ\hat g$ for a unique $\hat g:D^{p+1,q-1}\to\ker(d:E^{p+s+1,q-s-1}\to\cdot)$ (F1). Also $j\circ\hat g\circ f=g\circ f=d=j\circ\hat d$ for $f:E^{p-1,q}\to D^{p+1,q-1}$, so $\hat g\circ f=\hat d$ and $\pi\circ\hat g\circ f=\pi\circ\hat d=0$. The kernel of the epimorphism $\bar\alpha:D^{p+1,q-1}\twoheadrightarrow D'^{p,q}$ is $\ker\alpha$ ($\iota$ mono), which equals $\operatorname{im}(f:E^{p-1,q}\to D^{p+1,q-1})$ by exactness; as $f$ factors through the epimorphism $E^{p-1,q}\twoheadrightarrow\operatorname{im}f$, $\pi\circ\hat g$ vanishes on $\ker\bar\alpha$. In an abelian category an epimorphism is a cokernel [def-sp-abelian], namely of its kernel, so there is a unique $g':D'^{p,q}\to E'^{p+s+1,q-s-1}$ with $g'\circ\bar\alpha=\pi\circ\hat g$; it has bidegree $(s+1,-s-1)$. This is \"$g\circ\alpha^{-1}$\" of Stacks: changing a preimage under $\alpha$ by $\ker\alpha=\operatorname{im}f$ changes $g$ of it by $\operatorname{im}d$ (Step 3).

**Step 8.** **Step 7 (exactness at $D'$ between $f'$ and $\alpha'$).** Using elements. Claim: $\ker(\alpha':D'^{p,q}\to D'^{p-1,q+1})=\operatorname{im}(f':E'^{p-2,q+1}\to D'^{p,q})$. Complex: $\iota\circ\alpha'\circ f'\circ\pi=\alpha\circ\iota\circ f'\circ\pi=\alpha\circ f\circ j=0$; $\iota$ mono and $\pi$ epi give $\alpha'\circ f'=0$. Exact: let $y:T\to D'^{p,q}$ with $\alpha'\circ y=0$. Then $\alpha\circ(\iota\circ y)=\iota\circ\alpha'\circ y=0$, so $\iota\circ y$ lies in $\ker\alpha=\operatorname{im}(f:E^{p-2,q+1}\to D^{p,q})$: up to refinement $\iota\circ y\circ\rho=f\circ x$ (F2). Then $d\circ x=g\circ f\circ x=(g\circ\iota)\circ y\circ\rho=0$ because $g\circ\iota=0$ ($D'^{p,q}=\operatorname{im}\alpha=\ker g$). So $x=j\circ\hat x$ (F1) and $\iota\circ f'\circ(\pi\circ\hat x)=f\circ j\circ\hat x=f\circ x=\iota\circ y\circ\rho$, hence $y\circ\rho=f'\circ(\pi\circ\hat x)$ ($\iota$ mono). By (F7) the complex is exact.

**Step 9.** **Step 8 (exactness at $E'$ between $g'$ and $f'$).** Using elements. Claim: $\ker(f':E'^{p,q}\to D'^{p+2,q-1})=\operatorname{im}(g':D'^{p-s-1,q+s+1}\to E'^{p,q})$. Complex: $\iota\circ f'\circ g'\circ\bar\alpha=\iota\circ f'\circ\pi\circ\hat g=f\circ j\circ\hat g=f\circ g=0$, and $\iota$ mono, $\bar\alpha$ epi, so $f'\circ g'=0$. Exact: let $e:T\to E'^{p,q}$ with $f'\circ e=0$. As $\pi$ is epi, up to refinement $e\circ\rho=\pi\circ z$ with $z:T'\to\ker d$ (F7). Then $f\circ(j\circ z)=\iota\circ f'\circ\pi\circ z=\iota\circ f'\circ e\circ\rho=0$, so $j\circ z$ lies in $\ker f=\operatorname{im}(g:D^{p-s,q+s}\to E^{p,q})$: up to refinement $j\circ z\circ\rho'=g\circ w$, $w:T''\to D^{p-s,q+s}$ (F2). Then $j\circ\hat g\circ w=g\circ w=j\circ z\circ\rho'$, so $\hat g\circ w=z\circ\rho'$, and for the element $\bar\alpha\circ w$ of $D'^{p-s-1,q+s+1}$: $g'\circ\bar\alpha\circ w=\pi\circ\hat g\circ w=\pi\circ z\circ\rho'=e\circ\rho\circ\rho'$. So $e$ lifts along $g'$ up to refinement; by (F7) the complex is exact.

**Step 10.** **Step 9 (exactness at $D'$ between $\alpha'$ and $g'$).** Using elements. Claim: $\ker(g':D'^{p,q}\to E'^{p+s+1,q-s-1})=\operatorname{im}(\alpha':D'^{p+1,q-1}\to D'^{p,q})$. Complex: by Step 4, $g'\circ\alpha'\circ\bar\alpha=g'\circ\bar\alpha\circ\alpha=\pi\circ\hat g\circ\alpha$ (with $\alpha:D^{p+2,q-2}\to D^{p+1,q-1}$), and $j\circ\hat g\circ\alpha=g\circ\alpha=0$, so $\hat g\circ\alpha=0$; $\bar\alpha$ epi gives $g'\circ\alpha'=0$. Exact: let $y:T\to D'^{p,q}$ with $g'\circ y=0$. As $\bar\alpha$ is epi, up to refinement $y\circ\rho=\bar\alpha\circ y'$, $y':T'\to D^{p+1,q-1}$. Then $\pi\circ\hat g\circ y'=g'\circ y\circ\rho=0$, so by (F6) ($\pi$ a cokernel of $\hat d$) up to refinement $\hat g\circ y'\circ\rho'=\hat d\circ x$ with $x:T''\to E^{p-1,q}$; applying $j$: $g\circ y'\circ\rho'=d\circ x=g\circ(f\circ x)$. In the group $\operatorname{Hom}(T'',D^{p+1,q-1})$, $g\circ(y'\circ\rho'-f\circ x)=0$, so $y'\circ\rho'-f\circ x$ lies in $\ker g=\operatorname{im}(\alpha:D^{p+2,q-2}\to D^{p+1,q-1})$: up to refinement $(y'\circ\rho'-f\circ x)\circ\rho''=\alpha\circ z$ (F2). Then, using $\alpha\circ f=0$, $\iota\circ y\circ\rho\circ\rho'\circ\rho''=\alpha\circ y'\circ\rho'\circ\rho''=\alpha\circ f\circ x\circ\rho''+\alpha\circ\alpha\circ z=\alpha\circ\alpha\circ z=\iota\circ\bar\alpha\circ\alpha\circ z=\iota\circ\alpha'\circ(\bar\alpha\circ z)$ (Step 4), so $y\circ\rho\circ\rho'\circ\rho''=\alpha'\circ(\bar\alpha\circ z)$ with $\bar\alpha\circ z$ an element of $D'^{p+1,q-1}$. By (F7) the complex is exact.

**Step 11.** **Step 10 (claims 2 and 3).** Steps 4-6 construct $\alpha',f',g'$ with the stated equations and uniqueness, of bidegrees $(-1,+1)$, $(+2,-1)$, $(s+1,-s-1)$. Steps 7-9, read at every $(p,q)$, are exactly the three conditions for an exact couple of type $s+1$: the sequences $E'^{p-2,q+1}\xrightarrow{f'}D'^{p,q}\xrightarrow{\alpha'}D'^{p-1,q+1}$ (Step 7), $D'^{p-s-1,q+s+1}\xrightarrow{g'}E'^{p,q}\xrightarrow{f'}D'^{p+2,q-1}$ (Step 8) and $D'^{p+1,q-1}\xrightarrow{\alpha'}D'^{p,q}\xrightarrow{g'}E'^{p+s+1,q-s-1}$ (Step 9) are complexes and exact.

**Step 12.** **Step 11 (claim 4, iteration).** Induction on $r\geq2$. $C_2=C$ is an exact couple of type $0$ [def-sp-exact-couple] with $D_2=D=\operatorname{im}(\alpha^0)$. If $C_r$ is an exact couple of type $r-2$ with $D_r^{p,q}=\operatorname{im}(\alpha^{r-2}:D^{p+r-2,q-r+2}\to D^{p,q})$ and $\alpha_r$ the restriction of $\alpha$, then by Step 10 $C_{r+1}=C_r'$ is an exact couple of type $r-1$, $\alpha_{r+1}$ is the restriction of $\alpha_r$, hence of $\alpha$ (Step 4), $f_{r+1}$ has bidegree $(+2,-1)$ and $g_{r+1}$ bidegree $(r-1,1-r)$; and $D_{r+1}^{p,q}=\operatorname{im}(\alpha_r:D_r^{p+1,q-1}\to D_r^{p,q})$, viewed in $D^{p,q}$ through the inclusions, is $\alpha(D_r^{p+1,q-1})=\alpha(\operatorname{im}\alpha^{r-2})$, which equals $\operatorname{im}(\alpha^{r-1}:D^{p+r-1,q-r+1}\to D^{p,q})$ by the argument of Step 3 (an element lies in either iff up to refinement it is $\alpha\circ\alpha^{r-2}\circ c$; (F2), (F5), (F4)). The differential $d_r=g_r\circ f_r$ of a couple of type $r-2$ has bidegree $(2+(r-2),-1-(r-2))=(r,1-r)$, and $E_{r+1}=E_r'=\ker d_r/\operatorname{im}d_r$ by the definition of the derived couple. This is the spectral sequence of the couple, Stacks Definition 12.21.3 (Tag 011S), in the bigraded form of Remark 12.21.5 (Tag 0AMJ), with the indexing $E_2=E$ of [def-sp-exact-couple].
