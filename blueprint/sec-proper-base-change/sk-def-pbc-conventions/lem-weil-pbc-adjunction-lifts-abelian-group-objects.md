Let $\mathcal A,\mathcal B$ be categories with finite products [def-weil-category-product] (final objects $*$; for $f:Z\to X$, $g:Z\to Y$ write $\langle f,g\rangle:Z\to X\times Y$ and $f\times g=\langle f\mathrm{pr}_1,g\mathrm{pr}_2\rangle$, and $!_Z:Z\to*$). An abelian group object of $\mathcal A$ is $(X,+,0,i)$ with $+:X\times X\to X$, $0:*\to X$, $i:X\to X$ such that $+\circ(+\times\mathrm{id})=+\circ(\mathrm{id}\times+)\circ a$ ($a:(X\times X)\times X\to X\times(X\times X)$ the associativity isomorphism), $+\circ\langle\mathrm{pr}_2,\mathrm{pr}_1\rangle=+$, $+\circ\langle0\circ!_X,\mathrm{id}_X\rangle=\mathrm{id}_X$, $+\circ\langle\mathrm{id}_X,i\rangle=0\circ!_X$; a morphism of abelian group objects is $f:X\to X'$ with $f\circ+=+'\circ(f\times f)$, $f\circ0=0'$, $f\circ i=i'\circ f$ (the last follows from the first two). Let $L:\mathcal A\to\mathcal B$ be left adjoint to $R:\mathcal B\to\mathcal A$, with unit $\eta$ and counit $\varepsilon$, and assume $L$ and $R$ preserve finite products: $\lambda_{X,Y}=\langle L\mathrm{pr}_1,L\mathrm{pr}_2\rangle:L(X\times Y)\to LX\times LY$ and $\lambda_*:L(*)\to*$ are isomorphisms, and likewise $\rho_{Y,Y'}=\langle R\mathrm{pr}_1,R\mathrm{pr}_2\rangle$, $\rho_*$ for $R$. For an abelian group object $(X,+,0,i)$ of $\mathcal A$ put $L^{\mathrm{gr}}X=(LX,\ L(+)\circ\lambda_{X,X}^{-1},\ L(0)\circ\lambda_*^{-1},\ L(i))$, and define $R^{\mathrm{gr}}Y$ likewise with $\rho$. Then $L^{\mathrm{gr}}X$ and $R^{\mathrm{gr}}Y$ are abelian group objects; $f\mapsto Lf$ and $g\mapsto Rg$ send morphisms of abelian group objects to morphisms of abelian group objects, giving functors $L^{\mathrm{gr}}$, $R^{\mathrm{gr}}$ between the categories of abelian group objects that commute with the forgetful functors to $\mathcal A$, $\mathcal B$; and $L^{\mathrm{gr}}\dashv R^{\mathrm{gr}}$ by an adjunction whose unit and counit have components (with underlying morphisms) $\eta_X:X\to RLX$ and $\varepsilon_Y:LRY\to Y$; in particular $\eta_X$ and $\varepsilon_Y$ are morphisms of abelian group objects, and the adjunction bijection is $\alpha\mapsto R(\alpha)\circ\eta_X$, inverse $\beta\mapsto\varepsilon_Y\circ L(\beta)$.
