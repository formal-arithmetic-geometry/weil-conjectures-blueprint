*The ring system.* For $i\in J$ let $W_i$ be the inverse image of $W$ under $D(i\to i_0)$; it is affine since the transition morphisms are affine [pqf-def-affine-morphism]. For $i\le i'$ in $J$ the transition $D(i'\to i)$ maps $W_{i'}$ into $W_i$, and taking global sections gives ring maps $\Gamma(W_i)\to\Gamma(W_{i'})$; put $F(i)=\Gamma(W_i,\mathcal O)$. At the least element $o$, $D(\mathrm{id})=\mathrm{id}$ identifies $W_o$ with $W$, and $e_0$ is this identification followed by the canonical isomorphism of the affine scheme $W$ with the spectrum of its global sections. The isomorphisms $e_i:W_i\cong\operatorname{Spec}F(i)$ are the canonical ones; their naturality gives $e_0\circ(W_i\to W)=\operatorname{Spec}(F(o\to i))\circ e_i$ (both sides agree after composition with the monomorphism $W\hookrightarrow D(i_0)$).

*The limit.* The upper tail $J$ is initial in the index category of the limit (every index has an index above it in $J$, and the preorder has at most one arrow between two objects), so the restriction of the limit cone to $J$, and further to the inverse images of $W$, is still a limit cone; its vertex is the inverse image $W_L$ of $W$ in $c_L$. A limit of affine schemes along affine transition maps is affine, and global sections take it to a colimit of rings: $\Gamma(W_L)\cong\operatorname{colim}F$, compatibly with the coprojections. Define $e_L$ as the canonical isomorphism $W_L\cong\operatorname{Spec}\Gamma(W_L)$ followed by $\operatorname{Spec}$ of this identification. At $o$ the coprojection corresponds to the global-sections map of the leg $W_L\to W$, which gives $e_0\circ(W_L\to W)=\operatorname{Spec}(\iota_o)\circ e_L$. No nonemptiness of $W$ is used.
