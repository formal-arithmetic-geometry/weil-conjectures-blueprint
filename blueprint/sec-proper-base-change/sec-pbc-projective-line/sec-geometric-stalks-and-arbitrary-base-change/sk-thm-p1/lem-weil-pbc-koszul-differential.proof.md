Existence in (i). Left contraction by $\varphi$ gives an $R$-linear map $D:\wedge(E)\to\wedge(E)$ with $D(1)=0$ and $D(v\wedge x)=\varphi(v)x-v\wedge D(x)$ for $v\in E$, $x\in\wedge(E)$. (Equivalently, as in Stacks, it is obtained from the map on the tensor algebra given by the explicit formula, which kills $e\otimes e$ and so descends.) Fix this $D$ for the rest of the proof.

Uniqueness in (i). Let $D'$ be a second $R$-linear map with $D'(1)=0$ and $D'(v\wedge x)=\varphi(v)x-v\wedge D'(x)$. Then $\Delta=D-D'$ is $R$-linear with $\Delta(1)=0$ and $\Delta(v\wedge x)=-v\wedge\Delta(x)$. By induction on $n\ge0$, $\Delta(v_1\wedge\dots\wedge v_n)=0$ for all $v_i\in E$: for $n=0$ the product is $1$; for $n\ge1$, $\Delta(v_1\wedge(v_2\wedge\dots\wedge v_n))=-v_1\wedge\Delta(v_2\wedge\dots\wedge v_n)=0$ by associativity and the induction hypothesis. Since $\wedge(E)=\bigoplus_n\wedge^n_R(E)$ and each $\wedge^n_R(E)$ is spanned over $R$ by such products ($\wedge^0_R(E)=R\cdot1$), we get $\Delta=0$ by linearity, i.e. $D'=D$.

(ii). For $v\in E$: $D(v)=D(v\wedge1)=\varphi(v)\cdot1-v\wedge D(1)=\varphi(v)\cdot1$. Degree: we show $D(\wedge^n_R(E))\subset\wedge^{n-1}_R(E)$ by induction on $n$. For $n=0$, $\wedge^0_R(E)=R\cdot1$ and $D(r\cdot1)=rD(1)=0\in\wedge^{-1}_R(E)=0$. For $n\ge1$, $\wedge^n_R(E)$ is spanned by products $v\wedge y$ with $v\in E$ and $y=v_2\wedge\dots\wedge v_n\in\wedge^{n-1}_R(E)$; then $D(v\wedge y)=\varphi(v)y-v\wedge D(y)$, where $\varphi(v)y\in\wedge^{n-1}_R(E)$ and, by the induction hypothesis, $D(y)\in\wedge^{n-2}_R(E)$, so $v\wedge D(y)\in\wedge^1_R(E)\wedge\wedge^{n-2}_R(E)\subset\wedge^{n-1}_R(E)$ (for $n=1$, $D(y)=0$). By $R$-linearity of $D$ the inclusion follows.

(iii). Induction on $n\ge1$. For $n=1$ this is $D(v_1)=\varphi(v_1)$, step (ii). For $n\ge2$ put $w=v_2\wedge\dots\wedge v_n$; in $w$ the factor $v_j$ ($2\le j\le n$) sits at position $j-1$, so the induction hypothesis gives $D(w)=\sum_{j=2}^n(-1)^{j}\varphi(v_j)\,v_2\wedge\dots\wedge\widehat{v_j}\wedge\dots\wedge v_n$. Hence $D(v_1\wedge w)=\varphi(v_1)w-v_1\wedge D(w)=\varphi(v_1)\,v_2\wedge\dots\wedge v_n+\sum_{j=2}^n(-1)^{j+1}\varphi(v_j)\,v_1\wedge\dots\wedge\widehat{v_j}\wedge\dots\wedge v_n$, and the first term is the summand $i=1$ (sign $(-1)^{2}=1$) of the formula.

(iv), derivation property. Fix $y\in\wedge(E)$; we prove $D(x\wedge y)=D(x)\wedge y+(-1)^m x\wedge D(y)$ for $x\in\wedge^m_R(E)$ by induction on $m$. Both sides are $R$-linear in $x$, and $\wedge^m_R(E)$ is spanned by products of $m$ elements of $E$, so it suffices to take $x$ a product of $m$ elements of $E$. For $m=0$, $x=r\cdot1$: $D(ry)=rD(y)=D(r\cdot1)\wedge y+r\cdot1\wedge D(y)$ since $D(1)=0$. For $m\ge1$ write $x=v\wedge x'$ with $v\in E$, $x'\in\wedge^{m-1}_R(E)$ a product of $m-1$ elements. By associativity, the defining identity of $D$ and the induction hypothesis for $x'$: $D(x\wedge y)=D(v\wedge(x'\wedge y))=\varphi(v)\,x'\wedge y-v\wedge D(x'\wedge y)=\varphi(v)\,x'\wedge y-v\wedge D(x')\wedge y-(-1)^{m-1}v\wedge x'\wedge D(y)=(\varphi(v)x'-v\wedge D(x'))\wedge y+(-1)^m(v\wedge x')\wedge D(y)=D(x)\wedge y+(-1)^m x\wedge D(y)$. Together with (ii), $D$ is a derivation of degree $-1$ with $D(v)=\varphi(v)$.

(iv), uniqueness. Let $D'$ be $R$-linear with $D'(x\wedge y)=D'(x)\wedge y+(-1)^mx\wedge D'(y)$ for $x\in\wedge^m_R(E)$, $y\in\wedge(E)$, and $D'(v)=\varphi(v)\cdot1$ for $v\in E$. Taking $m=0$, $x=y=1$: $D'(1)=D'(1)\wedge1+1\wedge D'(1)=2D'(1)$, so $D'(1)=0$. Taking $m=1$, $x=v\in E=\wedge^1_R(E)$: $D'(v\wedge y)=\varphi(v)y-v\wedge D'(y)$. So $D'$ satisfies the two conditions of (i), and $D'=D$ by the uniqueness of step 2.
