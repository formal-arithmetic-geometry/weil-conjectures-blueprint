(Residue field.) Write $R_i=k[x_1,\dots,x_i]\subseteq P$ for $0\le i\le n$ ($R_0=k$, $R_n=P$) and $\varphi:P\to\kappa=P/\mathfrak M$ for the quotient map; $\kappa$ is a field because $\mathfrak M$ is maximal, and it is a finitely generated $k$-algebra (generated by the images $\alpha_i=\varphi(x_i)$), so by Zariski's lemma (the weak Nullstellensatz) $\kappa$ is a finite field extension of $k$; in particular every element of $\kappa$ is algebraic over every subfield containing $k$ (the powers of an element are $k$-linearly dependent). Put $\kappa_0=k$ (its image in $\kappa$) and let $\kappa_i=\kappa_{i-1}(\alpha_i)\subseteq\kappa$ be the smallest subfield containing $\kappa_{i-1}$ and $\alpha_i$, so $\kappa_i=k(\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_i)$ and $\kappa_n\supseteq\varphi(P)=\kappa$, i.e. $\kappa_n=\kappa$..

(The elements $f_i$.) For $1\le i\le n$ let $P_i\in\kappa_{i-1}[T]$ be the monic minimal polynomial of $\alpha_i$ over $\kappa_{i-1}$ (it exists by step 1). Then $d_i=\deg P_i\ge1$, the map $\kappa_{i-1}[T]\to\kappa$, $T\mapsto\alpha_i$, induces an isomorphism $\kappa_{i-1}[T]/(P_i)\xrightarrow{\sim}\kappa_i$, and $\kappa_i=\{g(\alpha_i):g\in\kappa_{i-1}[T]\}$. By induction on $i$ this gives $\kappa_i=k[\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_i]=\varphi(R_i)$ (for $i=0$ trivially; if $\kappa_{i-1}=\varphi(R_{i-1})$, every $g(\alpha_i)$ with $g\in\kappa_{i-1}[T]$ is $\varphi$ of a polynomial in $x_1,\dots,x_i$). So the restriction $\varphi_i=\varphi|_{R_i}:R_i\to\kappa_i$ is surjective. Lift each coefficient of $P_i$ along the surjection $\varphi_{i-1}$, taking $1$ as the lift of the leading coefficient: this gives a monic $Q_i\in R_{i-1}[T]$ of degree $d_i$ whose coefficientwise image is $P_i$. Put $f_i=Q_i(x_i)\in R_i$. Then $\varphi(f_i)=P_i(\alpha_i)=0$, so $f_i\in\mathfrak M\cap R_i$.

(Claim: $\ker(\varphi_i)=I_i:=(f_1,\dots,f_i)R_i$ for $0\le i\le n$, i.e. $\varphi_i$ induces an isomorphism $\psi_i:R_i/(f_1,\dots,f_i)\xrightarrow{\sim}\kappa_i$.) Induction on $i$. For $i=0$, $\varphi_0:k\to\kappa_0=k$ is the identity and $I_0=0$. Suppose $\ker\varphi_i=I_i$ with $i<n$. Identify $R_{i+1}=R_i[T]$ with $T=x_{i+1}$ (both are polynomial rings over $k$ in $x_1,\dots,x_{i+1}$; the two mutually inverse $k$-algebra maps are given on the variables by the universal property of polynomial rings). Since $\varphi_i:R_i\to\kappa_i$ is surjective with kernel $I_i$, applying it to coefficients (one variable $T$) gives a coefficient map $c:R_i[T]\to\kappa_i[T]$ is surjective with kernel $I_iR_{i+1}$. Let $e:\kappa_i[T]\to\kappa_{i+1}$ be the surjection $T\mapsto\alpha_{i+1}$, whose kernel is $(P_{i+1})$ by step 2. The composite $e\circ c:R_{i+1}\to\kappa_{i+1}$ agrees with $\varphi_{i+1}$ on $k$ and on $x_1,\dots,x_{i+1}$, hence equals $\varphi_{i+1}$ (uniqueness in the universal property). Now $\ker\varphi_{i+1}=c^{-1}((P_{i+1}))$. It contains $I_iR_{i+1}=\ker c$ and $f_{i+1}$ (as $c(f_{i+1})=P_{i+1}(T)$). Conversely if $c(g)=hP_{i+1}$, choose $h'\in R_{i+1}$ with $c(h')=h$ (surjectivity of $c$); then $c(g-h'f_{i+1})=0$, so $g\in I_iR_{i+1}+(f_{i+1})=I_{i+1}$. Hence $\ker\varphi_{i+1}=I_{i+1}$..

($\mathfrak M=(f_1,\dots,f_n)$ and a chain of primes of length $n$.) For $i=n$, $\varphi_n=\varphi$ and $\ker\varphi=\mathfrak M$, so step 3 gives $\mathfrak M=(f_1,\dots,f_n)P$. For $0\le i\le n$ put $\mathfrak p_i=(f_1,\dots,f_i)P=I_iP$. Identify $P=R_i[x_{i+1},\dots,x_n]$ as in step 3; applying the surjection $\varphi_i:R_i\to\kappa_i$, with kernel $I_i$ (step 3), to the coefficients of polynomials in $x_{i+1},\dots,x_n$, the coefficient map $c_i:P\to\kappa_i[x_{i+1},\dots,x_n]$ is surjective with kernel $I_iP=\mathfrak p_i$, so $P/\mathfrak p_i\cong\kappa_i[x_{i+1},\dots,x_n]$. This is an integral domain (a polynomial ring over a field), so $\mathfrak p_i$ is a prime ideal. Moreover $\mathfrak p_i\subsetneq\mathfrak p_{i+1}$ for $i<n$: the inclusion is clear, and $c_i(f_{i+1})=P_{i+1}(x_{i+1})$ is a nonzero polynomial (monic of degree $d_{i+1}\ge1$, step 2), so $f_{i+1}\notin\mathfrak p_i$. Hence $(0)=\mathfrak p_0\subsetneq\mathfrak p_1\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq\mathfrak p_n=\mathfrak M$ is a chain of prime ideals of $P$ of length $n$.

($\dim P_{\mathfrak M}\ge n$.) The chain of step 4 is a chain of primes of $P$ of length $n$ whose last member $\mathfrak p_n=\mathfrak M$ is contained in $\mathfrak M$, so, the Krull dimension of $P_{\mathfrak M}$ being the height of $\mathfrak M$, it is at least $n$.

($\dim P_{\mathfrak M}\le n$.) A field $k$ is a Noetherian ring (its only ideals are $0$ and $k$), so $P$, a finite type $k$-algebra, is Noetherian by the Hilbert basis theorem, and its localization $P_{\mathfrak M}$ is Noetherian (localizations of Noetherian rings are Noetherian). $P_{\mathfrak M}$ is a local ring with maximal ideal $\mathfrak MP_{\mathfrak M}$, and by step 4 this ideal is generated by the $n$ elements $f_1/1,\dots,f_n/1$ (an element $a/s$ with $a=\sum g_jf_j\in\mathfrak M$ is $\sum(g_j/s)(f_j/1)$). By Krull's height theorem (the dimension of a Noetherian local ring is at most the number of generators of its maximal ideal), $\dim P_{\mathfrak M}\le n$. With step 5, $\dim P_{\mathfrak M}=n$.
