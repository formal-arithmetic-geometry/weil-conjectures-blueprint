*Reduction to one piece.* By the definition of constructible subsets write $E=\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}U_i\cap(X\setminus V_i)$ with $U_i,V_i\subseteq X$ open and retrocompact ($n\ge 0$; for $n=0$, $E=\emptyset$). Fix a quasi-compact open $W\subseteq X$. Distributing the intersection over the finite union gives $E\cap W=\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}P_i$ with $P_i=U_i\cap(X\setminus V_i)\cap W=(U_i\cap W)\setminus V_i$. So it suffices to show each $P_i$ is quasi-compact (step 2) and that a finite union of quasi-compact subsets is quasi-compact (step 3).

*Each piece is quasi-compact.* Fix $i$. Since $U_i$ is retrocompact and $W$ is a quasi-compact open of $X$, the set $Q=U_i\cap W$ is quasi-compact in its induced topology. Since $V_i$ is open in $X$, $Q\cap V_i$ is open in the subspace $Q$, so $P_i=Q\setminus(Q\cap V_i)$ is closed in $Q$. By the fact that closed subsets of quasi-compact spaces are quasi-compact (Stacks 005C) applied to the quasi-compact space $Q$ and its closed subset $P_i$, $P_i$ is quasi-compact in the topology induced from $Q$, which equals the topology induced from $X$ (the subspace topology of a subspace of a subspace is the subspace topology). Hence $P_i$ is a quasi-compact subset of $X$. (Only openness of $V_i$ is used here, not its retrocompactness.)

*Finite unions.* A finite union $P_1\cup\dots\cup P_n$ of quasi-compact subsets of $X$ is quasi-compact: given a family of open subsets of $X$ covering the union, each $P_i$ is covered by finitely many of its members, and the union of these $n$ finite subfamilies is a finite subfamily covering $P_1\cup\dots\cup P_n$ (for $n=0$ the empty set is quasi-compact). With step 1 and step 2, $E\cap W$ is quasi-compact for every quasi-compact open $W\subseteq X$, i.e. $E$ is retrocompact in $X$. All cases are valid in arbitrary topological spaces, with no separation, compactness or nonemptiness assumption on $X$.
