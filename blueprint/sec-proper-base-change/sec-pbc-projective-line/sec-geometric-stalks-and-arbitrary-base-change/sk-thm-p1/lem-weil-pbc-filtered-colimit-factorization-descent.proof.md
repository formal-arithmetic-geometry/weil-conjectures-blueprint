*Lifting finitely many polynomials.* Every element of the filtered colimit $A$ is $\iota_i(a)$ for some $i$ and $a\in A_i$, and since $\Lambda$ is directed, finitely many elements can be represented at one common stage. Applying this to the finitely many coefficients of a finite family of polynomials over $A$ gives a stage $i$ and polynomials over $A_i$ mapping to them. A monic polynomial of degree $d$ can be lifted to a monic polynomial of degree $d$, by lifting its coefficients in degrees $<d$ and taking leading coefficient $1$. This also covers the case $A=0$.

*Vanishing modulo the ideals at a later stage.* Let $a\in A_i$ with $\iota_i(a)\in I$. By hypothesis $\iota_i(a)=\iota_k(c)$ for some $k$ and $c\in J_k$. Two representatives with the same image in $A$ become equal at a common later stage $l\ge i,k$, and there the image of $c$ lies in $J_l$ by compatibility of the ideals, so the image of $a$ in $A_l$ lies in $J_l$. For a finite family of polynomials over $A_i$ whose images in $A[X]$ vanish modulo $I$, apply this to their finitely many nonzero coefficients and take one common upper bound $l\ge i$ of the resulting stages; using compatibility once more, at that stage every coefficient lies in $J_l$, i.e. the polynomials vanish modulo $J_l$.

*Conclusion.* Lift $g_0$ and $h_0$ to monic polynomials $g,h\in A[X]$, using surjectivity of $A\to A/I$ and the monic lifting above, and lift Bezout witnesses $a_0,b_0\in(A/I)[X]$ with $a_0g_0+b_0h_0=1$ to polynomials $a,b\in A[X]$. Apply the first step to the five polynomials $f,g,h,a,b$: there are a stage $i$, monic $f_i,g_i,h_i\in A_i[X]$ and $a_i,b_i\in A_i[X]$ mapping to $f,g,h,a,b$. The polynomials $f_i-g_ih_i$ and $a_ig_i+b_ih_i-1$ map to $f-gh$ and $ag+bh-1$, which vanish modulo $I$ because $f\bmod I=g_0h_0$ and $a_0g_0+b_0h_0=1$. Apply the second step to these two polynomials and pass to the resulting later stage. There their reductions modulo $J$ vanish, which gives the factorization $f_i\equiv g_ih_i$ and the Bezout identity $a_ig_i+b_ih_i\equiv1$, so the reductions of $g_i$ and $h_i$ are coprime with product $f_i\bmod J_i$. Transition maps preserve monicity, and compatibility of the coprojections gives $\iota_i(f_i)=f$ and that $\iota_i(g_i)$, $\iota_i(h_i)$ reduce to $g_0$, $h_0$ modulo $I$. The argument also handles a zero colimit or zero quotient ring without assuming nontriviality.
