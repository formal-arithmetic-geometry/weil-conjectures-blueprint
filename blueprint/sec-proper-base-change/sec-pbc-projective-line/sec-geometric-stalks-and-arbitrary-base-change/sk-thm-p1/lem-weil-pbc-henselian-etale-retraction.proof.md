**Step 1.** *The residue map $\rho$.* Write $\pi:S\to\kappa=S/\mathfrak m$ and $\iota:S\to B$ for the structure map. The composite $\kappa\to B/\mathfrak q'\hookrightarrow\kappa(\mathfrak q')$ [def-weil-residue-field] (the residue field of $\operatorname{Spec}B$ at $\mathfrak q'$ is the fraction field of the domain $B/\mathfrak q'$) is an isomorphism by hypothesis, so the subring $B/\mathfrak q'$ contains the image of $\kappa$, which is all of $\kappa(\mathfrak q')$; hence $S\to B/\mathfrak q'$ is surjective with kernel $\mathfrak q'\cap S=\mathfrak m$, and we get a surjective $S$-algebra map $\rho:B\to B/\mathfrak q'\cong\kappa$ with $\ker\rho=\mathfrak q'$. Moreover: (*) two surjective $S$-algebra maps $\psi_1,\psi_2:A\to\kappa$ from an $S$-algebra $A$ with the same kernel are equal, because $\psi_2=\alpha\circ\psi_1$ for a ring automorphism $\alpha$ of $\kappa$ which is $S$-linear, and $\kappa$ is generated by $1$ as an $S$-module, so $\alpha=\mathrm{id}$. In particular, for an $S$-algebra map $\sigma:B\to S$ (a retraction, $\sigma\circ\iota=\mathrm{id}_S$, so $\sigma$ and $\pi\circ\sigma$ are surjective) we have $\sigma^{-1}(\mathfrak m)=\ker(\pi\circ\sigma)$, and $\sigma^{-1}(\mathfrak m)=\mathfrak q'$ iff $\pi\circ\sigma=\rho$.

**Step 2.** *Standard etale presentation (Stacks: by Proposition 00UE).* By [lem-weil-pbc-etale-locally-standard] applied to $S\to B$ and $\mathfrak q'$ there are $g\in B\setminus\mathfrak q'$, a monic $f\in S[t ]$, an $h\in S[t ]$ with $f'p_1+fp_2=h^n$ in $S[t ]$ (some $p_1,p_2\in S[t ]$, $n\ge0$) and an $S$-algebra isomorphism $\varphi:B_g\xrightarrow{\sim}C=C(f,h)=S[t ][Y ]/(f,Yh-1)$; let $x\in C$ be the class of $t$ and $\lambda:B\to B_g$ the localization map. This is the only place where etaleness of $\operatorname{Spec}B\to\operatorname{Spec}S$ is used.

**Step 3.** *Reduction to $C$ (Stacks: 'after replacing $S$ by $S_g$ we may assume $S=R[t ]_g/(f)$ is standard etale (details omitted)').* Since $\rho(g)\neq0$ in the field $\kappa$, the universal property of the localization gives a unique ring map $\rho':B_g\to\kappa$ with $\rho'\circ\lambda=\rho$ (an $S$-algebra map); put $\rho_C=\rho'\circ\varphi^{-1}:C\to\kappa$. Claim: $\tau\mapsto\tau\circ\varphi\circ\lambda$ is a bijection from the $S$-algebra maps $\tau:C\to S$ with $\pi\circ\tau=\rho_C$ onto the $S$-algebra maps $\sigma:B\to S$ with $\sigma^{-1}(\mathfrak m)=\mathfrak q'$. (a) Well defined: $\pi\circ\tau\circ\varphi\circ\lambda=\rho_C\circ\varphi\circ\lambda=\rho$, so $\sigma^{-1}(\mathfrak m)=\mathfrak q'$ by step 1. (b) Injective: $\lambda$ is an epimorphism of rings (a ring map out of $B_g$ is determined by its composite with $\lambda$, uniqueness in the universal property of localization) and $\varphi$ is bijective. (c) Surjective: let $\sigma$ be given, so $\pi\circ\sigma=\rho$ (step 1); $\sigma(g)\notin\mathfrak m$ (as $g\notin\mathfrak q'$), so $\sigma(g)$ is a unit of the local ring $S$ and $\sigma=\sigma'\circ\lambda$ for a ring map $\sigma':B_g\to S$, an $S$-algebra map. Put $\tau=\sigma'\circ\varphi^{-1}$; then $\tau\circ\varphi\circ\lambda=\sigma$, and $\pi\circ\sigma'$ and $\rho'$ are two ring maps $B_g\to\kappa$ with the same composite $\rho$ with $\lambda$, so they are equal and $\pi\circ\tau=\rho_C$.

**Step 4.** *The residue root $a_0$ (Stacks: 'the prime $\mathfrak q$ has residue field $\kappa$, so it corresponds to a root $a_0$ of $\overline f$ which is not a root of $\overline g$; by definition of standard etale also $\overline{f'}(a_0)\ne0$').* Put $a_0=\rho_C(x)\in\kappa$. As $\rho_C$ is an $S$-algebra map, $\rho_C(P(x))=\overline P(a_0)$ for every $P\in S[t ]$ ($\overline P$ the reduction mod $\mathfrak m$). By [lem-weil-pbc-standard-etale-pair-maps] (2) with $L=\kappa$, $\chi=\rho_C$: $\overline f(a_0)=0$ and $\overline h(a_0)\neq0$ (a unit of $\kappa$). Reducing $f'p_1+fp_2=h^n$ and evaluating at $a_0$ gives $\overline{f'}(a_0)\overline{p_1}(a_0)=\overline h(a_0)^n\neq0$, so $\overline{f'}(a_0)\neq0$ (and $\overline{f'}=\overline f\,'$).

**Step 5.** *Existence.* $S$ is henselian [def-weil-pbc-henselian-terminology], so by [lem-weil-pbc-henselian-local-root-lifting] ((i)$\Rightarrow$(ii)), applied to the monic $f$ and the simple root $a_0$ of $\overline f$ (step 4), there is $a\in S$ with $f(a)=0$ and $\pi(a)=a_0$. Then $\pi(h(a))=\overline h(a_0)\neq0$, so $h(a)\notin\mathfrak m$ is a unit of the local ring $S$. By [lem-weil-pbc-standard-etale-pair-maps] (1) with $L=S$, $b=a$, there is an $S$-algebra map $\tau:C\to S$ with $\tau(x)=a$. The $S$-algebra maps $\pi\circ\tau$ and $\rho_C$ from $C$ to $\kappa$ both send $x$ to $a_0$, so they are equal by [lem-weil-pbc-standard-etale-pair-maps] (2) (determined by the image of $x$). By step 3, $\sigma=\tau\circ\varphi\circ\lambda:B\to S$ is an $S$-algebra map, i.e. a retraction of $S\to B$, with $\sigma^{-1}(\mathfrak m)=\mathfrak q'$.

**Step 6.** *Uniqueness (Stacks: 'By Lemma 06RR the map $\tau$ is unique').* Let $\sigma_1,\sigma_2:B\to S$ be $S$-algebra maps with $\sigma_i^{-1}(\mathfrak m)=\mathfrak q'$. By step 3, $\sigma_i=\tau_i\circ\varphi\circ\lambda$ with $S$-algebra maps $\tau_i:C\to S$, $\pi\circ\tau_i=\rho_C$. Put $a_i=\tau_i(x)\in S$. By [lem-weil-pbc-standard-etale-pair-maps] (2) (with $L=S$), $f(a_i)=0$; $\pi(a_i)=\rho_C(x)=a_0$, so $a_1-a_2\in\mathfrak m$; and $\pi(f'(a_1))=\overline{f'}(a_0)\neq0$ (step 4), so $f'(a_1)\notin\mathfrak m$. By [lem-weil-pbc-simple-root-uniqueness], $a_1=a_2$; so $\tau_1=\tau_2$ ([lem-weil-pbc-standard-etale-pair-maps] (2): an $S$-algebra map out of $C$ is determined by the image of $x$), and $\sigma_1=\sigma_2$.
