Both sides are maps of abelian groups, so we compare them on each cohomology class. Each edge map is the cohomology pullback followed by $H^q$ of the counit. Naturality of the cohomology pullback gives two commutations: pullback along $g'$ carries $H^q(\varepsilon_f)$ to $H^q(g'^{-1}\varepsilon_f)$, i.e. $g'^*\circ H^q(\varepsilon_f)=H^q(g'^{-1}\varepsilon_f)\circ g'^*$, and pullback along $f'$ carries $H^q(\Ex^0)$ to $H^q(f'^{-1}\Ex^0)$, i.e. $f'^*\circ H^q(\Ex^0)=H^q(f'^{-1}\Ex^0)\circ f'^*$. This uses only the cohomology maps already constructed, with no new choice of injective resolutions. ([def-weil-pbc-direct-image], [def-weil-pbc-inverse-image], [def-weil-pbc-cohomology-pullback], [lem-weil-pbc-cohomology-pullback-functorial]).

Set $A=f_*F$ and $h=f\circ g'=g\circ f'$, which is possible since the square commutes. By the composition clause of [lem-weil-pbc-cohomology-pullback-functorial], the successive pullbacks are $h^*$ followed by the composition isomorphisms: $f'^*\circ g^*=H^q(c_{g,f'}^{-1})\circ h^*$ and $g'^*\circ f^*=H^q(c_{f,g'}^{-1})\circ h^*$ on $H^q(Y,A)$, with $c$ the composition isomorphisms of [lem-weil-pbc-inverse-image-composition]. ([def-weil-pbc-cartesian-square], [lem-weil-pbc-inverse-image-composition], [lem-weil-pbc-cohomology-pullback-functorial]).

By definition $\theta_\square=c_{f,g'}^{-1}\circ c_{g,f'}$, so $\theta_\square\circ c_{g,f'}^{-1}=c_{f,g'}^{-1}$. By functoriality of $H^q$ and the previous step, $H^q(\theta_\square)\circ f'^*\circ g^*=g'^*\circ f^*$ on $H^q(Y,f_*F)$. ([def-weil-pbc-cartesian-square], [lem-weil-pbc-inverse-image-composition]).

The defining compatibility of the underived exchange morphism with the counits says $\varepsilon_{f',g'^{-1}F}\circ f'^{-1}(\Ex^0_{\square,F})=g'^{-1}(\varepsilon_{f,F})\circ\theta_{\square,f_*F}$ as maps $f'^{-1}g^{-1}f_*F\to g'^{-1}F$. Applying $H^q$ and combining with the previous steps,
$$e^q_{f',g'^{-1}F}\circ H^q(\Ex^0)\circ g^*=H^q(\varepsilon_{f'})\circ H^q(f'^{-1}\Ex^0)\circ f'^*\circ g^*=H^q(g'^{-1}\varepsilon_f)\circ H^q(\theta_\square)\circ f'^*\circ g^*=H^q(g'^{-1}\varepsilon_f)\circ g'^*\circ f^*=g'^*\circ H^q(\varepsilon_f)\circ f^*=g'^*\circ e^q_{f,F}.$$
This is the asserted equality; no hypothesis on $f$ was used. ([def-weil-pbc-underived-exchange]).

If $f$ is finite, so is its base change $f'$, finite morphisms being stable under base change. By [lem-weil-pbc-finite-pushforward-cohomology] the edge maps $e^q_{f,F}$ and $e^q_{f',g'^{-1}F}$ are then bijective. If moreover $\Ex^0_{\square,F}$ is an isomorphism, then $H^q(\Ex^0)$ is bijective, with inverse $H^q$ of the inverse of $\Ex^0$ by functoriality of $H^q$. Hence $e^q_{f',g'^{-1}F}\circ H^q(\Ex^0)$ is an isomorphism, and the displayed equality identifies $g^*$ on $H^q(Y,f_*F)$ with $g'^*$ on $H^q(X,F)$ through the isomorphisms $e^q_{f,F}$ and $e^q_{f',g'^{-1}F}\circ H^q(\Ex^0)$.
