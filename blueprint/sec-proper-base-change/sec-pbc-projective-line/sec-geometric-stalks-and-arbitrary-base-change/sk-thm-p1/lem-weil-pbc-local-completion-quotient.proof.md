*Construction of $\theta$.* Since $\lambda(\mathfrak m^k)=\mathfrak n^k$, $\lambda$ induces a surjective ring map $q_k:A/\mathfrak m^k\to B/\mathfrak n^k$ for every $k$. The $q_k$ are compatible with the transition maps $A/\mathfrak m^{k+1}\to A/\mathfrak m^k$ and $B/\mathfrak n^{k+1}\to B/\mathfrak n^k$ (check on representatives). Composing $q_k$ with the projections $A^\wedge\to A/\mathfrak m^k$ therefore gives a compatible family of ring maps, hence by the universal property of the inverse limit a ring map $\theta:A^\wedge\to B^\wedge$, $(x_k)_k\mapsto(\lambda(x_k))_k$. Comparing coordinates gives $\theta\circ c_A=c_B\circ\lambda$.

*$\theta$ is surjective.* Regard $B$ as an $A$-module via $\lambda$. Then $\mathfrak m^kB=\lambda(\mathfrak m^k)B=\mathfrak n^k$, so the $\mathfrak n$-adic completion of the ring $B$ is the $\mathfrak m$-adic completion of the $A$-module $B$, and $\theta$ is the map induced on $\mathfrak m$-adic completions by the surjective $A$-linear map $\lambda:A\to B$. Adic completion preserves surjections: given an element of $B^\wedge$, choose compatible preimages of its coordinates under the surjections $q_k$ step by step, which gives a Cauchy sequence in $A$ whose class maps to the given element. Hence $\theta$ is surjective.

*The kernel is contained in $JA^\wedge$.* Let $J=\ker\lambda$. Since $A$ is Noetherian, $J$, $A$ and $A/J$ are finitely generated $A$-modules, and $\mathfrak m$-adic completion is exact on finitely generated modules over a Noetherian ring; so $J^\wedge\to A^\wedge\to(A/J)^\wedge$ is exact. Let $x\in\ker\theta$, represented by a Cauchy sequence $(s_k)_k$ in $A$. Then $\lambda(s_k)\in\mathfrak n^k=\lambda(\mathfrak m^k)$, so there is $a_k\in\mathfrak m^k$ with $\lambda(a_k)=\lambda(s_k)$. Then $s_k-a_k\in J$, so the class of $s_k$ in $A/J$ lies in $\mathfrak m^k(A/J)$. Thus every coordinate of the image of $x$ in $(A/J)^\wedge$ is zero, and by exactness $x$ is the image of some $y\in J^\wedge$.

Since $J$ is finitely generated, the natural map $A^\wedge\otimes_AJ\to J^\wedge$ is surjective; write $y$ as the image of a tensor $t\in A^\wedge\otimes_AJ$. A pure tensor $a\otimes j$ maps to $a\,c_A(j)\in A^\wedge$, so by additivity the image $x$ of $t$ in $A^\wedge$ lies in $JA^\wedge$. Conversely, for $j\in J$ one has $\theta(c_A(j))=c_B(\lambda(j))=0$, so $JA^\wedge\subseteq\ker\theta$. Hence $\ker\theta=JA^\wedge$.

*Conclusion.* As $\theta$ is surjective with kernel $JA^\wedge$, it induces a ring isomorphism $A^\wedge/JA^\wedge\xrightarrow{\sim}B^\wedge$ sending the class of $x$ to $\theta(x)$.
