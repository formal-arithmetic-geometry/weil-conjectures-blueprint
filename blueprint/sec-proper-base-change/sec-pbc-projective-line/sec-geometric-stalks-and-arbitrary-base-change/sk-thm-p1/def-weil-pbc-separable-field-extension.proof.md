(The notions used in (1).) A transcendence basis $(x_i)_{i\in I}$ of $K/k$ is a family algebraically independent over $k$ with $K/k(x_i;i\in I)$ algebraic, and $k(x_i;i\in I)$ is a subfield of $K$ containing $k$, so $K$ is a field extension of $k(x_i;i\in I)$ and the condition 'separable algebraic' applies to it. Its algebraicity half holds already by the definition of a transcendence basis, so (1) only adds that every $\alpha\in K$ is separable over $k(x_i;i\in I)$. Clause (1) asks for the existence of one such basis.

(The subextensions in (2).) For finitely many $a_1,\dots,a_n\in K$, $K'=k(a_1,\dots,a_n)$ is the smallest subfield of $K$ containing $k$ and the $a_j$; it contains $k$, so $K'/k$ is a field extension and (1) applies to it, with transcendence bases taken inside $K'$. Hence (2) is a well-formed condition on $K/k$.
