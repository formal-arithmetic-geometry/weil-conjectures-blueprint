Choose a presentation $E\cong k[X_1,\dots,X_n]/J$ with $J$ generated by finitely many polynomials $g_1,\dots,g_r$. The set of all coefficients of the $g_j$ is finite; since the $K_i$ are directed with union $k$ (and $I$ is nonempty, which handles the case of no coefficients), there is $i$ with all these coefficients in $K_i$. So each $g_j$ is the image of a polynomial $g_j^0\in K_i[X_1,\dots,X_n]$. Let $J_0=(g_1^0,\dots,g_r^0)$ and $B=K_i[X_1,\dots,X_n]/J_0$.

Extension of scalars of polynomial rings gives $k\otimes_{K_i}K_i[X_1,\dots,X_n]\cong k[X_1,\dots,X_n]$, and tensoring a quotient gives $k\otimes_{K_i}B\cong k[X_1,\dots,X_n]/J_0k[X_1,\dots,X_n]$. The extended ideal is generated by the images of the $g_j^0$, i.e. by the $g_j$, so it equals $J$. Hence $k\otimes_{K_i}B\cong k[X_1,\dots,X_n]/J\cong E$ as $k$-algebras. This also covers the zero algebra and presentations with no variables or no relations.
