Let $\Lambda$ be the finite subsets $A\subseteq R$, ordered by inclusion. It is nonempty since $\varnothing\in\Lambda$, and $A\cup B$ is a common upper bound of $A,B$. Put $C_A=\mathbf Z[A]\subseteq R$, the subring of polynomial expressions in the elements of $A$ with integer coefficients, and $q_A=C_A\cap\mathfrak m$. This is a prime ideal: $1\notin q_A$ and $ab\in q_A$ implies $a\in q_A$ or $b\in q_A$, since $\mathfrak m$ is maximal and hence prime. Define $R_A=(C_A)_{q_A}$ and $\mathfrak p_A=q_AR_A$ (localization at the prime $q_A$). Being a localization at a prime, $R_A$ is local with maximal ideal $\mathfrak p_A$. The ring $C_A$ is of finite type over $\mathbf Z$, generated by the finite set $A$. Thus its localization $R_A$ is essentially of finite type over $\mathbf Z$.

If $A\subseteq B$, then $C_A\subseteq C_B$ and $q_B\cap C_A=q_A$. For $s\in C_A\setminus q_A$, the same element lies outside $q_B$. Define $r_{BA}:R_A\to R_B$ by $a/s\mapsto a/s$. To check well-definedness, an equality in $R_A$ is witnessed by $u\in C_A\setminus q_A$ with $u(as'-a' s)=0$; the same equation and denominator $u$ witness equality in $R_B$. The usual fraction formulas for addition and multiplication show this is a ring map. It carries the generators $a/1$ of $q_AR_A$, $a\in q_A$, into $q_BR_B$, so is local. The formula gives $r_{AA}=\mathrm{id}$ and $r_{CB}r_{BA}=r_{CA}$ for $A\subseteq B\subseteq C$.

For $s\in C_A\setminus q_A$ one has $s\notin\mathfrak m$, so, $R$ being local, $s$ is a unit, with inverse $s^{-1}\in R$. Define $\pi_A(a/s)=as^{-1}$. If $u(as'-a' s)=0$ witnesses equality of fractions, then $u,s,s'$ are units in $R$, so multiplication by their inverses gives $as^{-1}=a'(s')^{-1}$. The fraction formulas show that $\pi_A$ is a ring map, and $\pi_A(a/1)=a$ for $a\in C_A$. Since $\pi_A$ takes each generator of $q_AR_A$ into $\mathfrak m$, it is local. The same formula gives $\pi_Br_{BA}=\pi_A$. Moreover $\pi_A$ is injective: if $\pi_A(a/s)=\pi_A(b/t)$, multiplication by $st$ gives $at-bs=0$ in $R$, hence in the subring $C_A$; the equality criterion with witness $1$ gives $a/s=b/t$ in $R_A$.

Every $x\in R$ is $\pi_{\{x\}}(x/1)$. We now check the universal property of the colimit. Let $T$ be any commutative ring and $f_A:R_A\to T$ ring maps with $f_Br_{BA}=f_A$ for $A\subseteq B$. Define $f(x)=f_A(y)$ whenever $\pi_A(y)=x$, a representation which exists by the preceding observation. If also $\pi_B(z)=x$, put $C=A\cup B$. Then $\pi_C(r_{CA}(y))=x=\pi_C(r_{CB}(z))$; injectivity of $\pi_C$ gives $r_{CA}(y)=r_{CB}(z)$, and compatibility gives $f_A(y)=f_B(z)$. Thus $f$ is well defined. For $x=\pi_A(y)$ and $x'=\pi_B(z)$, use their images in $R_{A\cup B}$: since both $\pi_{A\cup B}$ and $f_{A\cup B}$ are ring maps, this gives $f(x+x')=f(x)+f(x')$ and $f(xx')=f(x)f(x')$; representing $0,1$ at $\varnothing$ gives $f(0)=0$, $f(1)=1$. Hence $f$ is a ring map, and its defining formula gives $f\pi_A=f_A$ for all $A$. Any other such map takes $x=\pi_{\{x\}}(x/1)$ to $f_{\{x\}}(x/1)$, so equals $f$. This proves that the compatible local maps $\pi_A$ exhibit $R$ as the colimit in commutative rings.
