Choose a finite presentation $\Phi:R[x_1,\ldots,x_n]/(f_1,\ldots,f_m)\xrightarrow{\sim}S$ as an $R$-algebra. Let $C\subseteq R$ be the $\mathbf Z$-subalgebra generated by all coefficients of the finitely many $f_j$. Each polynomial has finite support, so this is a finite set of coefficients, including when $n=0$ or $m=0$. Write $f_{j,T}\in T[x_1,\ldots,x_n]$ for the same expression whenever $C\subseteq T\subseteq R$.

Let $\Lambda$ be the finitely generated $\mathbf Z$-subalgebras $T\subseteq R$ containing $C$, ordered by inclusion. It contains $C$. Given $T,U\in\Lambda$, the subalgebra generated by their finite generating sets contains both, so $\Lambda$ is directed. Every $r\in R$ belongs to the member generated by $C\cup\{r\}$, and the transition maps are inclusions. Thus $(R,(T\hookrightarrow R)_{T\in\Lambda})$ is a colimit of the $T$ in commutative rings by [lemma-nagata-ring-finite-type-subalgebras], restricting its full directed system to this cofinal subsystem. Put $R_T=T$; each is a finite type $\mathbf Z$-algebra.

For $T\in\Lambda$ set $S_T=T[x_1,\ldots,x_n]/(f_{1,T},\ldots,f_{m,T})$ and let $\rho_T:T\to S_T$ be the structure map. The inclusion $T\subseteq U$ sends every $f_{j,T}$ to $f_{j,U}$, hence induces a ring map $S_T\to S_U$ compatible with $\rho_T$; identities and compositions agree because these maps agree on $T$ and the classes of all $x_i$. Coefficient inclusion also gives $\kappa_T:S_T\to S$ through $\Phi$, compatible with these maps and satisfying $\kappa_T\rho_T=\varphi|_T$. Each $S_T$ is generated as a $T$-algebra by the $n$ classes of $x_i$, proving clause (3).

Fix $T\subseteq U$ in $\Lambda$. Since polynomial rings commute with base change, $S_T\otimes_TU$ is the quotient of $U[x_1,\ldots,x_n]$ by the extended ideal generated by $f_{1,T},\ldots,f_{m,T}$: explicitly, the maps $U[x]\to S_T\otimes_TU$, $u\mapsto1\otimes u$, $x_i\mapsto\bar x_i\otimes1$, and $S_T\otimes_TU\to U[x]/(f_{1,U},\ldots,f_{m,U})$, $\bar p\otimes u\mapsto u\bar p$, respect all relations. Each map descends through the indicated quotient, and their composites fix $U$ and every $x_i$, so they are inverse $U$-algebra maps. The latter is exactly the canonical map $S_T\otimes_TU\to S_U$, proving (4).

The same two explicit maps with $U=R$ identify $S_T\otimes_TR$ with $R[x_1,\ldots,x_n]/(f_1,\ldots,f_m)$, and composing with $\Phi$ gives the map $s\otimes r\mapsto\kappa_T(s)\varphi(r)$ of (5). In particular it is an isomorphism for every $T\in\Lambda$.

Finally $S$ is the colimit of $(S_T)$ as a ring, completing (1). Indeed a compatible cocone of ring maps $h_T:S_T\to B$ gives compatible maps $T\to B$, hence a unique $h_R:R\to B$ by Step 2. The images $b_i=h_T(\bar x_i)$ do not depend on $T$, because every transition sends $\bar x_i$ to $\bar x_i$. Since $f_{j,C}(\bar x)=0$ in $S_C$, we have $f_j(b_1,\ldots,b_n)=0$ with coefficients mapped by $h_R$. The quotient presentation of Step 1 therefore gives a unique ring map $h_S:S\to B$ sending $r\in R$ to $h_R(r)$ and $\Phi(\bar x_i)$ to $b_i$. For every $T$, $h_S\kappa_T=h_T$ because both maps agree on $T$ and the $\bar x_i$; conversely any map $S\to B$ gives such a compatible cocone. Thus $(S,(\kappa_T))$ has the colimit universal property, and the two colimits form the claimed colimit of ring maps $R_T\to S_T$. ([lemma-nagata-ring-finite-type-subalgebras]).
