(The generic fibre.) Let $\varphi:R\to S$ be the given injective map and $U=R\setminus\{0\}$, a multiplicative subset since $R$ is a domain. Let $K=R_{(0)}=U^{-1}R$ be the fraction field of $R$ (a field: a nonzero $r/u$ has inverse $u/r$), and $S_K=U^{-1}S$ the localization of $S$ at the image $\varphi(U)$: fractions $s/u$ ($s\in S$, $u\in U$) with $s/u=s'/u'$ iff $\varphi(v)(\varphi(u')s-\varphi(u)s')=0$ for some $v\in U$; write $\lambda:S\to S_K$, $s\mapsto s/1$. The map $K\to S_K$, $r/u\mapsto\varphi(r)/u$, is a well-defined ring homomorphism, making $S_K$ a $K$-algebra. Choose $x_1,\ldots,x_n\in S$ generating $S$ as an $R$-algebra. Then $S_K$ is of finite type over $K$, generated by $x_1/1,\ldots,x_n/1$: for $s=p(x_1,\ldots,x_n)$ with $p$ a polynomial over $R$, $s/u=(1/u)\cdot p(x_1/1,\ldots,x_n/1)$. And $1\ne0$ in $S_K$: $1/1=0$ would give $\varphi(v)\cdot1=0$ for some $v\in U$, i.e. $\varphi(v)=0=\varphi(0)$ with $v\ne0$, contradicting injectivity of $\varphi$.

(Noether normalization over $K$, with the $y_j$ in $S$.) By Noether normalization applied to the finite type $K$-algebra $S_K\ne0$ (Step 1) there are $d\ge0$ and an injective $K$-algebra map $\psi:K[Y_1,\ldots,Y_d]\to S_K$ from the polynomial ring such that $S_K$ is a finitely generated $K[Y]$-module through $\psi$. Write $\psi(Y_j)=s_j/u_j$ with $s_j\in S$, $u_j\in U$. Let $\sigma$ be the $K$-algebra endomorphism of $K[Y_1,\ldots,Y_d]$ with $Y_j\mapsto(u_j/1)Y_j$ (universal property of the polynomial ring); the endomorphism $Y_j\mapsto(1/u_j)Y_j$ is inverse to it, as both composites fix every $Y_j$ and hence are the identity by the uniqueness in the universal property. So $\psi'=\psi\circ\sigma$ is injective, has the same image $\psi(K[Y])$ as $\psi$, and $S_K$ is a finitely generated $K[Y]$-module through $\psi'$ (a finite set generating $S_K$ as a module over the subring $\psi(K[Y])$ serves for both). Moreover $\psi'(Y_j)=(u_j/1)(s_j/u_j)=s_j/1$. Put $y_j=s_j\in S$; thus $\psi':K[Y]\to S_K$ is the $K$-algebra map with $Y_j\mapsto\lambda(y_j)$.

(Algebraic independence of $y_1,\ldots,y_d$ over $R$.) Let $\alpha:R[Y_1,\ldots,Y_d]\to S$ be the $R$-algebra map with $Y_j\mapsto y_j$ and $\iota:R[Y]\to K[Y]$ the map applying $r\mapsto r/1$ to the coefficients. $\iota$ is injective: $r/1=0$ in $K$ means $vr=0$ for some $v\ne0$, so $r=0$ as $R$ is a domain. The two $R$-algebra maps $\lambda\circ\alpha$ and $\psi'\circ\iota:R[Y]\to S_K$ both send $Y_j$ to $y_j/1$, so they are equal by the uniqueness in the universal property of the polynomial ring. As $\psi'\circ\iota$ is injective (Step 2), so is $\alpha$: this is the injectivity in (1), and $R[y_1,\ldots,y_d]$ denotes the image of $\alpha$.

(Integral equations over $K[Y]$.) Since $S_K$ is a finitely generated $K[Y]$-module through $\psi'$ (Step 2), every element of $S_K$ is integral over $K[Y]$ (a finite algebra is integral). So for each $i$ there are $m_i\ge1$ and $c_{i1},\ldots,c_{im_i}\in K[Y]$ such that, with $P_i(T)=T^{m_i}+c_{i1}T^{m_i-1}+\cdots+c_{im_i}$, we have $P_i(x_i/1)=(x_i/1)^{m_i}+\sum_{k=1}^{m_i}\psi'(c_{ik})(x_i/1)^{m_i-k}=0$ in $S_K$.

(Clearing denominators, in $K$ and in $S$.) The finitely many coefficients in $K$ of the polynomials $c_{ik}\in K[Y]$ can be written $a/u$ ($a\in R$, $u\in U$); let $f_0\in U$ be the product of all these denominators. Then $f_0c_{ik}=\iota(b_{ik})$ with $b_{ik}\in R[Y]$. Put $F_i=\varphi(f_0)x_i^{m_i}+\sum_{k=1}^{m_i}\alpha(b_{ik})x_i^{m_i-k}\in S$. Using $\lambda\circ\alpha=\psi'\circ\iota$ (Step 3), $\lambda(F_i)=(f_0/1)\bigl((x_i/1)^{m_i}+\sum_k\psi'(c_{ik})(x_i/1)^{m_i-k}\bigr)=0$ (Step 4), so by the definition of $S_K$ there is $v_i\in U$ with $\varphi(v_i)F_i=0$ in $S$. Let $w=v_1\cdots v_n$ and $f=f_0w$; $f\ne0$ as $R$ is a domain. With $a_{ik}=w\,b_{ik}\in R[Y]$ we get $fc_{ik}=\iota(a_{ik})$ and $\varphi(f)x_i^{m_i}+\sum_{k=1}^{m_i}\alpha(a_{ik})x_i^{m_i-k}=\varphi(w)F_i=0$ in $S$ for every $i$.

(The subalgebra $S'$ and its finiteness.) Write $f$ also for $\varphi(f)\in S$. Put $x_i'=fx_i$ and let $S'\subseteq S$ be the $R$-subalgebra generated by $y_1,\ldots,y_d,x_1',\ldots,x_n'$; it contains $R[y_1,\ldots,y_d]=\alpha(R[Y])$, which gives the inclusion in (1), and it is generated as an $R[y]$-algebra by $x_1',\ldots,x_n'$. Let $Q_i(T)=T^{m_i}+\sum_{k=1}^{m_i}f^{k-1}\alpha(a_{ik})T^{m_i-k}$, a monic polynomial with coefficients in $R[y]$. Then $Q_i(x_i')=f^{m_i}x_i^{m_i}+\sum_kf^{k-1}\alpha(a_{ik})f^{m_i-k}x_i^{m_i-k}=f^{m_i-1}\bigl(fx_i^{m_i}+\sum_k\alpha(a_{ik})x_i^{m_i-k}\bigr)=0$ by Step 5 ($m_i\ge1$), so each $x_i'$ is integral over $R[y]$. An algebra generated by finitely many integral elements is a finitely generated module, so $S'$ is a finitely generated $R[y_1,\ldots,y_d]$-module: this is (2). (Here $Q_i(T)=f^{m_i}P_i(T/f)$.)

($S'_f\to S_f$ is bijective.) The map $S'_f\to S_f$, $s/f^e\mapsto s/f^e$, is well defined and injective: for $s,t\in S'$, $s/f^e=t/f^{e'}$ in $S_f$ means $f^u(f^{e'}s-f^et)=0$ in $S$ for some $u\ge0$, and this is the same equation in the subring $S'$, which says $s/f^e=t/f^{e'}$ in $S'_f$ (the definition of $T_f$ in the statement). It is surjective: let $s\in S$ and $e\ge0$. By the choice of the $x_i$ (Step 1), $s=\sum_\beta r_\beta x^\beta$ ($r_\beta\in R$, $\beta\in\mathbf Z_{\ge0}^n$, finitely many) with all $|\beta|\le N$; then $f^Ns=\sum_\beta r_\beta f^{N-|\beta|}(x')^\beta\in S'$, and $s/f^e=(f^Ns)/f^{N+e}$ is the image of $(f^Ns)/f^{N+e}\in S'_f$. With $f\ne0$ from Step 5 this is (3).
