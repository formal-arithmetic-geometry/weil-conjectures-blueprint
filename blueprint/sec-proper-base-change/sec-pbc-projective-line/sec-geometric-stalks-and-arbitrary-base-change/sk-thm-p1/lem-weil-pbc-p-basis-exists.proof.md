($k^p$ and $p$-independent subsets.) Since $p=0$ in $k$, the map $F:k\to k$, $a\mapsto a^p$, is a ring homomorphism: it is multiplicative, and $(a+b)^p=a^p+b^p$ because the binomial coefficients $\binom pj$, $0<j<p$, are divisible by the prime $p$. So $k^p=F(k)$ is a subring of $k$, and a subfield since $(a^p)^{-1}=(a^{-1})^p$ for $a\ne0$. For $c$ in the prime field $\mathbf F_p\subseteq k$ we have $c^p=c$, so $\mathbf F_p\subseteq k^p$. For a subset $S\subseteq k$ let $\mathcal M(S)$ be the set of families $E=(e_s)_{s\in S}$ of integers $0\le e_s<p$, almost all $0$, and $s^E=\prod_{s\in S}s^{e_s}$. Call $S$ $p$-independent if the family $(s^E)_{E\in\mathcal M(S)}$ is linearly independent over $k^p$: whenever $\sum_{E}a_Es^E=0$ with $a_E\in k^p$, almost all $0$, all $a_E=0$. For $S'\subseteq S$, extension by zero identifies $\mathcal M(S')$ with the $E\in\mathcal M(S)$ vanishing outside $S'$, with the same monomials. The lemma asserts that some $S$ is $p$-independent and its monomials span $k$ over $k^p$ (then take $I=S$, $t_i=i$). ([def-weil-field]).

(A maximal $p$-independent subset, by Zorn's lemma.) Let $\mathcal P$ be the set of $p$-independent subsets of $k$. Every chain $\mathcal C\subseteq\mathcal P$ has an upper bound in $\mathcal P$. If $\mathcal C=\emptyset$, take $\emptyset\in\mathcal P$: $\mathcal M(\emptyset)=\{0\}$ and its only monomial $1\ne0$ is independent over the field $k^p$. If $\mathcal C\ne\emptyset$, let $U=\bigcup\mathcal C$, which contains every member of $\mathcal C$; we show $U\in\mathcal P$. Let $\sum_{E\in\mathcal F}a_Eu^E=0$ with $\mathcal F\subseteq\mathcal M(U)$ finite and $a_E\in k^p$. The union of the supports of the $E\in\mathcal F$ is a finite set $\{u_1,\dots,u_r\}\subseteq U$ with $u_j\in S_j\in\mathcal C$. As any two members of $\mathcal C$ are comparable for inclusion, by induction on $r$ one of $S_1,\dots,S_r$ (any member of $\mathcal C$ if $r=0$), call it $S_0$, contains all the others, hence all $u_j$. So every $E\in\mathcal F$ is the extension by zero of an element of $\mathcal M(S_0)$, distinct $E$ giving distinct elements, with the same monomial, and the relation is a $k^p$-linear relation among monomials of $S_0\in\mathcal P$; hence all $a_E=0$, and $U\in\mathcal P$. By Zorn's lemma (applied to $\mathcal P$ ordered by inclusion) there is a maximal $T\in\mathcal P$: $T$ is $p$-independent, and no $p$-independent subset of $k$ strictly contains $T$.

(The $k^p$-span $L$ of the monomials of $T$ is a subfield of $k$.) Let $L=\{\sum_{E\in\mathcal M(T)}a_Et^E: a_E\in k^p,\ \text{almost all }0\}$. It is a $k^p$-subspace of $k$ containing $k^p$ ($E=0$) and $T$ ($E$ the indicator of $t\in T$). For $E,E'\in\mathcal M(T)$, $t^Et^{E'}=\prod_{t\in T}t^{e_t+e'_t}$; for each $t$ with $e_t+e'_t\ge p$ write $t^{e_t+e'_t}=t^p\cdot t^{e_t+e'_t-p}$ with $t^p\in k^p$ and $0\le e_t+e'_t-p<p$. Hence $t^Et^{E'}=c\,t^{E''}$ with $c\in k^p$ and $E''\in\mathcal M(T)$, and by bilinearity of the product $L$ is closed under multiplication: $L$ is a subring of $k$. For a finite $G\subseteq T$ let $L_G\subseteq L$ be the $k^p$-span of the $t^E$ with $E$ vanishing outside $G$; the same computation (with $E''$ vanishing outside $G$) shows that $L_G$ is a subring, and it is spanned over $k^p$ by the at most $p^{|G|}$ monomials, so finite-dimensional. Let $0\ne y\in L$; $y$ lies in $L_G$ for $G$ the union of the supports of the finitely many $E$ occurring in $y$. Multiplication by $y$ is a $k^p$-linear map $L_G\to L_G$, injective since $k$ is a field, hence surjective (rank–nullity in the finite-dimensional $k^p$-space $L_G$), so $yz=1$ for some $z\in L_G\subseteq L$. Thus $L$ is a subfield of $k$ with $k^p\subseteq L$ and $T\subseteq L$.

(An element outside $L$ has degree $p$ over $L$.) Let $t\in k$ with $t\notin L$, and $c=t^p\in k^p\subseteq L$. Then $t$ is algebraic over $L$, a root of the nonzero polynomial $X^p-c\in L[X]$. Hence the subfield $L(t)$ is finite over $L$ and, with $e=[L(t):L]\ge1$, the elements $1,t,\dots,t^{e-1}$ form an $L$-basis of $L(t)$. Expressing $t^e$ in this basis gives a monic $m\in L[X]$ of degree $e$ with $m(t)=0$, and every $g\in L[X]$ with $g(t)=0$ is divisible by $m$ in $L[X]$ (write $g=qm+r$ with $\deg r<e$; $r(t)=0$ forces $r=0$ by the independence of $1,\dots,t^{e-1}$). In particular $m$ divides $X^p-c$, and $X^p-c=X^p-t^p=(X-t)^p$ in $k[X]$ ($p\mid\binom pj$ for $0<j<p$). So $m(Y+t)\,h(Y)=Y^p$ in $k[Y]$ for some $h\in k[Y]$. Let $a,b$ be the least degrees of a nonzero coefficient of $g(Y):=m(Y+t)$ and of $h$; since $k$ is a domain, the product of the lowest terms is the lowest term of $gh=Y^p$, so $a+b=p$, and likewise $\deg g+\deg h=p$. As $a\le\deg g$ and $b\le\deg h$, $a=\deg g=e$, so $g$ is a monic monomial, $g=Y^e$, and $m=(X-t)^e$; in particular $e\le p$. The coefficient of $X^{e-1}$ in $(X-t)^e$ is $-e\,t$, and it lies in $L$. If $e<p$, then $e\cdot1$ is a nonzero element of $\mathbf F_p\subseteq k^p\subseteq L$ (Step 1), hence invertible in $L$, and $t\in L$, a contradiction. So $e=p$: the elements $1,t,\dots,t^{p-1}$ are linearly independent over $L$.

(The maximal $T$ spans: $L=k$.) Suppose $t\in k$ with $t\notin L$. Then $t\notin T$ (as $T\subseteq L$), and $T'=T\cup\{t\}$ is $p$-independent: $\mathcal M(T')$ consists of the pairs $(E,j)$ with $E\in\mathcal M(T)$ and $0\le j<p$, with monomial $t^E t^j$. Given $\sum_{(E,j)}a_{E,j}t^Et^j=0$ with $a_{E,j}\in k^p$, almost all $0$, put $b_j=\sum_Ea_{E,j}t^E\in L$; then $\sum_{j<p}b_jt^j=0$, so all $b_j=0$ by Step 4, and then all $a_{E,j}=0$ because $T$ is $p$-independent (Step 2). So $T'\in\mathcal P$ strictly contains $T$, contradicting the maximality of $T$ (Step 2). Hence every $t\in k$ lies in $L$, i.e. $L=k$.

(Conclusion.) Let $I=T$ and $t_i=i$ for $i\in T$, so that the families $E$ of the statement are the elements of $\mathcal M(T)$. The monomials $t^E$, $E\in\mathcal M(T)$, are linearly independent over $k^p$ (Step 2: $T$ is $p$-independent) and span $L=k$ over $k^p$ (Steps 3 and 5). Hence they form a basis of $k$ over $k^p$: $(t_i)_{i\in I}$ is a $p$-basis of $k$.
