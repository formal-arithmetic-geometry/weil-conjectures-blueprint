Let $G$ be the set of $s\in B$ for which $\operatorname{Spec}B_s\to\operatorname{Spec}A$ is etale, and $J$ the ideal of $B$ generated by $G$. If a prime $\mathfrak P$ contained both $\mathfrak b$ and $J$, the hypothesis would give $s\in G$ with $s\notin\mathfrak P$, contradicting $G\subseteq J\subseteq\mathfrak P$. Hence no prime contains $\mathfrak b+J$, i.e. $\mathfrak b+J=B$.

Write $1=f+g$ with $f\in\mathfrak b$ and $g\in J$. Then $g$ maps to $1$ in $B/\mathfrak b$, in particular to a unit.

Set $T=B_g$. The image of $g$ in $T$ is a unit and lies in $JT$, so $JT=T$; that is, the images $s/1$ of the elements $s\in G$ generate the unit ideal of $T$, so the principal opens $D(s/1)$, $s\in G$, cover $\operatorname{Spec}T$. This also holds when $T$ is the zero ring.

For $s\in G$ put $C=T_{s/1}=B_{gs}$; it is the localization of $B_s$ at the image of $g$. Hence $\operatorname{Spec}C\to\operatorname{Spec}B_s$ is the inclusion of a principal open, an open immersion and so etale, and composing with the etale morphism $\operatorname{Spec}B_s\to\operatorname{Spec}A$ shows that $\operatorname{Spec}C\to\operatorname{Spec}A$ is etale.

Being etale is local on the source: since the opens $D(s/1)=\operatorname{Spec}T_{s/1}$, $s\in G$, cover $\operatorname{Spec}T$ and each $\operatorname{Spec}T_{s/1}\to\operatorname{Spec}A$ is etale, $\operatorname{Spec}B_g=\operatorname{Spec}T\to\operatorname{Spec}A$ is etale. Together with the second step, $g$ has the required properties.
