**Step 1.** *Two elementary facts used throughout.* (a) If $X=\operatorname{Spec}B$ with $B$ local and $\varphi:X\to Z$ is a morphism of schemes whose closed point maps into an open $Z'\subset Z$, then $\varphi$ factors (uniquely, $Z'\to Z$ being a monomorphism) through $Z'$: every prime of $B$ lies in the maximal ideal, so every point of $X$ generalizes the closed point, its image generalizes a point of $Z'$, and open subsets are stable under generization. (b) For an affine $U=\operatorname{Spec}A$, morphisms $\operatorname{Spec}B\to U$ are the same as ring maps $A\to B$ [def-weil-affine-spectrum]; for a field $K$ a morphism $\operatorname{Spec}K\to\operatorname{Spec}B$ is a ring map $B\to K$, whose kernel is the prime that is the image point, and which factors as $B\to\kappa(\mathfrak q)\to K$ with $\kappa(\mathfrak q)\to K$ the field map of [def-weil-geometric-point].

**Step 2.** *$\kappa(\bar x)$ is separably closed (last part of (1)).* $\Omega$ is algebraically closed, hence separably closed, so by [def-weil-pbc-relative-separable-closure] the separable algebraic closure $\kappa(\bar x)$ of $\kappa(y)$ in $\Omega$ is separably closed [def-pc-separably-closed] and separable algebraic over $\kappa(y)$: it is a separable closure of $\kappa(y)$.

**Step 3.** *$\mathcal O^{\mathrm{sh}}_{Y,\bar x}$ is strictly henselian, and its residue map (first part of (1)).* Choose an affine open $U=\operatorname{Spec}A\subset Y$ containing $y$, with prime $\mathfrak p$ of $y$. By [lem-weil-pbc-etale-local-ring-triples] the affine pointed etale neighbourhoods of $\bar x$ over $U$ are initial, and $\mathcal O^{\mathrm{sh}}_{Y,\bar x}=\varinjlim_{(B,\mathfrak q,\phi)}B$ over the triples with $A\to B$ etale, $\mathfrak q$ over $\mathfrak p$, $\phi:\kappa(\mathfrak q)\to\kappa(\bar x)$ a $\kappa(\mathfrak p)$-map, the triple of $(V=\operatorname{Spec}B,\bar v)$ being $(B,\mathfrak q_v,\phi_v)$ with $\phi_v$ the field map of $\bar v$. Apply [lem-weil-pbc-strict-henselization-triples] with $R=A$, $K=\kappa(\bar x)$ (a separable closure of $\kappa(\mathfrak p)=\kappa(y)$ by the previous step): the colimit $C=\mathcal O^{\mathrm{sh}}_{Y,\bar x}$ is a strictly henselian local ring [def-weil-pbc-henselian-terminology], the colimit $\psi:C\to\kappa(\bar x)$ of the maps $B\to\kappa(\mathfrak q)\xrightarrow{\phi}\kappa(\bar x)$ is surjective with kernel the maximal ideal $\mathfrak m_C$, so it induces an isomorphism of $\kappa(y)$-algebras $\bar\psi:C/\mathfrak m_C\to\kappa(\bar x)$, and $\mathfrak m_C\cap A=\mathfrak p$.

**Step 4.** *The factorization of the neighbourhoods and of $\bar x$ (rest of (1)).* Let $\lambda:\operatorname{Spec}\Omega\to Y_{(\bar x)}$ be the morphism given by the ring map $C\xrightarrow{\psi}\kappa(\bar x)\subset\Omega$, i.e. $\operatorname{Spec}\Omega\to\operatorname{Spec}\kappa(\bar x)\to Y_{(\bar x)}$ with the second map the closed point (the kernel of $\psi$ is $\mathfrak m_C$) and residue identification $\bar\psi$. For $(V=\operatorname{Spec}B,\bar v)$ over $U$, $c_V\circ\lambda$ corresponds to $B\to C\to\Omega$, which by construction of $\psi$ is $B\to\kappa(\mathfrak q_v)\xrightarrow{\phi_v}\Omega$, the ring map of $\bar v$; so $c_V\circ\lambda=\bar v$. For an arbitrary affine $(V,\bar v)$, choose by initiality a morphism $(V',\bar v')\to(V,\bar v)$ from a neighbourhood over $U$; then $c_V=(V'\to V)\circ c_{V'}$ (canonical maps of the limit [def-weil-pbc-strict-henselization-limit]) and $c_V\circ\lambda=(V'\to V)\circ\bar v'=\bar v$. Taking $V=U$ (an open immersion is etale, so $(U,\bar x)$ is an affine pointed etale neighbourhood) gives $j_Y\circ\lambda=\bar x$, hence $j_Y$ maps the closed point (the image of $\lambda$) to $y$. Uniqueness of the residue isomorphism: if $\sigma:C/\mathfrak m_C\to\kappa(\bar x)$ is a $\kappa(y)$-isomorphism with the stated factorizations, the morphism $\lambda_\sigma:\operatorname{Spec}\Omega\to Y_{(\bar x)}$ it defines satisfies $c_V\circ\lambda_\sigma=\bar v=c_V\circ\lambda$ for all $V$; since $C=\varinjlim A_V$, the two ring maps $C\to\Omega$ agree on the image of every $A_V$, hence are equal, so $\sigma=\bar\psi$ (both are $C/\mathfrak m_C\to\Omega$ followed by the injective $\kappa(\bar x)\subset\Omega$).

**Step 5.** *Clause (2).* The point $\bar s=g\circ\bar t$ corresponds [def-weil-geometric-point] to $s$ and the field map $\kappa(s)\to\Omega$ which is the residue map of $g\circ\bar t$ at the point of $\operatorname{Spec}\Omega$; residue maps of a composite are the composites of the residue maps (stalk maps of morphisms of locally ringed spaces compose [def-weil-locally-ringed-space-morphism], and the residue maps are induced by them [def-weil-residue-field]), so it is $\kappa(s)\to\kappa(t)\to\Omega$ with $\kappa(s)\to\kappa(t)$ induced by $g$. Hence the image of $\kappa(s)$ in $\Omega$ lies in that of $\kappa(t)$. An element of $\Omega$ that is a root of a separable polynomial with coefficients in (the image of) $\kappa(s)$ is a root of the same separable polynomial regarded over $\kappa(t)$; so $\kappa(\bar s)\subset\kappa(\bar t)$ [def-weil-pbc-relative-separable-closure], and this inclusion is a $\kappa(s)$-algebra map.

**Step 6.** *Setting up clause (3).* Write $A^{\mathrm{sh}}=\mathcal O^{\mathrm{sh}}_{S,\bar s}$, $B^{\mathrm{sh}}=\mathcal O^{\mathrm{sh}}_{T,\bar t}$, with maximal ideals $\mathfrak m_A,\mathfrak m_B$ and the maps $\lambda_S:\operatorname{Spec}\Omega\to S_{(\bar s)}$, $\lambda_T:\operatorname{Spec}\Omega\to T_{(\bar t)}$ of the factorizations in (1). Choose an affine open $U_S=\operatorname{Spec}A\subset S$ containing $s$, with prime $\mathfrak p$ of $s$. By (1) $g\circ j_T$ maps the closed point of $T_{(\bar t)}$ to $g(t)=s\in U_S$, so by step 1(a) it factors through $U_S$ and is given (step 1(b)) by a ring map $\alpha:A\to B^{\mathrm{sh}}$; this makes $B^{\mathrm{sh}}$ an $A$-algebra. Likewise $j_S$ factors through $U_S$ as $c_{U_S}$, given by the colimit map $A\to A^{\mathrm{sh}}$. The composite $A\xrightarrow{\alpha}B^{\mathrm{sh}}\to B^{\mathrm{sh}}/\mathfrak m_B\cong\kappa(\bar t)\subset\Omega$ is the ring map of $g\circ j_T\circ\lambda_T=g\circ\bar t=\bar s$ on $U_S$, whose kernel is $\mathfrak p$ (step 1(b)); so $\alpha^{-1}(\mathfrak m_B)=\mathfrak p$, and the induced $\kappa(\mathfrak p)=\kappa(s)\to\kappa(\bar t)$ is the embedding of $\kappa(s)$ by $\bar s$. By steps 3 and 4 and [lem-weil-pbc-etale-local-ring-triples], $A^{\mathrm{sh}}$ is a filtered colimit of etale $A$-algebras, with maximal ideal $\mathfrak m_A$ lying over $\mathfrak p$; and $B^{\mathrm{sh}}$ is strictly henselian, in particular henselian [def-weil-pbc-henselian-terminology]. Under the identifications of (1), the inclusion $\tau:\kappa(\bar s)\subset\kappa(\bar t)$ of (2) is a homomorphism of $\kappa(\mathfrak p)$-algebras $A^{\mathrm{sh}}/\mathfrak m_A\to B^{\mathrm{sh}}/\mathfrak m_B$.

**Step 7.** *Clause (3), existence.* By [lem-weil-pbc-map-into-henselian-colimit] (applied to $A\xrightarrow{\alpha}B^{\mathrm{sh}}$, the $A$-algebra $A^{\mathrm{sh}}$, its prime $\mathfrak m_A$ over $\mathfrak p=\alpha^{-1}(\mathfrak m_B)$, and $\tau$) there is an $A$-algebra map $h:A^{\mathrm{sh}}\to B^{\mathrm{sh}}$ with $h^{-1}(\mathfrak m_B)=\mathfrak m_A$, i.e. $h$ is local [def-weil-locally-ringed-space-morphism], inducing $\tau$ on residue fields. Since $h$ is an $A$-algebra map, $c_{U_S}\circ\operatorname{Spec}h$ and the factorization of $g\circ j_T$ through $U_S$ are given by the same ring map $\alpha:A\to B^{\mathrm{sh}}$ (step 1(b)), hence equal; composing with $U_S\subset S$ gives $j_S\circ\operatorname{Spec}h=g\circ j_T$.

**Step 8.** *Clause (3), uniqueness.* Let $h':A^{\mathrm{sh}}\to B^{\mathrm{sh}}$ be local with $j_S\circ\operatorname{Spec}h'=g\circ j_T$ inducing $\tau$. The morphism $c_{U_S}\circ\operatorname{Spec}h'$ composed with $U_S\subset S$ is $g\circ j_T$; as $U_S\to S$ is a monomorphism, $c_{U_S}\circ\operatorname{Spec}h'$ is the factorization of $g\circ j_T$ through $U_S$, so $A\to A^{\mathrm{sh}}\xrightarrow{h'}B^{\mathrm{sh}}$ is $\alpha$ (step 1(b)): $h'$ is an $A$-algebra map. Being local, $h'^{-1}(\mathfrak m_B)=\mathfrak m_A$, and it induces $\tau$. By the uniqueness in [lem-weil-pbc-map-into-henselian-colimit], $h'=h$.

**Step 9.** *Clause (4).* Let $v,w$ be the images of $\bar v,\bar w$; $w(w)=v$ as $w\circ\bar w=\bar v$. The point $v$ lies over $s\in U_S$, so choose an affine open $V''=\operatorname{Spec}D\subset V$ containing $v$ and mapping into $U_S$; $V''\to U_S$ is etale (an open immersion followed by the etale $V\to S$ [def-weil-etale-morphism]), so $A\to D$ is an etale $A$-algebra. Both morphisms $\varphi_1=w\circ c_W$ and $\varphi_2=c_V\circ\operatorname{Spec}h$ map the closed point of $T_{(\bar t)}$ to $v$: by (1) $c_W$ maps it to $w$, and $\operatorname{Spec}h$ ($h$ local) maps it to the closed point of $S_{(\bar s)}$, which $c_V$ maps to $v$. By step 1(a) both factor through $V''$, given by ring maps $f_1,f_2:D\to B^{\mathrm{sh}}$. (i) Both are $A$-algebra maps for $\alpha$: composed with $V\to S$, $\varphi_1$ is $(V\to S)\circ w\circ c_W=g\circ(W\to T)\circ c_W=g\circ j_T$, and $\varphi_2$ is $j_S\circ\operatorname{Spec}h=g\circ j_T$ by (3); both land in $U_S$, so step 1(b) gives $f_1|_A=f_2|_A=\alpha$. (ii) Both composites $D\xrightarrow{f_i}B^{\mathrm{sh}}\to\kappa(\bar t)\subset\Omega$ equal the ring map of $\bar v$ (which factors through $V''$): for $f_1$, $\varphi_1\circ\lambda_T=w\circ c_W\circ\lambda_T=w\circ\bar w=\bar v$ by (1) for $T$; for $f_2$, since $h$ induces $\tau$ on residue fields, $\operatorname{Spec}h\circ\lambda_T=\lambda_S$, so $\varphi_2\circ\lambda_T=c_V\circ\lambda_S=\bar v$ by (1) for $S$. Hence $f_1^{-1}(\mathfrak m_B)=f_2^{-1}(\mathfrak m_B)$ is the prime $\mathfrak q_v$ of $v$ (the kernel of the ring map of $\bar v$) and $f_1,f_2$ induce the same map $\kappa(\mathfrak q_v)\to B^{\mathrm{sh}}/\mathfrak m_B$ (both followed by the injective $B^{\mathrm{sh}}/\mathfrak m_B\cong\kappa(\bar t)\subset\Omega$ give the field map of $\bar v$). The uniqueness in [lem-weil-pbc-map-into-henselian-colimit] for the single etale $A$-algebra $D$ and the henselian $B^{\mathrm{sh}}$ gives $f_1=f_2$, so $\varphi_1=\varphi_2$.
