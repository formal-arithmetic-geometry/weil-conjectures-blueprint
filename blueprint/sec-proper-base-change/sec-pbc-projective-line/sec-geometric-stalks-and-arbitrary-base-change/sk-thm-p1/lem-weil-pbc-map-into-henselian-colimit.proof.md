*Step 1: a single etale algebra, lifting into a residue field.* Let $A$ be a single etale $R$-algebra, and let $K$ be any field with an $S$-algebra structure whose structure map $S\to K$ is surjective with kernel $\mathfrak m_S$; let $\chi:A\to K$ be an $R$-algebra map. Put $B=S\otimes_RA$ and let $\phi:B\to K$ be the $S$-algebra map $s\otimes a\mapsto s\chi(a)$ corresponding to $\chi$. As a base change of an etale algebra, $B$ is etale over $S$ [def-weil-pbc-henselian-terminology].

Put $\mathfrak q'=\ker\phi$. It is prime since $K$ is a field, and its preimage in $S$ is $\mathfrak m_S$ since $\phi$ is $S$-linear. The induced embeddings of residue fields $\eta:\kappa(\mathfrak m_S)\to K$ and $\psi:\kappa(\mathfrak q')\to K$ satisfy $\psi\circ\theta=\eta$, where $\theta:\kappa(\mathfrak m_S)\to\kappa(\mathfrak q')$ is the canonical map. Here $\eta$ is surjective because $S\to K$ is, and $\psi$ is injective because its domain is a field. Given $y\in\kappa(\mathfrak q')$, choose $x$ with $\eta(x)=\psi(y)$; injectivity of $\psi$ gives $\theta(x)=y$. Thus $\theta$ is bijective.

Since $\operatorname{Spec}B\to\operatorname{Spec}S$ is etale and $\mathfrak q'$ lies over $\mathfrak m_S$ with trivial residue field extension, the henselian etale retraction theorem [lem-weil-pbc-henselian-etale-retraction] gives a unique $S$-algebra map $\sigma:B\to S$ with $\sigma^{-1}(\mathfrak m_S)=\mathfrak q'$.

The reduction of $\sigma$ is $\phi$: for $b\in B$ choose $s\in S$ whose image in $K$ is $\phi(b)$; then $b-s\cdot1\in\mathfrak q'$, so $\sigma(b)-s\in\mathfrak m_S$, and $\sigma(b)$ and $s$ have the same image in $K$. Restricting $\sigma$ to $A\to B$, $a\mapsto1\otimes a$, gives an $R$-algebra map $A\to S$ lifting $\chi$. Conversely, an $R$-algebra map $f:A\to S$ lifting $\chi$ extends to the $S$-algebra map $B\to S$, $s\otimes a\mapsto sf(a)$, whose reduction agrees with $\phi$ on pure tensors and hence on $B$; so the preimage of $\mathfrak m_S$ under it is $\mathfrak q'$, and by uniqueness of $\sigma$ it equals $\sigma$. Since $f$ is recovered from its extension by restriction, $f$ is unique. Thus every $R$-algebra map $\chi:A\to K$ has exactly one lift to an $R$-algebra map $A\to S$.

In particular we may take $K=\kappa(\mathfrak m_S)=S/\mathfrak m_S$ with the residue map, which is surjective with kernel $\mathfrak m_S$ since $S$ is local [def-weil-pbc-henselian-terminology]: for $A$ etale over $R$, every $R$-algebra map $\chi:A\to\kappa(\mathfrak m_S)$ is the reduction of exactly one $R$-algebra map $A\to S$.

*Step 2: reformulating the conditions.* Let $\chi:A\to\kappa(\mathfrak m_S)$ be the composite $A\to A/\mathfrak q\to\kappa(\mathfrak q)\xrightarrow{\tau}S/\mathfrak m_S$; it is an $R$-algebra map because $\tau$ is compatible with the images of $R$ in $\kappa(\mathfrak p)$. For an $R$-algebra map $f:A\to S$, the conditions $f^{-1}(\mathfrak m_S)=\mathfrak q$ and "$f$ induces $\tau$ on residue fields" together are equivalent to the single condition that the reduction of $f$ is $\chi$. Indeed, if the reduction of $f$ is $\chi$, then $f^{-1}(\mathfrak m_S)=\ker\chi=\mathfrak q$ because $\tau$, a map of fields, is injective, and the induced map $\kappa(\mathfrak q)\to S/\mathfrak m_S$ agrees with $\tau$ on the image of $A$, which generates $\kappa(\mathfrak q)$ as a field, so equals $\tau$. The converse is immediate.

*Step 3: the filtered colimit.* Write $A=\varinjlim_iA_i$ with coprojections $\iota_i:A_i\to A$. By Step 1, for every $i$ there is a unique $R$-algebra map $f_i:A_i\to S$ whose reduction is $\chi\circ\iota_i$. For a transition map $t:A_i\to A_j$, $f_j\circ t$ has reduction $\chi\circ\iota_j\circ t=\chi\circ\iota_i$, so $f_j\circ t=f_i$ by uniqueness in Step 1. Thus $(f_i)_i$ is a compatible family.

A filtered colimit of $R$-algebras has as underlying set the filtered colimit of the underlying sets: every element of $A$ is $\iota_i(a)$ for some $i$ and $a\in A_i$, and two such elements agree iff their images agree at a common later stage. Hence the compatible family $(f_i)$ descends to a map $f:A\to S$ with $f\circ\iota_i=f_i$. It is an $R$-algebra map: two elements of $A$ can be represented at a common stage, where sums and products are preserved by $f_i$, and $1$ and the images of $R$ are handled at a single stage.

*Step 4: conclusion.* Since every element of $A$ comes from some stage and each $f_i$ has reduction $\chi\circ\iota_i$, the reduction of $f$ is $\chi$; by Step 2, $f$ satisfies $f^{-1}(\mathfrak m_S)=\mathfrak q$ and induces $\tau$. If $g:A\to S$ is another $R$-algebra map with these properties, then by Step 2 its reduction is $\chi$, so $g\circ\iota_i$ has reduction $\chi\circ\iota_i$ and equals $f_i$ by uniqueness in Step 1. As every element of $A$ comes from a stage, $g=f$.
