*$\mathfrak p$ is maximal.* The closed subsets of $\operatorname{Spec}B$ in the Zariski topology are the sets $V(I)=\{\mathfrak q:\ I\subseteq\mathfrak q\}$ for ideals $I\subseteq B$. Since $\{\mathfrak p\}$ is open, its complement is $V(I)$ for some ideal $I$, so $\{\mathfrak p\}=\{\mathfrak q\in\operatorname{Spec}B:\ I\not\subseteq\mathfrak q\}$. In particular $I\not\subseteq\mathfrak p$; choose $f\in I$ with $f\notin\mathfrak p$. A finite type algebra over a field is a Jacobson ring, so every prime of $B$ is the intersection of the maximal ideals containing it; hence there is a maximal ideal $\mathfrak m\supseteq\mathfrak p$ with $f\notin\mathfrak m$. Then $I\not\subseteq\mathfrak m$, so $\mathfrak m\notin V(I)$, i.e. $\mathfrak m\in\{\mathfrak p\}$, hence $\mathfrak p=\mathfrak m$ is maximal. (This is the argument of Lemma 00G3, that the closed points of $V(J)$ are dense in it, applied to $Z=\operatorname{Spec}B$ and the open set $\{\mathfrak p\}$.)

*$\kappa(\mathfrak p)$ is a finite type $k$-algebra.* Write $\mathfrak m=\mathfrak p$, now maximal, so $B/\mathfrak m$ is a field. The composite $B\to B_{\mathfrak m}\to\kappa(\mathfrak m)=B_{\mathfrak m}/\mathfrak mB_{\mathfrak m}$ is surjective: for $b\in B$ and $s\in B\setminus\mathfrak m$ choose $t\in B$ with $st-1\in\mathfrak m$ (the class of $s$ is invertible in the field $B/\mathfrak m$); then $b/s-bt/1=b(1-st)/s\in\mathfrak mB_{\mathfrak m}$, so $b/s$ and the image of $bt$ have the same class in $\kappa(\mathfrak m)$. If $b_1,\dots,b_n$ generate $B$ as a $k$-algebra, their images generate $\kappa(\mathfrak m)$ as a $k$-algebra (a $k$-algebra surjection carries polynomials in the $b_i$ to the same polynomials in their images). So $\kappa(\mathfrak m)$ is a field which is a finite type $k$-algebra via $k\to B\to\kappa(\mathfrak m)$.

*$\kappa(\mathfrak p)$ is finite over $k$.* By step 2, $K=\kappa(\mathfrak p)$ with $k\to B\to K$ is a field that is a finitely generated $k$-algebra; by Zariski's lemma (the Nullstellensatz) $\dim_kK<\infty$, i.e. $K/k$ is a finite field extension, with exactly the $k$-structure of the statement.
