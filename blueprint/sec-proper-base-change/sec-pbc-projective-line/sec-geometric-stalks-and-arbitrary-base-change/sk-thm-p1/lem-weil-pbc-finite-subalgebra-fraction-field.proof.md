($A$ is a domain; the field $K$.) The field $k$ is an integral domain, so $k[[x_1,\ldots,x_n]]$ is an integral domain (with $d=n$). The ring $A$ is the polynomial ring in the finitely many variables $y_1,\ldots,y_m$ over this domain (the iterated one-variable polynomial ring is the ring of finite linear combinations of monomials in $y_1,\ldots,y_m$), hence an integral domain. So $(0)$ is a prime of $A$ and $K=A_{(0)}$ is the ring of fractions $a/s$, $a\in A$, $s\in A\setminus\{0\}$, with $a/s=a'/s'$ iff $as'=a's$ (the factor $u\neq0$ cancels in the domain $A$); it is a field ($a/s\neq0$ means $a\neq0$, and then $s/a$ is its inverse) and $A\to K$, $a\mapsto a/1$, is injective. The field homomorphism $K\to M$ is injective; we regard $A\subseteq K\subseteq M$. From here on only the facts that $A$ is a domain and $K$ is its fraction field are used.

(Monic equations over $K$.) $M$ is a finitely generated $K$-module: choose $e_1,\ldots,e_r\in M$ with $M=Ke_1+\dots+Ke_r$. Since $M$ is a finite, hence integral, $K$-algebra, each $e_i$ is a root of a monic polynomial with coefficients in $K$: $e_i^{d_i}+c_{i1}e_i^{d_i-1}+\dots+c_{id_i}=0$ with $d_i\ge1$ and $c_{ij}\in K$.

(Clearing denominators.) Write $c_{ij}=a_{ij}/s_{ij}$ with $a_{ij}\in A$, $s_{ij}\in A\setminus\{0\}$ (Step 1), and let $\delta=\prod_{i,j}s_{ij}\in A$; $\delta\neq0$ since $A$ is a domain, and $\delta c_{ij}=a_{ij}\prod_{(i',j')\neq(i,j)}s_{i'j'}\in A$. Put $f_i=\delta e_i\in M$ and $d=d_i$. Then $f_i^{d}+(\delta c_{i1})f_i^{d-1}+\delta(\delta c_{i2})f_i^{d-2}+\dots+\delta^{d-1}(\delta c_{id})=\delta^{d}\bigl(e_i^{d}+c_{i1}e_i^{d-1}+\dots+c_{id}\bigr)=0$, since the coefficient of $f_i^{d-j}$ is $\delta^{j-1}(\delta c_{ij})=\delta^jc_{ij}$ and $\delta^jc_{ij}f_i^{d-j}=\delta^dc_{ij}e_i^{d-j}$. The coefficients $\delta^{j-1}(\delta c_{ij})$ lie in $A$, so each $f_i$ is a root of a monic polynomial with coefficients in $A$, i.e. integral over $A$.

(The ring $B$.) Let $B=A[f_1,\ldots,f_r]\subseteq M$ be the set of all values $P(f_1,\ldots,f_r)$ of polynomials $P$ in $r$ variables with coefficients in $A$ (the image of the evaluation map from the polynomial ring over $A$ in $r$ variables to $M$); it is a subring of $M$ containing $A$, a domain as a subring of a field, generated as an $A$-algebra by $f_1,\ldots,f_r$, each integral over $A$ (Step 3). An algebra generated by finitely many integral elements is a finite module, so $B$ is a finitely generated $A$-module.

(Fraction field.) Let $u\in M$. By Step 2, $u=\sum_{i=1}^rk_ie_i$ with $k_i\in K$; write $k_i=a_i/t_i$ with $a_i\in A$, $t_i\in A\setminus\{0\}$ (Step 1). Put $s=\delta t_1\cdots t_r\in A\setminus\{0\}\subseteq B$ ($A$ a domain), nonzero in $M$ since $A\to M$ is injective (Step 1). Then $su=\sum_ia_i\bigl(\prod_{l\neq i}t_l\bigr)\delta e_i=\sum_ia_i\bigl(\prod_{l\neq i}t_l\bigr)f_i=:b\in B$, so $u=b/s$ with $b,s\in B$, $s\neq0$. Hence $M$ is the fraction field of $B$, and $B$ has all the properties claimed.
