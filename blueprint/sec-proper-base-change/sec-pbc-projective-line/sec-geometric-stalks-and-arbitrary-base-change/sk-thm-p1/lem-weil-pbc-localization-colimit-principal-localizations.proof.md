*(1) The preorder is directed and filtered.* For $f\in S$, $f=f\cdot1$, so $f\le f$; if $g=fh$ and $k=gh'$ then $k=f(hh')$, so $\le$ is a preorder. For $f,f'\in S$, $ff'\in S$ ($S$ is multiplicative) and $ff'=f\cdot f'=f'\cdot f$, so $f\le ff'$ and $f'\le ff'$. Regard $(S,\le)$ as a category $J$ with exactly one arrow $f\to g$ when $f\le g$ and none otherwise; it is small ($S$ is a set). It is filtered: it is nonempty ($1\in S$), any two objects $f,f'$ map to the common object $ff'$, and two parallel arrows $f\to g$ are equal, hence equalized by the identity of $g$.

*(2) The transition maps.* Let $f\le g$ in $S$ and $h\in P$ with $g=fh$. In $P_g$ one has $(f/1)(h/g)=fh/g=g/g=1$ (as $g\cdot1-1\cdot g=0$), so the image $f/1$ of $f$ under the localization map $P\to P_g$, $a\mapsto a/1$, is a unit with inverse $h/g$; since the inverse of a unit is unique, $h/g=h'/g$ in $P_g$ for every $h'$ with $g=fh'$. By the universal property of the localization $P_f$ (a ring map out of $P$ sending $f$ to a unit factors uniquely through $P\to P_f$), applied to the ring map $P\to P_g$, $a\mapsto a/1$, there is a unique ring homomorphism $\rho_{fg}:P_f\to P_g$ with $\rho_{fg}(a/1)=a/1$ for all $a\in P$, and it is $\rho_{fg}(a/f^e)=(a/1)(f/1)^{-e}=(a/1)(h/g)^e=ah^e/g^e$. So the formula of the statement defines a well defined ring homomorphism, and it does not depend on the choice of $h$: $\rho_{fg}$ is characterized by $\rho_{fg}(a/1)=a/1$, in which $h$ does not occur (equivalently $ah^e/g^e=(a/1)(h/g)^e=(a/1)(h'/g)^e=ah'^e/g^e$).

*(2) Functoriality.* $\mathrm{id}_{P_f}$ is a ring homomorphism with $a/1\mapsto a/1$, so by the uniqueness in the universal property of $P_f$ (two ring homomorphisms out of $P_f$ that agree on all $a/1$ are equal) $\rho_{ff}=\mathrm{id}$. For $f\le g\le k$, $\rho_{gk}\circ\rho_{fg}$ and $\rho_{fk}$ are ring homomorphisms $P_f\to P_k$ that both send $a/1$ to $a/1$ (step 2), so they are equal by the same uniqueness. Hence $f\mapsto P_f$, $(f\le g)\mapsto\rho_{fg}$ is a functor $J\to$ commutative rings.

*(3) The cocone $\kappa$, and the last clause.* By the construction of the localization by fractions, $S^{-1}P$ is a commutative ring with $(a/s)+(b/t)=(ta+sb)/st$, $(a/s)(b/t)=ab/st$, unit $1/1$, and the localization map $\lambda:P\to S^{-1}P$, $a\mapsto a/1$, is a ring homomorphism; for $s\in S$, $(s/1)(1/s)=s/s=1/1$, so $\lambda(s)$ is a unit with inverse $1/s$. In particular for $f\in S$, $\lambda(f)$ is a unit, and the universal property of $P_f$ gives a unique ring homomorphism $\kappa_f:P_f\to S^{-1}P$ with $\kappa_f(a/1)=a/1$, namely $\kappa_f(a/f^e)=(a/1)(1/f)^e=a/f^e$. The property $\kappa_f(a/1)=a/1$ is the last clause: $\kappa_f$ composed with $P\to P_f$ is $\lambda$. For $f\le g$, $\kappa_g\circ\rho_{fg}$ and $\kappa_f$ are ring homomorphisms $P_f\to S^{-1}P$ both sending $a/1$ to $a/1$ (step 2), so they are equal by the uniqueness in the universal property of $P_f$. Thus $(S^{-1}P,(\kappa_f)_{f\in S})$ is a cocone on the functor of step 3.

*Every element comes from some $P_s$, and two elements with the same image agree at a common later stage.* (i) Every $x\in S^{-1}P$ is $a/s$ with $a\in P$, $s\in S$, so $x=\kappa_s(a/s)$ (the element $a/s^1$ of $P_s$; step 4). (ii) Let $s,s'\in S$, $y=a/s^e\in P_s$, $y'=a'/s'^{e'}\in P_{s'}$ with $\kappa_s(y)=\kappa_{s'}(y')$, i.e. $a/s^e=a'/s'^{e'}$ in $S^{-1}P$; by the equality criterion for fractions there is $u\in S$ with $u(as'^{e'}-a's^e)=0$. Put $t=ss'u\in S$; then $s\le t$ ($t=s\cdot s'u$) and $s'\le t$ ($t=s'\cdot su$), and by step 2 $\rho_{st}(y)=a(s'u)^e/t^e$, $\rho_{s't}(y')=a'(su)^{e'}/t^{e'}$. These are equal in $P_t$: $a(s'u)^et^{e'}-a'(su)^{e'}t^e=a\,s'^eu^e\,s^{e'}s'^{e'}u^{e'}-a'\,s^{e'}u^{e'}\,s^es'^eu^e=s^{e'}s'^eu^{e+e'}(as'^{e'}-a's^e)$, and multiplying by $t=ss'u$ gives $ss'^{1+e}s^{e'}u^{e+e'}\cdot u(as'^{e'}-a's^e)=0$, which is the criterion $t^1(\cdots)=0$.

*(3) Existence of the induced map.* Let $(T,(\varphi_f)_{f\in S})$ be a cocone: ring homomorphisms $\varphi_f:P_f\to T$ with $\varphi_g\circ\rho_{fg}=\varphi_f$ for $f\le g$. For $x\in S^{-1}P$ choose $s\in S$, $y\in P_s$ with $\kappa_s(y)=x$ (step 5 (i)) and put $\varphi(x)=\varphi_s(y)$. This does not depend on the choice: if also $\kappa_{s'}(y')=x$, take $t$ as in step 5 (ii); then $\varphi_s(y)=\varphi_t(\rho_{st}(y))=\varphi_t(\rho_{s't}(y'))=\varphi_{s'}(y')$. By construction $\varphi\circ\kappa_f=\varphi_f$ for every $f\in S$. $\varphi$ is a ring homomorphism: given $x=\kappa_s(y)$, $x'=\kappa_{s'}(y')$, let $t=ss'$ (so $s,s'\le t$, step 1); by step 4, $x=\kappa_t(\rho_{st}(y))$ and $x'=\kappa_t(\rho_{s't}(y'))$, so, $\kappa_t$ being a ring homomorphism, $x+x'=\kappa_t(\rho_{st}(y)+\rho_{s't}(y'))$ and $xx'=\kappa_t(\rho_{st}(y)\rho_{s't}(y'))$; hence $\varphi(x+x')=\varphi_t(\rho_{st}(y))+\varphi_t(\rho_{s't}(y'))=\varphi(x)+\varphi(x')$ and likewise $\varphi(xx')=\varphi(x)\varphi(x')$, and $\varphi(1)=\varphi(\kappa_1(1))=\varphi_1(1)=1$ ($1\in S$).

*(3) Uniqueness, and conclusion.* If $\psi:S^{-1}P\to T$ is any ring homomorphism with $\psi\circ\kappa_f=\varphi_f$ for all $f\in S$, then for $x=\kappa_s(y)$ (step 5 (i)) $\psi(x)=\varphi_s(y)=\varphi(x)$, so $\psi=\varphi$. Together with steps 4 and 6 this says that the cocone $(S^{-1}P,(\kappa_f))$ has the universal property of a colimit: $S^{-1}P=\varinjlim_{f\in S}P_f$ in the category of commutative rings, a filtered colimit by step 1.
