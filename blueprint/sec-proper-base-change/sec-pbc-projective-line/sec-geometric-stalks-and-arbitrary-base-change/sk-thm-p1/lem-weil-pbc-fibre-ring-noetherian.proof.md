Let $\varphi:A\to B$ be the given homomorphism, $T=\varphi(A\setminus\mathfrak p)$, $B_{\mathfrak p}=T^{-1}B$ and $\mathfrak pB_{\mathfrak p}$ the ideal of $B_{\mathfrak p}$ generated by $\varphi(\mathfrak p)$. Since $B\otimes_AA_{\mathfrak p}=B_{\mathfrak p}$ and $\kappa(\mathfrak p)=A_{\mathfrak p}/\mathfrak pA_{\mathfrak p}$, there is an isomorphism of $B$-algebras (in particular of rings) $\theta:B\otimes_A\kappa(\mathfrak p)\xrightarrow{\sim}B_{\mathfrak p}/\mathfrak pB_{\mathfrak p}$.

$T$ is a multiplicative subset of $B$ ($1=\varphi(1)\in T$ and $\varphi(s)\varphi(s')=\varphi(ss')$ with $ss'\in A\setminus\mathfrak p$ since $\mathfrak p$ is prime), and $B\to B_{\mathfrak p}$, $b\mapsto b/1$, is the localization of $B$ at $T$. Since $B$ is Noetherian, so is its localization $B_{\mathfrak p}$.

The composite $\theta^{-1}\circ\pi:B_{\mathfrak p}\to B\otimes_A\kappa(\mathfrak p)$ of the quotient map $\pi:B_{\mathfrak p}\to B_{\mathfrak p}/\mathfrak pB_{\mathfrak p}$ with the inverse of the isomorphism $\theta$ of step 1 is a surjective ring homomorphism from the Noetherian ring $B_{\mathfrak p}$ (step 2). Hence $B\otimes_A\kappa(\mathfrak p)$ is Noetherian, a quotient of a Noetherian ring being Noetherian.
