**Step 1.** *Lifting principle (and its consequences).* Let $\mathcal A=\Ab(T_{\Et})$ for a scheme $T$, let $\sigma:A^\bullet\to B^\bullet$ be a quasi-isomorphism of complexes of $\mathcal A$ and $I^\bullet$ a K-injective complex. Then $-\circ\sigma:\operatorname{Hom}_{K(\mathcal A)}(B^\bullet,I^\bullet)\to\operatorname{Hom}_{K(\mathcal A)}(A^\bullet,I^\bullet)$ is bijective. Indeed, by [lem-weil-pbc-kinjective-hom-derived] the localization $Q$ [def-weil-abstract-derived-category] induces bijections $\operatorname{Hom}_{K(\mathcal A)}(N^\bullet,I^\bullet)\to\operatorname{Hom}_{D(\mathcal A)}(QN^\bullet,QI^\bullet)$ for $N^\bullet=A^\bullet,B^\bullet$; since $Q$ is a functor, $Q(g\circ\sigma)=Q(g)\circ Q(\sigma)$, so these bijections carry $-\circ\sigma$ to $-\circ Q(\sigma)$, which is bijective because $Q(\sigma)$ is an isomorphism of $D(\mathcal A)$ ($Q$ inverts quasi-isomorphisms). Consequences used below. (a) For K-injective resolutions $s:G\to I^\bullet$, $s':G'\to I'^\bullet$ [def-sp-kinjective-bridge] and $\varphi:G\to G'$, applying this to $\sigma=s$ and the class of $s'\circ\varphi$ gives $\tilde\varphi:I^\bullet\to I'^\bullet$ with $\tilde\varphi s\simeq s'\varphi$, unique up to homotopy; the same holds for complexes $K^\bullet$ in place of $G$. (b) An additive functor, here $\Gamma(T,-)$, carries a homotopy $h$ between morphisms of complexes to the homotopy $\Gamma(T,h)$, so homotopic morphisms induce the same maps $\mathcal H^q(\Gamma(T,-))$; hence $H^q(\varphi)$ of the statement is well defined, and $H^q(\varphi'\circ\varphi)=H^q(\varphi')\circ H^q(\varphi)$, $H^q(\mathrm{id})=\mathrm{id}$ (a composite of lifts is a lift of the composite, the identity a lift of the identity); for $\varphi=\mathrm{id}_G$ the lifts are the mutually inverse homotopy equivalences of [prop-weil-pbc-cohomology-resolution-independent]. (c) (Two out of three.) If $\gamma\circ\sigma\simeq\tau$ with $\sigma,\tau$ quasi-isomorphisms, then $\mathcal H^q(\gamma)\mathcal H^q(\sigma)=\mathcal H^q(\tau)$ (homotopic maps agree on cohomology), so $\mathcal H^q(\gamma)=\mathcal H^q(\tau)\mathcal H^q(\sigma)^{-1}$ is bijective and $\gamma$ is a quasi-isomorphism. (d) $u^{-1}$ is exact [prop-weil-pbc-inverse-image-exact], hence commutes with the cohomology of complexes [lem-weil-pbc-exact-functor-complex-cohomology] and carries quasi-isomorphisms to quasi-isomorphisms; a composite of quasi-isomorphisms is one.

**Step 2.** *Clause 1 (naturality).* Let $s:G\to I^\bullet$, $r:u^{-1}I^\bullet\to J^\bullet$ and $s':G'\to I'^\bullet$, $r':u^{-1}I'^\bullet\to J'^\bullet$ be the resolutions defining $u^*$ for $G$ and $G'$ [def-weil-pbc-cohomology-pullback]. By step 1(a) choose $\tilde\varphi:I^\bullet\to I'^\bullet$ with $\tilde\varphi s\simeq s'\varphi$; it computes $H^q(\varphi)$. The complex $J'^\bullet$ is K-injective and $r\circ u^{-1}(s):u^{-1}G\to J^\bullet$ is a quasi-isomorphism (step 1(d)), and $r:u^{-1}I^\bullet\to J^\bullet$ is one, so step 1 gives $\psi:J^\bullet\to J'^\bullet$ with $\psi r\simeq r'\circ u^{-1}(\tilde\varphi)$. Then $\psi\circ(r\,u^{-1}(s))\simeq r'u^{-1}(\tilde\varphi s)\simeq r'u^{-1}(s')\circ u^{-1}(\varphi)$ ($u^{-1}$ is additive, so it preserves homotopies), so $\psi$ is a lift of $u^{-1}\varphi$ and computes $H^q(u^{-1}\varphi)$. Naturality of the unit [def-weil-pbc-inverse-image] gives $\eta_{u,I'^\bullet}\circ\tilde\varphi=u_*u^{-1}(\tilde\varphi)\circ\eta_{u,I^\bullet}$ exactly; on global sections, with $\Gamma(X,u_*-)=\Gamma(Y,-)$ [def-weil-pbc-direct-image], this says $\eta_u(\tilde\varphi(a))=u^{-1}(\tilde\varphi)(\eta_u(a))$. Hence the two morphisms of complexes $\Gamma(X,I^\bullet)\to\Gamma(Y,J'^\bullet)$, $a\mapsto r'(\eta_u(\tilde\varphi(a)))=r'u^{-1}(\tilde\varphi)(\eta_u(a))$ and $a\mapsto\psi(r(\eta_u(a)))$, are homotopic ($\Gamma(Y,-)$ of the homotopy $\psi r\simeq r'u^{-1}(\tilde\varphi)$), so they agree on $\mathcal H^q$ (step 1(b)): $u^*\circ H^q(\varphi)=H^q(u^{-1}\varphi)\circ u^*$.

**Step 3.** *Independence of the choices.* Apply step 2 with $G'=G$, $\varphi=\mathrm{id}_G$, the first choice $(I^\bullet,J^\bullet)$ for $G$ and the second $(I'^\bullet,J'^\bullet)$ for $G'$: $u^*{}'\circ H^q(\mathrm{id}_G)=H^q(\mathrm{id}_{u^{-1}G})\circ u^*$, and $H^q(\mathrm{id})$ are the identifications of [prop-weil-pbc-cohomology-resolution-independent] (step 1(b)).

**Step 4.** *Clause 2 (composition and identity).* Choose $s:G\to I^\bullet$ and $r:u^{-1}I^\bullet\to J^\bullet$ defining $u^*$, and $r':v^{-1}J^\bullet\to L^\bullet$ a K-injective resolution on $Z_{\Et}$, so that $v^*$ for $u^{-1}G$ is computed from $r\,u^{-1}(s)$ and $r'$ by $b\mapsto r'(\eta_v(b))$. Hence $v^*u^*$ is $\mathcal H^q$ of $a\mapsto r'(\eta_{v,J}(r(\eta_{u,I}(a))))$. Naturality of $\eta_v$ gives $\eta_{v,J}\circ r=v_*v^{-1}(r)\circ\eta_{v,u^{-1}I}$, so this is $a\mapsto r''\bigl(u_*(\eta_{v,u^{-1}I})(\eta_{u,I}(a))\bigr)$ with $r''=r'\circ v^{-1}(r):v^{-1}u^{-1}I^\bullet\to L^\bullet$, a quasi-isomorphism (step 1(d)) to a K-injective complex. By [lem-weil-pbc-inverse-image-composition] (2), $u_*v_*(c_{u,v,I})\circ u_*(\eta_{v,u^{-1}I})\circ\eta_{u,I}=d_{u,v}\circ\eta_{u\circ v,I}$; on global sections $d_{u,v}$ is the identification $\Gamma(X,(u\circ v)_*F)=F(Z)=\Gamma(X,u_*v_*F)$, so $u_*(\eta_{v,u^{-1}I})(\eta_{u,I}(a))=c_{u,v,I}^{-1}(\eta_{u\circ v,I}(a))$. Therefore $v^*u^*$ is the map $(u\circ v)^*$ computed (step 3) with the K-injective resolution $r''\circ c_{u,v,I}^{-1}:(u\circ v)^{-1}I^\bullet\to L^\bullet$ of $(u\circ v)^{-1}G$, followed by the identification of the target $H^q(Z,v^{-1}u^{-1}G)$, computed from $r''\circ v^{-1}u^{-1}(s)$, with $H^q(Z,(u\circ v)^{-1}G)$, computed from $r''c_{u,v,I}^{-1}(u\circ v)^{-1}(s)$. By naturality of $c_{u,v}$, $r''c_{u,v,I}^{-1}(u\circ v)^{-1}(s)\circ c_{u,v,G}=r''\circ v^{-1}u^{-1}(s)$, so $\mathrm{id}_{L^\bullet}$ is a lift of $c_{u,v,G}$ and that identification is $H^q(c_{u,v,G})$ (step 1(b)): $(u\circ v)^*=H^q(c_{u,v,G})\circ v^*\circ u^*$. For the identity: by [lem-weil-pbc-inverse-image-composition] (3), $e_{X,I}\circ d_X\circ\eta_{\mathrm{id},I}=\mathrm{id}$, so with $J^\bullet=I^\bullet$ and $r=e_{X,I}:\mathrm{id}_X^{-1}I^\bullet\to I^\bullet$, $\mathrm{id}_X^*$ is $\mathcal H^q$ of $a\mapsto e_{X,I}(\eta_{\mathrm{id},I}(a))=a$, from $H^q(X,G)$ computed by $s$ to $H^q(X,\mathrm{id}_X^{-1}G)$ computed by $e_{X,I}\circ\mathrm{id}_X^{-1}(s)=s\circ e_{X,G}$; so $\mathrm{id}_{I^\bullet}$ lifts $e_{X,G}$ and $H^q(e_{X,G})\circ\mathrm{id}_X^*=\mathrm{id}$.
