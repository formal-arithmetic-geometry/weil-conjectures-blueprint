Besides the comparison in degree zero of Theorem [sk-thm-pbc-henselian-h0],
the argument uses two cohomological facts about henselian pairs.

The model case is $R$ a complete discrete valuation ring. Then
$\operatorname{Spec}R$ plays the role of a small disc around its closed
point, and $\mathbf P^1_R$ that of a family of projective lines over the
disc, which in topology retracts onto the central fibre. The claim is
that, for torsion sheaves, the cohomology of $\mathbf P^1_R$ is that of
its closed fibre. For the spectrum of a ring $B$ that is henselian
along an ideal $J$ this is Gabber's theorem (i) below, in all degrees.
The affine charts of $\mathbf P^1_R$ are not henselian along the closed
fibre, but their henselizations are, and applying (i) to these
henselizations and to restrictions of injective sheaves controls the
cohomology of the closed fibre in degrees at least two. In degree one a
class on the closed fibre must be lifted. Classes in degree one with
finite coefficients are torsors, that is, finite étale covers, and
these lift over a proper base by (ii). The
theorem on curves that follows is needed for the last step of the proof,
the change of separably closed ground field.
