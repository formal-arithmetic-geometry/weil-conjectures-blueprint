Let $S$ be a scheme and let $V$ be an affine scheme equipped with a morphism
$g\colon V\to S$ that is locally of finite type. Then there are a finite set
$n$ and an immersion $j\colon V\to\mathbf P^n_S$ with
$j$ followed by $\mathbf P^n_S\to S$ equal to $g$, where the homogeneous
coordinates $T_i$ of $\mathbf P^n_S$ are indexed by the finite set $n$.

## Proof

Write $B=\Gamma(V,\mathcal O_V)$, so that $V=\Spec B$. We give an elementary
construction; it does not use ample invertible sheaves.

*Step 1: a cover adapted to $g$.* Every point of $V$ has a basic open
neighbourhood $D(b)$ with $g(D(b))$ contained in an affine open of $S$: pick an
affine open $W\subseteq S$ around the image point and then a basic open of $V$
inside $g^{-1}(W)$. Since $V$ is quasi-compact, finitely many of these cover
$V$: there are a finite set $\Lambda$, sections $b_\lambda\in B$ and affine
opens $W_\lambda\subseteq S$ with $D(b_\lambda)\subseteq g^{-1}(W_\lambda)$ and
$V=\bigcup_{\lambda}D(b_\lambda)$.

*Step 2: generators with a common denominator.* Fix $\lambda$ and put
$A_\lambda=\Gamma(W_\lambda,\mathcal O_S)$. The induced morphism
$D(b_\lambda)\to W_\lambda$ is a morphism of affine schemes that is locally of
finite type, so $B_{b_\lambda}=\Gamma(D(b_\lambda),\mathcal O_V)$ is a finitely
generated $A_\lambda$-algebra. Clearing denominators, we may choose the
generators of the shape $c_\mu/b_\lambda^{N}$ with $c_\mu\in B$ and a single
exponent $N$, indexed by a finite set $M_\lambda$. Put

$$
t_{\lambda,\ast}=1,\qquad t_{\lambda,\mu}=c_\mu b_\lambda
\quad(\mu\in M_\lambda),
$$

a family indexed by the finite set $M_\lambda^{+}=M_\lambda\sqcup\{\ast\}$.
In $B_{b_\lambda}$ we have $t_{\lambda,\ast}/b_\lambda=1/b_\lambda$ and
$t_{\lambda,\mu}/b_\lambda=c_\mu$, hence
$c_\mu/b_\lambda^{N}=(1/b_\lambda)^{N}\cdot c_\mu$ lies in the subring
generated by $A_\lambda$ and the quotients $t_{\lambda,\bullet}/b_\lambda$.
Therefore $B_{b_\lambda}$ is generated as a ring by the image of $A_\lambda$
together with the quotients $t_{\lambda,\bullet}/b_\lambda$.

*Step 3: the morphism.* Let
$n=\Lambda\sqcup\coprod_{\lambda\in\Lambda}M_\lambda^{+}$, a finite set, and
let $s\colon n\to B$ be the family given by $s_\lambda=b_\lambda$ for
$\lambda\in\Lambda$ and $s_{(\lambda,\mu)}=t_{\lambda,\mu}$. The
$D(b_\lambda)$ cover $V$, so the $s_i$ generate the unit ideal of $B$, and
therefore they define a morphism $j\colon V\to\mathbf P^n_S$ over $S$ with
$j^{-1}\bigl(D_+(T_i)\bigr)=D(s_i)$.

*Step 4: $j$ is an immersion.* The opens $D_+(T_{\lambda})$ for
$\lambda\in\Lambda$ have preimages $D(b_\lambda)$, which cover $V$; hence the
image of $j$ is contained in $U=\bigcup_{\lambda\in\Lambda}D_+(T_\lambda)$.
Being an immersion is local on the target, so it suffices to show that the
restriction of $j$ over each $D_+(T_\lambda)$ is an immersion. On the standard
chart $D_+(T_\lambda)\cong\mathbf A^{\,n\setminus\{\lambda\}}_S$ this
restriction is the morphism

$$
D(b_\lambda)\longrightarrow\mathbf A^{\,n\setminus\{\lambda\}}_S,
\qquad Y_i\longmapsto s_i/b_\lambda .
$$

Since $D(b_\lambda)\subseteq g^{-1}(W_\lambda)$, it factors through the open
subscheme $\mathbf A^{\,n\setminus\{\lambda\}}_{W_\lambda}$, and the resulting
morphism
$D(b_\lambda)\to\mathbf A^{\,n\setminus\{\lambda\}}_{W_\lambda} =\Spec A_\lambda[Y_i]$ is $\Spec$ of the $A_\lambda$-algebra map
$Y_i\mapsto s_i/b_\lambda$. That map is surjective by Step 2, because its
image already contains the quotients $t_{\lambda,\bullet}/b_\lambda$. Hence
$D(b_\lambda)\to\mathbf A^{\,n\setminus\{\lambda\}}_{W_\lambda}$ is a closed
immersion, and composing with the open immersion
$\mathbf A^{\,n\setminus\{\lambda\}}_{W_\lambda}\to \mathbf A^{\,n\setminus\{\lambda\}}_S$ shows that the restriction of $j$ over
$D_+(T_\lambda)$ is an immersion.
