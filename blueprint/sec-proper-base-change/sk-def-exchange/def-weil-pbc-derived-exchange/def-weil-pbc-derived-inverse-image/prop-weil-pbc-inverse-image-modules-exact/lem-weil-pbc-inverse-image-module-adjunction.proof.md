**Step 1.** Notation for all steps. Write $L=u^{-1}$ and $R=u_*$ for the inverse and direct image on sheaves of sets, with unit $\eta$ and counit $\varepsilon$ of $L\dashv R$ [lem-weil-pbc-etale-inverse-image-algebraic-structures] (1). Since $L$ commutes with finite products (ibid. (1)), $\lambda_{X,Y}=(L\mathrm{pr}_1,L\mathrm{pr}_2):L(X\times Y)\to LX\times LY$ is an isomorphism, natural in $X,Y$ ($\lambda\circ L(f\times g)=(Lf\times Lg)\circ\lambda$, checked on the two projections); $R$ is sectionwise, $(RX)(W)=X(U\times_VW)$, so $R(X\times Y)=RX\times RY$ with $R\mathrm{pr}_i=\mathrm{pr}_i$. By ibid. (2), (3), the abelian inverse image of an abelian sheaf $G$ has as underlying sheaf of sets $LG$ (through the canonical isomorphism of (3), suppressed below), its group structure is $L$ of that of $G$, and the adjunction bijection of the abelian adjunction, its unit and its counit are on underlying maps those of $L\dashv R$; the counit, being the adjoint of the identity, is then determined by the bijection. So for an abelian map $\alpha:LG\to F$ its abelian adjoint is $\beta=R(\alpha)\circ\eta_G$, and $\alpha=\varepsilon_F\circ L(\beta)$ [lem-weil-pbc-adjunct-counit-identity]. Write $\mu_{LG}=L(\mu_G)\circ\lambda^{-1}$ and $\mu_{RF}=R(\mu_F)\circ(\eta_{\mathcal O}\times\mathrm{id}_{RF}):\mathcal O\times RF\to RL\mathcal O\times RF=R(L\mathcal O\times F)\to RF$ for the two actions of the statement. *Step 1 (the module structures are well defined; Stacks 03D1, 03D2).* By [lem-weil-pbc-sheaf-algebraic-structures-objects] finite products of sheaves of sets are sectionwise and sheaves of abelian groups (commutative rings) are the abelian group (commutative ring) objects of $\Sh$; hence, by [def-weil-sheaf-of-modules-category], a sheaf of $\mathcal O$-modules is the same as an abelian group object $G$ of $\Sh(V_{\Et})$ with a morphism $\mu_G:\mathcal O\times G\to G$ (a family of maps $\mathcal O(X)\times G(X)\to G(X)$ compatible with restrictions, i.e. scalar multiplications for which restrictions are semilinear) satisfying the module axioms $\mu\circ(\mathrm{id}\times+)=+\circ(\mu\circ(\mathrm{pr}_1,\mathrm{pr}_2),\mu\circ(\mathrm{pr}_1,\mathrm{pr}_3))$, $\mu\circ(+\times\mathrm{id})=+\circ(\mu\circ(\mathrm{pr}_1,\mathrm{pr}_3),\mu\circ(\mathrm{pr}_2,\mathrm{pr}_3))$, $\mu\circ(\cdot\times\mathrm{id})=\mu\circ(\mathrm{id}\times\mu)$, $\mu\circ(1\times\mathrm{id})=\mathrm{id}$ as equalities of morphisms of sheaves (they hold as morphisms iff they hold on sections over every $X$), and an $\mathcal O$-linear map is a morphism of group objects $\varphi$ with $\varphi\circ\mu_G=\mu_{G'}\circ(\mathrm{id}\times\varphi)$. (a) Inverse image (Stacks 03D2). Applying $L$ to the module axioms of $\mu_G$ and rewriting $L$ of a morphism out of a finite product through the comparison isomorphisms $\lambda$ (natural, and compatible with the projections), using that the ring structure of $L\mathcal O$ and the group structure of $LG$ are $L$ of those of $\mathcal O$ and $G$ composed with $\lambda^{-1}$ [lem-weil-pbc-etale-inverse-image-algebraic-structures] (2), gives the module axioms for $\mu_{LG}=L(\mu_G)\circ\lambda^{-1}$; so $LG$ is a sheaf of $L\mathcal O$-modules. If $\varphi:G\to G'$ is $\mathcal O$-linear, $L\varphi$ is additive (ibid. (2)) and $L\varphi\circ\mu_{LG}=L(\varphi\circ\mu_G)\circ\lambda^{-1}=L(\mu_{G'}\circ(\mathrm{id}\times\varphi))\circ\lambda^{-1}=L(\mu_{G'})\circ\lambda^{-1}\circ(\mathrm{id}\times L\varphi)=\mu_{LG'}\circ(\mathrm{id}\times L\varphi)$ by naturality of $\lambda$: $u^{-1}$ is a functor $\Mod(\mathcal O)\to\Mod(u^{-1}\mathcal O)$. (b) Direct image (Stacks 03D1). By ibid. (2) the unit $\eta_{\mathcal O}:\mathcal O\to RL\mathcal O$ is a morphism of sheaves of rings (it is the unit of the adjunction on sheaves of rings). For a sheaf of $L\mathcal O$-modules $F$ on $U_{\Et}$, $(RF)(W)=F(U\times_VW)$ is a module over $(L\mathcal O)(U\times_VW)=(RL\mathcal O)(W)$, with semilinear restrictions; restricting scalars along the ring maps $\eta_{\mathcal O,W}:\mathcal O(W)\to(RL\mathcal O)(W)$, which commute with restrictions, makes $RF$ a sheaf of $\mathcal O$-modules whose action map is exactly $\mu_{RF}=R(\mu_F)\circ(\eta_{\mathcal O}\times\mathrm{id})$ (products and $R$ being sectionwise); its abelian sheaf is the direct image of [def-weil-pbc-direct-image]. If $\psi:F\to F'$ is $L\mathcal O$-linear, each $\psi_{U\times_VW}$ is $(L\mathcal O)(U\times_VW)$-linear, hence $\mathcal O(W)$-linear through $\eta_{\mathcal O,W}$: $R\psi$ is $\mathcal O$-linear and $u_*$ is a functor $\Mod(u^{-1}\mathcal O)\to\Mod(\mathcal O)$.

**Step 2.** *Step 2 (the unit $\eta_G$ is $\mathcal O$-linear; Stacks 03D3, proof).* $\eta_G:G\to RLG$ is additive, being the abelian unit (notation above). Linearity is $\eta_G\circ\mu_G=\mu_{RLG}\circ(\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal O}\times\eta_G)$ with $\mu_{RLG}=R(L(\mu_G)\circ\lambda^{-1})\circ(\eta_{\mathcal O}\times\mathrm{id})$. First, $R(\lambda)\circ\eta_{\mathcal O\times G}=\eta_{\mathcal O}\times\eta_G$ as maps $\mathcal O\times G\to R(L\mathcal O\times LG)=RL\mathcal O\times RLG$: composing with $\mathrm{pr}_i=R(\mathrm{pr}_i)$ gives $R(L\mathrm{pr}_i)\circ\eta_{\mathcal O\times G}=\eta\circ\mathrm{pr}_i$ by naturality of $\eta$. Then, by naturality of $\eta$ at $\mu_G$, $\eta_G\circ\mu_G=RL(\mu_G)\circ\eta_{\mathcal O\times G}=R(L(\mu_G)\circ\lambda^{-1})\circ R(\lambda)\circ\eta_{\mathcal O\times G}=R(L(\mu_G)\circ\lambda^{-1})\circ(\eta_{\mathcal O}\times\eta_G)=\mu_{RLG}\circ(\mathrm{id}\times\eta_G)$. This is the commutative square of the source ($\mathcal O\times G\to f_*f^{-1}(\mathcal O\times G)\to f_*f^{-1}G$ against $\mathcal O\times G\to G\to f_*f^{-1}G$).

**Step 3.** *Step 3 (the counit $\varepsilon_F$ is $u^{-1}\mathcal O$-linear; omitted in the source, written here).* $\varepsilon_F:LRF\to F$ is additive, being the abelian counit. Linearity is $\varepsilon_F\circ L(\mu_{RF})\circ\lambda^{-1}=\mu_F\circ(\mathrm{id}_{L\mathcal O}\times\varepsilon_F)$, i.e. (composing with the isomorphism $\lambda$) $\varepsilon_F\circ L(\mu_{RF})=\mu_F\circ(\mathrm{id}\times\varepsilon_F)\circ\lambda$ as maps $L(\mathcal O\times RF)\to F$. By naturality of $\varepsilon$ at $\mu_F$, $\varepsilon_F\circ L(\mu_{RF})=\varepsilon_F\circ LR(\mu_F)\circ L(\eta_{\mathcal O}\times\mathrm{id})=\mu_F\circ\varepsilon_{L\mathcal O\times F}\circ L(\eta_{\mathcal O}\times\mathrm{id}_{RF})$. It remains to see $\varepsilon_{L\mathcal O\times F}\circ L(\eta_{\mathcal O}\times\mathrm{id})=(\mathrm{id}\times\varepsilon_F)\circ\lambda$ as maps into the product $L\mathcal O\times F$, i.e. after each projection. First projection: by naturality of $\varepsilon$, $\mathrm{pr}_1\circ\varepsilon_{L\mathcal O\times F}=\varepsilon_{L\mathcal O}\circ LR(\mathrm{pr}_1)$, and $R(\mathrm{pr}_1)\circ(\eta_{\mathcal O}\times\mathrm{id})=\eta_{\mathcal O}\circ\mathrm{pr}_1$, so the left side gives $\varepsilon_{L\mathcal O}\circ L(\eta_{\mathcal O})\circ L(\mathrm{pr}_1)=L(\mathrm{pr}_1)$ by the triangle identity $\varepsilon_{LX}\circ L(\eta_X)=\mathrm{id}$; the right side gives $\mathrm{pr}_1\circ\lambda=L(\mathrm{pr}_1)$. Second projection: likewise $\mathrm{pr}_2\circ\varepsilon_{L\mathcal O\times F}\circ L(\eta_{\mathcal O}\times\mathrm{id})=\varepsilon_F\circ L(R(\mathrm{pr}_2)\circ(\eta_{\mathcal O}\times\mathrm{id}))=\varepsilon_F\circ L(\mathrm{pr}_2)$, and $\mathrm{pr}_2\circ(\mathrm{id}\times\varepsilon_F)\circ\lambda=\varepsilon_F\circ\mathrm{pr}_2\circ\lambda=\varepsilon_F\circ L(\mathrm{pr}_2)$. Hence $\varepsilon_F$ is $L\mathcal O$-linear.

**Step 4.** *Step 4 (the two implications).* Let $\alpha:LG\to F$ be a morphism of abelian sheaves and $\beta=R(\alpha)\circ\eta_G$ its abelian adjoint; then $\alpha=\varepsilon_F\circ L(\beta)$ [lem-weil-pbc-adjunct-counit-identity] (a Mathlib leaf). (i) (Stacks) If $\alpha$ is $L\mathcal O$-linear, $R(\alpha):RLG\to RF$ is $\mathcal O$-linear by step 1(b) and $\eta_G$ is $\mathcal O$-linear by step 2, so their composite $\beta$ is $\mathcal O$-linear (a composite of linear maps is linear). (ii) (omitted in the source) If $\beta:G\to RF$ is $\mathcal O$-linear, $L(\beta):LG\to LRF$ is $L\mathcal O$-linear by step 1(a) (the module structure of $LRF$ being the one of step 1(a) applied to the $\mathcal O$-module $RF$ of step 1(b)) and $\varepsilon_F$ is $L\mathcal O$-linear by step 3, so $\alpha=\varepsilon_F\circ L(\beta)$ is $L\mathcal O$-linear.

**Step 5.** *Step 5 (restriction of the bijection, naturality).* By step 4 the abelian adjunction bijection $\operatorname{Hom}_{\Ab}(u^{-1}G,F)\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{\Ab}(G,u_*F)$ [def-weil-pbc-inverse-image] maps the subset of $u^{-1}\mathcal O$-linear maps onto the subset of $\mathcal O$-linear maps, so it restricts to a bijection $\operatorname{Hom}_{u^{-1}\mathcal O}(u^{-1}G,F)\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal O}(G,u_*F)$. For $\mathcal O$-linear $g:G'\to G$ and $u^{-1}\mathcal O$-linear $f:F\to F'$ the abelian bijection satisfies $(f\circ\alpha\circ u^{-1}g)^\flat=u_*f\circ\alpha^\flat\circ g$; here $u^{-1}g$ and $u_*f$ are the module maps of step 1 (same underlying abelian maps), and morphisms of $\Mod$ are abelian maps [def-weil-sheaf-of-modules-category], so the restricted bijection is natural in $G$ and $F$ with respect to the functors $u^{-1}:\Mod(\mathcal O)\to\Mod(u^{-1}\mathcal O)$, $u_*:\Mod(u^{-1}\mathcal O)\to\Mod(\mathcal O)$ of step 1. Hence $u^{-1}\dashv u_*$ on modules; its unit and counit (the adjoints of the identities) are the abelian $\eta_G$, $\varepsilon_F$, which are linear by steps 2 and 3.

*Source: Stacks project, sites-modules.tex, Lemma 03D3 (lemma-adjoint-push-pull-modules) with Lemmas 03D1, 03D2; the implication 'beta linear => alpha linear' (steps 3 and 4(ii)) is omitted in the source and was written by an agent.*
