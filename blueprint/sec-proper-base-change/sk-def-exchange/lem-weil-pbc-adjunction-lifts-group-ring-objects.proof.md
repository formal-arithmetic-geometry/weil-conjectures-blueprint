**Step 1.** *Step 1 (abelian group objects: Mathlib).* By [lem-weil-pbc-right-adjoint-preserves-limits] $R$ preserves finite products, so $\rho_{Y,Y'}$ and $\rho_*$ are isomorphisms and $R(*)$ is final; together with the hypothesis on $L$, [lem-weil-pbc-adjunction-lifts-abelian-group-objects] applies to $L\dashv R$ and gives clause (1) for abelian group objects (the transported structures are those of the statement), the adjunction $L^{\mathrm{gr}}\dashv R^{\mathrm{gr}}$ with bijection $\Psi(\alpha)=R(\alpha)\circ\eta_X$, inverse $\beta\mapsto\varepsilon_Y\circ L(\beta)$, and unit and counit with components $\eta_X$, $\varepsilon_Y$, which are therefore morphisms of abelian group objects. The equivalence of clause (2) for groups: if $\alpha:L^{\mathrm{gr}}X\to Y$ is a morphism of group objects, then $\Psi(\alpha)$ computed in the lifted adjunction is a morphism of group objects with underlying morphism $R(\alpha)\circ\eta_X$; conversely if $\beta=R(\alpha)\circ\eta_X$ is a morphism of group objects $X\to R^{\mathrm{gr}}Y$, then $\varepsilon_Y\circ L(\beta)$ is a morphism of group objects (a composite of two), and it equals $\alpha$ by [lem-weil-pbc-adjunct-counit-identity]. So clauses (1) and (2) hold for abelian group objects; steps 2-6 do the commutative ring objects, whose additive parts are those of this step.

**Step 2.** *Step 2 (comparison isomorphisms; identities used below).* (a) $\mathrm{pr}_k\circ\lambda=L(\mathrm{pr}_k)$ ($k=1,2$) by definition of $\lambda$. (b) For $f:Z\to X$, $g:Z\to Y$: $\lambda\circ L\langle f,g\rangle=\langle Lf,Lg\rangle$, since both have projections $L(\mathrm{pr}_1)\circ L\langle f,g\rangle=L(\mathrm{pr}_1\circ\langle f,g\rangle)=Lf$ and $Lg$, and a morphism into a product is determined by its projections [def-weil-category-product]. Taking $f\circ\mathrm{pr}_1$, $g\circ\mathrm{pr}_2$: $\lambda\circ L(f\times g)=(Lf\times Lg)\circ\lambda$, i.e. $L(f\times g)\circ\lambda^{-1}=\lambda^{-1}\circ(Lf\times Lg)$ (naturality of $\lambda$). (c) $\lambda_*^{-1}\circ!_{LZ}=L(!_Z)$, both being morphisms $LZ\to L(*)$, a final object. (d) For a morphism $m:X\times X\to X$ put $m_L=L(m)\circ\lambda^{-1}$, and for $c:*\to X$ put $c_L=L(c)\circ\lambda_*^{-1}$. Then for $u,v:Z\to X$: $m_L\circ\langle Lu,Lv\rangle=L(m)\circ\lambda^{-1}\circ\lambda\circ L\langle u,v\rangle=L(m\circ\langle u,v\rangle)$ by (b), and $c_L\circ!_{LZ}=L(c)\circ L(!_Z)=L(c\circ!_Z)$ by (c). (e) For $X^3=(X\times X)\times X$ let $q_1,q_2,q_3$ be the coordinate projections of $(LX)^3$; $\theta=(\lambda_{X,X}\times\mathrm{id})\circ\lambda_{X\times X,X}:L(X^3)\to(LX)^3$ is an isomorphism (a composite of isomorphisms) with $q_j\circ\theta=L(p_j)$: $q_3\circ\theta=\mathrm{pr}_2\circ\lambda_{X\times X,X}=L(\mathrm{pr}_2)=L(p_3)$, and $\mathrm{pr}_1\circ\theta=\lambda_{X,X}\circ L(\mathrm{pr}_1)=\lambda_{X,X}\circ L\langle p_1,p_2\rangle=\langle Lp_1,Lp_2\rangle$ by (b). On $X\times X$ put $\theta=\lambda_{X,X}$ ($q_j\circ\theta=L(\mathrm{pr}_j)$ by (a)) and on $X$ put $\theta=\mathrm{id}$. Since $R$ preserves finite products [lem-weil-pbc-right-adjoint-preserves-limits], (a)-(e) hold verbatim for $R$ with $\rho$, $\rho_*$ in place of $\lambda$, $\lambda_*$ (the proofs used only that $\lambda,\lambda_*$ are isomorphisms with the stated projections).

**Step 3.** *Step 3 (clause (1) for rings: objects).* Let $(X,+,0,i,\cdot,1)$ be a commutative ring object of $\mathcal A$; by step 1 $(LX,+_L,0_L,i_L=L(i))$ is an abelian group object. Call a *term* on $Z\in\{X,X\times X,X^3\}$ a morphism $Z\to X$ built from the coordinate projections by the rules $u,v\mapsto m\circ\langle u,v\rangle$ ($m\in\{+,\cdot\}$), $u\mapsto i\circ u$, and the constants $c\circ!_Z$ ($c\in\{0,1\}$); every axiom (G1)-(G4), (R1)-(R4) is an equation $u=u'$ between two terms on one $Z$ (with $\mathrm{id}_X$ the coordinate projection of $Z=X$, $+\circ\langle\mathrm{pr}_2,\mathrm{pr}_1\rangle$ the term with $u=\mathrm{pr}_2,v=\mathrm{pr}_1$, and $+$ itself $=+\circ\langle\mathrm{pr}_1,\mathrm{pr}_2\rangle$). For a term $u$ let $u_L$ be the term on $(LX)^n$ built by the same rules from the $q_j$ and $+_L,\cdot_L,i_L,0_L,1_L$. Claim: $u_L\circ\theta=L(u)$, by induction on $u$ using step 2: for $u=p_j$ it is (e); for $u=m\circ\langle v,w\rangle$, $u_L\circ\theta=m_L\circ\langle v_L\theta,w_L\theta\rangle=m_L\circ\langle Lv,Lw\rangle=L(m\circ\langle v,w\rangle)$ by (d); for $u=i\circ v$, $L(i)\circ v_L\circ\theta=L(i)\circ L(v)=L(i\circ v)$; for $u=c\circ!_Z$, $c_L\circ!_{(LX)^n}\circ\theta=c_L\circ!_{LZ}=L(c\circ!_Z)$ by (d). Hence an axiom $u=u'$ gives $u_L\circ\theta=L(u)=L(u')=u'_L\circ\theta$, and since $\theta$ is an isomorphism $u_L=u'_L$: the transported data satisfy (G1)-(G4), (R1)-(R4), so $(LX,+_L,0_L,i_L,\cdot_L,1_L)$ is a commutative ring object of $\mathcal B$. The same argument with $\rho$ (step 2, last sentence) shows that $RY$ with $R(+)\circ\rho^{-1}$, $R(0)\circ\rho_*^{-1}$, $R(i)$, $R(\cdot)\circ\rho^{-1}$, $R(1)\circ\rho_*^{-1}$ is a commutative ring object of $\mathcal A$ for a commutative ring object $Y$ of $\mathcal B$. (This is the source's 'the axioms are preserved because the functor commutes with finite limits', written out.)

**Step 4.** *Step 4 (clause (1) for rings: morphisms and functors).* Let $f:X\to X'$ be a morphism of commutative ring objects. It is a morphism of the abelian group objects, so $Lf$ commutes with $+_L,0_L,i_L$ (step 1). Multiplication: $Lf\circ\cdot_L=L(f\circ\cdot)\circ\lambda^{-1}=L(\cdot'\circ(f\times f))\circ\lambda^{-1}=L(\cdot')\circ L(f\times f)\circ\lambda^{-1}=L(\cdot')\circ\lambda^{-1}\circ(Lf\times Lf)=\cdot'_L\circ(Lf\times Lf)$ by naturality of $\lambda$ (step 2 (b)). Unit: $Lf\circ1_L=L(f\circ1)\circ\lambda_*^{-1}=L(1')\circ\lambda_*^{-1}=1'_L$. So $Lf$ is a morphism of commutative ring objects $L^{\mathrm{rg}}X\to L^{\mathrm{rg}}X'$; the same computation with $\rho$ for $R$. Morphisms of ring objects contain the identities and are closed under composition ($g\circ f\circ\cdot=g\circ\cdot'\circ(f\times f)=\cdot''\circ(g\times g)\circ(f\times f)=\cdot''\circ(gf\times gf)$, the last equality checked on the two projections [def-weil-category-product]; likewise for the other structure maps), so commutative ring objects form a category, and $L^{\mathrm{rg}}$, $R^{\mathrm{rg}}$ are functors ($L(\mathrm{id})=\mathrm{id}$, $L(gf)=L(g)L(f)$) which, being $L$ and $R$ on underlying objects and morphisms, commute with the forgetful functors.

**Step 5.** *Step 5 (the unit and the counit respect $\cdot$ and $1$).* (i) $\eta_X:X\to R^{\mathrm{rg}}L^{\mathrm{rg}}X$, whose target carries $\cdot_{RL}=R(\cdot_L)\circ\rho^{-1}=R(L(\cdot)\circ\lambda^{-1})\circ\rho^{-1}$ and $1_{RL}=R(1_L)\circ\rho_*^{-1}$. First, $\rho\circ R(\lambda)\circ\eta_{X\times X}=\eta_X\times\eta_X$ as maps $X\times X\to RLX\times RLX$: on the projection $k$, $\mathrm{pr}_k\circ\rho\circ R(\lambda)\circ\eta_{X\times X}=R(\mathrm{pr}_k)\circ R(\lambda)\circ\eta_{X\times X}=RL(\mathrm{pr}_k)\circ\eta_{X\times X}=\eta_X\circ\mathrm{pr}_k$ by step 2 (a) and naturality of $\eta$, which is also $\mathrm{pr}_k\circ(\eta_X\times\eta_X)$ [def-weil-category-product]. Then, by naturality of $\eta$ at $\cdot$, $\eta_X\circ\cdot=RL(\cdot)\circ\eta_{X\times X}=R(\cdot_L)\circ R(\lambda)\circ\eta_{X\times X}=R(\cdot_L)\circ\rho^{-1}\circ(\eta_X\times\eta_X)=\cdot_{RL}\circ(\eta_X\times\eta_X)$. And $\eta_X\circ1=RL(1)\circ\eta_*=R(1_L)\circ R(\lambda_*)\circ\eta_*=R(1_L)\circ\rho_*^{-1}=1_{RL}$, since $R(\lambda_*)\circ\eta_*$ and $\rho_*^{-1}$ are morphisms $*\to R(*)$, a final object. (ii) $\varepsilon_Y:L^{\mathrm{rg}}R^{\mathrm{rg}}Y\to Y$, whose source carries $\cdot_{LR}=L(R(\cdot)\circ\rho^{-1})\circ\lambda^{-1}$ and $1_{LR}=L(R(1)\circ\rho_*^{-1})\circ\lambda_*^{-1}$. First, $\varepsilon_{Y\times Y}\circ L(\rho^{-1})=(\varepsilon_Y\times\varepsilon_Y)\circ\lambda_{RY,RY}$ as maps $L(RY\times RY)\to Y\times Y$: on the projection $k$, $\mathrm{pr}_k\circ\varepsilon_{Y\times Y}\circ L(\rho^{-1})=\varepsilon_Y\circ LR(\mathrm{pr}_k)\circ L(\rho^{-1})=\varepsilon_Y\circ L(\mathrm{pr}_k\circ\rho\circ\rho^{-1})=\varepsilon_Y\circ L(\mathrm{pr}_k)$ (naturality of $\varepsilon$, and $R(\mathrm{pr}_k)=\mathrm{pr}_k\circ\rho$), and $\mathrm{pr}_k\circ(\varepsilon_Y\times\varepsilon_Y)\circ\lambda=\varepsilon_Y\circ\mathrm{pr}_k\circ\lambda=\varepsilon_Y\circ L(\mathrm{pr}_k)$. Then $\varepsilon_Y\circ\cdot_{LR}=\varepsilon_Y\circ LR(\cdot)\circ L(\rho^{-1})\circ\lambda^{-1}=\cdot\circ\varepsilon_{Y\times Y}\circ L(\rho^{-1})\circ\lambda^{-1}=\cdot\circ(\varepsilon_Y\times\varepsilon_Y)$, and $\varepsilon_Y\circ1_{LR}=\varepsilon_Y\circ LR(1)\circ L(\rho_*^{-1})\circ\lambda_*^{-1}=1\circ\varepsilon_*\circ L(\rho_*^{-1})\circ\lambda_*^{-1}=1$, the composite $\varepsilon_*\circ L(\rho_*^{-1})\circ\lambda_*^{-1}$ being an endomorphism of the final object $*$, hence the identity. With step 1 ($\eta_X$, $\varepsilon_Y$ respect $+,0,i$) $\eta_X$ and $\varepsilon_Y$ are morphisms of commutative ring objects.

**Step 6.** *Step 6 (clause (2) for rings).* Let $X$, $Y$ be commutative ring objects of $\mathcal A$, $\mathcal B$, $\alpha:LX\to Y$ and $\beta=R(\alpha)\circ\eta_X$. If $\alpha$ is a morphism of ring objects, $R(\alpha):R^{\mathrm{rg}}L^{\mathrm{rg}}X\to R^{\mathrm{rg}}Y$ is one by step 4 and $\eta_X$ is one by step 5, so $\beta$ is a composite of morphisms of ring objects (step 4). Conversely, if $\beta:X\to R^{\mathrm{rg}}Y$ is one, then $L(\beta):L^{\mathrm{rg}}X\to L^{\mathrm{rg}}R^{\mathrm{rg}}Y$ is one (step 4) and so is $\varepsilon_Y$ (step 5), and $\alpha=\varepsilon_Y\circ L(\beta)$ [lem-weil-pbc-adjunct-counit-identity]. So the bijection $\Psi_{X,Y}(\alpha)=R(\alpha)\circ\eta_X$ of $L\dashv R$ maps the set of ring morphisms $L^{\mathrm{rg}}X\to Y$ into the set of ring morphisms $X\to R^{\mathrm{rg}}Y$, and onto it: a ring morphism $\beta$ is $\Psi(\varepsilon_Y\circ L(\beta))$ with $\varepsilon_Y\circ L(\beta)$ a ring morphism. The naturality $\Psi(b\circ a\circ L(f))=R(b)\circ\Psi(a)\circ f$ of $\Psi$ holds for all morphisms of $\mathcal A$, $\mathcal B$, hence for ring morphisms $f$, $b$, whose images under $L^{\mathrm{rg}}$, $R^{\mathrm{rg}}$ are $Lf$, $Rb$. By [lem-weil-pbc-adjunction-of-natural-bijection] the restricted bijections make $L^{\mathrm{rg}}\dashv R^{\mathrm{rg}}$ an adjunction, with unit $\Psi(\mathrm{id}_{LX})=R(\mathrm{id})\circ\eta_X=\eta_X$ and counit $\Psi^{-1}(\mathrm{id}_{RY})=\varepsilon_Y\circ L(\mathrm{id})=\varepsilon_Y$, those of $L\dashv R$. Commutativity of the multiplication is used nowhere in steps 3-6 beyond (R2) being one of the transported axioms, so the same holds for ring objects without (R2) (Stacks' 'rings (with unit)').
