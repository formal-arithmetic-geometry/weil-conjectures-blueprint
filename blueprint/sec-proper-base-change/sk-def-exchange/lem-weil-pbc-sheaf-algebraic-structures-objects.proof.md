**Step 1.** *(1) Finite products in $\Sh(\mathcal C)$ are sectionwise; morphisms and equalities of morphisms are sectionwise.* (a) For sheaves of sets $\mathcal F_1,\dots,\mathcal F_n$ ($n\ge0$) the presheaf $X\mapsto\mathcal F_1(X)\times\dots\times\mathcal F_n(X)$, restriction componentwise, is a sheaf: given a covering $\{U_i\to U\}$ and a compatible family $(s_i)$, $s_i=(s_{i,1},\dots,s_{i,n})\in\prod_k\mathcal F_k(U_i)$, each $(s_{i,k})_i$ is a compatible family of $\mathcal F_k$ (restriction is componentwise), so it glues to a unique $s_k\in\mathcal F_k(U)$, and $(s_1,\dots,s_n)$ is the unique section restricting to the $s_i$ (Sites, Lemma 00W2 and its proof, for the finite discrete diagram); for $n=0$ this is the presheaf $*$ with $*(X)$ a singleton, a sheaf because an equalizer of maps between singletons is a singleton (Sites, Example 00W3). With the sectionwise projections it is a product in presheaves (a morphism $\mathcal H\to\prod_k\mathcal F_k$ is a family of maps $\mathcal H(X)\to\prod_k\mathcal F_k(X)$ natural in $X$, i.e. an $n$-tuple of morphisms $\mathcal H\to\mathcal F_k$), hence in the full subcategory $\Sh(\mathcal C)$ [def-weil-category-product]; for $n=0$ it is the final sheaf $*$. Any other choice of product in $\Sh(\mathcal C)$ has the same sections via its projections, since the inclusion of sheaves into presheaves and the evaluations $\mathcal G\mapsto\mathcal G(X)$ preserve finite limits [lem-weil-pbc-presheaf-finite-limits-objectwise] (1), (2); so we use the sectionwise product throughout, with $(\mathcal F\times\mathcal F)(X)=\mathcal F(X)\times\mathcal F(X)$, $(\mathcal F\times\mathcal F\times\mathcal F)(X)=\mathcal F(X)^3$, and the morphisms $\langle f,g\rangle$, $f\times g$, $\mathrm{pr}_k$, $!_{\mathcal F}:\mathcal F\to*$ given on $X$-sections by the same formulas on sets. (b) Hence a morphism of sheaves $\mathcal F\times\mathcal F\to\mathcal F$ (resp. $*\to\mathcal F$, $\mathcal F\to\mathcal F$) is exactly a family of maps $\mathcal F(X)\times\mathcal F(X)\to\mathcal F(X)$ (resp. elements $0_X\in\mathcal F(X)$, maps $\mathcal F(X)\to\mathcal F(X)$) compatible with all restriction maps $\rho:\mathcal F(X)\to\mathcal F(Y)$ along $Y\to X$ (for $*$: $\rho(0_X)=0_Y$), since a morphism of sheaves is a natural transformation of the underlying functors. (c) Two morphisms of sheaves are equal iff their components on every $X$ are equal (equality of natural transformations), and the component of a composite is the composite of components; so an equation between composites of structure maps, products and projections holds in $\Sh(\mathcal C)$ iff the corresponding equation of maps of sets holds on $\mathcal F(X)$, $\mathcal F(X)^2$, $\mathcal F(X)^3$ for every $X$.

**Step 2.** *(2) From a sheaf of abelian groups to an abelian group object.* Let $\mathcal A$ be a sheaf of abelian groups and $\mathcal F=|\mathcal A|$ its underlying presheaf of sets, a sheaf of sets by [lem-weil-pbc-algebraic-sheaf-iff-underlying-sets]. Put $+_X(a,b)=a+b$, $0_X=0\in\mathcal A(X)$, $i_X(a)=-a$. Every restriction map $\rho$ of $\mathcal A$ is a group homomorphism, so $\rho(a+b)=\rho(a)+\rho(b)$, $\rho(0)=0$, $\rho(-a)=-\rho(a)$: by (1)(b) these families are morphisms $+:\mathcal F\times\mathcal F\to\mathcal F$, $0:*\to\mathcal F$, $i:\mathcal F\to\mathcal F$ of $\Sh(\mathcal C)$. By (1)(c) the axioms are checked on $X$-sections for every $X$, where they read: associativity $(a+b)+c=a+(b+c)$ on $\mathcal F(X)^3$; commutativity $b+a=a+b$; unit $0+a=a$ (the component of $0\circ!_{\mathcal F}$ at $X$ is the constant map $a\mapsto0_X$); inverse $a+(-a)=0$; these hold in the abelian group $\mathcal A(X)$. So $(\mathcal F,+,0,i)$ is an abelian group object. If $\varphi:\mathcal A\to\mathcal B$ is a morphism of sheaves of abelian groups, $|\varphi|$ is a morphism of sheaves of sets with components the homomorphisms $\varphi_X$, hence $\varphi_X(a+b)=\varphi_X(a)+\varphi_X(b)$, $\varphi_X(0)=0$, $\varphi_X(-a)=-\varphi_X(a)$, i.e. by (1)(c) $|\varphi|\circ+=+\circ(|\varphi|\times|\varphi|)$, $|\varphi|\circ0=0$, $|\varphi|\circ i=i\circ|\varphi|$: a morphism of abelian group objects. Identities and composites are preserved since they are computed on components. This defines a functor $\Phi$ from sheaves of abelian groups to abelian group objects, with $\mathrm{forget}\circ\Phi=|-|$ (underlying sheaf of sets, underlying morphism).

**Step 3.** *(3) From an abelian group object to a sheaf of abelian groups.* Let $(\mathcal F,+,0,i)$ be an abelian group object of $\Sh(\mathcal C)$. By (1)(b) its structure maps are families $+_X:\mathcal F(X)^2\to\mathcal F(X)$, $0_X\in\mathcal F(X)$, $i_X:\mathcal F(X)\to\mathcal F(X)$, and by (1)(c) the axioms, taken on $X$-sections, say: $+_X$ is associative and commutative, $+_X(0_X,a)=a$ (hence also $+_X(a,0_X)=a$ by commutativity), $+_X(a,i_X(a))=0_X$. So $(\mathcal F(X),+_X,0_X,i_X)$ is an abelian group. The compatibility of the families with restriction (1)(b) says that every restriction map $\rho:\mathcal F(X)\to\mathcal F(Y)$ satisfies $\rho(a+_Xb)=\rho(a)+_Y\rho(b)$, so it is a group homomorphism. Hence $\Psi(\mathcal F):X\mapsto(\mathcal F(X),+_X,0_X,i_X)$, with the restriction maps of $\mathcal F$, is a functor $\mathcal C^{op}\to$ abelian groups whose underlying presheaf of sets is the sheaf $\mathcal F$; by [lem-weil-pbc-algebraic-sheaf-iff-underlying-sets] it is a sheaf of abelian groups. If $\varphi:\mathcal F\to\mathcal G$ is a morphism of abelian group objects, then $\varphi\circ+=+\circ(\varphi\times\varphi)$ on $X$-sections says $\varphi_X(a+_Xb)=\varphi_X(a)+_X\varphi_X(b)$, so every $\varphi_X$ is a homomorphism and $\varphi$ (a natural transformation) is a morphism $\Psi(\mathcal F)\to\Psi(\mathcal G)$ of sheaves of abelian groups; $\Psi$ is a functor (identity on underlying maps) with $|-|\circ\Psi=\mathrm{forget}$.

**Step 4.** *(4) $\Phi$ and $\Psi$ are inverse isomorphisms of categories; morphisms.* On objects: for a sheaf of abelian groups $\mathcal A$, $\Psi\Phi(\mathcal A)$ has the sets $\mathcal A(X)$, the restriction maps of $\mathcal A$ and the operations $+_X$, $0_X$, $i_X$ of (2), which are the addition, zero and negation of $\mathcal A(X)$; so $\Psi\Phi(\mathcal A)=\mathcal A$. For an abelian group object $(\mathcal F,+,0,i)$, $\Phi\Psi(\mathcal F)$ has underlying sheaf $\mathcal F$ and structure maps with components $+_X$, $0_X$, $i_X$, which are the components of $+,0,i$; by (1)(c) they are $+,0,i$. On morphisms both functors leave the underlying morphism of sheaves of sets unchanged, so $\Psi\Phi$ and $\Phi\Psi$ are the identity functors, and $\Phi$ is an isomorphism of categories commuting with the forgetful functors to $\Sh(\mathcal C)$, with inverse $\Psi$ giving $\mathcal F(X)$ the operations $+_X,0_X,i_X$. In particular, for sheaves of abelian groups $\mathcal A,\mathcal B$ and a morphism of sheaves of sets $\varphi:|\mathcal A|\to|\mathcal B|$: if $\varphi$ underlies a morphism of sheaves of abelian groups it commutes with $+,0,i$ by (2); conversely if it commutes with $+$ alone, its components are homomorphisms by (3), so it is a morphism of sheaves of abelian groups (and then it commutes with $0$ and $i$ too, homomorphisms preserving zero and negatives).

**Step 5.** *(5) Commutative rings.* A commutative ring object $(\mathcal F,+,0,i,\cdot,1)$ is an abelian group object $(\mathcal F,+,0,i)$ together with a commutative monoid object structure $(\cdot,1)$ ((G1)-(G3) for $\cdot,1$) satisfying distributivity $\cdot\circ\langle p_1,+\circ\langle p_2,p_3\rangle\rangle=+\circ\langle\cdot\circ\langle p_1,p_2\rangle,\cdot\circ\langle p_1,p_3\rangle\rangle$ (the other distributive law follows by commutativity of $\cdot$). The argument of (2)-(4) applies verbatim, with the additional families $\cdot_X:\mathcal F(X)^2\to\mathcal F(X)$ and $1_X\in\mathcal F(X)$: for a sheaf of commutative rings $\mathcal O$, $|\mathcal O|$ is a sheaf of sets [lem-weil-pbc-algebraic-sheaf-iff-underlying-sets], restriction maps are ring homomorphisms so $\cdot_X$, $1_X$ are natural, hence morphisms $\cdot:\mathcal F\times\mathcal F\to\mathcal F$, $1:*\to\mathcal F$ (1)(b), and the ring axioms hold on every $\mathcal F(X)$, hence as morphisms (1)(c); conversely, for a commutative ring object, (1)(c) makes each $(\mathcal F(X),+_X,0_X,i_X,\cdot_X,1_X)$ a commutative ring, naturality makes the restriction maps ring homomorphisms, and [lem-weil-pbc-algebraic-sheaf-iff-underlying-sets] (case of commutative rings) makes the resulting presheaf of commutative rings a sheaf. The two constructions are inverse on objects and the identity on morphisms as in (4). Morphisms: a morphism of sheaves of sets $\varphi$ between underlying sheaves of sheaves of commutative rings is a morphism of sheaves of rings iff every $\varphi_X$ is a ring homomorphism, iff (by (1)(c)) $\varphi$ commutes with $+$, $\cdot$ and $1$; a ring homomorphism also preserves $0$ and negatives, so this is iff $\varphi$ commutes with all the structure maps $+,0,i,\cdot,1$.
