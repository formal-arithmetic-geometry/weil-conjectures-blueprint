Let $A$ be a commutative ring, $\mathfrak K\subseteq A$ an ideal, $t\ge1$ an integer, and $u:M\to N$ an $A$-linear map of $A$-modules such that $\mathfrak K^t\operatorname{Ker}(u)=0$ and $\mathfrak K^t\operatorname{Coker}(u)=0$, where $\operatorname{Coker}(u)=N/u(M)$ (equivalently: there is an exact sequence $0\to T\to M\xrightarrow{u}N\to Q\to0$ of $A$-modules with $\mathfrak K^tT=\mathfrak K^tQ=0$). (a) For all $f,g\in\mathfrak K^t$ and every $y\in N$ there is $x\in M$ with $u(x)=fy$, and $gx$ does not depend on the choice of $x$; the resulting map $h_{f,g}=\text{``}fg\text{''}:N\to M$, $y\mapsto gx$, is $A$-linear and satisfies $h_{f,g}\circ u=fg\cdot\mathrm{id}_M$ and $u\circ h_{f,g}=fg\cdot\mathrm{id}_N$. (b) (Naturality.) If $u':M'\to N'$ is another $A$-linear map with $\mathfrak K^t\operatorname{Ker}(u')=\mathfrak K^t\operatorname{Coker}(u')=0$ and $a:M\to M'$, $b:N\to N'$ are $A$-linear maps with $u'\circ a=b\circ u$, then $a\circ h_{f,g}=h'_{f,g}\circ b$ for all $f,g\in\mathfrak K^t$, where $h'_{f,g}$ is the map of (a) for $u'$. (c) For every ideal $J\subseteq A$ the kernel and the cokernel of the induced map $\bar u:M/JM\to N/JN$ are annihilated by $\mathfrak K^{2t}$. (d) (Converse criterion.) Let $v:P\to R$ be any $A$-linear map of $A$-modules such that for all $f,g\in\mathfrak K^t$ there is an $A$-linear map $k:R\to P$ with $k\circ v=fg\cdot\mathrm{id}_P$ and $v\circ k=fg\cdot\mathrm{id}_R$. Then $\operatorname{Ker}(v)$ and $\operatorname{Coker}(v)$ are annihilated by $\mathfrak K^{2t}$.
