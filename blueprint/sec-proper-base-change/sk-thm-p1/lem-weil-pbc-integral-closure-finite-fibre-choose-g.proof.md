Write $R=B'$ for the integral closure and let $q:R\to C'_1\times C'_2$ be the surjective quotient map $R\to R/IR$ followed by the given isomorphism $R/IR\cong C'_1\times C'_2$. The composite $f=\mathrm{pr}_1\circ q:R\to C'_1$ is surjective. Let $K=\ker f$. Since $f$ is surjective, the image of $\operatorname{Spec}(C'_1)\to\operatorname{Spec}(R)$ is $V(K)$, and by hypothesis it is contained in $U$. Let $J$ be the vanishing ideal of the closed complement of $U$, so $V(J)$ is that complement (a closed set is the zero locus of its vanishing ideal). Disjointness gives $V(J+K)=V(J)\cap V(K)=\emptyset$, hence $J+K=R$. Choose $j\in J$ and $k\in K$ with $j+k=1$, and $g_0\in R$ with $q(g_0)=(1,0)$. Then the first component of $q(j)$ is $1$, so $q(g_0j)=(1,0)$, and $g=g_0j$ has image $\varepsilon'$ modulo $IR$. Because $g\in J$, $D(g)\cap V(J)=\emptyset$, i.e. $D(g)\subseteq U$.

Write $\phi:\operatorname{Spec}B\to\operatorname{Spec}R$ for the map induced by $R\to B$. Restricting the isomorphism $\phi|_U$ further over the open $D(g)\subseteq U$ shows that $\phi$ restricted over $D(g)$ is an isomorphism. The restriction of $\phi$ over $D(g)$ is $\operatorname{Spec}$ of the localized map $R_g\to B_g$, as $\phi^{-1}(D(g))=D(g)\subseteq\operatorname{Spec}B$ and the basic opens are the spectra of the localizations. A ring map whose $\operatorname{Spec}$ is an isomorphism of schemes is itself an isomorphism, so $R_g\to B_g$ is bijective.
