*(1)* The contraction $\mathfrak q=\lambda^{-1}(\mathfrak q')$ of a prime is prime, and it is disjoint from $W$ because $\lambda(w)$ is a unit for $w\in W$ and so does not lie in $\mathfrak q'$. Conversely let $\mathfrak p$ be a prime of $A$ disjoint from $W$. By (b) every element of $\mathfrak pA'$ has the form $\lambda(a)\lambda(w)^{-1}$ with $a\in\mathfrak p$, $w\in W$. If $\lambda(b)\in\mathfrak pA'$, then $\lambda(bw)=\lambda(a)$ for some $a\in\mathfrak p$, $w\in W$, so by (c) $vbw=va\in\mathfrak p$ for some $v\in W$, and $b\in\mathfrak p$ since $v,w\notin\mathfrak p$; thus $\lambda^{-1}(\mathfrak pA')=\mathfrak p$, which also shows $1\notin\mathfrak pA'$. Moreover $\mathfrak pA'$ is prime: if $\lambda(a)\lambda(w)^{-1}\cdot\lambda(b)\lambda(w')^{-1}\in\mathfrak pA'$ then $\lambda(ab)\in\mathfrak pA'$, so $ab\in\mathfrak p$, hence $a\in\mathfrak p$ or $b\in\mathfrak p$.

For a prime $\mathfrak q'$ of $A'$, $\lambda(\lambda^{-1}(\mathfrak q'))A'\subseteq\mathfrak q'$ trivially, and if $x=\lambda(a)\lambda(w)^{-1}\in\mathfrak q'$ then $\lambda(a)=x\lambda(w)\in\mathfrak q'$, so $a\in\lambda^{-1}(\mathfrak q')$ and $x\in\lambda(\lambda^{-1}(\mathfrak q'))A'$. Hence the two maps $\mathfrak q'\mapsto\lambda^{-1}(\mathfrak q')$ and $\mathfrak p\mapsto\mathfrak pA'$ are mutually inverse bijections between the primes of $A'$ and the primes of $A$ disjoint from $W$.

*(2)* By transitivity of localization, the composite $A\to A'\to A'_{\mathfrak q'}$ is a localization of $A$ at the set of $a\in A$ with $\lambda(a)\notin\mathfrak q'$, i.e. at $A\setminus\mathfrak q$ (elements of $W$ map to units of $A'$ already). By uniqueness of localizations there is an $A$-algebra isomorphism $e:A_{\mathfrak q}\xrightarrow{\sim}A'_{\mathfrak q'}$. It agrees with the canonical map $a/s\mapsto\lambda(a)/\lambda(s)$ on the image of $A$, and a ring map out of $A_{\mathfrak q}$ is determined by its restriction to $A$, so $e$ is this canonical map.

*(3)* The extended ideals $\mathfrak qA_{\mathfrak q}$ and $\mathfrak q'A'_{\mathfrak q'}$ are the maximal ideals of these local rings, and a ring isomorphism of local rings carries the maximal ideal onto the maximal ideal. So $e$ induces an isomorphism of residue fields $\sigma:\kappa(\mathfrak q)\xrightarrow{\sim}\kappa(\mathfrak q')$, which is an $A$-algebra map since $e$ is, and which sends the residue class of $y$ to the residue class of $e(y)$.
