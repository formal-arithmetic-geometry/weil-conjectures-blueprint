For a minimal prime p, let S be the finite set of the other minimal primes. Minimality implies p is not contained in any r in S. Finite prime avoidance `Ideal.subset_union_prime` ([Mathlib source](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/master/Mathlib/RingTheory/Ideal/Operations.lean)) gives b in p outside all r in S. The finite intersection of S is not contained in p: otherwise primality of p implies one r in S is contained in p, contradicting minimality. Choose a in this intersection outside p. Every minimal prime contains ab, so ab=0 by reducedness and the intersection-of-minimal-primes formula `PrimeSpectrum.zeroLocus_eq_iff` ([Mathlib source](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/master/Mathlib/RingTheory/Spectrum/Prime/Topology.lean)). The sum a+b avoids every minimal prime, hence is a nonzerodivisor by `Ideal.iUnion_minimalPrimes` ([Mathlib source](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/master/Mathlib/RingTheory/Ideal/MinimalPrime/Localization.lean)).

Set x=a/(a+b) in the total fraction ring. Clearing the invertible denominator twice and using ab=0 proves x*x=x. Thus x is integral, being a root of the monic polynomial T(T-1) `integralClosure, IsIntegral, IsIntegrallyClosedIn` ([Mathlib source](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/master/Mathlib/RingTheory/IntegralClosure/Algebra/Basic.lean)). Integral closedness gives e in R mapping to x, and injectivity yields e(a+b)=a and (1-e)(a+b)=b. For every other minimal prime q, primality and a+b not in q imply e in q. Likewise 1-e is in p. Hence p and q are coprime, with the explicit witnesses 1-e and e. This constructs the separating coordinate idempotents directly, without first decomposing the entire total fraction ring.

Enumerate minimalPrimes R by Fin n using its finiteness. Their intersection is the nilradical and hence zero by reducedness `PrimeSpectrum.zeroLocus_eq_iff` ([Mathlib source](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/master/Mathlib/RingTheory/Spectrum/Prime/Topology.lean)). Apply Mathlib Ideal.quotientInfRingEquivPiQuotient to the pairwise coprime family and compose with Ideal.quotEquivOfEq and RingEquiv.quotientBot to obtain R isomorphic to the product of R/p. Every quotient is a domain because p is prime. Package these explicitly defined quotients as CommRingCat objects. The same Chinese remainder theorem handles the empty family, so no nontriviality assumption or separate zero-ring construction is required.
