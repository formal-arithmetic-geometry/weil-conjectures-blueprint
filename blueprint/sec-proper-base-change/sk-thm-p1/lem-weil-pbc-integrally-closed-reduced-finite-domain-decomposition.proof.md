For a minimal prime p, let S be the finite set of the other minimal primes. Minimality implies p is not contained in any r in S. Finite prime avoidance gives b in p outside all r in S. The finite intersection of S is not contained in p: otherwise primality of p implies one r in S is contained in p, contradicting minimality. Choose a in this intersection outside p. Every minimal prime contains ab, so ab=0 by reducedness, since the intersection of the minimal primes is the nilradical. The sum a+b avoids every minimal prime, hence is a nonzerodivisor, since in a reduced ring the set of zero divisors is the union of the minimal primes.

Set x=a/(a+b) in the total fraction ring. Clearing the invertible denominator twice and using ab=0 proves x*x=x. Thus x is integral, being a root of the monic polynomial T(T-1). Integral closedness gives e in R mapping to x, and injectivity yields e(a+b)=a and (1-e)(a+b)=b. For every other minimal prime q, primality and a+b not in q imply e in q. Likewise 1-e is in p. Hence p and q are coprime, with the explicit witnesses 1-e and e. This constructs the separating coordinate idempotents directly, without first decomposing the entire total fraction ring.

Enumerate the minimal primes as $\mathfrak p_1,\dots,\mathfrak p_n$. Their intersection is the nilradical and hence zero by reducedness. By the Chinese remainder theorem for the pairwise coprime family $(\mathfrak p_i)$, $R=R/(\mathfrak p_1\cap\dots\cap\mathfrak p_n)\cong\prod_{i=1}^nR/\mathfrak p_i$. Every quotient $A_i=R/\mathfrak p_i$ is a domain because $\mathfrak p_i$ is prime. The Chinese remainder theorem also covers the empty family (then $R=0$ and $n=0$), so no nontriviality assumption or separate zero-ring argument is required.
