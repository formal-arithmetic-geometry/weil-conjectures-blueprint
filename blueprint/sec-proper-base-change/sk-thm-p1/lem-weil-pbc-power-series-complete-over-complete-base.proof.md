Notation. Write elements of $R$ as $F=\sum_{E\in\mathbf N^d}a_E(F)X^E$ `MvPowerSeries` ([Mathlib source](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/master/Mathlib/RingTheory/MvPowerSeries/Basic.lean)), put $|E|=e_1+\cdots+e_d$, and set $J^m=\Lambda$ for $m\le0$. For $n\ge0$ let $S_n=\{F\in R:\ a_E(F)\in J^{\,n-|E|}\text{ for every }E\text{ with }|E|<n\}$ and $U_n=\{F\in R:\ a_E(F)=0\text{ for every }E\text{ with }|E|<n\}$; so $S_0=U_0=R$ and $U_n\subseteq S_n$. Then $S_n$ is an additive subgroup of $R$ (the coefficient maps $a_E$ are additive and $J^{n-|E|}$ is an additive subgroup), and $S_nS_m\subseteq S_{n+m}$: the coefficient of $FG$ at $G_0$ is the finite sum $\sum_{E+E'=G_0}a_E(F)a_{E'}(G)$, and each term lies in $J^{\max(n-|E|,0)}J^{\max(m-|E'|,0)}\subseteq J^{\max(n-|E|,0)+\max(m-|E'|,0)}\subseteq J^{\,n+m-|G_0|}$ because $|G_0|=|E|+|E'|$. With $S_0=R$ this shows that $S_n$ is an ideal of $R$. Likewise $U_n$ is an ideal of $R$ (if $a_E(F)=0$ for $|E|<n$ then every term $a_E(F)a_{E'}(G)$ with $|E+E'|<n$ has $|E|<n$).

$(X_1,\ldots,X_d)^n=U_n$ for $n\ge0$. The ideal $(X_1,\ldots,X_d)^n$ is generated by the monomials $X^M$ with $|M|=n$, which lie in the ideal $U_n$ (step 1); so $(X)^n\subseteq U_n$. Conversely let $F\in U_n$. The set $\mathcal M_n=\{M\in\mathbf N^d:|M|=n\}$ is finite. For each $E$ with $|E|\ge n$ choose $\mu(E)\in\mathcal M_n$ with $\mu(E)\le E$ componentwise (lower the coordinates of $E$ one unit at a time until the total degree is $n$). For $M\in\mathcal M_n$ put $G_M=\sum_{E:\,|E|\ge n,\ \mu(E)=M}a_E(F)X^{E-M}\in R$ (a family indexed by distinct exponents $E-M$, hence a power series). Then $F=\sum_{M\in\mathcal M_n}X^MG_M$: comparing coefficients at $E$, the right side has $a_E(F)$ if $|E|\ge n$ (the only contribution is from $M=\mu(E)$) and $0$ if $|E|<n$, which is $a_E(F)$ since $F\in U_n$. Hence $F\in(X)^n$. (`MvPowerSeries` ([Mathlib source](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/master/Mathlib/RingTheory/MvPowerSeries/Basic.lean))).

$\mathfrak a^n=S_n$ for every $n\ge0$. ($n=0$: both are $R$.) ($\subseteq$) $\mathfrak a\subseteq S_1$: an element of $JR$ is a finite sum $\sum_k j_kF_k$ with $j_k\in J$, whose constant coefficient $\sum_kj_ka_0(F_k)$ lies in $J$, and $X_i$ has constant coefficient $0$; the condition defining $S_1$ concerns only $E=0$. Since $S_1$ is an ideal and $S_1^n\subseteq S_n$ (step 1, $S_nS_m\subseteq S_{n+m}$), $\mathfrak a^n\subseteq S_1^n\subseteq S_n$. ($\supseteq$) Let $F\in S_n$, $n\ge1$. Write $F=\sum_{|E|<n}a_E(F)X^E+F'$ where $F'$ has the coefficients of $F$ at the $E$ with $|E|\ge n$ and $0$ elsewhere; the first sum is finite. Then $F'\in U_n=(X)^n\subseteq\mathfrak a^n$ (step 2). For $|E|<n$, $a_E(F)\in J^{\,n-|E|}\subseteq(JR)^{n-|E|}\subseteq\mathfrak a^{n-|E|}$ and $X^E\in(X)^{|E|}\subseteq\mathfrak a^{|E|}$, so $a_E(F)X^E\in\mathfrak a^n$. Hence $F\in\mathfrak a^n$.

Injectivity of $\iota:R\to\varprojlim_nR/\mathfrak a^n$. The map is a ring homomorphism `IsAdicComplete` ([Mathlib source](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/master/Mathlib/RingTheory/AdicCompletion/Basic.lean)), so it suffices that its kernel $\bigcap_n\mathfrak a^n$ is $0$. Let $F\in\bigcap_n\mathfrak a^n=\bigcap_nS_n$ (step 3) and fix $E$. For every $m\ge1$, taking $n=m+|E|$ gives $a_E(F)\in J^m$. So $a_E(F)$ maps to $0$ in every $\Lambda/J^m$, i.e. to $0$ under the canonical map $\Lambda\to\varprojlim_m\Lambda/J^m$, which is injective because $\Lambda$ is $J$-adically complete `IsAdicComplete` ([Mathlib source](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/master/Mathlib/RingTheory/AdicCompletion/Basic.lean)); thus $a_E(F)=0$. As $E$ was arbitrary, $F=0$.

Surjectivity of $\iota$. Let $(y_n)_n\in\varprojlim_nR/\mathfrak a^n$ and choose $F_n\in R$ with $F_n\bmod\mathfrak a^n=y_n$. Compatibility gives $F_{n'}-F_n\in\mathfrak a^n=S_n$ for $n\le n'$ (step 3). Fix $E$ and put $c_n=a_E(F_n)\in\Lambda$. For $1\le k\le k'$, $F_{k'+|E|}-F_{k+|E|}\in S_{k+|E|}$, so $c_{k'+|E|}-c_{k+|E|}\in J^k$; hence $z_k=c_{k+|E|}\bmod J^k$ ($k\ge1$) is a compatible family, an element of $\varprojlim_k\Lambda/J^k$. Since $\Lambda$ is $J$-adically complete `IsAdicComplete` ([Mathlib source](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/master/Mathlib/RingTheory/AdicCompletion/Basic.lean)), the canonical map $\Lambda\to\varprojlim_k\Lambda/J^k$ is surjective: there is $b_E\in\Lambda$ with $b_E-c_{k+|E|}\in J^k$ for all $k\ge1$. Let $F=\sum_Eb_EX^E\in R$ `MvPowerSeries` ([Mathlib source](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/master/Mathlib/RingTheory/MvPowerSeries/Basic.lean)). For $n\ge1$ and $|E|<n$, taking $k=n-|E|\ge1$ gives $a_E(F-F_n)=b_E-c_n\in J^{\,n-|E|}$, so $F-F_n\in S_n=\mathfrak a^n$ (step 3), i.e. $\iota(F)_n=F_n\bmod\mathfrak a^n=y_n$ for all $n$. So $\iota(F)=(y_n)_n$.

Conclusion. By steps 4 and 5 the canonical map $R\to\varprojlim_nR/\mathfrak a^n$ is bijective, i.e. $R$ is $\mathfrak a$-adically complete `IsAdicComplete` ([Mathlib source](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/master/Mathlib/RingTheory/AdicCompletion/Basic.lean)). (The hypothesis that $\Lambda$ is Noetherian is not used: the argument holds for every ring $\Lambda$ complete for an arbitrary ideal $J$.)
