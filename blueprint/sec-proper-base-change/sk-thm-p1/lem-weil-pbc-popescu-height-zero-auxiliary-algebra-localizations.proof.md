(Notation and the three universal properties used.) Write $S=A[x_k,y_i,z,t_{ij}]$ for the polynomial ring `MvPolynomial, MvPolynomial.aeval, MvPolynomial.algHom_ext` ([Mathlib source](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/master/Mathlib/Algebra/MvPolynomial/Basic.lean)) and $I\subseteq S$ for the ideal generated by $f_j-\sum_iy_it_{ij}$ ($1\le j\le m$) and $zy_i$ ($1\le i\le r$), so $B=S/I$ with quotient map $q:S\to B$, and $C=P/(f_1,\dots,f_m)$ with quotient map $p:P\to C$ `AlgHom, Ideal.Quotient.algebra, Ideal.Quotient.mkₐ, Ideal (IdemCommSemiring: I * J, I ^ k)` ([Mathlib source](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/master/Mathlib/Algebra/Algebra/Hom.lean), [Mathlib source](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/master/Mathlib/RingTheory/Ideal/Operations.lean), [Mathlib source](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/master/Mathlib/RingTheory/Ideal/Quotient/Operations.lean)). Since $A[x_1,\dots,x_n]=P\subseteq S$ (and $P\subseteq Q_\ell$), $f_j$ is also an element of $S$ and of $Q_\ell$. (a) Polynomial rings: an $A$-algebra homomorphism out of a polynomial ring is given by arbitrary images of the variables, and two that agree on the variables are equal `MvPolynomial, MvPolynomial.aeval, MvPolynomial.algHom_ext` ([Mathlib source](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/master/Mathlib/Algebra/MvPolynomial/Basic.lean)); the same holds over $C$ for $T=C[t_{ij},z]$ with $C$-algebra homomorphisms. (b) Quotients: if $\varphi:D\to E$ is a homomorphism of $A$-algebras with $\varphi(J)=0$ for an ideal $J$, then $\bar\varphi(d+J)=\varphi(d)$ is a well defined homomorphism of $A$-algebras $D/J\to E$ (if $d-d'\in J$ then $\varphi(d)=\varphi(d')$), and it is the only one with $\bar\varphi\circ(D\to D/J)=\varphi$, as $D\to D/J$ is surjective `AlgHom, Ideal.Quotient.algebra, Ideal.Quotient.mkₐ, Ideal (IdemCommSemiring: I * J, I ^ k)` ([Mathlib source](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/master/Mathlib/Algebra/Algebra/Hom.lean), [Mathlib source](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/master/Mathlib/RingTheory/Ideal/Operations.lean), [Mathlib source](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/master/Mathlib/RingTheory/Ideal/Quotient/Operations.lean)). In particular two homomorphisms out of $B$ (resp. $C$) that agree on the classes of the variables are equal, by (a) applied to their composites with $q$ (resp. $p$). (c) Localization at one element: let $g\in D$ and $\varphi:D\to E$ a homomorphism of $A$-algebras with $u=\varphi(g)$ a unit of $E$. Then $\tilde\varphi(d/g^n)=\varphi(d)u^{-n}$ is a well defined homomorphism of $A$-algebras $D_g\to E$: if $d/g^n=d'/g^{n'}$, i.e. $g^k(dg^{n'}-d'g^n)=0$ for some $k$ `Localization.Away` ([Mathlib source](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/master/Mathlib/GroupTheory/MonoidLocalization/Away.lean)), then $u^k(\varphi(d)u^{n'}-\varphi(d')u^n)=0$, and multiplying by $u^{-k-n-n'}$ gives $\varphi(d)u^{-n}=\varphi(d')u^{-n'}$; additivity and multiplicativity follow from the formulas $d/g^n+d'/g^{n'}=(dg^{n'}+d'g^n)/g^{n+n'}$ and $(d/g^n)(d'/g^{n'})=dd'/g^{n+n'}$. It is the only homomorphism with $\tilde\varphi(d/1)=\varphi(d)$, because $d/g^n=(d/1)\,(g/1)^{-n}$. In particular two homomorphisms out of $D_g$ that agree after composition with $D\to D_g$ are equal.

(The map $B_z\to C[t_{ij},z]_z$.) Let $T=C[t_{ij},z]$ ($rm+1$ variables over $C$) and $\iota:T\to T_z$ the localization map. By (a) there is a homomorphism of $A$-algebras $\Phi:S\to T_z$ with $x_k\mapsto\iota(\bar x_k)$, $y_i\mapsto0$, $z\mapsto\iota(z)$, $t_{ij}\mapsto\iota(t_{ij})$. Its restriction to $P\subseteq S$ equals $\iota\circ(C\to T)\circ p$, both being $A$-algebra homomorphisms $P\to T_z$ sending $x_k$ to $\iota(\bar x_k)$ (a); so $\Phi(f_j)=\iota(p(f_j))=0$. Hence $\Phi(f_j-\sum_iy_it_{ij})=0-\sum_i0\cdot\iota(t_{ij})=0$ and $\Phi(zy_i)=\iota(z)\cdot0=0$, so $\Phi(I)=0$ and by (b) $\Phi$ induces $\varphi:B\to T_z$ with $\varphi\circ q=\Phi$; this is the homomorphism of the statement, which is thus well defined. As $\varphi(z)=\iota(z)=z/1$ is a unit of $T_z$, by (c) $\varphi$ induces $\psi:B_z\to T_z$ with $\psi(b/1)=\varphi(b)$. (`MvPolynomial, MvPolynomial.aeval, MvPolynomial.algHom_ext` ([Mathlib source](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/master/Mathlib/Algebra/MvPolynomial/Basic.lean)), `AlgHom, Ideal.Quotient.algebra, Ideal.Quotient.mkₐ, Ideal (IdemCommSemiring: I * J, I ^ k)` ([Mathlib source](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/master/Mathlib/Algebra/Algebra/Hom.lean), [Mathlib source](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/master/Mathlib/RingTheory/Ideal/Operations.lean), [Mathlib source](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/master/Mathlib/RingTheory/Ideal/Quotient/Operations.lean)), `Localization.Away` ([Mathlib source](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/master/Mathlib/GroupTheory/MonoidLocalization/Away.lean))).

(The map back $C[t_{ij},z]_z\to B_z$.) Let $\lambda:B\to B_z$ be the localization map. In $B_z$ one has $\lambda(y_i)=\lambda(zy_i)\,\lambda(z)^{-1}=0$ for every $i$, as $zy_i=0$ in $B$; hence, as $f_j=\sum_iy_it_{ij}$ in $B$, $\lambda(q(f_j))=\sum_i\lambda(y_i)\lambda(t_{ij})=0$. Let $\chi_P:P\to B_z$ be the $A$-algebra homomorphism with $x_k\mapsto\lambda(x_k)$ (a); it equals $\lambda\circ q$ restricted to $P$ (same values on the $x_k$), so $\chi_P(f_j)=0$ for all $j$, $\chi_P$ kills $(f_1,\dots,f_m)$ and induces by (b) an $A$-algebra homomorphism $\chi_C:C\to B_z$ with $\bar x_k\mapsto\lambda(x_k)$. Regard $B_z$ as a $C$-algebra through $\chi_C$; by (a) over $C$ there is a $C$-algebra homomorphism $\chi_T:T\to B_z$ with $t_{ij}\mapsto\lambda(t_{ij})$ and $z\mapsto\lambda(z)$. As $\chi_T(z)=\lambda(z)$ is a unit of $B_z$, (c) gives $\chi:T_z\to B_z$ with $\chi\circ\iota=\chi_T$. All these maps are homomorphisms of $A$-algebras. (`MvPolynomial, MvPolynomial.aeval, MvPolynomial.algHom_ext` ([Mathlib source](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/master/Mathlib/Algebra/MvPolynomial/Basic.lean)), `AlgHom, Ideal.Quotient.algebra, Ideal.Quotient.mkₐ, Ideal (IdemCommSemiring: I * J, I ^ k)` ([Mathlib source](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/master/Mathlib/Algebra/Algebra/Hom.lean), [Mathlib source](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/master/Mathlib/RingTheory/Ideal/Operations.lean), [Mathlib source](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/master/Mathlib/RingTheory/Ideal/Quotient/Operations.lean)), `Localization.Away` ([Mathlib source](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/master/Mathlib/GroupTheory/MonoidLocalization/Away.lean))).

(Proof of (1): $\psi$ and $\chi$ are inverse.) $\psi\circ\chi$: for each $k$, $\psi\chi\iota(\bar x_k)=\psi(\chi_C(\bar x_k))=\psi(\lambda(x_k))=\varphi(x_k)=\iota(\bar x_k)$, so $\psi\circ\chi\circ\iota\circ(C\to T)$ and $\iota\circ(C\to T)$ agree on the $\bar x_k$ and are equal (b); thus $\psi\chi\iota:T\to T_z$ is a $C$-algebra homomorphism, and it sends $t_{ij}\mapsto\psi(\lambda(t_{ij}))=\varphi(t_{ij})=\iota(t_{ij})$ and $z\mapsto\psi(\lambda(z))=\iota(z)$, so $\psi\chi\iota=\iota$ by (a) over $C$, and $\psi\chi=\mathrm{id}$ by (c). $\chi\circ\psi$: on the classes of the variables, $\chi\psi\lambda(x_k)=\chi\varphi(x_k)=\chi\iota(\bar x_k)=\chi_C(\bar x_k)=\lambda(x_k)$, $\chi\psi\lambda(y_i)=\chi(0)=0=\lambda(y_i)$ (step 3), $\chi\psi\lambda(z)=\chi\iota(z)=\lambda(z)$, $\chi\psi\lambda(t_{ij})=\chi\iota(t_{ij})=\lambda(t_{ij})$; so $\chi\psi\lambda=\lambda$ by (b) and $\chi\psi=\mathrm{id}$ by (c). Hence $\psi:B_z\to C[t_{ij},z]_z$ is an isomorphism of $A$-algebras induced by $\varphi$, which is (1). (`MvPolynomial, MvPolynomial.aeval, MvPolynomial.algHom_ext` ([Mathlib source](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/master/Mathlib/Algebra/MvPolynomial/Basic.lean)), `AlgHom, Ideal.Quotient.algebra, Ideal.Quotient.mkₐ, Ideal (IdemCommSemiring: I * J, I ^ k)` ([Mathlib source](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/master/Mathlib/Algebra/Algebra/Hom.lean), [Mathlib source](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/master/Mathlib/RingTheory/Ideal/Operations.lean), [Mathlib source](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/master/Mathlib/RingTheory/Ideal/Quotient/Operations.lean)), `Localization.Away` ([Mathlib source](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/master/Mathlib/GroupTheory/MonoidLocalization/Away.lean))).

(The map $(Q_\ell)_{y_\ell}\to B_{y_\ell}$.) Fix $\ell$ and let $\alpha:Q_\ell\to B$ be the $A$-algebra homomorphism sending each variable $x_k,y_i,t_{ij}$ ($i\ne\ell$) to its class (a), and $\mu:B\to B_{y_\ell}$, $\nu:Q_\ell\to(Q_\ell)_{y_\ell}$ the localization maps. Since $\mu\alpha(y_\ell)=\mu(y_\ell)$ is a unit of $B_{y_\ell}$, (c) gives an $A$-algebra homomorphism $\alpha':(Q_\ell)_{y_\ell}\to B_{y_\ell}$ with $\alpha'\nu=\mu\alpha$. Note $\alpha(f_j)=q(f_j)$, as $\alpha|_P$ and $q|_P$ agree on the $x_k$ (a). (`MvPolynomial, MvPolynomial.aeval, MvPolynomial.algHom_ext` ([Mathlib source](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/master/Mathlib/Algebra/MvPolynomial/Basic.lean)), `Localization.Away` ([Mathlib source](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/master/Mathlib/GroupTheory/MonoidLocalization/Away.lean))).

(The map back $B_{y_\ell}\to(Q_\ell)_{y_\ell}$.) Let $v=\nu(y_\ell)^{-1}\in(Q_\ell)_{y_\ell}$ and $u_j=v\cdot\nu\big(f_j-\sum_{i\ne\ell}y_it_{ij}\big)$ for $1\le j\le m$. By (a) there is an $A$-algebra homomorphism $\beta:S\to(Q_\ell)_{y_\ell}$ with $x_k\mapsto\nu(x_k)$, $y_i\mapsto\nu(y_i)$, $t_{ij}\mapsto\nu(t_{ij})$ for $i\ne\ell$, $z\mapsto0$, $t_{\ell j}\mapsto u_j$. On $P\subseteq S$ it agrees with $\nu$ (same values on the $x_k$), so $\beta(f_j)=\nu(f_j)$. Then $\beta(f_j-\sum_iy_it_{ij})=\nu(f_j)-\sum_{i\ne\ell}\nu(y_i)\nu(t_{ij})-\nu(y_\ell)\,v\,\nu\big(f_j-\sum_{i\ne\ell}y_it_{ij}\big)=\nu\big(f_j-\sum_{i\ne\ell}y_it_{ij}\big)-\nu\big(f_j-\sum_{i\ne\ell}y_it_{ij}\big)=0$, and $\beta(zy_i)=0\cdot\nu(y_i)=0$. So $\beta(I)=0$ and (b) gives $\bar\beta:B\to(Q_\ell)_{y_\ell}$ with $\bar\beta q=\beta$; since $\bar\beta(y_\ell)=\nu(y_\ell)$ is a unit, (c) gives $\beta':B_{y_\ell}\to(Q_\ell)_{y_\ell}$ with $\beta'\mu=\bar\beta$. By construction $\beta'$ sends $z\mapsto0$ and $t_{\ell j}\mapsto y_\ell^{-1}(f_j-\sum_{i\ne\ell}y_it_{ij})$, the formulas of the statement. (`MvPolynomial, MvPolynomial.aeval, MvPolynomial.algHom_ext` ([Mathlib source](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/master/Mathlib/Algebra/MvPolynomial/Basic.lean)), `AlgHom, Ideal.Quotient.algebra, Ideal.Quotient.mkₐ, Ideal (IdemCommSemiring: I * J, I ^ k)` ([Mathlib source](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/master/Mathlib/Algebra/Algebra/Hom.lean), [Mathlib source](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/master/Mathlib/RingTheory/Ideal/Operations.lean), [Mathlib source](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/master/Mathlib/RingTheory/Ideal/Quotient/Operations.lean)), `Localization.Away` ([Mathlib source](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/master/Mathlib/GroupTheory/MonoidLocalization/Away.lean))).

(Proof of (2): $\alpha'$ and $\beta'$ are inverse.) $\beta'\alpha'$: $\beta'\alpha'\nu=\beta'\mu\alpha=\bar\beta\alpha$, and $\bar\beta\alpha$ sends $x_k,y_i,t_{ij}$ ($i\ne\ell$) to $\beta(x_k)=\nu(x_k)$, $\nu(y_i)$, $\nu(t_{ij})$; so $\beta'\alpha'\nu=\nu$ (a) and $\beta'\alpha'=\mathrm{id}$ (c). $\alpha'\beta'$: in $B_{y_\ell}$, $\mu(z)=\mu(zy_\ell)\mu(y_\ell)^{-1}=0$; and since $f_j-\sum_{i\ne\ell}y_it_{ij}=y_\ell t_{\ell j}$ in $B$ (the relation $f_j=\sum_iy_it_{ij}$), $\alpha'(u_j)=\mu(y_\ell)^{-1}\mu\alpha\big(f_j-\sum_{i\ne\ell}y_it_{ij}\big)=\mu(y_\ell)^{-1}\mu\big(q(f_j)-\sum_{i\ne\ell}y_it_{ij}\big)=\mu(y_\ell)^{-1}\mu(y_\ell)\mu(t_{\ell j})=\mu(t_{\ell j})$, using $\alpha'(v)=\alpha'(\nu(y_\ell))^{-1}=\mu(y_\ell)^{-1}$ and $\alpha(f_j)=q(f_j)$ (step 5). Hence $\alpha'\beta'\mu=\alpha'\bar\beta$ sends $x_k\mapsto\mu(x_k)$, $y_i\mapsto\mu(y_i)$, $t_{ij}\mapsto\mu(t_{ij})$ ($i\ne\ell$), $z\mapsto0=\mu(z)$, $t_{\ell j}\mapsto\alpha'(u_j)=\mu(t_{\ell j})$, so $\alpha'\beta'\mu=\mu$ (b) and $\alpha'\beta'=\mathrm{id}$ (c). Thus $\alpha':(Q_\ell)_{y_\ell}\to B_{y_\ell}$, induced by $Q_\ell\to B$, is an isomorphism of $A$-algebras with inverse $\beta'$, which is (2).
