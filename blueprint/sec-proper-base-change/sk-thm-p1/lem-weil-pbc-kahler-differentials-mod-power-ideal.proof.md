*(The exact sequence of Lemma 00RU, tensored with $S/I^n$.)* Put $\mathfrak a=I^{n+1}$, so $S'=S/\mathfrak a$. By `KaehlerDifferential.exact_kerCotangentToTensor_mapBaseChange` ([Mathlib source](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/master/Mathlib/RingTheory/Kaehler/Basic.lean)) (with $(A,B,\mathfrak a,C):=(R,S,I^{n+1},S')$) there are maps $\mathfrak a/\mathfrak a^2\xrightarrow{\delta}S'\otimes_S\Omega_{S/R}\xrightarrow{w}\Omega_{S'/R}$, $\delta(\bar a)=1\otimes\mathrm da$, $w(c\otimes\mathrm ds)=c\,\mathrm d\bar s$, with $\ker w=\operatorname{im}\delta$ and $w$ surjective. All three modules are killed by $\mathfrak a$, hence are $S'$-modules, and the $S$-linear map $\delta$ is then $S'$-linear; $w$ is $S'$-linear. Since $I^{n+1}\subseteq I^n$, $S/I^n$ is an $S'$-module (a quotient of $S'$). By right exactness of the tensor product `rTensor_exact, lTensor_exact` ([Mathlib source](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/master/Mathlib/LinearAlgebra/TensorProduct/RightExactness.lean)) (ring $S'$, $N=S/I^n$) the sequence $$(\mathfrak a/\mathfrak a^2)\otimes_{S'}S/I^n\xrightarrow{\delta\otimes1}(S'\otimes_S\Omega_{S/R})\otimes_{S'}S/I^n\xrightarrow{w\otimes1}\Omega_{S'/R}\otimes_{S'}S/I^n\to0$$ is exact: $\ker(w\otimes1)=\operatorname{im}(\delta\otimes1)$ and $w\otimes1$ is surjective.

*(The middle term is $\Omega_{S/R}\otimes_SS/I^n$, and the map is the map of the statement.)* By `TensorProduct.AlgebraTensorModule.cancelBaseChange` ([Mathlib source](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/master/Mathlib/LinearAlgebra/TensorProduct/Tower.lean)) (ring map $S\to S'$, $S'$-module $M=S/I^n$, $S$-module $N=\Omega_{S/R}$), together with the symmetry of the tensor product, there is an isomorphism of $S/I^n$-modules $c:(S'\otimes_S\Omega_{S/R})\otimes_{S'}S/I^n\xrightarrow{\sim}\Omega_{S/R}\otimes_SS/I^n$, $(c'\otimes\omega)\otimes\bar t\mapsto\omega\otimes c'\bar t$, with inverse $\omega\otimes\bar t\mapsto(1\otimes\omega)\otimes\bar t$. The composite $\phi=(w\otimes1)\circ c^{-1}:\Omega_{S/R}\otimes_SS/I^n\to\Omega_{S'/R}\otimes_{S'}S/I^n$ sends $\mathrm ds\otimes\bar t\mapsto(1\otimes\mathrm ds)\otimes\bar t\mapsto w(1\otimes\mathrm ds)\otimes\bar t=\mathrm d\bar s\otimes\bar t$; since the $\mathrm ds$ generate $\Omega_{S/R}$ as an $S$-module (`KaehlerDifferential` ([Mathlib source](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/master/Mathlib/RingTheory/Kaehler/Basic.lean)): $\Omega_{S/R}$ is generated by the classes $\mathrm da$), $\phi$ is the $S/I^n$-linear map of the statement. By step 1, $\phi$ is surjective and $\ker\phi=c(\operatorname{im}(\delta\otimes1))$, which is the $S/I^n$-submodule generated by the elements $c((1\otimes\mathrm da)\otimes\bar t)=\mathrm da\otimes\bar t$, $a\in I^{n+1}$, $t\in S$ (the image of a tensor map is generated by the images of elementary tensors).

*(Leibniz: $\mathrm d(I^{n+1})\subseteq I^n\Omega_{S/R}$.)* For $a_0,\dots,a_n\in S$ the Leibniz rule of the derivation $\mathrm d$ (`KaehlerDifferential` ([Mathlib source](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/master/Mathlib/RingTheory/Kaehler/Basic.lean))), applied $n$ times (induction on $n$: $\mathrm d(a_0\cdots a_n)=a_n\,\mathrm d(a_0\cdots a_{n-1})+a_0\cdots a_{n-1}\,\mathrm da_n$), gives $\mathrm d(a_0a_1\cdots a_n)=\sum_{i=0}^n\big(\prod_{j\ne i}a_j\big)\mathrm da_i$. If all $a_j\in I$, each coefficient $\prod_{j\ne i}a_j$ is a product of $n$ elements of $I$, so lies in $I^n$, and $\mathrm d(a_0\cdots a_n)\in I^n\Omega_{S/R}$. Every $a\in I^{n+1}$ is a finite sum of such products and $\mathrm d$ is additive, so $\mathrm da\in I^n\Omega_{S/R}$ for all $a\in I^{n+1}$.

*(Conclusion.)* Let $a\in I^{n+1}$, $t\in S$. By step 3 write $\mathrm da=\sum_kx_k\omega_k$ with $x_k\in I^n$, $\omega_k\in\Omega_{S/R}$; then in $\Omega_{S/R}\otimes_SS/I^n$ one has $\mathrm da\otimes\bar t=\sum_k\omega_k\otimes\overline{x_kt}=0$, because $x_kt\in I^n$. So the generators of $\ker\phi$ found in step 2 are all $0$, i.e. $\ker\phi=0$ and $\phi$ is injective; it is surjective by step 2. A bijective $S/I^n$-linear map is an isomorphism of $S/I^n$-modules, so the map of the statement is an isomorphism.
