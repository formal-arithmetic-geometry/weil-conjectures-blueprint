Abelian sheaves on the small etale site form a Grothendieck abelian category, hence have enough injectives [lem-sp-grothendieck-enough-injectives]; choose an injective resolution $F\to I^\bullet$. Put $f=p_*$, $K=f(I^\bullet)$ and $L=\iota(K)$, where $\iota$ is the inclusion of sheaves into presheaves. The classical right derived functors $R^qf(F)$ are computed as $H^q(K)$ using this resolution [def-sp-injective-resolutions]; [def-sp-left-exact-additive]; [lem-sp-derived-well-defined]. For a bounded-below complex such as $F[0]$, the $q$-th cohomology of the total derived functor $Rf(F[0])$ is canonically the classical right derived value $R^qf(F)$ [def-weil-pbc-direct-image]. Thus no separate choice or comparison of arbitrary K-injective resolutions is needed. ([def-sp-injective-resolutions], [def-sp-left-exact-additive], [lem-sp-derived-well-defined]).

For $V$ in $S_{\Et}$, the value of $L$ at $V$ is by definition the value of $I^\bullet$ at the base-change object $X\times_SV$, since $p_*$ is precomposition with the base-change functor $S_{\Et}\to X_{\Et}$, $V\mapsto X\times_SV$, which preserves finite limits [def-weil-pbc-direct-image]. The same identification holds for differentials and restriction maps. ([def-weil-pbc-direct-image]).

Construct an isomorphism $e_P$ from the specified presheaf $P_q:V\mapsto H^q(X\times_SV,F)$ to the presheaf cohomology $\mathcal H^q(L)$. Its component at $V$ is the identification $H^q(X\times_SV,F)\cong H^q(I^\bullet(X\times_SV))$ given by the resolution, followed by the identification $H^q(I^\bullet(X\times_SV))=H^q(L(V))\cong\mathcal H^q(L)(V)$; the latter holds because evaluation at $V$ is exact on presheaves (limits and colimits of presheaves are computed pointwise), hence commutes with cohomology. For a restriction $g:V'\to V$, the derived restriction map is the map induced on cohomology by the restriction maps of $I^\bullet$ [lem-sp-derived-well-defined], and the identification above is natural in the evaluation point. This proves naturality of $e_P$, including the specified derived restriction maps.

Sheafification $a$ is exact (it is a left adjoint and preserves finite limits). The counit gives a termwise natural isomorphism $a(\iota(K))\cong K$, hence an isomorphism of complexes $e_K:a(L)\cong K$. Exactness of $a$ identifies $a(\mathcal H^q(L))$ with $\mathcal H^q(a(L))$, and applying $\mathcal H^q$ to $e_K$ identifies this with $\mathcal H^q(K)$.

Compose $a(e_P)$, the identification $a(\mathcal H^q(L))\cong\mathcal H^q(a(L))$, $\mathcal H^q(e_K)$, the identification $\mathcal H^q(K)\cong R^qf(F)$ given by the resolution, and the identification of $R^qf(F)$ with $\mathcal H^q(Rp_*(F[0]))$. This is the required isomorphism from the sheafification of $P_q$ to $R^qp_*F=\mathcal H^q(Rp_*(F[0]))$. All components are the canonical resolution or cohomology comparisons; the restriction naturality was verified in the preceding step.
