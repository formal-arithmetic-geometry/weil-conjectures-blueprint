(The intermediate field and its constants.) Put $B=kK^p\subseteq K$. Since $K$ has characteristic $p$, Frobenius $a\mapsto a^p$ is a field homomorphism onto the subfield $K^p$: $(a+b)^p=a^p+b^p$ and $(a^{-1})^p=(a^p)^{-1}$ for $a\ne0$. The $k$-derivation $\mathrm d_{K/k}:K\to\Omega_{K/k}$ `KaehlerDifferential` ([Mathlib source](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/master/Mathlib/RingTheory/Kaehler/Basic.lean)) kills $k$ by definition and kills $K^p$, since $\mathrm d(a^p)=p a^{p-1}\mathrm da=0$. Its kernel is a subfield: additivity and Leibniz give closure under sums and products, and $0=\mathrm d(1)=\mathrm d(aa^{-1})=a\mathrm d(a^{-1})+a^{-1}\mathrm da$ gives $\mathrm d(a^{-1})=-a^{-2}\mathrm da$. Therefore $\mathrm d_{K/k}$ kills every $b\in B$.

(The presentation supplied by the $p$-basis.) Form $P=B[X_i:i\in I]$ `MvPolynomial, MvPolynomial.aeval, MvPolynomial.algHom_ext` ([Mathlib source](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/master/Mathlib/Algebra/MvPolynomial/Basic.lean)) and its evaluation map $e:P\to K$, $X_i\mapsto t_i$. Let $J=(X_i^p-t_i^p:i\in I)\subseteq P$; $t_i^p\in K^p\subseteq B$, so $J\subseteq\ker e$. Every polynomial involves only finitely many variables. In $P/J$ replace each factor $X_i^{q_ip+r_i}$, $0\le r_i<p$, by $(t_i^p)^{q_i}X_i^{r_i}$; applying this to each monomial shows that the classes of $X^E=\prod_iX_i^{e_i}$ for finitely supported $0\le e_i<p$ span $P/J$ over $B$. Their images $t^E$ form a $B$-basis of $K$ by the hypothesis, so the induced $B$-linear map $P/J\to K$ is surjective and injective: any relation among the spanning classes maps to a relation among the independent $t^E$, forcing all its coefficients to be zero. Hence $\ker e=J$ and $K\cong P/J$ as $B$-algebras, with the class of $X_i$ identified with $t_i$.

(Differentials over $B$.) By `KaehlerDifferential.mvPolynomialBasis` ([Mathlib source](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/master/Mathlib/RingTheory/Kaehler/Polynomial.lean)), $\Omega_{P/B}$ is free over $P$ on $(\mathrm dX_i)_{i\in I}$ and $K\otimes_P\Omega_{P/B}$ is free over $K$ on $(1\otimes\mathrm dX_i)_{i\in I}$. Apply `KaehlerDifferential.exact_kerCotangentToTensor_mapBaseChange` ([Mathlib source](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/master/Mathlib/RingTheory/Kaehler/Basic.lean)) to $B\to P\to P/J\cong K$: its exact sequence is $J/J^2\xrightarrow{\delta}K\otimes_P\Omega_{P/B}\xrightarrow{w}\Omega_{K/B}\to0$, where $\delta(f\bmod J^2)=1\otimes\mathrm df$ and $w(1\otimes\mathrm dX_i)=\mathrm dt_i$. For each generator $f_i=X_i^p-t_i^p$ of $J$, $\mathrm df_i=pX_i^{p-1}\mathrm dX_i-\mathrm d_B(t_i^p)=0$, because $p=0$ in $B$ and $t_i^p$ is a constant of $P/B$. For $f=\sum_{j=1}^nh_jf_{i_j}\in J$ the Leibniz rule gives $1\otimes\mathrm df=\sum_j e(h_j)\otimes\mathrm df_{i_j}+\sum_j e(f_{i_j})\otimes\mathrm dh_j=0$. Thus $\delta=0$, $w$ is an isomorphism, and $(\mathrm d_{K/B}t_i)_{i\in I}$ is a $K$-basis of $\Omega_{K/B}$. (`KaehlerDifferential` ([Mathlib source](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/master/Mathlib/RingTheory/Kaehler/Basic.lean))).

(Return to differentials over $k$.) Apply `KaehlerDifferential.exact_mapBaseChange_map` ([Mathlib source](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/master/Mathlib/RingTheory/Kaehler/Basic.lean)) to $k\to B\to K$. It gives $K\otimes_B\Omega_{B/k}\xrightarrow{u}\Omega_{K/k}\xrightarrow{v}\Omega_{K/B}\to0$, where $u(c\otimes\mathrm d_{B/k}b)=c\,\mathrm d_{K/k}b$ and $v(\mathrm d_{K/k}a)=\mathrm d_{K/B}a$. Step 1 shows $u=0$ on these generators, hence $u=0$. Exactness says $\ker v=\operatorname{im}u=0$ and $v$ is surjective, so $v$ is a $K$-linear isomorphism. It sends each $\mathrm d_{K/k}t_i$ to $\mathrm d_{K/B}t_i$, which is a basis by Step 3. Therefore $(\mathrm d_{K/k}t_i)_{i\in I}$ is a $K$-basis of $\Omega_{K/k}$, including the case $I=\emptyset$.
