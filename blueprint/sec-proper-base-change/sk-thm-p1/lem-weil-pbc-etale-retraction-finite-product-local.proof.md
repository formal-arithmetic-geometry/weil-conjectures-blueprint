**Step 1.** *Setup and decomposition upstairs.* Let $B$ be a finite $R$-algebra. Apply [lem-weil-pbc-finite-algebra-etale-idempotent-decomposition] to $R\to B$ and the prime $\mathfrak m$: there are a ring map $R\to R'$ with $\operatorname{Spec}R'\to\operatorname{Spec}R$ etale, a prime $\mathfrak m'\subset R'$ with preimage $\mathfrak m$ in $R$ and $\kappa=\kappa(\mathfrak m)\to\kappa(\mathfrak m')$ bijective (for the maximal ideal of the local ring $R$, $R_{\mathfrak m}=R$ and $\kappa(\mathfrak m)=R/\mathfrak m=\kappa$), an integer $n\ge0$, complete orthogonal idempotents $e_1,\dots,e_n,e_\infty$ of $C=R'\otimes_RB$, and primes $Q_1,\dots,Q_n$ of $C$ over $\mathfrak m'$, such that $e_i\notin Q_i$, and every prime $Q$ of $C$ over $\mathfrak m'$ contains $e_\infty$ and equals $Q_i$ whenever $e_i\notin Q$.

**Step 2.** *The retraction.* $(R',\mathfrak m')$ satisfies the hypothesis of the statement ($\operatorname{Spec}R'\to\operatorname{Spec}R$ etale, $\mathfrak m'$ over $\mathfrak m$, $\kappa\to\kappa(\mathfrak m')$ an isomorphism, by step 1), so there is an $R$-algebra homomorphism $\tau:R'\to R$ with $\tau^{-1}(\mathfrak m)=\mathfrak m'$.

**Step 3.** *Base change back along $\tau$.* Let $\varphi:C=R'\otimes_RB\to B$ be the ring homomorphism $r'\otimes b\mapsto\tau(r')b$ (well defined by the universal property of the tensor product of commutative $R$-algebras, applied to the $R$-algebra maps $R'\xrightarrow{\tau}R\to B$ and $\mathrm{id}_B$). It is surjective, since $\varphi(1\otimes b)=b$; this is the identification $B=(B\otimes_RR')\otimes_{R',\tau}R$ of the source, i.e. $B=C/\ker\varphi$. Its restriction to $R'$ (via $r'\mapsto r'\otimes1$) is $r'\mapsto\tau(r')\cdot1_B$. Hence for every prime $\mathfrak q$ of $B$ whose preimage in $R$ is $\mathfrak m$, the prime $\varphi^{-1}(\mathfrak q)$ of $C$ has preimage $\tau^{-1}(\mathfrak m)=\mathfrak m'$ in $R'$ (step 2), i.e. lies over $\mathfrak m'$; and $\mathfrak q=\varphi(\varphi^{-1}(\mathfrak q))$ by surjectivity.

**Step 4.** *Idempotents of $B$ and the primes over $\mathfrak m$.* Put $f_i=\varphi(e_i)$ ($1\le i\le n$) and $f_\infty=\varphi(e_\infty)$; as $\varphi$ is a ring homomorphism these are complete orthogonal idempotents of $B$. Let $\mathfrak q$ be a prime of $B$ over $\mathfrak m$ and $Q=\varphi^{-1}(\mathfrak q)$, a prime of $C$ over $\mathfrak m'$ (step 3). By step 1, $e_\infty\in Q$, so $f_\infty\in\mathfrak q$. If $f_i\notin\mathfrak q$, then $e_i\notin Q$, so $Q=Q_i$ (step 1) and $\mathfrak q=\varphi(Q_i)$ (step 3). Hence: every prime of $B$ over $\mathfrak m$ contains $f_\infty$, and for each $i$ there is at most one prime of $B$ over $\mathfrak m$ not containing $f_i$, namely $\varphi(Q_i)$.

**Step 5.** *The factors.* For an idempotent $f$ of $B$, the ideal $fB$ is a ring with unit $f$ and an $R$-algebra, and $\pi_f:B\to fB$, $b\mapsto fb$, is a surjective $R$-algebra homomorphism with kernel $(1-f)B$ (if $fb=0$ then $b=(1-f)b$). So $fB$ is a finite $R$-algebra (images of $R$-module generators of $B$ generate it), and $\mathfrak n\mapsto\pi_f^{-1}(\mathfrak n)$ is a bijection from the primes of $fB$ to the primes of $B$ containing $1-f$, which are exactly the primes of $B$ not containing $f$ (as $f(1-f)=0$, a prime contains $f$ or $1-f$, and not both since $f+(1-f)=1$); since $R\to fB$ is $\pi_f\circ(R\to B)$, $\mathfrak n$ and $\pi_f^{-1}(\mathfrak n)$ have the same preimage in $R$. If $fB\neq0$ it has a maximal ideal, and every maximal ideal of the finite $R$-algebra $fB$ has preimage $\mathfrak m$ in $R$ [lem-weil-pbc-finite-algebra-local-radical]. Apply this to $f_\infty$: a maximal ideal of $f_\infty B$ would give a prime of $B$ over $\mathfrak m$ not containing $f_\infty$, impossible by step 4; so $f_\infty B=0$, i.e. $f_\infty=0$. Apply it to $f_i$ with $f_i\neq0$: every maximal ideal of $f_iB$ corresponds to a prime of $B$ over $\mathfrak m$ not containing $f_i$, of which there is at most one (step 4), and the correspondence is injective; so $f_iB$ has exactly one maximal ideal, i.e. is a local ring.

**Step 6.** *Conclusion.* Let $i_1<\dots<i_r$ be the indices $i\in\{1,\dots,n\}$ with $f_i\neq0$ ($r\ge0$). Since $f_\infty=0$ and $f_i=0$ for the other $i$, $f_{i_1},\dots,f_{i_r}$ are orthogonal idempotents with sum $1$, so $B\to f_{i_1}B\times\dots\times f_{i_r}B$, $b\mapsto(f_{i_1}b,\dots,f_{i_r}b)$, is an $R$-algebra isomorphism (its inverse is $(b_1,\dots,b_r)\mapsto b_1+\dots+b_r$: $\sum_kf_{i_k}b=b$, and $f_{i_k}(\sum_lb_l)=b_k$ for $b_l\in f_{i_l}B$ by orthogonality). By step 5 each $B_k=f_{i_k}B$ is a local ring. (If $B=0$, then $r=0$ and $B$ is the empty product.)
