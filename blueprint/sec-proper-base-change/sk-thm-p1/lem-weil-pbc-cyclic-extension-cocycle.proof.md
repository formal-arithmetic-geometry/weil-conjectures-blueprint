The map $F\to A$, $e_i\mapsto f_i$, kills every $f_ie_j-f_je_i$, so it induces $\mu:M_f\to A$ with image $J$. For $x\in Z_f(P)$, the map $F\to P$, $e_i\mapsto x_i$, kills the same relations and induces $\hat x$. Every element of $K_f$ is represented by $\sum_i a_i e_i$ with $\sum_i a_i f_i=0$. Consequently $x$ is admissible exactly when $\sum_i a_i x_i=0$ for every such relation among the $f_i$. For admissible $x$, the map $(p,a)\mapsto a\bmod J$ kills each generator $(x_i,-f_i)$ of $W_x$, hence gives $\pi_x$; it is surjective. If $[(p,a)]\in\ker\pi_x$, write $a=\sum_i b_i f_i$. Adding $\sum_i b_i(x_i,-f_i)$ shows $[(p,a)]=[(p+\sum_i b_i x_i,0)]$, so $\ker\pi_x=\operatorname{Im}\iota_x$. If $\iota_x(p)=0$, write $(p,0)=\sum_i b_i(x_i,-f_i)$; then $\sum_i b_i f_i=0$, and admissibility from step 1 gives $p=\sum_i b_i x_i=0$. Thus $\iota_x$ is injective and the sequence in (1) is exact. In the extension of (2), $q(f_ie)=f_i(1\bmod J)=0$, so exactness gives a unique $x_i\in P$ with $j(x_i)=f_ie$. Applying the injective $j$ to $f_ix_j-f_jx_i$ gives $f_if_je-f_jf_ie=0$, hence $x\in Z_f(P)$. If $\sum_i a_i f_i=0$, then $j(\sum_i a_i x_i)=(\sum_i a_i f_i)e=0$; injectivity of $j$ and step 1 show that $x$ is admissible. The map $h:P\oplus A\to E$, $(p,a)\mapsto j(p)+ae$, kills $(x_i,-f_i)$ because $j(x_i)=f_ie$; it therefore induces $\theta:E_f(P,x)\to E$. For $y\in E$, choose $a\in A$ representing $q(y)\in A/J$; then $y-ae\in\ker q=\operatorname{Im}j$, proving surjectivity. If $h(p,a)=0$, then $a\in J$; write $a=\sum_i b_i f_i$. Injectivity of $j$ gives $p=-\sum_i b_i x_i$, so $(p,a)=-\sum_i b_i(x_i,-f_i)\in W_x$, proving injectivity. The defining formulas give $\theta\iota_x=j$ and $q\theta=\pi_x$. For $x'_i=x_i-f_im$, one has $f_ix'_j-f_jx'_i=f_ix_j-f_jx_i=0$. For every relation $\sum_i a_i f_i=0$, also $\sum_i a_i x'_i=\sum_i a_i x_i-(\sum_i a_i f_i)m=0$, so step 1 makes $x'$ admissible. The map $h_m:P\oplus A\to P\oplus A$, $(p,a)\mapsto(p+am,a)$, sends each generator $(x_i,-f_i)$ of $W_x$ to $(x'_i,-f_i)$ of $W_{x'}$. Its inverse $h_{-m}$ sends each generator back. Thus $h_m$ induces the claimed isomorphism of quotients; its restriction to $(p,0)$ and its second coordinate show compatibility with $\iota$ and $\pi$.
