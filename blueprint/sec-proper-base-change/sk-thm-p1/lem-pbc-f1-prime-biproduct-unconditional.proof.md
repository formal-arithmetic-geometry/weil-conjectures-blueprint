Taking underlying abelian sheaves is additive by reflexivity, and Sheaf.functorH is additive. For their composite F, identify F(H) with the product of F(G i) using F.mapIso(e), Functor.mapBiproduct and AddCommGrpCat.biproductIsoPi. The coordinate at i is F.map(e.hom composed with biproduct.π G i). This is a product equivalence also for the empty index type. Subsingleton and finiteness are equivalent to their coordinatewise versions: evaluation is surjective because every factor contains zero; conversely products of subsingletons are subsingletons and finite products of finite types are finite. (`CategoryTheory.Limits.HasFiniteBiproducts` ([Mathlib source](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/master/Mathlib/CategoryTheory/Limits/Shapes/Biproducts.lean))).

For any scheme map a and degree q, form the natural transformation with components Etale.cohomologyMap a (-) q from the underlying-sheaf cohomology functor to its inverse-image counterpart. Both functors are additive; naturality is the existing Etale.cohomologyMap_map. In the product coordinates, surjectivity implies surjectivity of each factor by lifting a vector supported at that factor, and the converse follows by choosing coordinate preimages. Injectivity implies factorwise injectivity because each summand has injection biproduct.ι G i composed with e.inv, retracted by e.hom composed with biproduct.π G i, and the square commutes by naturality. The converse follows by the joint injectivity of coordinates. Combine injectivity and surjectivity for bijectivity. Unfold C1, C2, C5, C7 and C8 and apply the coordinatewise tests degree by degree. For C2 and C7 the affine or proper instance is shared. For C5 use the assumed constructibility of H in the forward direction and hGc i in the reverse direction; no constructibility-of-biproduct theorem is required. Assemble the five implications into the stated equivalence by introducing or projecting the five conjunctions. ([def-weil-pbc-eight-curve-assertions]).
