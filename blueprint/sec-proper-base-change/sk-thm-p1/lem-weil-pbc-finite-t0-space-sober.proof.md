*Step 1 (an irreducible set in a finite union of closed sets lies in one of them).* Let $Z\subseteq Y$ be irreducible `irreducibleComponents` ([Mathlib source](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/master/Mathlib/Topology/Irreducible.lean)) and let $F_1,\dots,F_n$ ($n\ge1$) be closed subsets of $Y$ with $Z\subseteq F_1\cup\dots\cup F_n$. We show by induction on $n$ that $Z\subseteq F_i$ for some $i$. For $n=1$ there is nothing to prove. For $n\ge2$ put $G=F_2\cup\dots\cup F_n$, closed in $Y$ as a finite union of closed sets. Then $Z=(Z\cap F_1)\cup(Z\cap G)$, and $Z\cap F_1$, $Z\cap G$ are closed in the subspace $Z$. As $Z$ is irreducible, $Z=Z\cap F_1$ or $Z=Z\cap G$, i.e. $Z\subseteq F_1$ or $Z\subseteq G=F_2\cup\dots\cup F_n$; in the second case the induction hypothesis (for $n-1$ sets) gives $Z\subseteq F_i$ for some $i\ge2$.

*Step 2 (existence of a generic point).* Let $Z\subseteq Y$ be irreducible and closed. Then $Z$ is nonempty (irreducible sets are nonempty `irreducibleComponents` ([Mathlib source](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/master/Mathlib/Topology/Irreducible.lean))) and finite ($Y$ is finite). For $z\in Z$ the closure $\overline{\{z\}}$ is closed, contains $z$, and is contained in $Z$ because $Z$ is a closed set containing $z$. Hence $Z=\bigcup_{z\in Z}\overline{\{z\}}$, a union of finitely many ($|Z|\ge1$) closed subsets of $Y$. By Step 1 there is $\eta\in Z$ with $Z\subseteq\overline{\{\eta\}}$; together with $\overline{\{\eta\}}\subseteq Z$ this gives $\overline{\{\eta\}}=Z$.

*Step 3 (uniqueness, from $T_0$).* Let $\eta,\eta'\in Z$ with $\overline{\{\eta\}}=Z=\overline{\{\eta'\}}$, and suppose $\eta\ne\eta'$. By the $T_0$ hypothesis there is an open $U\subseteq Y$ containing exactly one of them; by symmetry of the hypotheses in $\eta,\eta'$ we may assume $\eta\in U$ and $\eta'\notin U$. Then $Y\setminus U$ is a closed set containing $\eta'$, hence it contains $\overline{\{\eta'\}}=Z\ni\eta$, so $\eta\notin U$: a contradiction. Hence $\eta=\eta'$. With Step 2, every irreducible closed $Z\subseteq Y$ has exactly one point whose closure is $Z$, which is the statement.
