Inclusions of submodules. For $k\ge0$: $J^{dk}=(J^d)^k\subseteq I^k$ (since $J^d\subseteq I$ and powers of ideals are monotone), hence $J^{dk}M\subseteq I^kM$; likewise $I^{cn}=(I^c)^n\subseteq J^n$, hence $I^{cn}M\subseteq J^nM$. So there are surjective $A$-linear quotient maps $\pi^{JI}_k:M/J^{dk}M\to M/I^kM$ and $\pi^{IJ}_n:M/I^{cn}M\to M/J^nM$, $x\bmod(\cdot)\mapsto x\bmod(\cdot)$. Any composite of such reductions $M/N_1\to M/N_2\to M/N_3$ along inclusions $N_1\subseteq N_2\subseteq N_3$ of submodules is the reduction $M/N_1\to M/N_3$ (both send $x\bmod N_1$ to $x\bmod N_3$); this is used below without further mention.

$\theta$ and $\psi$ are well defined. Let $(x_n)_n\in M^\wedge_J$, i.e. $x_n\in M/J^nM$ with $x_{n'}\bmod J^nM=x_n$ for $n\le n'$. Put $y_k=\pi^{JI}_k(x_{dk})=x_{dk}\bmod I^kM$. For $k\le k'$: $y_{k'}\bmod I^kM=x_{dk'}\bmod I^kM=(x_{dk'}\bmod J^{dk}M)\bmod I^kM=x_{dk}\bmod I^kM=y_k$, using $J^{dk}M\subseteq I^kM$ and compatibility of $(x_n)$ for $dk\le dk'$. So $\theta((x_n)_n)=(y_k)_k\in M^\wedge_I$. Symmetrically, for $(y_k)_k\in M^\wedge_I$ the family $\psi((y_k)_k)=(y_{cn}\bmod J^nM)_n$ is compatible (for $n\le n'$: $y_{cn'}\bmod J^nM=(y_{cn'}\bmod I^{cn}M)\bmod J^nM=y_{cn}\bmod J^nM$), so $\psi:M^\wedge_I\to M^\wedge_J$ is defined. (Stacks uses the equivalent systems $M/I^nM\to M/J^{\lfloor n/c\rfloor}M$, $M/J^mM\to M/I^{\lfloor m/d\rfloor}M$; the reindexing by $dk$, $cn$ in the statement gives the same maps on the limits, by compatibility.)

$\theta$ and $\psi$ are $A$-linear: the $A$-module structures of $M^\wedge_I$, $M^\wedge_J$ are componentwise, and each component of $\theta$ (resp. $\psi$) is the composite of the projection $(x_n)_n\mapsto x_{dk}$ (resp. $(y_k)_k\mapsto y_{cn}$), which is $A$-linear, with the $A$-linear quotient map $\pi^{JI}_k$ (resp. $\pi^{IJ}_n$).

$\psi\circ\theta=\mathrm{id}$. For $(x_n)_n\in M^\wedge_J$ and $n\ge0$, the $n$-th component of $\psi(\theta((x_n)))$ is $(x_{d\cdot cn}\bmod I^{cn}M)\bmod J^nM=x_{dcn}\bmod J^nM$ (reduction along $J^{dcn}M\subseteq I^{cn}M\subseteq J^nM$), and since $n\le dcn$ ($c,d\ge1$) compatibility of $(x_n)$ gives $x_{dcn}\bmod J^nM=x_n$. $\theta\circ\psi=\mathrm{id}$. For $(y_k)_k\in M^\wedge_I$ and $k\ge0$, the $k$-th component of $\theta(\psi((y_k)))$ is $(y_{c\cdot dk}\bmod J^{dk}M)\bmod I^kM=y_{cdk}\bmod I^kM=y_k$ since $k\le cdk$. Hence $\theta$ is an isomorphism of $A$-modules with inverse $\psi$.

$\theta\circ\iota_J=\iota_I$. For $x\in M$, $\iota_J(x)=(x\bmod J^nM)_n$, and $\theta(\iota_J(x))_k=(x\bmod J^{dk}M)\bmod I^kM=x\bmod I^kM=\iota_I(x)_k$. Consequently $\psi\circ\iota_I=\psi\circ\theta\circ\iota_J=\iota_J$.

Completeness. Since $\iota_I=\theta\circ\iota_J$ with $\theta$ bijective, $\iota_I$ is bijective if and only if $\iota_J$ is ($\iota_J=\psi\circ\iota_I$ with $\psi=\theta^{-1}$). So $M$ is $I$-adically complete iff it is $J$-adically complete (completeness meaning bijectivity of the canonical map, as in `AdicCompletion` ([Mathlib source](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/master/Mathlib/RingTheory/AdicCompletion/Basic.lean)) for $M=A$ and in the statement for modules).

Ring isomorphism for $M=A$. Then $I^kM=I^k$, $J^nM=J^n$, and $M^\wedge_I=\varprojlim_kA/I^k$, $M^\wedge_J=\varprojlim_nA/J^n$ are the completion rings of `AdicCompletion` ([Mathlib source](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/master/Mathlib/RingTheory/AdicCompletion/Basic.lean)) with componentwise operations. Each component of $\theta$ is the projection to $A/J^{dk}$ (a ring map, operations being componentwise) followed by the quotient map $A/J^{dk}\to A/I^k$, which is a ring homomorphism since $J^{dk}\subseteq I^k$. So $\theta$ preserves $1$ and products; being an additive bijection (previous steps), it is a ring isomorphism, with inverse the ring map $\psi$.
