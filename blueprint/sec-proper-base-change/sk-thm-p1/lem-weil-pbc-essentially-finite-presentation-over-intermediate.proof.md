Choose presentations $S\cong W^{-1}B$ as $R$-algebras, with $B$ generated over $R$ by $b_1,\ldots,b_n$ (definition of essentially of finite type), and $S'\cong U^{-1}P$ as $R$-algebras, with $P$ finitely presented over $R$ (the hypothesis). Transport $\psi$ through these isomorphisms. The image of $b_i/1$ in $U^{-1}P$ has a representative $p_i/u_i$ with $p_i\in P$, $u_i\in U$, for each $i$. These are finitely many choices even though $W$ and $U$ need not be finitely generated.

Set $A=S\otimes_RP$, viewed as an $S$-algebra, and let $Q=A[t_1,\ldots,t_n]/(u_it_i-1,\ b_i\otimes1-1\otimes p_i\,t_i\mid 1\le i\le n)$, where $u_i$ and $p_i$ denote $1\otimes u_i$ and $1\otimes p_i$ in $A$. The $S$-algebra $A$ is finitely presented by base change from $P$ (finite presentation is stable under base change). Adjoining finitely many polynomial variables is of finite presentation by definition; quotienting by the displayed finite set of relations preserves finite presentation. Hence $Q$ is a finitely presented $S$-algebra, by composition (finite presentation is stable under composition).

The $S$-algebra maps $S\xrightarrow{\psi}U^{-1}P$ and $P\to U^{-1}P$ give an $S$-algebra map $A\to U^{-1}P$. Send $t_i$ to $1/u_i$. The relation $u_it_i=1$ holds, and $b_i\otimes1-1\otimes p_i\,t_i$ maps to $\psi(b_i/1)-p_i/u_i=0$, so this induces an $S$-algebra map $Q\to U^{-1}P$. It sends the image of each $u\in U$ to a unit, and thus extends uniquely to $\theta:V^{-1}Q\to U^{-1}P$, where $V$ is the image of $U$ in $Q$ (the universal property of localization).

The localization map $P\to V^{-1}Q$ sends $U$ to units, hence gives an $R$-algebra map $\eta:U^{-1}P\to V^{-1}Q$ by Proposition 00CP. The composite $\theta\eta$ fixes $P$ and therefore fixes all $p/u\in U^{-1}P$. Conversely, $\eta\theta$ fixes $1\otimes P$ and $t_i$ (since $t_i=u_i^{-1}$ in $Q$). It fixes $b_i\otimes1$ because the second defining relation says $b_i\otimes1=p_it_i=\eta(\psi(b_i/1))$. Since $b_1,\ldots,b_n$ generate $B$ over $R$, it fixes the image of $B$ in $S\otimes_RP$; since the images of all $w\in W$ are units in $S$, it also fixes their inverses and hence the image of all of $S=W^{-1}B$. Thus it fixes $A$, then $Q$, then the inverses of $V$, so $\eta\theta=\mathrm{id}$. Therefore $S'\cong U^{-1}P\cong V^{-1}Q$ as $S$-algebras, with $Q$ finitely presented over $S$ by step 2.
