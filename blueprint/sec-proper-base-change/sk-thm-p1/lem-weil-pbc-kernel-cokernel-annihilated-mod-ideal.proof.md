(Conventions; the exact-sequence form of the hypothesis.) For an $A$-module $X$ and an ideal $\mathfrak a\subseteq A$, $\mathfrak aX=0$ means $ax=0$ for all $a\in\mathfrak a$, $x\in X$; $\mathfrak K^t$ is the ideal generated by the products $k_1\cdots k_t$ with $k_i\in\mathfrak K$, and $JM$ is the submodule of finite sums $\sum j_im_i$ ($j_i\in J$, $m_i\in M$). If $\mathfrak K^t\operatorname{Ker}(u)=\mathfrak K^t\operatorname{Coker}(u)=0$, then $0\to\operatorname{Ker}(u)\to M\xrightarrow{u}N\to N/u(M)\to0$ (inclusion, $u$, projection) is exact `CategoryTheory.ShortComplex.Exact` ([Mathlib source](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/master/Mathlib/Algebra/Homology/ShortComplex/Exact.lean)): the inclusion is injective with image $\operatorname{Ker}(u)$, the image of $u$ is $u(M)$, the kernel of the projection, and the projection is surjective; so $T=\operatorname{Ker}(u)$, $Q=\operatorname{Coker}(u)$ will do. Conversely, given an exact sequence $0\to T\xrightarrow{i}M\xrightarrow{u}N\xrightarrow{p}Q\to0$, $i$ is injective with image $\operatorname{Ker}(u)$, so $i:T\to\operatorname{Ker}(u)$ is an $A$-linear bijection, and $p$ is surjective with kernel $u(M)$, so it induces an $A$-linear bijection $N/u(M)\to Q$; an $A$-linear bijection $X\cong Y$ carries $ax$ to $a\cdot(\text{image of }x)$, so $\mathfrak K^tT=0\iff\mathfrak K^t\operatorname{Ker}(u)=0$ and $\mathfrak K^tQ=0\iff\mathfrak K^t\operatorname{Coker}(u)=0$.

((a), existence of $x$.) Let $f\in\mathfrak K^t$ and $y\in N$. The class $\bar y\in\operatorname{Coker}(u)=N/u(M)$ satisfies $f\bar y=\overline{fy}$, and $f\bar y=0$ because $\mathfrak K^t\operatorname{Coker}(u)=0$. Hence $fy\in u(M)$: there is $x\in M$ with $u(x)=fy$.

((a), $gx$ does not depend on $x$.) Let $g\in\mathfrak K^t$ and let $x,x'\in M$ with $u(x)=u(x')=fy$. Then $u(x-x')=0$, so $x-x'\in\operatorname{Ker}(u)$, and $g(x-x')=0$ because $\mathfrak K^t\operatorname{Ker}(u)=0$; thus $gx=gx'$. So $h_{f,g}(y)=gx$, for any $x$ with $u(x)=fy$, is a well-defined map $N\to M$.

((a), $h_{f,g}$ is $A$-linear.) Let $y_1,y_2\in N$, $a\in A$, and choose $x_1,x_2\in M$ with $u(x_1)=fy_1$, $u(x_2)=fy_2$. Then $u(x_1+x_2)=f(y_1+y_2)$ and $u(ax_1)=au(x_1)=f(ay_1)$, so $x_1+x_2$ and $ax_1$ are admissible choices for $y_1+y_2$ and $ay_1$; by Step 2, $h_{f,g}(y_1+y_2)=g(x_1+x_2)=h_{f,g}(y_1)+h_{f,g}(y_2)$ and $h_{f,g}(ay_1)=g(ax_1)=a\,h_{f,g}(y_1)$.

((a), the two composites.) For $m\in M$: $u(fm)=f\,u(m)$, so $x=fm$ is an admissible choice for $y=u(m)$, and $h_{f,g}(u(m))=gfm=fg\,m$; thus $h_{f,g}\circ u=fg\cdot\mathrm{id}_M$. For $y\in N$ with $x$ chosen as in Step 1: $u(h_{f,g}(y))=u(gx)=g\,u(x)=gfy=fg\,y$; thus $u\circ h_{f,g}=fg\cdot\mathrm{id}_N$.

((b), naturality.) Let $u',a,b$ be as in (b), $f,g\in\mathfrak K^t$ and $y\in N$; choose $x\in M$ with $u(x)=fy$ (Step 1). Then $u'(a(x))=b(u(x))=b(fy)=f\,b(y)$, so $a(x)$ is an admissible choice for the element $b(y)$ of $N'$ in the definition of $h'_{f,g}$ (which is defined by Steps 1-3 applied to $u'$, whose kernel and cokernel are killed by $\mathfrak K^t$). Hence $h'_{f,g}(b(y))=g\,a(x)=a(gx)=a(h_{f,g}(y))$, i.e. $a\circ h_{f,g}=h'_{f,g}\circ b$.

((d), converse criterion.) Let $v:P\to R$ be as in (d) and $f,g\in\mathfrak K^t$, with $k:R\to P$, $k\circ v=fg\cdot\mathrm{id}_P$, $v\circ k=fg\cdot\mathrm{id}_R$. If $p\in\operatorname{Ker}(v)$ then $fg\,p=k(v(p))=k(0)=0$. If $r\in R$ then $fg\,r=v(k(r))\in v(P)$, so $fg\,\bar r=0$ in $\operatorname{Coker}(v)=R/v(P)$. So every product $fg$ ($f,g\in\mathfrak K^t$) kills $\operatorname{Ker}(v)$ and $\operatorname{Coker}(v)$. Now $\mathfrak K^{2t}=\mathfrak K^t\mathfrak K^t$ (a product of $2t$ elements of $\mathfrak K$ is a product of two products of $t$ elements; conversely a product of two finite sums of products of $t$ elements times elements of $A$ expands into a sum of $A$-multiples of products of $2t$ elements), and every element of $\mathfrak K^t\mathfrak K^t$ is a finite sum $\sum_i f_ig_i$ with $f_i,g_i\in\mathfrak K^t$. Since the annihilator of a module is closed under sums, $\mathfrak K^{2t}$ kills $\operatorname{Ker}(v)$ and $\operatorname{Coker}(v)$.

((c), reduction modulo $J$.) Let $J\subseteq A$ be an ideal and $f,g\in\mathfrak K^t$, $h=h_{f,g}$ (Steps 1-4). As $u$ and $h$ are $A$-linear, $u(\sum j_im_i)=\sum j_iu(m_i)\in JN$ and $h(\sum j_iy_i)=\sum j_ih(y_i)\in JM$; so $u(JM)\subseteq JN$, $h(JN)\subseteq JM$, and $u,h$ induce $A$-linear maps $\bar u:M/JM\to N/JN$, $\bar x\mapsto\overline{u(x)}$, and $\bar h:N/JN\to M/JM$, $\bar y\mapsto\overline{h(y)}$. By Step 4, $\bar h(\bar u(\bar x))=\overline{h(u(x))}=\overline{fg\,x}=fg\,\bar x$ and $\bar u(\bar h(\bar y))=\overline{u(h(y))}=fg\,\bar y$. So $v=\bar u$ satisfies the hypothesis of (d) with $k=\bar h$ for every pair $f,g\in\mathfrak K^t$, and by Step 6 $\operatorname{Ker}(\bar u)$ and $\operatorname{Coker}(\bar u)$ are annihilated by $\mathfrak K^{2t}$.
