Naturality of $\epsilon$. For a scheme $S$, an object $W$ of $S_{\Et}$ and $\mathcal H\in\Mod(S_{\Et},\Lambda)$, $\epsilon_{\mathcal H}$ is the composite of $\mathrm{Hom}(j_{W!}\Lambda_W,\mathcal H)\to\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{PMod}(\Lambda_S)}(\Lambda_S[h_W],\mathcal H)$, $\varphi\mapsto\varphi\circ\sigma$ with $\sigma:\Lambda_S[h_W]\to j_{W!}\Lambda_W$ the sheafification map (bijective by the universal property of sheafification of presheaves of modules), and $\beta\mapsto\beta_W(e_{\mathrm{id}_W})$ (bijective by the Yoneda property of the free presheaf of modules $\Lambda_S[h_W]$ on the generator $e_{\mathrm{id}_W}$). Both are compatible with postcomposition by a morphism $\gamma:\mathcal H\to\mathcal H'$ (the first since $(\gamma\circ\varphi)\circ\sigma=\gamma\circ(\varphi\circ\sigma)$, the second since $(\gamma\circ\beta)_W(e_{\mathrm{id}_W})=\gamma_W(\beta_W(e_{\mathrm{id}_W}))$). Hence $\epsilon$ is a bijection natural in $\mathcal H$: $\epsilon_{\mathcal H'}(\gamma\circ\varphi)=\gamma_W(\epsilon_{\mathcal H}(\varphi))$. The same holds on $Y_{\Et}$ for $W'$; write $\epsilon'_{\mathcal G}:\mathrm{Hom}(j_{W'!}\Lambda_{W'},\mathcal G)\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathcal G(W')$ for $\mathcal G\in\Mod(Y_{\Et},\Lambda)$.

The right adjoint evaluated at $W$. The base-change functor $b:T_{\Et}\to Y_{\Et}$, $W\mapsto W\times_TY$, of [lem-weil-pbc-etale-base-change-continuous] sends $W$ to the object $W'$ of $Y_{\Et}$, and the direct image of sheaves of modules is $h_*\mathcal G=\mathcal G\circ b$ [def-weil-pbc-direct-image], [prop-weil-pbc-inverse-image-modules-exact](3): $(h_*\mathcal G)(W)=\mathcal G(W')$, and for a morphism $\chi:\mathcal G\to\mathcal G'$ of $\Mod(Y_{\Et},\Lambda)$, $(h_*\chi)_W=\chi_{W'}$. So $\mathcal G\mapsto(h_*\mathcal G)(W)=\mathcal G(W')$ is one functor $\Mod(Y_{\Et},\Lambda)\to\mathrm{Sets}$, in both descriptions (this is Stacks' identity $f'_*(j')^{-1}=j^{-1}f_*$ on the generating sections).

A natural bijection of Hom functors. By [prop-weil-pbc-inverse-image-modules-exact](3), $h^{-1}\dashv h_*$ on sheaves of modules with unit $\eta$; its hom bijection is $\alpha_{\mathcal G}:\mathrm{Hom}(h^{-1}\mathcal A,\mathcal G)\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathrm{Hom}(\mathcal A,h_*\mathcal G)$, $\chi\mapsto h_*(\chi)\circ\eta_{\mathcal A}$, natural in $\mathcal G$. With $\mathcal A=j_{W!}\Lambda_W$ and $\mathcal G\in\Mod(Y_{\Et},\Lambda)$ define $\Phi_{\mathcal G}=\alpha_{\mathcal G}^{-1}\circ\epsilon_{h_*\mathcal G}^{-1}\circ\epsilon'_{\mathcal G}:\mathrm{Hom}(j_{W'!}\Lambda_{W'},\mathcal G)\to\mathcal G(W')=(h_*\mathcal G)(W)\to\mathrm{Hom}(j_{W!}\Lambda_W,h_*\mathcal G)\to\mathrm{Hom}(h^{-1}j_{W!}\Lambda_W,\mathcal G)$. Each factor is a bijection natural in $\mathcal G$: $\epsilon'$ by step 1; $\epsilon_{h_*\mathcal G}$ by step 1 applied to $h_*\chi$ together with $(h_*\chi)_W=\chi_{W'}$ (step 2); $\alpha$ by the adjunction. So $\Phi$ is a bijection natural in $\mathcal G$. ([def-weil-pbc-direct-image]).

Existence of $\theta$. By the Yoneda lemma (the co-Yoneda embedding is fully faithful) applied in $\Mod(Y_{\Et},\Lambda)$ with $A=j_{W'!}\Lambda_{W'}$, $B=h^{-1}(j_{W!}\Lambda_W)$ and $\Phi$ of step 3, $\theta=\Phi_A(\mathrm{id}_A):h^{-1}(j_{W!}\Lambda_W)\to j_{W'!}\Lambda_{W'}$ is an isomorphism with $\Phi_{\mathcal G}(\psi)=\psi\circ\theta$ for all $\mathcal G$ and $\psi$. Unwinding $\Phi$: for every $\mathcal G$ and every $\psi:j_{W'!}\Lambda_{W'}\to\mathcal G$,
$$(*)\qquad\epsilon'_{\mathcal G}(\psi)=\epsilon_{h_*\mathcal G}\bigl(h_*(\psi\circ\theta)\circ\eta_{j_{W!}\Lambda_W}\bigr)\in(h_*\mathcal G)(W)=\mathcal G(W').$$

The compatibility clause. Let $\mathcal H\in\Mod(T_{\Et},\Lambda)$, $s\in\mathcal H(W)$ and $\varphi_s$ with $\epsilon_{\mathcal H}(\varphi_s)=s$. Apply $(*)$ to $\mathcal G=h^{-1}\mathcal H$ and $\psi=h^{-1}(\varphi_s)\circ\theta^{-1}$, so $\psi\circ\theta=h^{-1}(\varphi_s)$. Naturality of the unit $\eta$ of $h^{-1}\dashv h_*$ [prop-weil-pbc-inverse-image-modules-exact] gives $h_*h^{-1}(\varphi_s)\circ\eta_{j_{W!}\Lambda_W}=\eta_{\mathcal H}\circ\varphi_s$, and naturality of $\epsilon$ (step 1) gives $\epsilon_{h_*h^{-1}\mathcal H}(\eta_{\mathcal H}\circ\varphi_s)=\eta_{\mathcal H,W}(\epsilon_{\mathcal H}(\varphi_s))=\eta_{\mathcal H,W}(s)$. Hence $\epsilon_{h^{-1}\mathcal H}(h^{-1}(\varphi_s)\circ\theta^{-1})=\eta_{\mathcal H,W}(s)$ (here $\epsilon_{h^{-1}\mathcal H}$ of the statement is $\epsilon'_{h^{-1}\mathcal H}$, for the object $W'$ of $Y_{\Et}$).

Uniqueness. Let $\theta_1,\theta_2$ both satisfy the clause. Take $\mathcal H=j_{W!}\Lambda_W$ and $s=\epsilon_{\mathcal H}(\mathrm{id})$, so $\varphi_s=\mathrm{id}$ and $h^{-1}(\varphi_s)=\mathrm{id}$. The clause gives $\epsilon'_{h^{-1}\mathcal H}(\theta_1^{-1})=\eta_{\mathcal H,W}(s)=\epsilon'_{h^{-1}\mathcal H}(\theta_2^{-1})$; since $\epsilon'_{h^{-1}\mathcal H}$ is injective (step 1), $\theta_1^{-1}=\theta_2^{-1}$, so $\theta_1=\theta_2$.
