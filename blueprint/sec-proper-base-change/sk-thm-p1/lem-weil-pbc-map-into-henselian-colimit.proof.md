**Step 1.** *Notation; the single etale case first.* Write $k=S/\mathfrak m_S$, $\pi:S\to k$ the quotient map, and for a prime $\mathfrak q$ of a ring $A$ write $\pi_{\mathfrak q}:A\to A/\mathfrak q\to\kappa(\mathfrak q)$ [def-weil-residue-field]. The residue condition on $f$ is $\pi\circ f=\tau\circ\pi_{\mathfrak q}$. Steps 2-6 prove the statement when $A$ is a single $R$-algebra with $\operatorname{Spec}A\to\operatorname{Spec}R$ etale; steps 7-9 pass to filtered colimits. Put $B=A\otimes_RS$, with the $R$-algebra maps $\iota_A:A\to B$, $a\mapsto a\otimes1$, and $\iota_S:S\to B$, $s\mapsto1\otimes s$, which make $B$ an $S$-algebra via $\iota_S$. By [lem-weil-pbc-etale-ring-base-change], $\operatorname{Spec}B\to\operatorname{Spec}S$ is etale.

**Step 2.** *The prime $\mathfrak q'$.* The maps $\tau\circ\pi_{\mathfrak q}:A\to k$ and $\pi:S\to k$ are $R$-algebra maps: $\pi_{\mathfrak q}$ restricted to $R$ is $R\to\kappa(\mathfrak p)\to\kappa(\mathfrak q)$ (as $\mathfrak q$ lies over $\mathfrak p$), $\tau$ is a $\kappa(\mathfrak p)$-algebra map, and $R\to\kappa(\mathfrak p)\to k$ is $R\to S\xrightarrow{\pi}k$ (the map $\kappa(\mathfrak p)\to k$ being induced by $R\to S$, as $\mathfrak p$ is the preimage of $\mathfrak m_S$). By the universal property of the tensor product of commutative $R$-algebras (it is their coproduct: an $R$-algebra map out of $A\otimes_RS$ is the same as a pair of $R$-algebra maps out of $A$ and $S$) there is a unique ring map $\varphi:B\to k$ with $\varphi\circ\iota_A=\tau\circ\pi_{\mathfrak q}$ and $\varphi\circ\iota_S=\pi$; this is Stacks' map $A\otimes_RS\to\kappa(\mathfrak q)\otimes_{\kappa(\mathfrak p)}k\xrightarrow{\tau\otimes1}k$. Since $\varphi\circ\iota_S=\pi$ is surjective, $\varphi$ is surjective, so $\mathfrak q'=\ker\varphi$ is a maximal, in particular prime, ideal of $B$ with $B/\mathfrak q'\cong k$ via $\varphi$; hence $\kappa(\mathfrak q')=B/\mathfrak q'$. It lies over $\mathfrak m_S$: $\iota_S^{-1}(\mathfrak q')=\ker(\varphi\circ\iota_S)=\ker\pi=\mathfrak m_S$. The residue map $k=S/\mathfrak m_S\to\kappa(\mathfrak q')=B/\mathfrak q'$ is an isomorphism, since composed with the isomorphism $B/\mathfrak q'\to k$ induced by $\varphi$ it is $\pi$ modulo $\mathfrak m_S$, the identity of $k$.

**Step 3.** *The retraction.* $S$ is henselian local, $\operatorname{Spec}B\to\operatorname{Spec}S$ is etale (step 1), and $\mathfrak q'$ lies over $\mathfrak m_S$ with trivial residue field extension (step 2). By [lem-weil-pbc-henselian-etale-retraction] there is exactly one $S$-algebra map $\sigma:B\to S$ (so $\sigma\circ\iota_S=\mathrm{id}_S$) with $\sigma^{-1}(\mathfrak m_S)=\mathfrak q'$. Put $f=\sigma\circ\iota_A:A\to S$. It is an $R$-algebra map: on $R$, $\sigma\circ\iota_A$ restricts to $\sigma\circ\iota_S\circ(R\to S)=R\to S$, since $\iota_A$ and $\iota_S$ agree on $R$.

**Step 4.** *Existence: $f$ has the required properties (Stacks: 'we omit the verification').* First, $\pi\circ\sigma=\varphi$. Both are ring maps $B\to k$; on $\iota_S$ both give $\pi$ ($\pi\circ\sigma\circ\iota_S=\pi$). Their kernels agree: $\ker(\pi\circ\sigma)=\sigma^{-1}(\mathfrak m_S)=\mathfrak q'=\ker\varphi$. So both factor through $B/\mathfrak q'$, giving two ring maps $B/\mathfrak q'\to k$ which agree on the image of $S$; that image is all of $B/\mathfrak q'$ (step 2: $S\to B/\mathfrak q'$ is surjective), so they are equal, and $\pi\circ\sigma=\varphi$. Hence $\pi\circ f=\pi\circ\sigma\circ\iota_A=\varphi\circ\iota_A=\tau\circ\pi_{\mathfrak q}$: $f$ induces $\tau$. And $f^{-1}(\mathfrak m_S)=\ker(\pi\circ f)=\ker(\tau\circ\pi_{\mathfrak q})=\ker\pi_{\mathfrak q}=\mathfrak q$, because $\tau$ is a homomorphism of fields, hence injective, and $\ker(A\to\kappa(\mathfrak q))=\mathfrak q$.

**Step 5.** *Uniqueness in the etale case (Stacks: 'details omitted').* Let $f':A\to S$ be an $R$-algebra map with $f'^{-1}(\mathfrak m_S)=\mathfrak q$ and $\pi\circ f'=\tau\circ\pi_{\mathfrak q}$. By the universal property of the tensor product (step 2) there is a ring map $\sigma':B\to S$ with $\sigma'\circ\iota_A=f'$ and $\sigma'\circ\iota_S=\mathrm{id}_S$; so $\sigma'$ is an $S$-algebra map. Then $\pi\circ\sigma'$ and $\varphi$ agree on $\iota_A$ ($\pi\circ f'=\tau\circ\pi_{\mathfrak q}$) and on $\iota_S$ (both $\pi$), so by the uniqueness clause of the universal property $\pi\circ\sigma'=\varphi$, and $\sigma'^{-1}(\mathfrak m_S)=\ker(\pi\circ\sigma')=\ker\varphi=\mathfrak q'$. By the uniqueness in [lem-weil-pbc-henselian-etale-retraction], $\sigma'=\sigma$, hence $f'=\sigma'\circ\iota_A=\sigma\circ\iota_A=f$. (The hypothesis $f'^{-1}(\mathfrak m_S)=\mathfrak q$ follows from the residue condition, as in step 4.)

**Step 6.** *The etale case is a special case of the colimit case and conversely the colimit case uses it stagewise.* A single etale $A$ is the colimit of the one-object diagram [def-weil-pbc-category-colimit]; for the general case write $A=\varinjlim_{i\in I}A_i$, a filtered colimit in commutative $R$-algebras, with coprojections $\iota_i:A_i\to A$ and transition maps $A(u):A_i\to A_j$ for $u:i\to j$. Put $\mathfrak q_i=\iota_i^{-1}(\mathfrak q)$, a prime of $A_i$; it lies over $\mathfrak p$, because $R\to A_i\to A$ is the structure map of $A$ and $\mathfrak q$ lies over $\mathfrak p$. Let $\kappa(\iota_i):\kappa(\mathfrak q_i)\to\kappa(\mathfrak q)$ be the induced map of residue fields, so $\kappa(\iota_i)\circ\pi_{\mathfrak q_i}=\pi_{\mathfrak q}\circ\iota_i$, and $\tau_i=\tau\circ\kappa(\iota_i)$, a $\kappa(\mathfrak p)$-algebra map. Steps 1-5 give a unique $R$-algebra map $f_i:A_i\to S$ with $f_i^{-1}(\mathfrak m_S)=\mathfrak q_i$ and $\pi\circ f_i=\tau_i\circ\pi_{\mathfrak q_i}=\tau\circ\pi_{\mathfrak q}\circ\iota_i$.

**Step 7.** *The $f_i$ form a cocone (Stacks: implicit in 'Set $f=\colim f_i$').* For $u:i\to j$, the $R$-algebra map $g=f_j\circ A(u):A_i\to S$ satisfies the two conditions characterizing $f_i$: $\pi\circ g=\tau\circ\pi_{\mathfrak q}\circ\iota_j\circ A(u)=\tau\circ\pi_{\mathfrak q}\circ\iota_i$ (as $\iota_j\circ A(u)=\iota_i$), and $g^{-1}(\mathfrak m_S)=A(u)^{-1}(\mathfrak q_j)=A(u)^{-1}\iota_j^{-1}(\mathfrak q)=\iota_i^{-1}(\mathfrak q)=\mathfrak q_i$. By the uniqueness of step 5 for $A_i$, $f_j\circ A(u)=f_i$. So $(S,(f_i))$ is a cocone in commutative $R$-algebras, and by the universal property of the colimit [def-weil-pbc-category-colimit] there is exactly one $R$-algebra map $f:A\to S$ with $f\circ\iota_i=f_i$ for all $i$.

**Step 8.** *$f$ has the required properties.* By [lem-weil-pbc-filtered-colimit-algebras-elementwise] every $x\in A$ is $\iota_i(x_i)$ for some $i$ and $x_i\in A_i$. Then $f(x)=f_i(x_i)\in\mathfrak m_S$ iff $x_i\in\mathfrak q_i=\iota_i^{-1}(\mathfrak q)$ iff $x\in\mathfrak q$, so $f^{-1}(\mathfrak m_S)=\mathfrak q$; and $\pi(f(x))=\pi(f_i(x_i))=\tau(\pi_{\mathfrak q}(\iota_i(x_i)))=\tau(\pi_{\mathfrak q}(x))$, so $\pi\circ f=\tau\circ\pi_{\mathfrak q}$.

**Step 9.** *Uniqueness in the colimit case.* Let $f':A\to S$ be an $R$-algebra map with $f'^{-1}(\mathfrak m_S)=\mathfrak q$ and $\pi\circ f'=\tau\circ\pi_{\mathfrak q}$. For each $i$, $f'\circ\iota_i$ is an $R$-algebra map with $(f'\circ\iota_i)^{-1}(\mathfrak m_S)=\iota_i^{-1}(\mathfrak q)=\mathfrak q_i$ and $\pi\circ f'\circ\iota_i=\tau\circ\pi_{\mathfrak q}\circ\iota_i=\tau_i\circ\pi_{\mathfrak q_i}$; by step 5 for $A_i$, $f'\circ\iota_i=f_i$. By the uniqueness in the universal property of the colimit [def-weil-pbc-category-colimit], $f'=f$.
