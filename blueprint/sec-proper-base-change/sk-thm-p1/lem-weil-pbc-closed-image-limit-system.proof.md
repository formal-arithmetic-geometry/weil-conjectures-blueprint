*The stage images.* Let $I_j\subseteq\mathcal O_{D_j}$ be the kernel ideal sheaf of $\pi_{Y,j}\circ i:X\to D_j$ (a quasi-compact morphism, so $I_j$ is quasi-coherent and computed on affine opens as the kernel on sections), and let $k_j:Z_j\to D_j$ be the closed subscheme it defines, the scheme-theoretic image of $\pi_{Y,j}\circ i$. For an arrow $a:j\to l$, naturality of the cone shows that $D(a)$ maps $Z_j$ into $Z_l$ (the ideal $I_l$ pulls back into $I_j$), which defines the transition maps of $Z$; the factorizations $\pi_{X,j}:X\to Z_j$ of $\pi_{Y,j}\circ i$ through $k_j$ are the canonical maps to the scheme-theoretic images. Since the $k_j$ are monomorphisms, the functor laws, naturality of $k$, the cone equations for $\pi_X$ and $\pi_{X,j}\mathbin;k_j=i\mathbin;\pi_{Y,j}$ all follow by cancellation.

*The kernel of $i$.* Let $K$ be the supremum of the pullbacks $\pi_{Y,j}^{-1}I_j$ of the stage ideals to $Y$. Each is contained in $\ker(i)$, so $K\subseteq\ker(i)$. Conversely, let $s$ be a section of $\ker(i)$ over an affine open $W\subseteq Y$ lying over an affine open of some stage. Since the transition maps are affine, $W$ is the limit of the affine inverse images $V_j$ in the stages, and its coordinate ring is the colimit of theirs; so $s$ comes from a section $t$ on some $V_j$. Since $i^\sharp(s)=0$, $t$ maps to zero in $\Gamma(X,\cdot)$ after possibly enlarging $j$, i.e. $t\in I_j(V_j)$, and hence $s\in K(W)$. These affine opens cover $Y$, so $\ker(i)=K$.

*The limit property.* Given a cone $s$ over $Z$, composing with $k$ gives a cone over $D$ and hence a map $m:s_{\mathrm{pt}}\to Y$. Every $I_j$ is killed by $s_j$ followed by $k_j$, so by the description $\ker(i)=K$ the ideal $\ker(i)$ is contained in $\ker(m)$, and $m$ factors through the closed immersion $i$. The cone equations follow since each $k_j$ is a monomorphism, and uniqueness since $i$ is a monomorphism and maps into the limit $Y$ are determined by their components. So $X$ with $\pi_X$ is a limit of $Z$.

*Affine transitions.* For $a:j\to l$, the composite $Z_j\to Z_l\xrightarrow{k_l}D_l$ equals $Z_j\xrightarrow{k_j}D_j\xrightarrow{D(a)}D_l$, which is affine (a closed immersion followed by an affine morphism). Since $k_l$ is a closed immersion, hence separated, the transition $Z_j\to Z_l$ is affine (if $g\circ h$ is affine and $g$ is separated, then $h$ is affine). The $k_j$ are closed immersions by construction.
