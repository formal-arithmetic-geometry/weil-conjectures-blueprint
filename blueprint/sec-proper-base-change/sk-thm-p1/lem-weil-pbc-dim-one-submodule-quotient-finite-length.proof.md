Write $\mathfrak m$ for the maximal ideal of $R$ and $\ell=\mathrm{length}_R(R/xR)$. Since $R$ is a domain and $x\ne0$, $x$ is a nonzerodivisor of $R$; since $R$ is Noetherian local of dimension $\le1$, the quotient of $R$ by a nonzerodivisor has finite length, so $\ell<\infty$. This is the first assertion.

If $x$ is a unit of $R$ then $xM=M$ (every $m\in M$ is $x\cdot(x^{-1}m)$ with $x^{-1}m\in M$), so $M/xM=0$ has length $0\le r\ell$. Hence we may assume $x$ is not a unit, i.e. $x\in\mathfrak m$, $R$ being local.

Reduction to the case that $M$ spans. Let $V\subseteq K^{\oplus r}$ be the $K$-linear span of $M$; it is a $K$-subspace of dimension $r'\le r$, and a choice of $K$-basis gives a $K$-linear (hence $R$-linear) isomorphism $\phi:V\to K^{\oplus r'}$. Then $\phi(M)$ is an $R$-submodule of $K^{\oplus r'}$ whose $K$-span is $K^{\oplus r'}$, and $\phi$ induces an $R$-isomorphism $M/xM\cong\phi(M)/x\phi(M)$; isomorphic modules have the same length (an isomorphism carries strict chains of submodules to strict chains). Since $r'\ell\le r\ell$, it suffices to prove $\mathrm{length}_R(M/xM)\le r'\ell$ for an $R$-submodule $M\subseteq K^{\oplus r'}$ that spans $K^{\oplus r'}$ over $K$. If $r'=0$ then $M=0$ and there is nothing to prove; assume $r'\ge1$. Steps 4-8 treat $M$ finitely generated, step 9 the general case.

Finite case, clearing denominators. Let $M$ be generated by $m_1,\dots,m_s$ and span $K^{\oplus r'}$. Each of the $s r'$ coordinates of the $m_k$ is a fraction $a/b$ with $a,b\in R$, $b\ne0$; let $d\ne0$ be the product of all these denominators. Then $dM\subseteq R^{\oplus r'}$. Multiplication by $d$ is an injective $R$-linear map $K^{\oplus r'}\to K^{\oplus r'}$ ($d$ is invertible in $K$), so it gives an isomorphism $M\cong dM$ carrying $xM$ onto $x\,dM$, hence $M/xM\cong dM/x\,dM$; and $dM$ still spans $K^{\oplus r'}$. Replacing $M$ by $dM$ (lengths agree) we may assume $M\subseteq F:=R^{\oplus r'}$.

Finite case, an integer $c$ with $x^cF\subseteq M$. For each standard basis vector $e_j$ of $K^{\oplus r'}$ ($1\le j\le r'$) write $e_j=\sum_k\lambda_{jk}m_k$ with $\lambda_{jk}\in K$, as $M$ spans; multiplying by a common denominator $d_j\in R\setminus\{0\}$ of the $\lambda_{jk}$ gives $d_je_j\in M$. Put $d=d_1\cdots d_{r'}\ne0$; then $dF\subseteq M$. Every prime ideal $\mathfrak p\supseteq dR$ equals $\mathfrak m$: $\mathfrak p\ne0$ since $d\ne0$, and $(0)$ is prime ($R$ a domain), so if $\mathfrak p\ne\mathfrak m$ then $(0)\subsetneq\mathfrak p\subsetneq\mathfrak m$ would be a chain of primes of length $2>\dim R=1$. Since $x\in\mathfrak m$, $x$ lies in every prime containing $dR$, so $x\in\sqrt{dR}$ ($\sqrt I$ is the intersection of the primes containing $I$): $x^c\in dR$ for some $c\ge1$. Hence $x^cF\subseteq dF\subseteq M$. (Stacks phrases this as: $F/M$ has finite length, in particular some $x^c$ kills it; the argument above is what that sentence stands for.)

Claim: for every $R$-submodule $N\subseteq K^{\oplus r'}$ (we use $N=M$ and $N=F$) and every $n\ge0$, $\mathrm{length}_R(N/x^nN)=n\cdot\mathrm{length}_R(N/xN)$ in $\{0,1,\dots\}\cup\{\infty\}$. For $i\ge0$ the map $N\to x^iN/x^{i+1}N$, $m\mapsto x^im\bmod x^{i+1}N$, is $R$-linear and surjective; its kernel is $xN$: if $x^im=x^{i+1}m'$ with $m'\in N$ then $m=xm'$ because $x^i\ne0$ is invertible in $K$, and conversely $x^i(xm')\in x^{i+1}N$. So $x^iN/x^{i+1}N\cong N/xN$. For $n\ge0$ the sequence $0\to x^nN/x^{n+1}N\to N/x^{n+1}N\to N/x^nN\to0$ (inclusion, and the projection, as $x^{n+1}N\subseteq x^nN$) is exact, so by additivity of length on short exact sequences $\mathrm{length}(N/x^{n+1}N)=\mathrm{length}(N/x^nN)+\mathrm{length}(N/xN)$; induction on $n$ from $N/x^0N=0$ proves the claim.

$\mathrm{length}_R(F/xF)=r'\ell$, hence $\mathrm{length}_R(F/x^nF)=nr'\ell<\infty$ for all $n\ge0$. Indeed $F/xF\cong(R/xR)^{\oplus r'}$ coordinatewise, and for $t\ge1$ the sequence $0\to(R/xR)^{\oplus t-1}\to(R/xR)^{\oplus t}\to R/xR\to0$ (inclusion of the first $t-1$ coordinates, projection to the last) is exact, so additivity of length and induction on $t$ give $\mathrm{length}((R/xR)^{\oplus t})=t\ell$. The second formula is step 6 for $N=F$; it is finite by step 1.

Finite case, the sandwich. For $R$-submodules $A\subseteq B\subseteq F$ the sequence $0\to B/A\to F/A\to F/B\to0$ is exact, so $\mathrm{length}(F/A)=\mathrm{length}(B/A)+\mathrm{length}(F/B)$ by additivity of length; in particular $\mathrm{length}(F/B)\le\mathrm{length}(F/A)$. Let $L=\mathrm{length}_R(M/xM)$ and $n\ge1$. From $x^cF\subseteq M$ (step 5) we get $x^{n+c}F\subseteq x^nM\subseteq x^nF$ and $x^nM\subseteq M\subseteq F$. Taking $A=x^nM$, $B=M$ and step 6 for $N=M$: $\mathrm{length}(F/x^nM)=nL+\mathrm{length}(F/M)$. Taking $A=x^{n+c}F$, $B=x^nM$ and step 7: $\mathrm{length}(F/x^nM)\le(n+c)r'\ell<\infty$. Hence $nL\le(n+c)r'\ell$; for $n=1$ this shows $L<\infty$, and for every $n$ it gives $L\le r'\ell+cr'\ell/n$, so (taking $n>cr'\ell$, all quantities being integers) $L\le r'\ell$. Conversely, $A=x^nM$, $B=x^nF$ gives $\mathrm{length}(F/x^nM)\ge nr'\ell$, and $A=x^cF$, $B=M$ gives $\mathrm{length}(F/M)\le cr'\ell$; so $nL\ge(n-c)r'\ell$ for all $n$, and $L=r'\ell$. Thus for finitely generated $M$, $\mathrm{length}_R(M/xM)=r'\ell\le r\ell$.

General case. Let $M\subseteq K^{\oplus r}$ be arbitrary and $k\ge0$ an integer with $\mathrm{length}_R(M/xM)\ge k$. By definition of length there is a strict chain $N_0\subsetneq N_1\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq N_k$ of submodules of $M/xM$. For $i=1,\dots,k$ choose $m_i\in M$ whose class mod $xM$ lies in $N_i$ but not in $N_{i-1}$. Let $M'=Rm_1+\cdots+Rm_k\subseteq M$, a finitely generated $R$-submodule of $K^{\oplus r}$. As $xM'\subseteq xM$, the inclusion induces an $R$-linear map $\pi:M'/xM'\to M/xM$; let $N'_i=\pi^{-1}(N_i)$, so $N'_0\subseteq N'_1\subseteq\cdots\subseteq N'_k$. The class of $m_i$ in $M'/xM'$ lies in $N'_i$ and not in $N'_{i-1}$ (its image is the class of $m_i$ in $M/xM$), so the chain is strict and $\mathrm{length}_R(M'/xM')\ge k$. By the finite case (steps 3-8 applied to $M'$) $k\le r\ell$. Since $\mathrm{length}_R(M/xM)$ is the supremum of such $k$, $\mathrm{length}_R(M/xM)\le r\ell<\infty$; in particular $M/xM$ has finite length.
