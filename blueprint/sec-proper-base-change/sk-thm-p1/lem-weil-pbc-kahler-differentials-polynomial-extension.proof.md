(Notation and the two maps.) Write $P=R[z_1,\dots,z_e]$ and let $u:B\otimes_A\Omega_{A/R}\to\Omega_{B/R}$, $u(b\otimes\mathrm d\alpha)=b\,\mathrm d\alpha$, and $v:\Omega_{B/R}\to\Omega_{B/A}$, $v(b\,\mathrm d_{B/R}\beta)=b\,\mathrm d_{B/A}\beta$, be the $B$-linear maps of the first fundamental exact sequence $B\otimes_A\Omega_{A/R}\xrightarrow{u}\Omega_{B/R}\xrightarrow{v}\Omega_{B/A}\to0$ for the ring maps $R\to A\to B$ (constants $A\subset B=A[z_1,\dots,z_e]$); that sequence says $v$ is surjective and $\ker v=\operatorname{im}u$. Let $s:B^e\to\Omega_{B/R}$, $s(b_1,\dots,b_e)=\sum_jb_j\,\mathrm dz_j$. The map $\varphi$ of the statement is $\varphi(x,b)=u(x)+s(b)$.

($\Omega_{B/A}$ is free on the $\mathrm dz_j$.) The module of differentials of a polynomial ring is free on the differentials of the variables; with base ring $A$, $\Omega_{B/A}$ is a free $B$-module with basis $\mathrm d_{B/A}z_1,\dots,\mathrm d_{B/A}z_e$. Since $v(s(b))=\sum_jb_j\,\mathrm d_{B/A}z_j$, the composite $v\circ s:B^e\to\Omega_{B/A}$ is the isomorphism sending the $j$-th standard basis vector to $\mathrm d_{B/A}z_j$.

($\varphi$ is surjective, and $\varphi$ is injective as soon as $u$ is.) Surjective: given $\omega\in\Omega_{B/R}$ put $b=(v\circ s)^{-1}(v(\omega))\in B^e$ (Step 2); then $v(\omega-s(b))=0$, so $\omega-s(b)=u(x)$ for some $x\in B\otimes_A\Omega_{A/R}$ because $\ker v=\operatorname{im}u$ (Step 1), and $\omega=\varphi(x,b)$. Injective: if $u(x)+s(b)=0$, apply $v$: since $v\circ u=0$ (as $\operatorname{im}u=\ker v$) we get $(v\circ s)(b)=0$, so $b=0$ by Step 2, and then $u(x)=0$; so if $u$ is injective, $x=0$.

($B$ as a base change of $A$ along $R\to P$.) Polynomial rings commute with base change: the map $A\otimes_RP\to B$, $a\otimes p\mapsto a\cdot\bar p$ ($\bar p$ the image of $p$ under the coefficient map $R\to A$) is an isomorphism of rings, sending $a\otimes1\mapsto a$ and $1\otimes z_j\mapsto z_j$. Transporting along it, the base-change property of Kähler differentials (for $R\to P$ and $R\to A$, $\Omega_{(P\otimes_RA)/P}\cong P\otimes_R\Omega_{A/R}$, with $P\otimes_RA\cong B$) gives an isomorphism of $B$-modules $\mu:B\otimes_A\Omega_{A/R}\xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }\Omega_{B/P}$, where $B$ is a $P$-algebra via $p\mapsto\bar p$ (i.e. $z_j\mapsto z_j$), with $\mu(1\otimes\mathrm d\alpha)=\mathrm d_{B/P}\alpha$ for $\alpha\in A$ (the base-change isomorphism sends $p\otimes\mathrm d\alpha$ to $p\,\mathrm d(1\otimes\alpha)$; here $B\otimes_A\Omega_{A/R}$ is identified with $P\otimes_R\Omega_{A/R}$, $b\otimes\omega$ corresponding to the $B$-multiple $b\cdot(1\otimes\omega)$).

($u$ is injective.) The ring maps $R\to P\to B$ are a factorization of the structure map $R\to A\to B$ (constants of $R$ go to constants), so the first fundamental exact sequence for $R\to P\to B$ gives the $B$-linear map $c:\Omega_{B/R}\to\Omega_{B/P}$, $c(b\,\mathrm d_{B/R}\beta)=b\,\mathrm d_{B/P}\beta$. The $B$-linear maps $c\circ u$ and $\mu$ (Step 4) agree on the elements $1\otimes\mathrm d\alpha$, $\alpha\in A$: both give $\mathrm d_{B/P}\alpha$. These elements generate $B\otimes_A\Omega_{A/R}$ as a $B$-module (the $\mathrm d\alpha$ generate $\Omega_{A/R}$ as an $A$-module by construction), so $c\circ u=\mu$, which is an isomorphism. Hence $u$ is injective (with left inverse $\mu^{-1}\circ c$), and by Step 3 $\varphi$ is an isomorphism.

(Free of rank $d+e$.) If $\omega_1,\dots,\omega_d$ is a basis of the $A$-module $\Omega_{A/R}$, then $\Omega_{A/R}\cong A^d$ and $B\otimes_A\Omega_{A/R}\cong B\otimes_AA^d\cong B^d$ (tensor product commutes with finite direct sums and $B\otimes_AA\cong B$), with basis $1\otimes\omega_1,\dots,1\otimes\omega_d$. By Step 5, $\Omega_{B/R}\cong B^d\oplus B^e=B^{d+e}$, with basis $\omega_1,\dots,\omega_d$ (images $u(1\otimes\omega_i)$) and $\mathrm dz_1,\dots,\mathrm dz_e$.
