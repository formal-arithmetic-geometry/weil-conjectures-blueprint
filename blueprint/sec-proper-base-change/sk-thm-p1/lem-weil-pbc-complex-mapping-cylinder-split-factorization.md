Let $\mathcal A$ be an additive category. A cochain complex $K^\bullet$ of $\mathcal A$ is a family of objects $K^n$ ($n\in\mathbf Z$) with morphisms $d^n:K^n\to K^{n+1}$ such that $d^{n+1}d^n=0$; a morphism of complexes is a family of morphisms commuting with the differentials; two morphisms $f,g:A^\bullet\to B^\bullet$ are homotopic if there are morphisms $h^n:A^n\to B^{n-1}$ with $f^n-g^n=d_B^{n-1}h^n+h^{n+1}d_A^n$ for all $n$. Let $\alpha:K^\bullet\to L^\bullet$ be a morphism of cochain complexes of $\mathcal A$. Then there are a cochain complex $\tilde L^\bullet$ and morphisms of complexes $\tilde\alpha:K^\bullet\to\tilde L^\bullet$, $\pi:\tilde L^\bullet\to L^\bullet$, $s:L^\bullet\to\tilde L^\bullet$ such that: (0) $\pi\circ\tilde\alpha=\alpha$; (1) $\tilde\alpha$ is a termwise split injection, i.e. for every $n$ there are an object $M^n$ and an isomorphism $\tilde L^n\cong K^n\oplus M^n$ under which $\tilde\alpha^n$ is the inclusion of the summand $K^n$ (the $M^n$ and these isomorphisms need not be compatible with the differentials); (2) $\pi\circ s=\mathrm{id}_{L^\bullet}$, and $s\circ\pi$ is homotopic to $\mathrm{id}_{\tilde L^\bullet}$. Moreover, if $K^n=L^n=0$ for all $n<n_0$ (resp. for all $n>n_1$, resp. both), then $\tilde L^n=0$ for all $n<n_0-1$ (resp. for all $n>n_1$, resp. both), so $\tilde L^\bullet$ is again bounded below (resp. above, resp. bounded).
