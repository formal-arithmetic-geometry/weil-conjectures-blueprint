(Expansion.) Write $[m]=\{1,\dots,m\}$. By the definition of the determinant `Matrix.det` ([Mathlib source](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/master/Mathlib/LinearAlgebra/Matrix/Determinant/Basic.lean)) and of the product, $\det(AB)=\sum_{\sigma\in S_m}\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)\prod_{k=1}^m\sum_{j=1}^n a_{\sigma(k)j}b_{jk}$. Expanding the product of the $m$ sums by distributivity, $\prod_{k=1}^m\sum_{j=1}^n x_{k,j}=\sum_{f:[m]\to[n]}\prod_{k=1}^m x_{k,f(k)}$ (one term for each choice of an index $f(k)$ in the $k$-th factor), so, exchanging the two finite sums and using commutativity of $R$, $$\det(AB)=\sum_{f:[m]\to[n]}\Big(\prod_{k=1}^m b_{f(k)k}\Big)\sum_{\sigma\in S_m}\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)\prod_{k=1}^m a_{\sigma(k)f(k)}=\sum_{f:[m]\to[n]}\Big(\prod_{k=1}^m b_{f(k)k}\Big)\det(A_f),$$ where $A_f$ is the $m\times m$ matrix `Matrix` ([Mathlib source](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/master/Mathlib/LinearAlgebra/Matrix/Defs.lean)) with $(A_f)_{ik}=a_{i,f(k)}$ (column $k$ of $A_f$ is column $f(k)$ of $A$), the last equality being the definition of $\det(A_f)$.

(Non-injective $f$ contribute $0$.) If $f:[m]\to[n]$ is not injective, there are $k\ne l$ with $f(k)=f(l)$; then $(A_f)_{ik}=a_{i,f(k)}=a_{i,f(l)}=(A_f)_{il}$ for all $i$, so columns $k$ and $l$ of $A_f$ are equal and $\det(A_f)=0$ by `Matrix.det_zero_of_column_eq` ([Mathlib source](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/master/Mathlib/LinearAlgebra/Matrix/Determinant/Basic.lean)). Hence in step 1 the sum may be restricted to the injective maps $f:[m]\to[n]$.

(Injective maps by image and permutation.) For $S\subseteq[n]$ with $|S|=m$ write $S=\{s_1<\dots<s_m\}$. The map $(S,\tau)\mapsto f$, $f(k)=s_{\tau(k)}$, from pairs of such a subset $S$ and a permutation $\tau\in S_m$ to injective maps $[m]\to[n]$ is a bijection: $f$ is injective as a composite of injective maps, with image $S$; conversely an injective $f$ has an image $S=f([m])$ with $|S|=m$, and $\tau(k)$ is then forced to be the unique $t$ with $s_t=f(k)$, which defines a permutation $\tau$ of $[m]$ because $f:[m]\to S$ and $t\mapsto s_t$, $[m]\to S$, are bijections. (If $n<m$ there is no such $S$ and no injective $f$.)

(The term of $f=s\circ\tau$.) Let $S=\{s_1<\dots<s_m\}$ and $f(k)=s_{\tau(k)}$ as in step 3. By the definition of the submatrices `Matrix.submatrix` ([Mathlib source](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/master/Mathlib/LinearAlgebra/Matrix/Defs.lean)) taken in increasing order, $(A^S)_{it}=a_{i,s_t}$ and $(B_S)_{tk}=b_{s_t,k}$ for $1\le i,t,k\le m$. Hence $(A_f)_{ik}=a_{i,s_{\tau(k)}}=(A^S)_{i,\tau(k)}$: $A_f$ is $A^S$ with its columns permuted by $\tau$, so $\det(A_f)=\operatorname{sgn}(\tau)\det(A^S)$ by `Matrix.det_permute'` ([Mathlib source](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/master/Mathlib/LinearAlgebra/Matrix/Determinant/Basic.lean)). And $\prod_k b_{f(k)k}=\prod_k b_{s_{\tau(k)},k}=\prod_k(B_S)_{\tau(k),k}$.

(Conclusion.) By steps 1–4, $$\det(AB)=\sum_{|S|=m}\sum_{\tau\in S_m}\Big(\prod_{k=1}^m(B_S)_{\tau(k),k}\Big)\operatorname{sgn}(\tau)\det(A^S)=\sum_{|S|=m}\det(A^S)\sum_{\tau\in S_m}\operatorname{sgn}(\tau)\prod_{k=1}^m(B_S)_{\tau(k),k}=\sum_{|S|=m}\det(A^S)\det(B_S),$$ the last equality being the definition of $\det(B_S)$ `Matrix.det` ([Mathlib source](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/master/Mathlib/LinearAlgebra/Matrix/Determinant/Basic.lean)). For $n<m$ both the sum over injective $f$ and the sum over $S$ are empty and $\det(AB)=0$; for $m=0$ the only $f$ is the empty map, the only $S$ is $\emptyset$, and both sides are $1$.
