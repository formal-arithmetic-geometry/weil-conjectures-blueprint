Choose, for every variable $x_t$ of $P$, an element $p'_t\in P'$ whose image under $\alpha'$ is $\alpha(x_t)$; these choices exist since $\alpha'$ is onto. The universal property of the polynomial ring `MvPolynomial, MvPolynomial.aeval, MvPolynomial.algHom_ext` ([Mathlib source](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/master/Mathlib/Algebra/MvPolynomial/Basic.lean)) gives an $R$-algebra map $\varphi:P\to P'$ with $\varphi(x_t)=p'_t$ and $\alpha'\varphi=\alpha$, since the two maps agree on $R$ and each $x_t$. Reversing the roles of the presentations gives $\psi:P'\to P$ with $\alpha\psi=\alpha'$.

For any $R$-algebra map $a:P\to P'$ over $S$ (so $\alpha'a=\alpha$), $a(I)\subseteq I'$ and $a(I^2)\subseteq I'^2$; multiplication on $I/I^2$ by $P$ therefore factors through $S=P/I$, and $a_1(f\bmod I^2)=a(f)\bmod I'^2$ is $S$-linear. By the universal property of Kähler differentials `KaehlerDifferential` ([Mathlib source](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/master/Mathlib/RingTheory/Kaehler/Basic.lean)), $a$ gives the $S$-linear map $a_0:S\otimes_P\Omega_{P/R}\to S\otimes_{P'}\Omega_{P'/R}$ determined by $a_0(s\otimes\mathrm dp)=s\otimes\mathrm d(a(p))$. The Leibniz rule makes $\mathrm d_\alpha$ well defined on $I/I^2$: for $f,g\in I$, $\mathrm d(fg)$ maps to $\alpha(f)\otimes\mathrm dg+\alpha(g)\otimes\mathrm df=0$; the same holds for $\alpha'$. On $f\bmod I^2$ one has $\mathrm d_{\alpha'}a_1(f)=1\otimes\mathrm d(a(f))=a_0\mathrm d_\alpha(f)$, so $(a_1,a_0)$ is a chain map.

Let $a,b:P\to P'$ be any two such maps over $S$. Since $\alpha'(a(p)-b(p))=0$, the difference defines $D:P\to I'/I'^2$, $D(p)=(a(p)-b(p))\bmod I'^2$. It is additive and vanishes on $R$. Moreover $a(p)-b(p)\in I'$ implies that $a(p)$ and $b(p)$ act on $I'/I'^2$ through the common scalar $\alpha(p)\in S$; hence the identity $a(pq)-b(pq)=a(p)(a(q)-b(q))+(a(p)-b(p))b(q)$ becomes $D(pq)=\alpha(p)D(q)+\alpha(q)D(p)$. Thus $D$ is an $R$-derivation into the $P$-module $I'/I'^2$ defined by $\alpha$. The universal property `KaehlerDifferential` ([Mathlib source](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/master/Mathlib/RingTheory/Kaehler/Basic.lean)), followed by extension of scalars from $P$ to $S$, gives a unique $S$-linear $H:S\otimes_P\Omega_{P/R}\to I'/I'^2$ with $H(s\otimes\mathrm dp)=sD(p)$.

The two homotopy identities follow on generators. For $f\in I$, $H\mathrm d_\alpha(f\bmod I^2)=H(1\otimes\mathrm df)=(a(f)-b(f))\bmod I'^2=(a_1-b_1)(f\bmod I^2)$. For $s\otimes\mathrm dp$, $\mathrm d_{\alpha'}H(s\otimes\mathrm dp)=s\otimes\mathrm d(a(p)-b(p))=(a_0-b_0)(s\otimes\mathrm dp)$; both sides are $S$-linear, and the differentials $\mathrm dp$ generate $\Omega_{P/R}$ by its construction in `KaehlerDifferential` ([Mathlib source](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/master/Mathlib/RingTheory/Kaehler/Basic.lean)). This supplies explicitly the calculation omitted in the source's proof of part (2).

For maps of presentations $a:P\to P'$ and $c:P'\to P''$ over the same base algebra, the induced maps compose termwise: on $f\bmod I^2$, $c_1a_1(f)=c(a(f))\bmod I''^2=(c\circ a)_1(f)$, and on $s\otimes\mathrm dp$, $c_0a_0(s\otimes\mathrm dp)=s\otimes\mathrm d(c(a(p)))=(c\circ a)_0(s\otimes\mathrm dp)$. The identity presentation map induces identities in both degrees. Apply steps 3-4 first to $a=\mathrm{id}_P$, $b=\psi\circ\varphi$ to obtain $h$; composition then gives $\mathrm{id}-\psi_1\varphi_1=h\mathrm d_\alpha$ and $\mathrm{id}-\psi_0\varphi_0=\mathrm d_\alpha h$. Apply them again to $a=\mathrm{id}_{P'}$, $b=\varphi\circ\psi$ to obtain $h'$ and the two analogous identities for $\alpha'$. Step 2 supplies the two chain-map identities. Thus the selected maps are mutual homotopy inverses, and the construction works for any choices of $\varphi,\psi$ over $S$. (`KaehlerDifferential` ([Mathlib source](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/master/Mathlib/RingTheory/Kaehler/Basic.lean))).
