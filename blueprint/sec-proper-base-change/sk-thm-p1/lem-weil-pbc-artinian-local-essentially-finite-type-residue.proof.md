*Presentation of the residue field and lifts of the generators.* Write $k=S/\mathfrak m$ and $\pi:S\to k$ for the quotient map; $k$ is an $R$-algebra through $R\to S\xrightarrow{\pi}k$. By hypothesis and `Algebra.EssFiniteType` ([Mathlib source](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/master/Mathlib/RingTheory/EssentialFiniteness.lean)) there are a finite type $R$-algebra $B$, a multiplicative subset $W\subseteq B$ and an isomorphism of $R$-algebras $\iota:W^{-1}B\xrightarrow{\sim}k$; by `Algebra.FiniteType` ([Mathlib source](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/master/Mathlib/RingTheory/FiniteType.lean)) there are $n\ge0$ and a surjective $R$-algebra map $\beta:R[x_1,\dots,x_n]\to B$ (so $B\cong R[x_1,\dots,x_n]/I$ with $I=\ker\beta$, as in the source). Let $\gamma:R[x_1,\dots,x_n]\to k$, $\gamma(P)=\iota(\beta(P)/1)$, an $R$-algebra map. Since $\pi$ is surjective, choose $f_1,\dots,f_n\in S$ with $\pi(f_i)=\gamma(x_i)$ ($i=1,\dots,n$): the congruence classes of the $f_i$ modulo $\mathfrak m$ correspond to the classes of the $x_i$ modulo $I$.

*The subring $A$ and the prime $\mathfrak p$.* Let $\phi:R[x_1,\dots,x_n]\to S$ be the $R$-algebra map with $\phi(x_i)=f_i$, $J=\ker\phi$, and $A=\phi(R[x_1,\dots,x_n])\cong R[x_1,\dots,x_n]/J$, an $R$-subalgebra of $S$; $A$ is a finite type $R$-algebra `Algebra.FiniteType` ([Mathlib source](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/master/Mathlib/RingTheory/FiniteType.lean)) (the surjection $R[x_1,\dots,x_n]\to A$). Let $\mathfrak p=A\cap\mathfrak m$, the inverse image of the prime ideal $\mathfrak m$ under the inclusion $A\subseteq S$, hence a prime ideal of $A$. The two $R$-algebra maps $\pi\circ\phi$ and $\gamma$ from $R[x_1,\dots,x_n]$ to $k$ agree on every $x_i$ (step 1), so they are equal (an $R$-algebra map from the polynomial ring is determined by the images of the variables). Hence $\pi(\phi(P))=\iota(\beta(P)/1)$ for all polynomials $P$: the image $\pi(A)\cong A/\mathfrak p$ of $A$ in $k$ is the image of $B$ under $b\mapsto\iota(b/1)$.

*The map $A_{\mathfrak p}\to S$, and $A_{\mathfrak p}\to S/\mathfrak m$ is surjective.* Let $A_{\mathfrak p}$ be the localization of $A$ at $\mathfrak p$ `Localization.AtPrime, Ideal.ResidueField, Ideal.Fiber` ([Mathlib source](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/master/Mathlib/RingTheory/Localization/AtPrime/Basic.lean)), local with maximal ideal $\mathfrak pA_{\mathfrak p}$ and residue field $\kappa(\mathfrak p)=A_{\mathfrak p}/\mathfrak pA_{\mathfrak p}$. Every $s\in A\setminus\mathfrak p$ lies in $S\setminus\mathfrak m$ and so is a unit of $S$ ($S$ is local with maximal ideal $\mathfrak m$). Define $\theta:A_{\mathfrak p}\to S$, $\theta(a/s)=as^{-1}$: it is well defined, since $a/s=a'/s'$ means $u(as'-a's)=0$ for some $u\in A\setminus\mathfrak p$, and $u$, $s$, $s'$ are units of $S$, so $as^{-1}=a's'^{-1}$; it is a ring map ($as^{-1}+a's'^{-1}=(as'+a's)(ss')^{-1}$, $(as^{-1})(a's'^{-1})=aa'(ss')^{-1}$, $\theta(1/1)=1$), and $\theta(a/1)=a$, so $R\to A\to A_{\mathfrak p}\xrightarrow{\theta}S$ is the structure map $R\to S$. Moreover $\theta(\mathfrak pA_{\mathfrak p})\subseteq\mathfrak m$, so $\pi\circ\theta$ induces a map of fields $\kappa(\mathfrak p)\to k$. *$\pi\circ\theta$ is surjective* (this is $\kappa(\mathfrak p)=S/\mathfrak m$ of the source): every element of $k$ is $\iota(b/w)$ with $b\in B$, $w\in W$; choose polynomials $P,Q$ with $\beta(P)=b$, $\beta(Q)=w$ (step 1) and put $a=\phi(P)$, $s=\phi(Q)\in A$. By step 2, $\pi(s)=\iota(w/1)$, a unit of $k$ ($w/1$ is a unit of $W^{-1}B$), so $\pi(s)\neq0$, $s\notin\mathfrak m$, $s\in A\setminus\mathfrak p$; and $\pi(\theta(a/s))=\pi(a)\pi(s)^{-1}=\iota(b/1)\iota(w/1)^{-1}=\iota(b/w)$.

*$S$ is a finitely generated $A_{\mathfrak p}$-module* (part (1) of 07DT, applied to $A_{\mathfrak p}\to S$). Regard $S$ as an $A_{\mathfrak p}$-algebra through $\theta$ (step 3). $S$ is Artinian, and its Jacobson radical $J(S)$, the intersection of all maximal ideals of $S$, is $\mathfrak m$, the only maximal ideal of the local ring $S$. By step 3, $A_{\mathfrak p}\to S\to S/J(S)=S/\mathfrak m$ is surjective, so $S/J(S)$ is generated by $1$ as an $A_{\mathfrak p}$-module, in particular finitely generated. By `IsSemiprimaryRing.finite_of_isArtinian` ([Mathlib source](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/master/Mathlib/RingTheory/HopkinsLevitzki.lean)) (with $A_{\mathfrak p}\to S$ in place of $A\to B$), $S$ is a finitely generated $A_{\mathfrak p}$-module.

*Both $R\to A_{\mathfrak p}$ and $\theta:A_{\mathfrak p}\to S$ are essentially of finite type.* $A$ is a finite type $R$-algebra (step 2) and $A_{\mathfrak p}=(A\setminus\mathfrak p)^{-1}A$ with the $R$-algebra structure through $R\to A\to A_{\mathfrak p}$ `Localization.AtPrime, Ideal.ResidueField, Ideal.Fiber` ([Mathlib source](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/master/Mathlib/RingTheory/Localization/AtPrime/Basic.lean)), so $R\to A_{\mathfrak p}$ is essentially of finite type `Algebra.EssFiniteType` ([Mathlib source](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/master/Mathlib/RingTheory/EssentialFiniteness.lean)). If $s_1,\dots,s_t$ generate $S$ as an $A_{\mathfrak p}$-module (step 4), the $A_{\mathfrak p}$-algebra map $A_{\mathfrak p}[y_1,\dots,y_t]\to S$, $y_j\mapsto s_j$, is surjective (its image contains every $A_{\mathfrak p}$-linear combination of the $s_j$), so $S$ is a finite type $A_{\mathfrak p}$-algebra `Algebra.FiniteType` ([Mathlib source](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/master/Mathlib/RingTheory/FiniteType.lean)); with the multiplicative subset $\{1\}\subseteq S$, $S\cong\{1\}^{-1}S$ ($c\mapsto c/1$) as $A_{\mathfrak p}$-algebras, so $\theta$ is essentially of finite type `Algebra.EssFiniteType` ([Mathlib source](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/master/Mathlib/RingTheory/EssentialFiniteness.lean)).

*Conclusion.* By step 5 and `RingHom.EssFiniteType.comp` ([Mathlib source](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/master/Mathlib/RingTheory/RingHom/EssFiniteType.lean)) the composite $R\to A_{\mathfrak p}\xrightarrow{\theta}S$ is essentially of finite type; by step 3 it is the structure map $R\to S$. Hence $R\to S$ is essentially of finite type.
