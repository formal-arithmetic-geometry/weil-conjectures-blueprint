Let $R$ be a commutative ring, $I\subset R$ an ideal and $M$ a finite (finitely generated) $R$-module `Module.Finite` ([Mathlib source](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/master/Mathlib/RingTheory/Finiteness/Defs.lean)); write $IM\subset M$ for the submodule generated by the products $am$ with $a\in I$, $m\in M$. The *$I$-depth* $\operatorname{depth}_I(M)\in\{0,1,2,\dots\}\cup\{\infty\}$ is defined as follows: (1) if $IM\neq M$, it is the supremum in $\{0,1,2,\dots\}\cup\{\infty\}$ of the lengths $r$ of the $M$-regular sequences $f_1,\dots,f_r$ in $I$ `RingTheory.Sequence.IsRegular` ([Mathlib source](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/master/Mathlib/RingTheory/Regular/RegularSequence.lean)) (the empty sequence, $r=0$, is one, since $M/0\cdot M=M\neq0$); (2) if $IM=M$ (for instance $M=0$, or $I=R$), $\operatorname{depth}_I(M)=\infty$. If $(R,\mathfrak m)$ is a local ring (a commutative ring with exactly one maximal ideal $\mathfrak m$), $\operatorname{depth}(M):=\operatorname{depth}_{\mathfrak m}(M)$ is the *depth* of $M$, and the depth of the local ring $R$ is $\operatorname{depth}(R)$, the depth of $R$ as a module over itself.
