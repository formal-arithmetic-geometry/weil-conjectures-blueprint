*The primes of a finite $R$-algebra.* Let $B$ be a finite $R$-algebra. Since $\mathfrak m$ is the only prime of $R$, `Algebra.QuasiFinite.discreteTopology_primeSpectrum` ([Mathlib source](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/master/Mathlib/RingTheory/QuasiFinite/Basic.lean)) gives: $B$ has finitely many prime ideals $\mathfrak q_1,\dots,\mathfrak q_r$, all maximal, all lying over $\mathfrak m$ (i.e. $\varphi^{-1}(\mathfrak q_i)=\mathfrak m$ for the structure map $\varphi:R\to B$, so $\varphi(\mathfrak m)\subseteq\mathfrak q_i$), and every element of $\mathfrak q_1\cap\dots\cap\mathfrak q_r$ is nilpotent. If $r=0$ then $B=0$ and both $B$ and $B/\mathfrak mB=0$ have the single idempotent $0$, so the lifting statement of step 4 holds trivially; assume $r\ge1$ below (the argument of steps 2-4 also covers $r=0$ with empty products).

*$B$ is a product of local rings.* By step 1 the maximal ideals of $B$ are exactly $\mathfrak q_1,\dots,\mathfrak q_r$, finitely many, and the Jacobson radical $\mathfrak q_1\cap\dots\cap\mathfrak q_r$ consists of nilpotent elements. By `MaximalSpectrum.toPiLocalizationEquiv` ([Mathlib source](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/master/Mathlib/RingTheory/Spectrum/Prime/Topology.lean)) the canonical map $\psi:B\to\prod_{i=1}^rB_i$, $B_i=B_{\mathfrak q_i}$, $b\mapsto(b/1)_i$, is a ring isomorphism; it is an $R$-algebra isomorphism since $R$ acts on each $B_i$ through $R\to B\to B_i$. Each $B_i$ is local with maximal ideal $\mathfrak n_i=\mathfrak q_iB_{\mathfrak q_i}$.

*Reduction modulo $\mathfrak m$ is computed factorwise.* Under $\psi$ the ideal $\mathfrak mB$ (generated by $\varphi(\mathfrak m)$) corresponds to $\prod_i\mathfrak mB_i$, where $\mathfrak mB_i$ is the ideal of $B_i$ generated by the image of $\mathfrak m$: an element $\sum_kx_kb_k$ ($x_k\in\mathfrak m$, $b_k\in B$) maps to $(\sum_kx_k\,b_k/1)_i\in\prod_i\mathfrak mB_i$, and conversely $(y_i)_i=\sum_i\epsilon_iy_i$ with $\epsilon_i$ the $i$-th unit vector and $y_i\in\mathfrak mB_i$ lies in $\mathfrak m\prod_iB_i$. Hence $\psi$ induces an isomorphism $B/\mathfrak mB\cong\prod_i\bar B_i$ with $\bar B_i=B_i/\mathfrak mB_i$, compatible with the reductions. Moreover $\mathfrak mB_i\subseteq\mathfrak n_i$: for $x\in\mathfrak m$ we have $\varphi(x)\in\mathfrak q_i$ (step 1), so $\varphi(x)/1\in\mathfrak q_iB_{\mathfrak q_i}=\mathfrak n_i$. So $\bar B_i$ is a nonzero ring ($1\notin\mathfrak mB_i$) and local (a quotient of a local ring by a proper ideal: its non-units are the images of $\mathfrak n_i$, which form the ideal $\mathfrak n_i/\mathfrak mB_i$).

*Idempotents lift uniquely.* A local ring $L$ has no idempotents other than $0,1$: if $\epsilon^2=\epsilon$ then $\epsilon(1-\epsilon)=0$, and since $\epsilon+(1-\epsilon)=1$ not both lie in the maximal ideal, so one of them is a unit and the other is $0$. Idempotents of a product are computed componentwise. So by steps 2 and 3 the idempotents of $B\cong\prod_iB_i$ are the vectors $(\epsilon_i)_i$ with $\epsilon_i\in\{0,1\}\subset B_i$, those of $B/\mathfrak mB\cong\prod_i\bar B_i$ are the vectors $(\bar\epsilon_i)_i$ with $\bar\epsilon_i\in\{0,1\}\subset\bar B_i$, and reduction modulo $\mathfrak mB$ sends $(\epsilon_i)_i$ to the vector with the same $0$s and $1$s. Since $0\neq1$ in every $\bar B_i$ (step 3), this map between the two sets of $0/1$-vectors indexed by $\{1,\dots,r\}$ is a bijection: every idempotent of $B/\mathfrak mB$ is the reduction of exactly one idempotent of $B$. As $B$ was an arbitrary finite $R$-algebra, the pair $(R,\mathfrak m)$ is henselian, i.e. $R$ is a henselian local ring, in the sense of [def-weil-pbc-henselian-terminology] (whose definition is exactly this idempotent-lifting property for finite algebras, so no passage through another characterization of henselian rings, such as Stacks 04GG (10)$\Rightarrow$(1), is needed beyond what is written here).
