Smallness and nonemptiness. The objects are pairs of a member of the set $\mathcal P$ and an algebra homomorphism to $\Lambda$, so they form a set. The finitely presented $R$-algebra $R$ with its structure map to $\Lambda$, transported to a representative in $\mathcal P$, gives an object.

Common successors. For objects $(A,\alpha)$ and $(B,\beta)$, the $R$-algebra $A\otimes_RB$ is finitely presented by base change and transitivity. Its two inclusions and the map $A\otimes_RB\to\Lambda$ induced by $\alpha$ and $\beta$ (via multiplication) give a common successor after transport to its representative in $\mathcal P$.

Equalize parallel arrows. A finite polynomial presentation of their source gives finitely many generators. In the target, form the ideal generated by the differences of the images of these generators. It is finitely generated and killed by the map of the target to $\Lambda$. The quotient is finitely presented and its quotient map equalizes the two maps: precompose with the surjective polynomial presentation, on which algebra homomorphisms are determined by the images of the variables. Transporting the quotient to $\mathcal P$ gives a morphism of $\mathcal I$ equalizing the two arrows. Together with the preceding two steps this shows that $\mathcal I$ is filtered.

Every element of $\Lambda$ is represented in a stage. The polynomial algebra $R[X]$ in one variable is finitely presented; map it to $\Lambda$ by evaluating $X$ at the element. Transport this algebra to a representative in $\mathcal P$. The image of its variable is the required element.

Equality is witnessed at a later stage. For elements $a,b$ in a stage $A$ with equal image in $\Lambda$, divide $A$ by the principal ideal $(a-b)$. This finitely generated ideal is killed by the stage map, which therefore factors through the finitely presented quotient. Transport to $\mathcal P$; the resulting arrow sends $a$ and $b$ to equal elements.

Colimit universal property. By the elementwise criterion for filtered colimits of sets, the preceding representation and equality conditions show that the underlying cocone of sets is a colimit. Any two elements of $\Lambda$ can be represented in one common stage by filteredness. For a cocone of algebras, the unique induced map of underlying sets therefore preserves addition and multiplication, as one sees by computing both elements at this common stage. A fixed stage verifies compatibility with $0$, $1$ and the structure maps from $R$, so this map is an algebra homomorphism. The factorization equations of the set colimit give its factorization, and representation of every element proves uniqueness. This shows that the canonical cocone of algebras is a colimit, without constructing a separate colimit of algebras.
