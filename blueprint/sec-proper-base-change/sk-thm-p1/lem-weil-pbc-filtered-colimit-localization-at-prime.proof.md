*Step 1 (the functor $i\mapsto(R_i)_{\mathfrak p_i}$).* Write $D_i=(R_i)_{\mathfrak p_i}$ and $S_i=R_i\setminus\mathfrak p_i$. Each $\mathfrak p_i=\iota_i^{-1}(\mathfrak p)$ is a prime ideal of $R_i$ (the preimage of a prime ideal under a ring map: $1\notin\mathfrak p_i$ since $\iota_i(1)=1\notin\mathfrak p$, and $xy\in\mathfrak p_i$ means $\iota_i(x)\iota_i(y)\in\mathfrak p$). For $a:i\to j$ the cocone identity $\iota_j\circ R(a)=\iota_i$ `CategoryTheory.Limits.colimit` ([Mathlib source](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/master/Mathlib/CategoryTheory/Limits/HasLimits.lean)) gives $R(a)^{-1}(\mathfrak p_j)=R(a)^{-1}\iota_j^{-1}(\mathfrak p)=(\iota_j\circ R(a))^{-1}(\mathfrak p)=\mathfrak p_i$, so $R(a)(S_i)\subset S_j$. Hence $D(a):D_i\to D_j$, $x/s\mapsto R(a)(x)/R(a)(s)$, is well defined `Localization.AtPrime, Ideal.ResidueField, Ideal.Fiber` ([Mathlib source](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/master/Mathlib/RingTheory/Localization/AtPrime/Basic.lean)): $R(a)(s)\in S_j$, and if $x/s=x'/s'$ in $D_i$, i.e. $u(xs'-x's)=0$ with $u\in S_i$, then $R(a)(u)\in S_j$ and $R(a)(u)\big(R(a)(x)R(a)(s')-R(a)(x')R(a)(s)\big)=0$. It is a ring map because the sum and product of fractions are given by the formulas $x/s+x'/s'=(xs'+x's)/(ss')$, $(x/s)(x'/s')=(xx')/(ss')$, $1=1/1$, which the ring map $R(a)$ respects. From $R(\mathrm{id}_i)=\mathrm{id}$ and $R(b\circ a)=R(b)\circ R(a)$ one reads off $D(\mathrm{id}_i)=\mathrm{id}$ and $D(b\circ a)=D(b)\circ D(a)$ on every fraction $x/s$: $i\mapsto D_i$ is a functor $I\to$ commutative rings. This is part (1).

*Step 2 (the cocone $\kappa_i$).* For $s\in S_i$ one has $\iota_i(s)\notin\mathfrak p$ by the definition of $\mathfrak p_i$, so $\kappa_i:D_i\to R_{\mathfrak p}$, $x/s\mapsto\iota_i(x)/\iota_i(s)$, is defined; it is well defined (if $u(xs'-x's)=0$ with $u\in S_i$, then $\iota_i(u)\notin\mathfrak p$ witnesses $\iota_i(x)/\iota_i(s)=\iota_i(x')/\iota_i(s')$) and a ring map, by the formulas for sum, product and $1$ of fractions `Localization.AtPrime, Ideal.ResidueField, Ideal.Fiber` ([Mathlib source](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/master/Mathlib/RingTheory/Localization/AtPrime/Basic.lean)), exactly as in step 1. For $a:i\to j$, $\kappa_j(D(a)(x/s))=\iota_j(R(a)(x))/\iota_j(R(a)(s))=\iota_i(x)/\iota_i(s)=\kappa_i(x/s)$ by $\iota_j\circ R(a)=\iota_i$; so $(R_{\mathfrak p},(\kappa_i))$ is a cocone on $i\mapsto D_i$ `CategoryTheory.Limits.colimit` ([Mathlib source](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/master/Mathlib/CategoryTheory/Limits/HasLimits.lean)).

*Step 3 (every element of $R_{\mathfrak p}$ comes from a stage).* Let $z\in R_{\mathfrak p}$, $z=r/\sigma$ with $r\in R$, $\sigma\in R\setminus\mathfrak p$ `Localization.AtPrime, Ideal.ResidueField, Ideal.Fiber` ([Mathlib source](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/master/Mathlib/RingTheory/Localization/AtPrime/Basic.lean)). Commutative rings are the commutative $\mathbf Z$-algebras (every ring map is $\mathbf Z$-linear), so $R$ is the colimit of $i\mapsto R_i$ in commutative $\mathbf Z$-algebras, and by part (2) of `CategoryTheory.Limits.Concrete.isColimit_exists_rep, CategoryTheory.Under.preservesColimitsOfShape_forget_of_isConnected, CommAlgCat.commAlgCatEquivUnder, CommRingCat.FilteredColimits.forget_preservesFilteredColimits` ([Mathlib source](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/master/Mathlib/CategoryTheory/Limits/ConcreteCategory/Basic.lean)) (with base ring $\mathbf Z$) $r=\iota_i(x)$, $\sigma=\iota_j(s)$ for some objects $i,j$ and $x\in R_i$, $s\in R_j$. As $I$ is filtered `CategoryTheory.Limits.colimit` ([Mathlib source](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/master/Mathlib/CategoryTheory/Limits/HasLimits.lean)) there are $a:i\to k$, $b:j\to k$; then $r=\iota_k(x')$, $\sigma=\iota_k(s')$ with $x'=R(a)(x)$, $s'=R(b)(s)$, and $s'\in S_k$ because $\iota_k(s')=\sigma\notin\mathfrak p$. So $z=\kappa_k(x'/s')$. Combining with filteredness and the cocone identity of step 2, finitely many elements of $R_{\mathfrak p}$ are images under one $\kappa_k$.

*Step 4 (equality criterion).* Let $w\in D_i$, $w'\in D_j$ with $\kappa_i(w)=\kappa_j(w')$. Then there are $e:i\to n$, $e':j\to n$ with $D(e)(w)=D(e')(w')$. Proof: choose $a:i\to k$, $b:j\to k$ ($I$ filtered) and replace $w,w'$ by $D(a)(w)=x/s$, $D(b)(w')=y/t$ in $D_k$ (step 2: $\kappa_k\circ D(a)=\kappa_i$). Then $\iota_k(x)/\iota_k(s)=\iota_k(y)/\iota_k(t)$ in $R_{\mathfrak p}$, so by the definition of equality of fractions `Localization.AtPrime, Ideal.ResidueField, Ideal.Fiber` ([Mathlib source](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/master/Mathlib/RingTheory/Localization/AtPrime/Basic.lean)) there is $u\in R\setminus\mathfrak p$ with $u\,\iota_k(xt-ys)=0$. By `CategoryTheory.Limits.Concrete.isColimit_exists_rep, CategoryTheory.Under.preservesColimitsOfShape_forget_of_isConnected, CommAlgCat.commAlgCatEquivUnder, CommRingCat.FilteredColimits.forget_preservesFilteredColimits` ([Mathlib source](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/master/Mathlib/CategoryTheory/Limits/ConcreteCategory/Basic.lean)) (2) (base ring $\mathbf Z$, as in step 3) $u=\iota_l(v)$ with $v\in R_l$; choose $c:k\to m$, $d:l\to m$ and put $v'=R(d)(v)$, $q=R(c)(xt-ys)$; then $\iota_m(v'q)=u\,\iota_k(xt-ys)=0$ and $v'\in S_m$ since $\iota_m(v')=u\notin\mathfrak p$. By `CategoryTheory.Limits.Concrete.isColimit_rep_eq_iff_exists` ([Mathlib source](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/master/Mathlib/CategoryTheory/Limits/ConcreteCategory/Basic.lean)) there is $f:m\to n$ with $R(f)(v'q)=0$. With $g=f\circ c:k\to n$ and $v''=R(f)(v')\in S_n$ (step 1: $R(f)(S_m)\subset S_n$) this reads $v''\big(R(g)(x)R(g)(t)-R(g)(y)R(g)(s)\big)=0$, i.e. $D(g)(x/s)=D(g)(y/t)$ in $D_n$; so $e=g\circ a$, $e'=g\circ b$ work. (Conversely $D(e)(w)=D(e')(w')$ implies $\kappa_i(w)=\kappa_j(w')$ by step 2.) (`CategoryTheory.Limits.colimit` ([Mathlib source](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/master/Mathlib/CategoryTheory/Limits/HasLimits.lean))).

*Step 5 (universal property).* Let $(T,(g_i:D_i\to T))$ be a cocone of commutative rings on $i\mapsto D_i$. Define $g:R_{\mathfrak p}\to T$ by $g(\kappa_i(w))=g_i(w)$: every $z\in R_{\mathfrak p}$ has the form $\kappa_i(w)$ (step 3), and if $\kappa_i(w)=\kappa_j(w')$ then with $e,e'$ from step 4, $g_i(w)=g_n(D(e)(w))=g_n(D(e')(w'))=g_j(w')$, so $g$ is well defined. It is a ring map: for $z,z'\in R_{\mathfrak p}$ choose by step 3 one $k$ and $w,w'\in D_k$ with $z=\kappa_k(w)$, $z'=\kappa_k(w')$; then $z+z'=\kappa_k(w+w')$, $zz'=\kappa_k(ww')$, so $g(z+z')=g_k(w)+g_k(w')$ and $g(zz')=g_k(w)g_k(w')$; and $g(1)=g_i(1)=1$ for any object $i$ ($I$ is nonempty, being filtered). By construction $g\circ\kappa_i=g_i$ for all $i$, and any ring map $h$ with $h\circ\kappa_i=g_i$ for all $i$ equals $g$, since the images of the $\kappa_i$ cover $R_{\mathfrak p}$ (step 3). Hence $(R_{\mathfrak p},(\kappa_i))$ is a colimit of $i\mapsto(R_i)_{\mathfrak p_i}$ in commutative rings `CategoryTheory.Limits.colimit` ([Mathlib source](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/master/Mathlib/CategoryTheory/Limits/HasLimits.lean)), which is part (2).
