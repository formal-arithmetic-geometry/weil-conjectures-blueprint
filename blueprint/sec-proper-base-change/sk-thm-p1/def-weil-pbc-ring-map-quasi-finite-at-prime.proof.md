*The definition is well formed.* $\mathfrak p=\varphi^{-1}(\mathfrak q)$ is a prime ideal of $R$ (the preimage of a prime ideal under a ring map), so $R_{\mathfrak p}$, its maximal ideal $\mathfrak pR_{\mathfrak p}$, the field $\kappa(\mathfrak p)$ and the fibre ring $F=S\otimes_R\kappa(\mathfrak p)$ with $j$ are defined `Localization.AtPrime, Ideal.ResidueField, Ideal.Fiber` ([Mathlib source](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/master/Mathlib/RingTheory/Localization/AtPrime/Basic.lean)). Since $\varphi^{-1}(\mathfrak q)=\mathfrak p$, `PrimeSpectrum.preimageOrderIsoFiber, PrimeSpectrum.preimageEquivFiber_apply_asIdeal, PrimeSpectrum.preimageEquivFiber_symm_apply_coe, Algebra.TensorProduct.comm` ([Mathlib source](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/master/Mathlib/RingTheory/LocalRing/ResidueField/Fiber.lean)) (applied to $A=R$, $B=S$) says that $\mathfrak Q\mapsto j^{-1}(\mathfrak Q)$ is a bijection from the primes of $F$ onto the primes of $S$ lying over $\mathfrak p$, so there is exactly one prime $\overline{\mathfrak q}$ of $F$ with $j^{-1}(\overline{\mathfrak q})=\mathfrak q$ (namely $\ker\psi_{\mathfrak q}$); no choice is made. $\overline{\mathfrak q}$ is a point of the topological space $\operatorname{Spec}(F)$ `AlgebraicGeometry.Spec` ([Mathlib source](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/master/Mathlib/AlgebraicGeometry/Scheme.lean)), and 'isolated' means that $\{\overline{\mathfrak q}\}$ is open. So 'quasi-finite at $\mathfrak q$' is a property of the pair $(\varphi,\mathfrak q)$; the finite-type hypothesis only restricts the ring maps for which it is used (Stacks states it there because the equivalent conditions of 00PK need it).
