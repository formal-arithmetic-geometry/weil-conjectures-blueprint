Put K = κ(x) and q : Spec K → U the residue-field map. Each i_j followed by p is an isomorphism. Thus s_j = q followed by its inverse, together with the identity of Spec K, is a pullback square over p ∘ i_j. Pullback.lift of s_j followed by i_j and the identity defines a_j : Spec K → Z_x, whose composite with the fibre projection is the identity. This is IsPullback.of_vert_isIso followed by pullback.lift_fst and pullback.lift_snd. Finite scheme coproducts are universal. Apply FinitaryPreExtensive.isUniversal_finiteCoproducts and IsUniversalColimit.nonempty_isColimit_of_isPullback_right to the squares just constructed. It follows that the a_j form a colimiting cofan for Z_x, and Sigma.desc a is an isomorphism. (`CategoryTheory.FinitaryPreExtensive.isUniversal_finiteCoproducts` ([Mathlib source](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/master/Mathlib/CategoryTheory/Extensive.lean))).

The finite-coproduct isomorphism sigmaSpec identifies the coproduct of J copies of Spec K with Spec(∏_J K). Compose it with the inverse of Sigma.desc a. Its composite with Spec of the diagonal algebra map K → ∏_J K equals the fibre projection: cancel Sigma.desc a, use sigmaSpec_comp_Spec_map_algebraMap, and check on each coprojection where both maps are the identity. Apply Scheme.Hom.finrankΓ_eq_of_iso and finrankΓ_SpecMap. The finite product ∏_J K has K-dimension |J|, by Module.finrank_fintype_fun_eq_card. For empty J this is the zero algebra and dimension zero. All these identifications are proved in Lean in the delivered module.
