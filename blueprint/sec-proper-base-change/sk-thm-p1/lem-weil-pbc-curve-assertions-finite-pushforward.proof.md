**Step 1.** *$f_*F$ is torsion.* Let $\bar y:\operatorname{Spec}\Omega\to Y$ be a geometric point. By the finite stalk formula [lemma-weil-pbc-finite-stalk-formula], $(f_*F)_{\bar y}\cong\prod_{\bar x\in|X_{\bar y}|}F_{\bar x}$, a finite product (= direct sum) of stalks of $F$ at geometric points of $X$. Each $F_{\bar x}$ is a torsion group because $F$ is torsion ((1)$\Rightarrow$(3) of [lem-weil-pbc-torsion-sheaf-characterizations]), and a finite direct sum of torsion groups is torsion. So every geometric stalk of $f_*F$ is torsion, and by (3)$\Rightarrow$(1),(2) of [lem-weil-pbc-torsion-sheaf-characterizations] $f_*F$ is a torsion sheaf (every local section locally killed by a positive integer, the condition of [def-weil-pbc-curve-setting]). As $Y$ is separated of finite type over $k$ with $\dim Y\le1$, $(Y,f_*F)$ is a curve coefficient pair. This is part (a).

**Step 2.** *Torsion type passes from $f_*F$ to $F$.* Let $\bar x:\operatorname{Spec}\Omega\to X$ be a geometric point and $\bar y=f\circ\bar x$. Then $(\bar x,\mathrm{id})$ is a geometric point of the fibre $X_{\bar y}=X\times_Y\operatorname{Spec}\Omega$, so $F_{\bar x}$ is one of the factors in the finite stalk formula $(f_*F)_{\bar y}\cong\prod_{\bar x'\in|X_{\bar y}|}F_{\bar x'}$ [lemma-weil-pbc-finite-stalk-formula], hence isomorphic to a direct summand of $(f_*F)_{\bar y}$. If every element of every geometric stalk of $f_*F$ is killed by a power of $p$ (resp. has finite order prime to $p$), the same holds for every element of $F_{\bar x}$. So, in the sense of [def-weil-pbc-primary-torsion]: if $f_*F$ is $p$-primary then $F$ is $p$-primary, and if $f_*F$ is prime to $\operatorname{char}k$ then $F$ is prime to $\operatorname{char}k$ (no condition in characteristic $0$).

**Step 3.** *Cohomology of $f_*F$.* By [lem-weil-pbc-finite-pushforward-cohomology] (for the finite $f$), the edge map $e^q_{f,F}=H^q(\varepsilon_{f,F})\circ f^*:H^q(Y_{\Et},f_*F)\to H^q(X_{\Et},F)$ is an isomorphism for every $q\ge0$. In particular $H^q(Y_{\Et},f_*F)=0$ if and only if $H^q(X_{\Et},F)=0$, and one is finite if and only if the other is.

**Step 4.** *Vanishing: $\mathcal C_1,\mathcal C_2,\mathcal C_3$.* ($\mathcal C_1$, [def-weil-pbc-C1]) For $q>2$, $H^q(Y_{\Et},f_*F)\cong H^q(X_{\Et},F)=0$ by step 3 and $\mathcal C_1(X,F)$. ($\mathcal C_2$, [def-weil-pbc-C2]) If $Y$ is affine, then $X$ is affine by (b) of [lem-weil-pbc-finite-morphism-permanence], so for $q>1$, $H^q(Y_{\Et},f_*F)\cong H^q(X_{\Et},F)=0$ by step 3 and $\mathcal C_2(X,F)$. ($\mathcal C_3$, [def-weil-pbc-C3]) If $\operatorname{char}k=p>0$ and $f_*F$ is $p$-primary, then $F$ is $p$-primary by step 2, so for $q>1$, $H^q(Y_{\Et},f_*F)\cong H^q(X_{\Et},F)=0$ by step 3 and $\mathcal C_3(X,F)$.

**Step 5.** *Finiteness: $\mathcal C_4,\mathcal C_5$.* If $f_*F$ is constructible [def-weil-pbc-constructible-torsion], then $F$ is constructible by [lem-weil-pbc-finite-pushforward-constructible-descent]. ($\mathcal C_4$, [def-weil-pbc-C4]) If $f_*F$ is constructible and prime to $\operatorname{char}k$, then $F$ is constructible and, by step 2, prime to $\operatorname{char}k$; by $\mathcal C_4(X,F)$ every $H^q(X_{\Et},F)$ is finite, hence so is $H^q(Y_{\Et},f_*F)$ by step 3. ($\mathcal C_5$, [def-weil-pbc-C5]) If $Y$ is proper over $k$ and $f_*F$ is constructible, then $X$ is proper over $k$ by (c) of [lem-weil-pbc-finite-morphism-permanence] (its structure morphism is that of $Y$ composed with $f$, as $f$ is a $k$-morphism) and $F$ is constructible; by $\mathcal C_5(X,F)$ every $H^q(X_{\Et},F)$ is finite, hence so is $H^q(Y_{\Et},f_*F)$ by step 3.

**Step 6.** *Base field extension: $\mathcal C_6,\mathcal C_7$.* Let $k'/k$ be an algebraically closed extension. Put $Y'=Y_{k'}=Y\times_kk'$ with projection $\pi_Y:Y'\to Y$ [def-pc-base-change], and form the cartesian square [def-weil-pbc-cartesian-square] $X'=X\times_YY'$, $g'=\pi_Y$-base change $g':X'\to X$, $f':X'\to Y'$, with $\theta_\square$ built from the composition isomorphisms as required in [lem-weil-pbc-edge-map-base-change]. By (a) of [lem-weil-pbc-finite-morphism-permanence], $f'$ is finite. By the pasting of fibre products, $X'=X\times_Y(Y\times_kk')\cong X\times_kk'=X_{k'}$; let $h:X_{k'}\xrightarrow{\sim}X'$ be this isomorphism, with $g'\circ h=\pi_X$, the projection of [def-weil-pbc-extension-pullback]. (i) By [lemma-weil-pbc-finite-exchange], $\Ex^0_{\square,F}:\pi_Y^{-1}f_*F\to f'_*g'^{-1}F$ [def-weil-pbc-underived-exchange] is an isomorphism, so $H^q(\Ex^0_{\square,F})$ is an isomorphism (functoriality of $H^q$, [lem-weil-pbc-cohomology-pullback-functorial]). (ii) By [lem-weil-pbc-finite-pushforward-cohomology] for $f$ and for $f'$, $e^q_{f,F}$ and $e^q_{f',g'^{-1}F}$ are isomorphisms. (iii) By [lem-weil-pbc-edge-map-base-change], $g'^*\circ e^q_{f,F}=e^q_{f',g'^{-1}F}\circ H^q(\Ex^0_{\square,F})\circ\pi_Y^*$; with (i), (ii) this shows: $\pi_Y^*:H^q(Y_{\Et},f_*F)\to H^q(Y'_{\Et},\pi_Y^{-1}f_*F)$, which is $\rho^q_{k'/k}(Y,f_*F)$ [def-weil-pbc-extension-pullback], is an isomorphism if and only if $g'^*:H^q(X_{\Et},F)\to H^q(X'_{\Et},g'^{-1}F)$ is. (iv) By clause 4 (composition) of [lem-weil-pbc-cohomology-pullback-functorial], $\pi_X^*=(g'\circ h)^*=H^q(c_{g',h,F})\circ h^*\circ g'^*$, where $c_{g',h}$ is the isomorphism of [lem-weil-pbc-inverse-image-composition]; and $h^*$ is an isomorphism, since by the same clause applied to $h\circ h^{-1}=\mathrm{id}_{X'}$ and $h^{-1}\circ h=\mathrm{id}_{X_{k'}}$ (with the normalisation $H^q(e)\circ\mathrm{id}^*=\mathrm{id}$) it has a two-sided inverse up to the isomorphisms $H^q(c)$, $H^q(e)$. Hence $\rho^q_{k'/k}(X,F)=\pi_X^*$ is an isomorphism if and only if $g'^*$ is, and by (iii) if and only if $\rho^q_{k'/k}(Y,f_*F)$ is. ($\mathcal C_6$, [def-weil-pbc-C6]) If $f_*F$ is prime to $\operatorname{char}k$, then so is $F$ (step 2), $\mathcal C_6(X,F)$ makes $\rho^q_{k'/k}(X,F)$ an isomorphism for every $q$, hence $\rho^q_{k'/k}(Y,f_*F)$ is one. ($\mathcal C_7$, [def-weil-pbc-C7]) If $Y$ is proper over $k$, then so is $X$ ((c) of [lem-weil-pbc-finite-morphism-permanence]), and $\mathcal C_7(X,F)$ gives the same conclusion.

**Step 7.** *Surjectivity: $\mathcal C_8$* ([def-weil-pbc-C8]). Let $a:U\hookrightarrow Y$ be an open immersion [def-weil-open-immersion]. Form the cartesian square $V=X\times_YU$ with $b:V\to X$ and $f_U:V\to U$ [def-weil-pbc-cartesian-square]; $b$ is an open immersion (it identifies $V$ with the open subscheme $f^{-1}(a(U))$ of $X$) and $f_U$ is finite by (a) of [lem-weil-pbc-finite-morphism-permanence]. By [lemma-weil-pbc-finite-exchange] the exchange morphism $\Ex^0:a^{-1}f_*F\to f_{U*}b^{-1}F$ [def-weil-pbc-underived-exchange] is an isomorphism, and by [lem-weil-pbc-finite-pushforward-cohomology] so are $e^2_{f,F}$ and $e^2_{f_U,b^{-1}F}$; so $\psi=e^2_{f_U,b^{-1}F}\circ H^2(\Ex^0)$ is an isomorphism, and [lem-weil-pbc-edge-map-base-change] gives $b^*\circ e^2_{f,F}=\psi\circ a^*$. Let $y\in H^2(U_{\Et},a^{-1}f_*F)$. By $\mathcal C_8(X,F)$ for the open immersion $b$, there is $x\in H^2(X_{\Et},F)$ with $b^*x=\psi(y)$. Put $w=(e^2_{f,F})^{-1}(x)\in H^2(Y_{\Et},f_*F)$. Then $\psi(a^*w)=b^*e^2_{f,F}(w)=b^*x=\psi(y)$, and since $\psi$ is injective, $a^*w=y$. So $a^*:H^2(Y_{\Et},f_*F)\to H^2(U_{\Et},a^{-1}f_*F)$ is surjective. (The restriction map of $\mathcal C_8$ is the pullback map along $a$, resp. $b$, as in [lem-weil-pbc-cohomology-pullback-functorial], clause 5.)

**Step 8.** *Conclusion.* Steps 4 to 7 give, for each $j$, $\mathcal C_j(Y,f_*F)$ from $\mathcal C_j(X,F)$, which is part (b); with part (a) (step 1), if $\mathcal C(X,F)$ holds then $\mathcal C(Y,f_*F)$ holds [def-weil-pbc-eight-curve-assertions].
