Restrict the deck action to H and regard it as a SingleObj H diagram of finite etale U-schemes. Take its quotient cocone, using the finite-group quotient and exact geometric fibre functor of lem-fet-galois-category. In Lean this is Scheme.FiniteEtale.quotientCocone; fiber_quotientCocone says that its leg p is surjective on a geometric fibre and identifies precisely H-orbits. The quotient structure map v is finite etale, as are morphisms between finite etale U-schemes. The conservative fibre functor implies that p is surjective (surjective_of_surjective_fiber). Hence V is connected as the continuous surjective image of the connected scheme c.P. ([lem-fet-galois-category]).

The quotient cocone is H-invariant. Two points of c.P over a geometric point of V have equal quotient images, so the quotient-fibre orbit relation gives an h in H carrying one to the other. Its equivalence closure is already the orbit relation: use 1 for reflexivity, inverse for symmetry, and multiplication for transitivity. Uniqueness follows directly from the original Q-torsor isomorphism: equality after acting by g and h gives equality of the two inclusions into the disjoint coproduct, forcing g=h. Thus H acts simply transitively on this fibre. The conservative finite etale fibre functor gives the H-torsor isomorphism (isIso_sigmaDesc_of_existsUnique). Construct cH using c.P itself, the map p, and the restricted action. Its connectedness is the original instance on c.P, and e is the identity. Cocone compatibility is p followed by v equals c.π. ([lem-fet-galois-category]).

For any finite torsor under a group G and any separably closed geometric point s, its geometric fibre has cardinality Nat.card G. Indeed the surjectivity and split finite etale fibre of lem-galois-geometric-fibre give a lift a of s. The map g to a followed by the action of g is injective by disjointness of coproduct summands. It is surjective because the pair (b,a) lifts through the torsor isomorphism, and a morphism from Spec of a field to a coproduct factors through a unique summand. This argument applies to both c and cH and to every separably closed field in the given universe. ([lem-galois-geometric-fibre]).

For each s, identify the fibre of p followed by v with the dependent sum over b in the fibre of v of the fibre of p over b. Since each latter fibre has cardinality Nat.card H, finite dependent-sum counting gives Nat.card Q = Nat.card(v.GeometricFiber s) * Nat.card H. Mathlib Subgroup.index_mul_card gives Nat.card Q = H.index * Nat.card H. Cancel the positive natural number Nat.card H. ULift does not change cardinality. This includes H=1, H=Q, and Q=1 without excluding rank one.
