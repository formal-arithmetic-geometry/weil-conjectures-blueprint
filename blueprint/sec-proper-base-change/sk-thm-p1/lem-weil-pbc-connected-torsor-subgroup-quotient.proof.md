*The quotient.* Restrict the deck action of $Q$ on $Y$ to $H$ and form the quotient $V=Y/H$ in the category of finite etale $U$-schemes, using finite-group quotients and the exact geometric fibre functor of [lem-fet-galois-category]: the quotient map $p:Y\to V$ is surjective on every geometric fibre, and two points of a geometric fibre of $Y$ have the same image exactly when they lie in one $H$-orbit. The structure map $v:V\to U$ is finite etale, and so is $p$ (a morphism between finite etale $U$-schemes). Since the fibre functor is conservative, $p$ is surjective; hence $V$ is connected, as the continuous surjective image of the connected scheme $Y$. ([lem-fet-galois-category]).

*$Y\to V$ is an $H$-torsor.* The quotient map is $H$-invariant. Over a geometric point of $V$, two points of $Y$ with the same image are related by some $h\in H$ (the orbit description), and $h$ is unique: equality after acting by $g$ and by $h$ forces $g=h$, since $Y$ is a $Q$-torsor over $U$ and the summands of $Q\times Y\cong Y\times_UY$ are disjoint. So $H$ acts simply transitively on the geometric fibres of $p$, and the conservative fibre functor shows that $H\times Y\to Y\times_VY$ is an isomorphism. Take $Y_H=Y$ with the map $c_H=p$ and the restricted action; it is connected because $Y$ is, $e$ is the identity, and $c\circ e=v\circ c_H$ holds by construction. ([lem-fet-galois-category]).

*Fibre cardinalities.* For a finite etale $G$-torsor and any geometric point $s$ with separably closed field, the geometric fibre has exactly $|G|$ points: by [lem-galois-geometric-fibre] there is a lift $a$ of $s$; the map $g\mapsto g\cdot a$ is injective by disjointness of the summands, and surjective because any other lift $b$ gives the point $(b,a)$ of $Y\times_UY\cong G\times Y$, and a morphism from the spectrum of a field into a coproduct factors through a unique summand. This applies to $c$ and to $c_H$. ([lem-galois-geometric-fibre]).

*Index count.* For each $s$, the geometric fibre of $v\circ c_H=c$ is the disjoint union, over the points $b$ of the geometric fibre of $v$, of the fibres of $c_H$ over $b$, each of cardinality $|H|$. Hence $|Q|=|v^{-1}(s)|\cdot|H|$, while $|Q|=[Q:H]\cdot|H|$; cancelling $|H|>0$ gives $|v^{-1}(s)|=[Q:H]$. This includes $H=1$, $H=Q$ and $Q=1$.
