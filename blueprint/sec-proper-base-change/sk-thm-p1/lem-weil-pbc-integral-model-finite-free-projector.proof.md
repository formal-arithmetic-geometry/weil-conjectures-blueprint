Lift the entries of the matrix M of the given projector to X over A using surjectivity of A → A/I. Set f = charpoly X and B = A[x]/(f). Monicity gives Module.Finite A B; Algebra.IsIntegral.of_finite gives integrality. Cayley–Hamilton lets Polynomial.aeval X factor through B, producing ψ and ψ(x)=X. (`Matrix.charpoly` ([Mathlib source](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/master/Mathlib/LinearAlgebra/Matrix/Charpoly/Basic.lean)), `LinearMap.charpoly_toMatrix, LinearEquiv.charpoly_conj, Matrix.charpoly_monic, Matrix.charpoly_natDegree_eq_dim, Module.Basis.baseChange, LinearMap.charpoly_baseChange` ([Mathlib source](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/master/Mathlib/LinearAlgebra/Charpoly/ToMatrix.lean)), `AdjoinRoot.powerBasis'` ([Mathlib source](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/master/Mathlib/RingTheory/AdjoinRoot.lean)), `Algebra.IsIntegral.of_finite` ([Mathlib source](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/master/Mathlib/RingTheory/IntegralClosure/Algebra/Basic.lean)), `LinearMap.aeval_self_charpoly` ([Mathlib source](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/master/Mathlib/LinearAlgebra/Charpoly/Basic.lean))).

In matrices over (A/I)[t], write S = scalar(t) and N = C(M). Since N²=N and SN=NS, expansion gives (S-N)(1-S-N)=scalar(t-t²). Apply Matrix.det_mul and the determinant of a scalar matrix: charpoly(M) * det(1-S-N) = (t-t²)^n. Thus charpoly(M) divides (t-t²)^n. This is the private Lean theorem charpoly_dvd_projector_polynomial; it holds in every commutative ring, including the zero ring. (`Matrix.charpoly` ([Mathlib source](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/master/Mathlib/LinearAlgebra/Matrix/Charpoly/Basic.lean))).

The quotient map A/I → B/IB is well defined. Base change of charpoly gives f mod I = charpoly M. Evaluation at the class of x in B/IB sends f mod I to zero because f(x)=0 in B. Divisibility in the preceding step therefore gives (x-x²)^(n+1)=0 after reduction; the exponent n+1 avoids a separate empty-matrix case. (`LinearMap.charpoly_toMatrix, LinearEquiv.charpoly_conj, Matrix.charpoly_monic, Matrix.charpoly_natDegree_eq_dim, Module.Basis.baseChange, LinearMap.charpoly_baseChange` ([Mathlib source](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/master/Mathlib/LinearAlgebra/Charpoly/ToMatrix.lean))).

Put y = 1-(1-x^(n+1))^(n+1) in B. Apply Mathlib isIdempotentElem_one_sub_one_sub_pow_pow to the preceding nilpotence equality; the class of y is idempotent. This formula follows by the geometric-sum identity: (z-z²)^N divides e-e² for e=1-(1-z^N)^N. Under the composite B → Mat_n(A) → Mat_n(A/I), x goes to M. Since M and 1-M equal every positive power of themselves, y goes to 1-(1-M)=M. This proves all the claims; no residue field or prime-spectrum lemmas occur.
