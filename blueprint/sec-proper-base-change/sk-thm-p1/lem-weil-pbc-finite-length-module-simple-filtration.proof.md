Choose a chain of maximal length. Let $\ell=\mathrm{length}_R(M)<\infty$ `Module.length` ([Mathlib source](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/master/Mathlib/RingTheory/Length.lean)). Let $S$ be the set of integers $n\ge0$ for which there is a chain of $R$-submodules $0=M_0\subsetneq M_1\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq M_n=M$. $S$ is nonempty: if $M=0$ the one-term chain $0=M_0=M$ gives $0\in S$, and if $M\ne0$ the chain $0\subsetneq M$ gives $1\in S$. Every $n\in S$ satisfies $n\le\ell$ by the definition of $\ell$ as the supremum. So $S$ is a nonempty set of integers bounded above by $\ell$ and has a largest element $r$; fix a chain $0=M_0\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq M_r=M$ realizing it. (Stacks says 'choose any maximal chain'; this step writes out why one exists: a chain of largest length cannot be refined.)

The quotients are simple. Fix $1\le i\le r$ and let $\pi:M_i\to M_i/M_{i-1}$ be the projection. Since $M_{i-1}\ne M_i$, the quotient $M_i/M_{i-1}$ is nonzero. Suppose $\bar N\subset M_i/M_{i-1}$ is an $R$-submodule with $\bar N\ne0$ and $\bar N\ne M_i/M_{i-1}$. Then $N=\pi^{-1}(\bar N)$ is an $R$-submodule of $M$ with $M_{i-1}\subseteq N\subseteq M_i$ and $\pi(N)=\bar N$ ($\pi$ is surjective); $N\ne M_{i-1}$ since $\pi(M_{i-1})=0\ne\bar N$, and $N\ne M_i$ since $\pi(M_i)=M_i/M_{i-1}\ne\bar N$. Inserting $N$ gives a chain $0=M_0\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq M_{i-1}\subsetneq N\subsetneq M_i\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq M_r=M$ with $r+1$ proper inclusions, so $r+1\in S$, contradicting the maximality of $r$. Hence every submodule of $M_i/M_{i-1}$ is $0$ or $M_i/M_{i-1}$, and $M_i/M_{i-1}$ is simple `IsSimpleModule` ([Mathlib source](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/master/Mathlib/RingTheory/SimpleModule/Basic.lean)). (Stacks 00J3 proof: 'If $M_i/M_{i-1}$ is not simple then we can refine the filtration and the filtration is not maximal.')
