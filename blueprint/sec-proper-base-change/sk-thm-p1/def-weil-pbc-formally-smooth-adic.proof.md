*The definition is well formed; remark (a).* $R$-algebras, their homomorphisms, the quotient $R$-algebra $D/J$ with the $R$-algebra map $q$, and the powers $\mathfrak n^k$, $J^2$ are `AlgHom, Ideal.Quotient.algebra, Ideal.Quotient.mkₐ, Ideal (IdemCommSemiring: I * J, I ^ k)` ([Mathlib source](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/master/Mathlib/Algebra/Algebra/Hom.lean), [Mathlib source](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/master/Mathlib/RingTheory/Ideal/Operations.lean), [Mathlib source](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/master/Mathlib/RingTheory/Ideal/Quotient/Operations.lean)). The condition quantifies over rings $D$, ideals $J$ and maps $g$ and asks for the existence of $f$; no choice is made, so it is a property of the pair $(\varphi,\mathfrak n)$. It depends only on the isomorphism class of $S$ as an $R$-algebra together with $\mathfrak n$ (transport $g$ and $f$ along an $R$-algebra isomorphism $\alpha:S'\to S$ with $\alpha(\mathfrak n')=\mathfrak n$, which carries $\mathfrak n'^k$ onto $\mathfrak n^k$). Remark (a): let $f$ be a lift of $g$ with $g(\mathfrak n^k)=0$. For $x\in\mathfrak n^k$, $q(f(x))=g(x)=0$, so $f(\mathfrak n^k)\subseteq\ker q=J$. The ideal $\mathfrak n^{2k}=\mathfrak n^k\mathfrak n^k$ is generated by products $xy$ with $x,y\in\mathfrak n^k$, and $f(xy)=f(x)f(y)\in J\cdot J=J^2=0$; as $f$ is additive and $f(sz)=f(s)f(z)$, the set of $z$ with $f(z)=0$ contains these generators and is closed under sums and $S$-multiples, so $f(\mathfrak n^{2k})=0$. This is the argument of the proof of Lemma 07EC(1): '$\psi(\mathfrak n^k)\subset J$ hence $\psi(\mathfrak n^{2k})=0$ as $J^2=0$'.

*Remark (b): continuity for adic and discrete topologies.* The $\mathfrak n$-adic topology on $S$ is the topology for which the sets $s+\mathfrak n^k$ ($s\in S$, $k\ge1$) form a basis; it makes $S$ a topological ring, and the $\mathfrak n^k$ are a fundamental system of neighbourhoods of $0$ (More on Algebra, Section 07E7, 'adic topology', with $\mathfrak n^0=S$ and $\mathfrak n^{k+1}\subseteq\mathfrak n^k$). Let $g:S\to E$ be a ring homomorphism, $E$ discrete. If $g$ is continuous, $g^{-1}(\{0\})=\ker g$ is open and contains $0$, so it contains a basic neighbourhood $\mathfrak n^k$ ($k\ge1$ after enlarging $k$), i.e. $g(\mathfrak n^k)=0$. Conversely, if $g(\mathfrak n^k)=0$, then for any subset $U\subseteq E$, $g^{-1}(U)$ is a union of cosets $s+\ker g$, and $s+\ker g=\bigcup_{t\in s+\ker g}(t+\mathfrak n^k)$ is open, so $g$ is continuous. This is Lemma 07E9 for the ideals $\mathfrak n\subseteq S$ and $0\subseteq E$ (the discrete topology is the $0$-adic topology; Stacks omits the proof). With $R$ discrete every ring map out of $R$ is continuous, and $D$, $D/J$ discrete (the quotient topology of a discrete ring is discrete), so a commutative diagram of homomorphisms of topological rings as in Definition 07EB is exactly the data $R\to D$, $J$ with $J^2=0$, and an $R$-algebra map $g:S\to D/J$ with $g(\mathfrak n^k)=0$; a dotted arrow of 07EB is an $R$-algebra map $f:S\to D$ with $q\circ f=g$ that is continuous, and by step 1 and the first part every lift is continuous. Hence the condition agrees with Definition 07EB for these topologies.
