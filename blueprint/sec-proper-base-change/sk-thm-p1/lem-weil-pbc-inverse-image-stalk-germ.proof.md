**Step 1.** *Notation; the model of $f^{-1}$ and its unit.* Let $b:X_{\Et}\to Y_{\Et}$, $U\mapsto U\times_XY$ (with projections $p_U:U\times_XY\to U$, $q_U:U\times_XY\to Y$), $g\mapsto g\times\mathrm{id}_Y$, be the base-change functor; $U\times_XY\to Y$ is etale [lem-weil-pbc-etale-base-change-finite-limits] (clause 1). For an object $V$ of $Y_{\Et}$ let $\mathcal I_V$ be the category of pairs $(U,\varphi)$, $U\in X_{\Et}$, $\varphi:V\to U\times_XY$ a morphism of $Y_{\Et}$, a morphism $(U,\varphi)\to(U',\varphi')$ being $g:U\to U'$ in $X_{\Et}$ with $(g\times\mathrm{id})\circ\varphi=\varphi'$. The presheaf pullback is $f_pG(V)=\varinjlim_{(U,\varphi)\in\mathcal I_V^{\mathrm{op}}}G(U)$ [def-weil-pbc-category-colimit], with coprojections $\iota_{(U,\varphi)}:G(U)\to f_pG(V)$; the transition relations are $\iota_{(U,\varphi)}(G(g)a')=\iota_{(U',\varphi')}(a')$ for $g:(U,\varphi)\to(U',\varphi')$ and $a'\in G(U')$; the restriction along $h:V'\to V$ is $f_pG(h)\circ\iota_{(U,\varphi)}=\iota_{(U,\varphi\circ h)}$; a morphism $\psi:G\to G'$ induces $f_p\psi$ with $f_p\psi\circ\iota_{(U,\varphi)}=\iota_{(U,\varphi)}\circ\psi_U$; and $f_p$ is left adjoint to $f^p:\mathcal F\mapsto\mathcal F\circ b$ [lem-weil-pbc-presheaf-pullback-left-exact] (clause 1, with $\mathcal C=X_{\Et}$, $\mathcal D=Y_{\Et}$), the unit $G(U)\to f_pG(U\times_XY)$ being the coprojection $\iota_{(U,\mathrm{id})}$ (Stacks 00VD/00VE: the unit is $c(\mathrm{id}_{u(U)})$). By [def-weil-pbc-inverse-image], $f^{-1}G=(f_pG)^\#$ is the sheafification [def-weil-pbc-sheafification] of $f_pG$, with $\theta:f_pG\to(f_pG)^\#$ the canonical map, and $f^{-1}\dashv f_*$ is the composite of $\#\dashv$ (inclusion) and $f_p\dashv f^p$, using $(f_*F)(U)=F(U\times_XY)$, i.e. $f_*=f^p$ on sheaves [def-weil-pbc-direct-image]. The unit of a composite adjunction is the composite of the units, so $\eta_{f,G}(U)=\theta_{U\times_XY}\circ\iota_{(U,\mathrm{id})}:G(U)\to(f^{-1}G)(U\times_XY)$, and $f^{-1}\psi=(f_p\psi)^\#$ with $f^{-1}\psi\circ\theta=\theta\circ f_p\psi$. (If $f^{-1}$ is taken to be another left adjoint of $f_*$, the unique isomorphism of left adjoints compatible with the units carries $\eta$ to $\eta$ and the statement over to it unchanged; so it suffices to prove it for this model.)

**Step 2.** *Reduction to the presheaf pullback.* Write $(f_pG)_{\bar y}=\varinjlim_{(V,\bar v)\in\mathcal N_{\bar y}^{\mathrm{op}}}f_pG(V)$ for the stalk of the presheaf $f_pG$ at $\bar y$, $\mathcal N_{\bar y}$ the pointed etale neighbourhoods of $\bar y$ [def-weil-pbc-pointed-etale-neighbourhoods], and $[s ]_{(V,\bar v)}$ for the class of $s\in f_pG(V)$. By [lem-weil-pbc-sheafification-geometric-stalk] (for $T=Y$, $\mathcal F=f_pG$), the homomorphism $\theta_{\bar y}:(f_pG)_{\bar y}\to(f^{-1}G)_{\bar y}$, $[s ]_{(V,\bar v)}\mapsto$ germ of $\theta_V(s)$ at $(V,\bar v)$, is an isomorphism, where $(f^{-1}G)_{\bar y}$ is the stalk of [def-weil-pbc-geometric-stalks]. So it suffices to build an isomorphism $\beta:(f_pG)_{\bar y}\to G_{\bar x}$ and to put $c=\beta\circ\theta_{\bar y}^{-1}$.

**Step 3.** *The map $\alpha:G_{\bar x}\to(f_pG)_{\bar y}$.* For a pointed etale neighbourhood $(U,\bar u)$ of $\bar x$ ($U\to X$ etale, $(U\to X)\circ\bar u=\bar x=f\circ\bar y$) the morphism $(\bar u,\bar y):\operatorname{Spec}\Omega\to U\times_XY$ is defined [def-pc-fibre-product] and lies over $\bar y$, so $(U\times_XY,(\bar u,\bar y))\in\mathcal N_{\bar y}$ (step 0: $U\times_XY\to Y$ is etale). Let $\alpha_{(U,\bar u)}:G(U)\to(f_pG)_{\bar y}$, $a\mapsto[\iota_{(U,\mathrm{id})}(a)]_{(U\times_XY,(\bar u,\bar y))}$, a homomorphism. Compatibility: let $h:(U',\bar u')\to(U,\bar u)$ be a morphism of $\mathcal N_{\bar x}$ ($h\circ\bar u'=\bar u$) and $a\in G(U)$. Then $h\times\mathrm{id}:(U'\times_XY,(\bar u',\bar y))\to(U\times_XY,(\bar u,\bar y))$ is a morphism of $\mathcal N_{\bar y}$, since $(h\times\mathrm{id})\circ(\bar u',\bar y)=(h\bar u',\bar y)=(\bar u,\bar y)$; hence $[\iota_{(U,\mathrm{id})}(a)]_{(U\times_XY,(\bar u,\bar y))}=[f_pG(h\times\mathrm{id})\iota_{(U,\mathrm{id})}(a)]_{(U'\times_XY,(\bar u',\bar y))}=[\iota_{(U,h\times\mathrm{id})}(a)]_{(U'\times_XY,\ldots)}$, and $h:(U',\mathrm{id})\to(U,h\times\mathrm{id})$ is a morphism of $\mathcal I_{U'\times_XY}$, so $\iota_{(U,h\times\mathrm{id})}(a)=\iota_{(U',\mathrm{id})}(G(h)a)$. Thus $\alpha_{(U',\bar u')}\circ G(h)=\alpha_{(U,\bar u)}$, and by the universal property of the colimit $G_{\bar x}=\varinjlim_{\mathcal N_{\bar x}^{\mathrm{op}}}G(U)$ [def-weil-pbc-category-colimit] there is a unique homomorphism $\alpha$ with $\alpha([a ]_{(U,\bar u)})=[\iota_{(U,\mathrm{id})}(a)]_{(U\times_XY,(\bar u,\bar y))}$.

**Step 4.** *The map $\beta:(f_pG)_{\bar y}\to G_{\bar x}$.* Fix $(V,\bar v)\in\mathcal N_{\bar y}$. For $(U,\varphi)\in\mathcal I_V$ put $\bar u_\varphi=p_U\circ\varphi\circ\bar v:\operatorname{Spec}\Omega\to U$; then $(U\to X)\circ\bar u_\varphi=f\circ q_U\circ\varphi\circ\bar v=f\circ(V\to Y)\circ\bar v=f\circ\bar y=\bar x$ ($\varphi$ is a $Y$-morphism), so $(U,\bar u_\varphi)\in\mathcal N_{\bar x}$. The homomorphisms $G(U)\to G_{\bar x}$, $a\mapsto[a ]_{(U,\bar u_\varphi)}$, are compatible over $\mathcal I_V^{\mathrm{op}}$: for $g:(U,\varphi)\to(U',\varphi')$ in $\mathcal I_V$, $g\circ\bar u_\varphi=g\,p_U\varphi\bar v=p_{U'}(g\times\mathrm{id})\varphi\bar v=p_{U'}\varphi'\bar v=\bar u_{\varphi'}$, so $g$ is a morphism $(U,\bar u_\varphi)\to(U',\bar u_{\varphi'})$ of $\mathcal N_{\bar x}$ and $[G(g)a']_{(U,\bar u_\varphi)}=[a']_{(U',\bar u_{\varphi'})}$. By the universal property of $f_pG(V)$ [def-weil-pbc-category-colimit] they induce $\beta_{(V,\bar v)}:f_pG(V)\to G_{\bar x}$ with $\beta_{(V,\bar v)}(\iota_{(U,\varphi)}(a))=[a ]_{(U,p_U\varphi\bar v)}$. These are compatible over $\mathcal N_{\bar y}^{\mathrm{op}}$: for $h:(V',\bar v')\to(V,\bar v)$ ($h\bar v'=\bar v$), $\beta_{(V',\bar v')}(f_pG(h)\iota_{(U,\varphi)}(a))=\beta_{(V',\bar v')}(\iota_{(U,\varphi h)}(a))=[a ]_{(U,p_U\varphi h\bar v')}=[a ]_{(U,p_U\varphi\bar v)}$; two homomorphisms out of $f_pG(V)$ that agree on all $\iota_{(U,\varphi)}(a)$ are equal (uniqueness in the universal property). So there is a unique homomorphism $\beta$ with $\beta([\iota_{(U,\varphi)}(a)]_{(V,\bar v)})=[a ]_{(U,p_U\circ\varphi\circ\bar v)}$: this is the correspondence of the source, the pair $((V,\bar v),(U,\varphi))$ going to the pointed neighbourhood $(U,p_U\varphi\bar v)$ of $\bar x$.

**Step 5.** *$\alpha$ and $\beta$ are mutually inverse* (this replaces the cofinality argument of the source by explicit inverse maps; no filteredness is used). The elements $[a ]_{(U,\bar u)}$ generate $G_{\bar x}$, and the elements $[\iota_{(U,\varphi)}(a)]_{(V,\bar v)}$ generate $(f_pG)_{\bar y}$ (a homomorphism out of a colimit is determined by its composites with the coprojections, applied twice) [def-weil-pbc-category-colimit]; so it suffices to check on these. (i) $\beta\alpha([a ]_{(U,\bar u)})=\beta([\iota_{(U,\mathrm{id})}(a)]_{(U\times_XY,(\bar u,\bar y))})=[a ]_{(U,p_U\circ(\bar u,\bar y))}=[a ]_{(U,\bar u)}$. (ii) Let $\bar u=p_U\varphi\bar v$, so $\alpha\beta([\iota_{(U,\varphi)}(a)]_{(V,\bar v)})=[\iota_{(U,\mathrm{id})}(a)]_{(U\times_XY,(\bar u,\bar y))}$. The $Y$-morphism $\varphi:V\to U\times_XY$ satisfies $p_U\varphi\bar v=\bar u$ and $q_U\varphi\bar v=(V\to Y)\bar v=\bar y$, so $\varphi\circ\bar v=(\bar u,\bar y)$ [def-pc-fibre-product] and $\varphi:(V,\bar v)\to(U\times_XY,(\bar u,\bar y))$ is a morphism of $\mathcal N_{\bar y}$. Hence $[\iota_{(U,\mathrm{id})}(a)]_{(U\times_XY,(\bar u,\bar y))}=[f_pG(\varphi)\iota_{(U,\mathrm{id})}(a)]_{(V,\bar v)}=[\iota_{(U,\varphi)}(a)]_{(V,\bar v)}$. So $\beta$ is an isomorphism with inverse $\alpha$.

**Step 6.** *Definition of $c$ and the germ formula.* Put $c_{f,\bar y,G}=\beta\circ\theta_{\bar y}^{-1}:(f^{-1}G)_{\bar y}\to G_{\bar x}$, an isomorphism by steps 1 and 4. For $(U,\bar u)\in\mathcal N_{\bar x}$ and $a\in G(U)$, by step 0 the germ of $\eta_{f,G}(U)(a)=\theta_{U\times_XY}(\iota_{(U,\mathrm{id})}(a))$ at $(U\times_XY,(\bar u,\bar y))$ is $\theta_{\bar y}([\iota_{(U,\mathrm{id})}(a)]_{(U\times_XY,(\bar u,\bar y))})=\theta_{\bar y}(\alpha([a ]_{(U,\bar u)}))$, hence $c$ sends it to $\beta\alpha([a ]_{(U,\bar u)})=[a ]_{(U,\bar u)}$, the germ of $a$ at $(U,\bar u)$. Case $U=X$, $\bar u=\bar x$: $q_X:X\times_XY\to Y$ is an isomorphism over $Y$ carrying $(\bar x,\bar y)$ to $\bar y$ [def-pc-fibre-product], i.e. an isomorphism $(X\times_XY,(\bar x,\bar y))\cong(Y,\bar y)$ in $\mathcal N_{\bar y}$, along which germs correspond [def-weil-pbc-geometric-stalks]; so $c((\eta_{f,G}(X)(a))_{\bar y})=a_{\bar x}$.

**Step 7.** *Naturality in $G$.* Let $\psi:G\to G'$. By step 0, $f^{-1}\psi\circ\theta=\theta\circ f_p\psi$ and $f_p\psi\circ\iota_{(U,\varphi)}=\iota_{(U,\varphi)}\circ\psi_U$, so on stalks $(f^{-1}\psi)_{\bar y}\theta_{\bar y}([\iota_{(U,\varphi)}(a)]_{(V,\bar v)})=\theta_{\bar y}([\iota_{(U,\varphi)}(\psi_Ua)]_{(V,\bar v)})$. The elements $\theta_{\bar y}([\iota_{(U,\varphi)}(a)]_{(V,\bar v)})$ generate $(f^{-1}G)_{\bar y}$ (step 4 and $\theta_{\bar y}$ bijective), and on them $c_{G'}\circ(f^{-1}\psi)_{\bar y}$ gives $[\psi_Ua]_{(U,p_U\varphi\bar v)}$, as does $\psi_{\bar x}\circ c_G$ ($\psi_{\bar x}([a ]_{(U,\bar u)})=[\psi_Ua]_{(U,\bar u)}$ by definition of the induced map on colimits [def-weil-pbc-category-colimit]). Hence $c_{G'}\circ(f^{-1}\psi)_{\bar y}=\psi_{\bar x}\circ c_G$.

**Step 8.** *Remark: $c$ is determined by the germ formula.* By steps 1, 2 and 4, the germs of $\eta_{f,G}(U)(a)$ at $(U\times_XY,(\bar u,\bar y))$ are the elements $\theta_{\bar y}\alpha([a ]_{(U,\bar u)})$, which generate $(f^{-1}G)_{\bar y}$ since $\theta_{\bar y}\alpha$ is bijective; so any homomorphism $(f^{-1}G)_{\bar y}\to G_{\bar x}$ satisfying the germ formula equals $c$. In particular the isomorphism of [lemma-openclosed-stalk-upperstar] (Lean: AlgebraicGeometry.Etale.upperStarCompSheafFiberIso) is $c$ as soon as it satisfies the germ formula; that node is not used in this proof.
