Let $x\in Q(R)$ be integral over $R$ and write $x=a/s$ with $a\in R$ and $s\in S$. Fix a maximal ideal $\mathfrak p$ of $R$. Since $R$ is normal, $A=R_{\mathfrak p}$ is an integrally closed domain; let $K$ be its fraction field. Localization is flat, so a nonzerodivisor of $R$ maps to a nonzerodivisor of $A$, hence to a nonzero element and so to a unit of $K$. By the universal property of $Q(R)=S^{-1}R$ there is an $R$-algebra map $f:Q(R)\to K$. The image $f(x)$ is integral over $R$, hence over $A$, so, $A$ being integrally closed, $f(x)$ is the image of some $b\in A$.

Applying $f$ to the identity $xs=a$ gives $b\cdot(s/1)=a/1$ in $K$, and since $A\to K$ is injective, $a/1=b\cdot(s/1)$ in $A$. Hence $a/1$ lies in the ideal $sR_{\mathfrak p}$ generated by the image of the principal ideal $(s)$.

This holds for every maximal ideal $\mathfrak p$, and membership in an ideal can be checked after localizing at all maximal ideals; hence $a\in(s)$, i.e. $a=sr$ for some $r\in R$. Then $x=a/s=r/1$ lies in the image of $R\to Q(R)$.
