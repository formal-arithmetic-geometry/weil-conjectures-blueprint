*The complex $\tilde L^\bullet$.* For every $n$ let $\tilde L^n=L^n\oplus K^n\oplus K^{n+1}$, the direct sum of three objects in the additive category $\mathcal A$ `CategoryTheory.Limits.HasFiniteBiproducts` ([Mathlib source](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/master/Mathlib/CategoryTheory/Limits/Shapes/Biproducts.lean)) (iterate the binary direct sum), with injections $i_1,i_2,i_3$ and projections $p_1,p_2,p_3$ satisfying $p_ai_b=\delta_{ab}\mathrm{id}$ and $i_1p_1+i_2p_2+i_3p_3=\mathrm{id}$. A morphism $X\oplus Y\oplus Z\to X'\oplus Y'\oplus Z'$ is the same as its $3\times3$ matrix of components $p'_a\,u\,i_b$, and composition is matrix multiplication, by bilinearity of composition `CategoryTheory.Preadditive` ([Mathlib source](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/master/Mathlib/CategoryTheory/Preadditive/Basic.lean)) and $\sum_b i_bp_b=\mathrm{id}$. Define $d^n_{\tilde L}:\tilde L^n\to\tilde L^{n+1}=L^{n+1}\oplus K^{n+1}\oplus K^{n+2}$ by the matrix $\begin{pmatrix}d_L^n&0&0\\0&d_K^n&\mathrm{id}_{K^{n+1}}\\0&0&-d_K^{n+1}\end{pmatrix}$, i.e. on components $(l,k,k^+)\mapsto(d_Ll,\ d_Kk+k^+,\ -d_Kk^+)$ (this is $L^\bullet\oplus C(1_{K^\bullet})$ in Stacks' words; components are written as tuples only as shorthand for the matrices).

*$d_{\tilde L}^{n+1}d_{\tilde L}^n=0$.* The product of the matrices is $\begin{pmatrix}d_L^{n+1}d_L^n&0&0\\0&d_K^{n+1}d_K^n&d_K^{n+1}\cdot\mathrm{id}-\mathrm{id}\cdot d_K^{n+1}\\0&0&d_K^{n+2}d_K^{n+1}\end{pmatrix}=0$, since $d_L^2=0$, $d_K^2=0$, and the $(2,3)$ entry is $d_K^{n+1}-d_K^{n+1}=0$. So $\tilde L^\bullet$ is a cochain complex. (`CategoryTheory.Limits.HasFiniteBiproducts` ([Mathlib source](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/master/Mathlib/CategoryTheory/Limits/Shapes/Biproducts.lean))).

*The morphisms $\tilde\alpha,\pi,s$ and clause (0), $\pi s=\mathrm{id}$.* Define $\tilde\alpha^n=\begin{pmatrix}\alpha^n\\\mathrm{id}_{K^n}\\0\end{pmatrix}:K^n\to\tilde L^n$, $\pi^n=(\mathrm{id}_{L^n}\ 0\ 0):\tilde L^n\to L^n$, $s^n=\begin{pmatrix}\mathrm{id}_{L^n}\\0\\0\end{pmatrix}:L^n\to\tilde L^n$. They commute with the differentials: $d_{\tilde L}^n\tilde\alpha^n=\begin{pmatrix}d_L^n\alpha^n\\d_K^n\\0\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\alpha^{n+1}d_K^n\\d_K^n\\0\end{pmatrix}=\tilde\alpha^{n+1}d_K^n$ because $\alpha$ is a morphism of complexes; $\pi^{n+1}d_{\tilde L}^n=(d_L^n\ 0\ 0)=d_L^n\pi^n$; $d_{\tilde L}^ns^n=\begin{pmatrix}d_L^n\\0\\0\end{pmatrix}=s^{n+1}d_L^n$. Finally $\pi^n\tilde\alpha^n=\alpha^n$ (clause (0)) and $\pi^ns^n=\mathrm{id}_{L^n}$, so $\pi\circ s=\mathrm{id}_{L^\bullet}$ (first half of clause (2)). (`CategoryTheory.Limits.HasFiniteBiproducts` ([Mathlib source](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/master/Mathlib/CategoryTheory/Limits/Shapes/Biproducts.lean))).

*Clause (1): $\tilde\alpha$ is a termwise split injection.* Put $M^n=L^n\oplus K^{n+1}$, so $K^n\oplus M^n=K^n\oplus L^n\oplus K^{n+1}$. Let $\varphi^n:K^n\oplus L^n\oplus K^{n+1}\to\tilde L^n=L^n\oplus K^n\oplus K^{n+1}$ be $(k,l,k^+)\mapsto(\alpha^nk+l,\ k,\ k^+)$, i.e. the matrix $\begin{pmatrix}\alpha^n&\mathrm{id}_{L^n}&0\\\mathrm{id}_{K^n}&0&0\\0&0&\mathrm{id}\end{pmatrix}$, and $\psi^n:\tilde L^n\to K^n\oplus L^n\oplus K^{n+1}$ be $(l,k,k^+)\mapsto(k,\ l-\alpha^nk,\ k^+)$, i.e. $\begin{pmatrix}0&\mathrm{id}_{K^n}&0\\\mathrm{id}_{L^n}&-\alpha^n&0\\0&0&\mathrm{id}\end{pmatrix}$. Multiplying the matrices, $\varphi^n\psi^n=\mathrm{id}$ (first row $(\mathrm{id},\ \alpha^n-\alpha^n,\ 0)$) and $\psi^n\varphi^n=\mathrm{id}$ (second row $(\alpha^n-\alpha^n,\ \mathrm{id},\ 0)$), so $\varphi^n$ is an isomorphism; and $\varphi^n$ composed with the inclusion of the summand $K^n$ is the first column $\begin{pmatrix}\alpha^n\\\mathrm{id}\\0\end{pmatrix}=\tilde\alpha^n$. Hence under $\tilde L^n\cong K^n\oplus M^n$ the map $\tilde\alpha^n$ is the inclusion of $K^n$, which is clause (1) as the statement defines it. (The source says only that $\tilde\alpha$ is clearly a split injection; its left inverse is $p_2$, and $\varphi^n$ makes the summand explicit.) (`CategoryTheory.Limits.HasFiniteBiproducts` ([Mathlib source](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/master/Mathlib/CategoryTheory/Limits/Shapes/Biproducts.lean))).

*Clause (2): $s\pi$ is homotopic to $\mathrm{id}_{\tilde L^\bullet}$.* Let $h^n:\tilde L^n=L^n\oplus K^n\oplus K^{n+1}\to\tilde L^{n-1}=L^{n-1}\oplus K^{n-1}\oplus K^n$ map the summand $K^n$ of $\tilde L^n$ by the identity to the summand $K^n$ of $\tilde L^{n-1}$: $h^n=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&0\\0&0&0\\0&\mathrm{id}_{K^n}&0\end{pmatrix}$, i.e. $(l,k,k^+)\mapsto(0,0,k)$. Then $d_{\tilde L}^{n-1}h^n=\begin{pmatrix}d_L^{n-1}&0&0\\0&d_K^{n-1}&\mathrm{id}_{K^n}\\0&0&-d_K^n\end{pmatrix}h^n=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&0\\0&\mathrm{id}_{K^n}&0\\0&-d_K^n&0\end{pmatrix}$ and $h^{n+1}d_{\tilde L}^n=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&0\\0&0&0\\0&\mathrm{id}_{K^{n+1}}&0\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}d_L^n&0&0\\0&d_K^n&\mathrm{id}\\0&0&-d_K^{n+1}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&0\\0&0&0\\0&d_K^n&\mathrm{id}_{K^{n+1}}\end{pmatrix}$. Their sum is $\begin{pmatrix}0&0&0\\0&\mathrm{id}&0\\0&0&\mathrm{id}\end{pmatrix}=\mathrm{id}_{\tilde L^n}-s^n\pi^n$, since $s^n\pi^n=\begin{pmatrix}\mathrm{id}_{L^n}&0&0\\0&0&0\\0&0&0\end{pmatrix}$. So $\mathrm{id}^n-(s\pi)^n=d_{\tilde L}^{n-1}h^n+h^{n+1}d_{\tilde L}^n$ for all $n$: $\mathrm{id}_{\tilde L^\bullet}$ and $s\circ\pi$ are homotopic in the sense of the statement (second half of clause (2)). (`CategoryTheory.Limits.HasFiniteBiproducts` ([Mathlib source](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/master/Mathlib/CategoryTheory/Limits/Shapes/Biproducts.lean))).

*Boundedness.* A direct sum of zero objects is zero: if $X=Y=Z=0$ then $\mathrm{id}_{X\oplus Y\oplus Z}=\sum_b i_bp_b$ factors each term through $0$, so it is the zero morphism and $X\oplus Y\oplus Z$ is a zero object `CategoryTheory.Limits.HasFiniteBiproducts` ([Mathlib source](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/master/Mathlib/CategoryTheory/Limits/Shapes/Biproducts.lean)). If $K^n=L^n=0$ for $n<n_0$, then for $n<n_0-1$ all of $L^n,K^n,K^{n+1}$ vanish ($n+1<n_0$), so $\tilde L^n=0$. If $K^n=L^n=0$ for $n>n_1$, then for $n>n_1$ also $n+1>n_1$, so $\tilde L^n=0$. Both together give both. Hence $\tilde L^\bullet$ is bounded below, above, or bounded when $K^\bullet$ and $L^\bullet$ are.
