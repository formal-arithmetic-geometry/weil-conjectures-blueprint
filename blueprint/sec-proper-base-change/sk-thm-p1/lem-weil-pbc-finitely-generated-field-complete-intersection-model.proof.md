*The base field $F$ and the tower.* Since $x_1,\dots,x_d$ is a transcendence basis, the $k$-algebra map $k[X_1,\dots,X_d]\to K$, $X_j\mapsto x_j$, is injective, so its image $k[x]=k[x_1,\dots,x_d]$ is a domain isomorphic to the polynomial ring, and $K$ is algebraic over $F:=k(x_1,\dots,x_d)$. The set $\{a/s: a,s\in k[x],\ s\ne0\}$ is a subfield of $K$ containing $k$ and the $x_j$ and is contained in every such subfield, so it is $F$. Put $r:=m$, $L_0:=F$ and $L_i:=F(b_1,\dots,b_i)$ (the smallest subfield of $K$ containing $F$ and $b_1,\dots,b_i$), so $L_i=L_{i-1}(b_i)$; $L_r$ contains $k$ and all $b_j$, hence $L_r\supseteq k(b_1,\dots,b_m)=K$, so $L_r=K$. Each $b_i$ is algebraic over $F$, hence over $L_{i-1}\supseteq F$ (the same nonzero polynomial).

*A monomial basis of $K$ over $F$.* For $i=1,\dots,r$ let $\mu_i\in L_{i-1}[T]$ be the minimal polynomial of $b_i$ over $L_{i-1}$ and $e_i:=\deg\mu_i$. Since $b_i$ is algebraic over $L_{i-1}$, $e_i\ge1$ and $L_{i-1}[T]/(\mu_i)\to L_i$, $T\mapsto b_i$, is an isomorphism of $L_{i-1}$-algebras; since $\mu_i$ is monic, the classes of $1,T,\dots,T^{e_i-1}$ are an $L_{i-1}$-basis of $L_{i-1}[T]/(\mu_i)$, so $1,b_i,\dots,b_i^{e_i-1}$ is an $L_{i-1}$-basis of $L_i$. By induction on $i$, using the tower law for bases of $F\subseteq L_{i-1}\subseteq L_i$, the monomials $b^\varepsilon:=b_1^{\varepsilon_1}\cdots b_i^{\varepsilon_i}$ with $0\le\varepsilon_j<e_j$ ($j\le i$) form an $F$-basis of $L_i$ (for $i=0$: the basis $\{1\}$ of $F$). In particular, for $i=r$, the $b^\varepsilon$, $\varepsilon\in E_r:=\prod_{j\le r}\{0,\dots,e_j-1\}$, form an $F$-basis of $K$, and $L_{i-1}$ is the $F$-span of the monomials in $b_1,\dots,b_{i-1}$.

*The polynomials $P_i$ over $F$.* Fix $i$ and write $\mu_i=T^{e_i}+\sum_{l<e_i}c_{il}T^l$ with $c_{il}\in L_{i-1}$. By step 2 each $c_{il}$ is an $F$-linear combination of monomials in $b_1,\dots,b_{i-1}$; replacing $b_j$ by the variable $Y_j$ gives $g_{il}\in F[Y_1,\dots,Y_{i-1}]$ with $g_{il}(b_1,\dots,b_{i-1})=c_{il}$. Put $P_i:=Y_i^{e_i}+\sum_{l<e_i}g_{il}Y_i^l\in F[Y_1,\dots,Y_i]$. Then $P_i$ is monic of degree $e_i$ in $Y_i$ with coefficients in $F[Y_1,\dots,Y_{i-1}]$, and $P_i(b_1,\dots,b_i)=\mu_i(b_i)=0$ in $K$.

*Clearing denominators: $h$ and $D$.* The polynomials $P_1,\dots,P_r\in F[Y_1,\dots,Y_r]$ have finitely many nonzero coefficients in $F$; by step 1 each is $a/s$ with $a,s\in k[x]$, $s\ne0$. Let $h\in k[x]$ be the product of all these denominators $s$; $h\ne0$ since $k[x]$ is a domain. Then each coefficient $a/s$ equals $(a\,h/s)/h$ with $a\,h/s=a\prod_{s'\ne s}s'\in k[x]$, so it lies in the subring $D:=\{a/h^j: a\in k[x],\ j\ge0\}\subseteq F$. Hence $P_i\in D[Y_1,\dots,Y_i]$, monic in $Y_i$ of degree $e_i$ with coefficients in $D[Y_1,\dots,Y_{i-1}]$, and $P_i(b_1,\dots,b_i)=0$.

*The presentation of $D[Y]$ and the $f$'s.* Let $R:=k[X_1,\dots,X_d]$ and $h(X)\in R$ the polynomial with $h(X)\mapsto h$ under the isomorphism $R\cong k[x]$ of step 1. As for any localization away from one element, $R[T]/(h(X)T-1)\cong R_{h}$, $T\mapsto1/h$; and $R_h\to F$, $a/h^j\mapsto a(x)/h^j$, is injective with image $D$ ($a/h^j\mapsto0$ forces $a(x)=0$, so $a=0$; $R$ is a domain). So $\psi_0:k[X,T]\to D$, $X_j\mapsto x_j$, $T\mapsto1/h$, is surjective with kernel $(h(X)T-1)$. Its coefficientwise extension $\psi:k[X,T,Y_1,\dots,Y_r]=k[X,T][Y]\to D[Y]$, $Y_j\mapsto Y_j$, is surjective with kernel the polynomials all of whose coefficients lie in $(h(X)T-1)$, i.e. $(h(X)T-1)k[X,T,Y]$. For each $i$ write every coefficient of $P_i$ as $a(x)/h^j$ and replace it by $a(X)T^j$: this gives $\tilde P_i\in k[X,T,Y_1,\dots,Y_i]$, monic in $Y_i$ of degree $e_i$ with coefficients in $k[X,T,Y_1,\dots,Y_{i-1}]$, with $\psi(\tilde P_i)=P_i$. Now take $n:=d+1+r$ variables $X_1,\dots,X_d,T,Y_1,\dots,Y_r$ (renumbered $X_1,\dots,X_n$), $c:=r+1$, $f_1:=h(X)T-1$, $f_{1+i}:=\tilde P_i$ ($1\le i\le r$). Then $n\ge c\ge1$ and $n-c=d$.

*$B_i$ is free over $D$.* For $0\le i\le r$ put $B_i:=D[Y_1,\dots,Y_i]/(P_1,\dots,P_i)$ and $E_i:=\prod_{j\le i}\{0,\dots,e_j-1\}$. Claim: the classes of $Y^\varepsilon$, $\varepsilon\in E_i$, form a basis of $B_i$ as a $D$-module. For $i=0$, $B_0=D$ with basis $\{1\}$. For $i\ge1$: $P_1,\dots,P_{i-1}$ do not involve $Y_i$, so taking the quotient in two stages $B_i\cong B_{i-1}[Y_i]/(\bar P_i)$, $\bar P_i$ the image of $P_i$, which is monic of degree $e_i$ in $Y_i$ (step 4). Since $\bar P_i$ is monic, $1,Y_i,\dots,Y_i^{e_i-1}$ is a $B_{i-1}$-basis of $B_i$, and by the induction hypothesis and the tower law for bases (for $D\to B_{i-1}$ and the $B_{i-1}$-module $B_i$) the products $Y^\varepsilon Y_i^l$, i.e. the $Y^{\varepsilon'}$, $\varepsilon'\in E_i$, form a $D$-basis of $B_i$.

*The map $\iota_0:B_r\to K$: injective, and $K$ is its field of fractions.* The $D$-algebra map $D[Y_1,\dots,Y_r]\to K$, $Y_j\mapsto b_j$ ($D\subseteq F\subseteq K$), kills every $P_i$ (step 4), so it induces a $k$-algebra map $\iota_0:B_r\to K$ with $\iota_0(Y^\varepsilon)=b^\varepsilon$. If $\iota_0(\sum_{\varepsilon\in E_r}d_\varepsilon Y^\varepsilon)=\sum d_\varepsilon b^\varepsilon=0$ with $d_\varepsilon\in D\subseteq F$, then all $d_\varepsilon=0$ because the $b^\varepsilon$ are $F$-linearly independent (step 2); by step 6 every element of $B_r$ is such a sum, so $\iota_0$ is injective. Every $z\in K$ is $\sum_\varepsilon q_\varepsilon b^\varepsilon$ with $q_\varepsilon\in F$ (step 2); writing $q_\varepsilon=a_\varepsilon/s$ with one common $s\in k[x]\setminus\{0\}$ (the product of the denominators, step 1) gives $z=\iota_0(\sum a_\varepsilon Y^\varepsilon)\,\iota_0(s)^{-1}$, where $\iota_0(s)=s\neq0$ in $K$ and hence $s\neq0$ in $B_r$.

*$A\cong B_r$ and the map $\iota$.* Let $A:=k[X_1,\dots,X_n]/(f_1,\dots,f_c)$. The composite of $\psi$ (step 5) with $D[Y]\to B_r$ is surjective, and its kernel is $\psi^{-1}((P_1,\dots,P_r))=\ker\psi+(\tilde P_1,\dots,\tilde P_r)=(f_1,f_2,\dots,f_c)$, since $\psi(\tilde P_i)=P_i$ and $\psi$ is surjective with kernel $(f_1)$. So it induces an isomorphism of $k$-algebras $A\cong B_r$, and $\iota:=\iota_0\circ(A\cong B_r):A\to K$ is a $k$-algebra homomorphism ($X_j\mapsto x_j$, $T\mapsto1/h$, $Y_i\mapsto b_i$) which by step 7 is injective, (a), and satisfies (b).

*$f_1,\dots,f_c$ is a regular sequence.* Check the conditions of a regular sequence for $M=R_0:=k[X_1,\dots,X_n]$. (i) $R_0$ is a domain and $f_1=h(X)T-1\ne0$ (its coefficient of $T$ is $h(X)\ne0$), so $f_1$ is a nonzerodivisor on $R_0$. (Note: $f_1$ is not monic in $T$; its leading coefficient is $h(X)$.) (ii) For $1\le i\le r$: as in step 8 (with $P_1,\dots,P_{i-1}$ in place of $P_1,\dots,P_r$), $R_0/(f_1,\dots,f_i)\cong D[Y_1,\dots,Y_r]/(P_1,\dots,P_{i-1})\cong B_{i-1}[Y_i,\dots,Y_r]=C[Y_i]$ with $C:=B_{i-1}[Y_{i+1},\dots,Y_r]$, and $f_{i+1}=\tilde P_i$ maps to the image of $P_i$, which lies in $B_{i-1}[Y_i]\subseteq C[Y_i]$ and is monic in $Y_i$ (step 4). A monic polynomial is a nonzerodivisor, so it is a nonzerodivisor on $C[Y_i]$, i.e. $f_{i+1}$ is a nonzerodivisor on $R_0/(f_1,\dots,f_i)$. (iii) $R_0/(f_1,\dots,f_c)=A\neq0$, since $\iota$ is injective (step 8) and $\iota(1)=1\neq0$. Hence $f_1,\dots,f_c$ is a regular sequence in $k[X_1,\dots,X_n]$.

*The fraction field.* $A$ is an integral domain, since $\iota$ embeds it in the field $K$. With $S=A\setminus\{0\}$ and $S^{-1}A$ the ring of fractions, the map $S^{-1}A\to K$, $a/s\mapsto\iota(a)\iota(s)^{-1}$, is well defined ($\iota(s)\neq0$ for $s\in S$; if $u(ta-sb)=0$ with $u\in S$, then $\iota(u)\neq0$ in the field $K$ gives $\iota(t)\iota(a)=\iota(s)\iota(b)$), a homomorphism of $k$-algebras, injective ($\iota(a)\iota(s)^{-1}=0$ forces $\iota(a)=0$, so $a=0$), and surjective by (b).
