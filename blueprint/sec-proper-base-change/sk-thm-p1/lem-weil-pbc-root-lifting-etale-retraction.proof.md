**Step 1.** *The residue map $\rho$.* Write $\pi:R\to\kappa=R/\mathfrak m$ and $\iota:R\to S$ for the structure map. The composite $\kappa\to S/\mathfrak q\hookrightarrow\kappa(\mathfrak q)$ [def-weil-residue-field] (the residue field of $\operatorname{Spec}S$ at $\mathfrak q$ is the fraction field of the domain $S/\mathfrak q$) is an isomorphism by hypothesis, so the subring $S/\mathfrak q$ contains the image of $\kappa$, which is all of $\kappa(\mathfrak q)$; hence $R\to S/\mathfrak q$ is surjective with kernel $\mathfrak q\cap R=\mathfrak m$, and we get a surjective $R$-algebra map $\rho:S\to S/\mathfrak q\cong\kappa$ with $\ker\rho=\mathfrak q$. Moreover: (*) two surjective $R$-algebra maps $\psi_1,\psi_2:A\to\kappa$ from an $R$-algebra $A$ with the same kernel are equal, because $\psi_2=\alpha\circ\psi_1$ for a ring automorphism $\alpha$ of $\kappa$ which is $R$-linear, and $\kappa$ is generated by $1$ as an $R$-module, so $\alpha=\mathrm{id}$. In particular, for an $R$-algebra map $\tau:S\to R$ ($\tau\circ\iota=\mathrm{id}_R$, so $\tau$ and $\pi\circ\tau$ are surjective) we have $\tau^{-1}(\mathfrak m)=\ker(\pi\circ\tau)$, and $\tau^{-1}(\mathfrak m)=\mathfrak q$ iff $\pi\circ\tau=\rho$.

**Step 2.** *Standard etale presentation (Stacks: by Proposition 00UE).* By [lem-weil-pbc-etale-locally-standard] applied to $R\to S$ and $\mathfrak q$ there are $g\in S\setminus\mathfrak q$, a monic $f\in R[t ]$, an $h\in R[t ]$ with $f'p_1+fp_2=h^n$ in $R[t ]$ (some $p_1,p_2\in R[t ]$, $n\ge0$) and an $R$-algebra isomorphism $\varphi:S_g\xrightarrow{\sim}C=C(f,h)=R[t ][Y ]/(f,Yh-1)$; let $x\in C$ be the class of $t$ and $\lambda:S\to S_g$ the localization map. This is the only place where etaleness of $\operatorname{Spec}S\to\operatorname{Spec}R$ is used.

**Step 3.** *Reduction to $C$ (Stacks: 'after replacing $S$ by $S_g$ we may assume that $S=R[t ]_g/(f)$ is standard etale (details omitted)').* Since $\rho(g)\neq0$ in the field $\kappa$ ($g\notin\mathfrak q=\ker\rho$), the universal property of the localization gives a unique ring map $\rho':S_g\to\kappa$ with $\rho'\circ\lambda=\rho$ (an $R$-algebra map); put $\rho_C=\rho'\circ\varphi^{-1}:C\to\kappa$. Claim: $\sigma\mapsto\sigma\circ\varphi\circ\lambda$ is a bijection from the $R$-algebra maps $\sigma:C\to R$ with $\pi\circ\sigma=\rho_C$ onto the $R$-algebra maps $\tau:S\to R$ with $\tau^{-1}(\mathfrak m)=\mathfrak q$. (a) Well defined: $\pi\circ\sigma\circ\varphi\circ\lambda=\rho_C\circ\varphi\circ\lambda=\rho$, so $\tau^{-1}(\mathfrak m)=\mathfrak q$ by step 1. (b) Injective: $\lambda$ is an epimorphism of rings (a ring map out of $S_g$ is determined by its composite with $\lambda$, uniqueness in the universal property of localization) and $\varphi$ is bijective. (c) Surjective: let $\tau$ be given, so $\pi\circ\tau=\rho$ (step 1); $\tau(g)\notin\mathfrak m$ (as $g\notin\mathfrak q=\tau^{-1}(\mathfrak m)$), so $\tau(g)$ is a unit of the local ring $R$ and $\tau=\tau'\circ\lambda$ for a ring map $\tau':S_g\to R$, an $R$-algebra map. Put $\sigma=\tau'\circ\varphi^{-1}$; then $\sigma\circ\varphi\circ\lambda=\tau$, and $\pi\circ\tau'$ and $\rho'$ are two ring maps $S_g\to\kappa$ with the same composite $\rho$ with $\lambda$, so they are equal and $\pi\circ\sigma=\rho_C$.

**Step 4.** *The residue root $a_0$ (Stacks: 'the prime $\mathfrak q$ has residue field $\kappa$, so it corresponds to a root $a_0$ of $\overline f$ which is not a root of $\overline g$; by definition of a standard etale algebra this also means that $\overline{f'}(a_0)\neq0$').* Put $a_0=\rho_C(x)\in\kappa$. As $\rho_C$ is an $R$-algebra map, $\rho_C(P(x))=\overline P(a_0)$ for every $P\in R[t ]$ ($\overline P$ the reduction mod $\mathfrak m$). By [lem-weil-pbc-standard-etale-pair-maps] (2) with $L=\kappa$, $\chi=\rho_C$: $\overline f(a_0)=0$ and $\overline h(a_0)\neq0$ (a unit of $\kappa$). Reducing $f'p_1+fp_2=h^n$ mod $\mathfrak m$ and evaluating at $a_0$ gives $\overline{f'}(a_0)\overline{p_1}(a_0)=\overline h(a_0)^n\neq0$, so $\overline{f'}(a_0)\neq0$ (and $\overline{f'}=\overline f\,'$).

**Step 5.** *Existence: the root lifts (Stacks: 'since also $f$ is monic ... we may use that $R$ is henselian to conclude that there exists an $a\in R$ with $a_0=\overline a$ such that $f(a)=0$. This implies that $g(a)$ is a unit of $R$ and we obtain the desired map $\tau$ by the rule $t\mapsto a$').* $f$ is monic and $a_0$ is a root of $\overline f$ with $\overline{f'}(a_0)\neq0$ (step 4), so the root-lifting hypothesis of the statement gives $a\in R$ with $f(a)=0$ and $\pi(a)=a_0$. Then $\pi(h(a))=\overline h(a_0)\neq0$, so $h(a)\notin\mathfrak m$ is a unit of the local ring $R$. By [lem-weil-pbc-standard-etale-pair-maps] (1) with $L=R$, $b=a$, there is an $R$-algebra map $\sigma:C\to R$ with $\sigma(x)=a$. The $R$-algebra maps $\pi\circ\sigma$ and $\rho_C$ from $C$ to $\kappa$ both send $x$ to $a_0$, so they are equal by [lem-weil-pbc-standard-etale-pair-maps] (2) (determined by the image of $x$). By step 3, $\tau=\sigma\circ\varphi\circ\lambda:S\to S_g\to R$ is an $R$-algebra map, i.e. a retraction of $R\to S$, with $\tau^{-1}(\mathfrak m)=\mathfrak q$ (Stacks: 'by construction $\tau^{-1}(\mathfrak m)=\mathfrak q$').

**Step 6.** *Uniqueness (Stacks: 'By Lemma 06RR the map $\tau$ is unique').* Let $\tau_1,\tau_2:S\to R$ be $R$-algebra maps with $\tau_i^{-1}(\mathfrak m)=\mathfrak q$. By step 3, $\tau_i=\sigma_i\circ\varphi\circ\lambda$ with $R$-algebra maps $\sigma_i:C\to R$, $\pi\circ\sigma_i=\rho_C$. Put $a_i=\sigma_i(x)\in R$. By [lem-weil-pbc-standard-etale-pair-maps] (2) (with $L=R$), $f(a_i)=0$; $\pi(a_i)=\rho_C(x)=a_0$, so $a_1-a_2\in\mathfrak m$; and $\pi(f'(a_1))=\overline{f'}(a_0)\neq0$ (step 4), so $f'(a_1)\notin\mathfrak m$. By [lem-weil-pbc-simple-root-uniqueness], $a_1=a_2$; so $\sigma_1=\sigma_2$ ([lem-weil-pbc-standard-etale-pair-maps] (2): an $R$-algebra map out of $C$ is determined by the image of $x$), and $\tau_1=\tau_2$.
