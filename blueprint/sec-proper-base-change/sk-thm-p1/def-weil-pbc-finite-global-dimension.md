Let $R$ be a commutative ring and $M$ an $R$-module. A *projective resolution of $M$ of length $\le n$* ($n\ge0$ an integer) is an exact sequence of $R$-modules $0\to P_n\to P_{n-1}\to\dots\to P_0\to M\to0$ with every $P_i$ a projective $R$-module (a direct summand of a free $R$-module) `Module.Projective` ([Mathlib source](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/master/Mathlib/Algebra/Module/Projective.lean)), exactness as in `CategoryTheory.ShortComplex.Exact` ([Mathlib source](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/master/Mathlib/Algebra/Homology/ShortComplex/Exact.lean)). $M$ has *finite projective dimension* if it has a projective resolution of length $\le n$ for some $n$; the minimal such $n$ is the *projective dimension* $\operatorname{pd}_R M$. The ring $R$ has *finite global dimension* if there is an integer $n\ge0$ such that every $R$-module (not only every finite one) has a projective resolution of length $\le n$; the minimal such $n$ is the *global dimension* $\operatorname{gl.dim}R$, and $R$ *has global dimension $\le n$* if every $R$-module has a projective resolution of length $\le n$. (That the minima exist, and that $\operatorname{pd}_RM\le n$, resp. $\operatorname{gl.dim}R\le n$, holds exactly when $M$ has, resp. every $R$-module has, a projective resolution of length $\le n$, is the statement lem-weil-pbc-projective-dimension-well-defined, recorded in pointers; it is not part of the definition.)
