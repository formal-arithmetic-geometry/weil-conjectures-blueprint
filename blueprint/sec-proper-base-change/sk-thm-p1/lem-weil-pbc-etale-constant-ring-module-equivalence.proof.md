Let $\underline\Lambda^{\mathrm{pre}}$ be the constant presheaf of rings with value $\Lambda$ and $\underline\Lambda$ its sheafification. A sheaf $F$ of $\Lambda$-modules is the same as an abelian sheaf with a module structure over $\underline\Lambda^{\mathrm{pre}}$. Sheafifying this presheaf of $\underline\Lambda^{\mathrm{pre}}$-modules gives a sheaf of $\underline\Lambda$-modules whose underlying abelian sheaf is the sheafification of the underlying abelian sheaf of $F$, which is $F$ itself since $F$ is already a sheaf. A morphism is sent to its sheafification, whose underlying map is the original morphism; identities and composites are therefore preserved, as one checks on sections. This defines the functor, and it leaves the underlying abelian sheaf unchanged ([def-weil-pbc-etale-site]).

For the inverse, restrict the scalars of a sheaf of $\underline\Lambda$-modules along the sheafification map $\underline\Lambda^{\mathrm{pre}}\to\underline\Lambda$. The result is a presheaf of $\Lambda$-modules whose underlying abelian presheaf is the original one; since the sheaf condition for module-valued presheaves can be checked on the underlying abelian presheaves, it is a sheaf. On morphisms the functor does not change the underlying maps.

Both composites leave the underlying abelian sheaves and maps unchanged. For a sheaf $F$ of $\Lambda$-modules, the canonical map from $F$ to its sheafification (followed by restriction of scalars) is the identity on every section, because the abelian sheafification map of a sheaf is the identity; its components are bijective $\Lambda$-linear maps, so it is a natural isomorphism. For a sheaf $G$ of $\underline\Lambda$-modules, the universal property of the sheafification of modules extends the identity of the scalar-restricted presheaf to a map of $\underline\Lambda$-module sheaves from the sheafification to $G$; its underlying map is again the identity, so its components are bijective linear maps and it is an isomorphism. Naturality of both isomorphisms is an equality on sections. These two natural isomorphisms show that the functors are mutually inverse equivalences. Nothing in the construction uses $\Lambda\ne0$ or $Y\ne\emptyset$.
