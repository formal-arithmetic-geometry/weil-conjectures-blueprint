It remains to remove the two hypotheses of
Theorem [sk-thm-dl-a1-upper], that the curve is an open of the affine
line and that the sheaf is unramified at infinity. Moving a point where
the sheaf is lisse to infinity removes the second, and the direct image
along a finite map to the line removes the first.

Each move replaces $H^1_c$ by a group that surjects onto it, which is
all an upper bound needs. Deleting a point $s$ where the sheaf is lisse gives a
surjection $H^1_c(W\setminus\{s\},F)\to H^1_c(W,F)$, because a point
has no $H^1_c$; after a finite extension of the constant field and an
automorphism of $\mathbf P^1$ sending $s$ to infinity, the sheaf is
unramified there. A finite map $\pi:C\to\mathbf A^1$ does not change compactly supported cohomology at all, since
$R\Gamma_c(\mathbf A^1,\pi_*j_!F)=R\Gamma_c(C,F)$, but $\pi_*j_!F$ is
lisse and pure only off the branch locus, and the bad points are
removed by one more localization. The existence of $\pi$ is
Riemann–Roch: a function with a single pole of order prime to $p$ is
finite and generically étale (Lemma [sk-lem-wft-finite-map]). Theorem [sk-thm-wft-curve-middle-upper] then treats a
curve that is not affine and all degrees $r$.
