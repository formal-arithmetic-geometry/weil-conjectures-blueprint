By [sk-p-dl-a1-perverse], the transform of the simple object
$(j_*M)[1]$ is simple, and so by [sk-lem-wft-curve-simples] it is
either punctual or of the displayed form. Restricting the kernel to
$\{a\}\times\mathbf A^1$, we see that the inverse transform of a
geometric skyscraper at $a$ is $\mathcal L_{\psi(-ax)}(1)[1]$. Hence,
if the transform were punctual, geometric base change and Fourier
inversion ([sk-thm-inversion]) would make the restriction of $M$ a sum
of these linear characters, which the hypothesis excludes. To see that
the open and the sheaf are defined over $k_0$, choose a Weil-stable
open on which the constructible transform is lisse.

For the last assertion, we first note that a nonzero linear character
is ramified at infinity. Write $t=1/x$ and express the character by
the Artin–Schreier equation $u^p-u=c/t$ with $c\ne0$. In the field
generated by a root, with normalized valuation and $v(t)=e$, the
equation gives $pv(u)=-e$. Hence $p\mid e$, and since $e\le p$ we get
$e=p$. Thus the cover is totally ramified, and its nontrivial
character of $\mathbf F_p$ is faithful, so that such a constituent
cannot occur in a representation with trivial inertia. It remains to
exclude the trivial character. By [sk-i-restriction], applied to the
normal geometric subgroup of the Weil group, the restriction of $M$ to
the geometric fundamental group is semisimple. A trivial geometric
constituent therefore gives nonzero geometric invariants. This
invariant space is Weil-stable, so by irreducibility $M$ is
geometrically constant.
