In the contracting argument at the end of this appendix we deform a
correspondence to the normal cone and compare trace classes on the
generic and special fibres. The following proposition makes this
comparison. A trace class is built from three pieces: the cohomological
correspondence $u$, the external product $K\boxtimes\mathbb DK$ in which
$u$ becomes a section, and the evaluation map along the diagonal to the
dualizing complex. The preceding subsections show that nearby cycles
commute with each piece: with external products
(Proposition [sk-prop-tf-nearby-external-kunneth]), with Verdier duality
(Proposition [sk-prop-tf-nearby-duality-foundation]), and with the four
operations through the base change maps of the previous subsection. The
proposition assembles these compatibilities. Its conclusion is the
algebraic counterpart of the invariance of a Lefschetz number in a
family: the trace class of the specialized correspondence $u_s$ is the
specialization $\operatorname{sp}_F$ of the trace class of $u$, a map of
sections of dualizing complexes on the fixed scheme. When the fixed
scheme is, up to nilpotents, a constant family $Y\times S$,
$\operatorname{sp}_F$ is the identity (Corollary [sk-cor-tf-sp-fixed-constant]),
and this is how Theorem [sk-thm-tf-contracting-local-term] transfers the
computation to the normal cone.
