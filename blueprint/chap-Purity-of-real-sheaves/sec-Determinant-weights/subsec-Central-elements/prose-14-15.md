In the arithmetic monodromy group we construct a central element of
positive degree
(Proposition [proposition-central-element-arithmetic-monodromy-group]). It acts by a scalar
on each simple factor, and this scalar determines the determinant
weight of the factor. As explained above, we construct the element for semisimple
representations, and then use stable filtrations to obtain the
formulas for arbitrary representations.

The degree must be positive. Indeed, an element of $H$ that is central in
$G$ also acts by a scalar on each simple factor $Q$. But, as for every element
of $H$, its determinant on $Q$ is a root of unity, so under $\iota$ the scalar
has absolute value one, and it records no weight. By contrast, an element
$z=h\rho(s)^N$ with $N>0$ contains $N$ factors of Frobenius, and its
scalar $c_Q$ on $Q$ satisfies
$\lambda_\iota(c_Q)=N\operatorname{dw}_\iota(Q)$
(Lemma [lemma-determinant-weight-from-central-scalar]).

We produce the central element by Lie theory, which applies to
algebraic groups and not to the abstract images of $\pi_1(U)$ and
$W(U_0)$. For $\mathcal B_b$ with $b$ not a root of unity, the image of
$W(U_0)$ in $\GL_1$ is the infinite cyclic group generated by $b$, which
is not an algebraic subgroup; its Zariski closure is all of $\GL_1$,
since the proper closed subgroups of $\mathbf G_m$ are finite. Passing
to the closure loses nothing that we need. Indeed, stabilizers are closed, so
a subspace of $V$ is stable under a group of matrices if and only if it is
stable under its Zariski closure. For this reason Lemma [lemma-identity-component] and
Proposition [proposition-central-element-arithmetic-monodromy-group]
are statements about these closures.
