Let $X$ be qcqs and let $\mathcal H\subseteq\mathcal O_X$ be a quasi-coherent
ideal. The finite type quasi-coherent subideals of $\mathcal H$ form a family
which is directed under inclusion and whose union is $\mathcal H$.

Suppose moreover that $U\subseteq X$ is a quasi-compact open with
$\mathcal H|_U=\mathcal O_U$. Then the subfamily of those finite type
subideals $\mathcal K\subseteq\mathcal H$ with $\mathcal K|_U=\mathcal O_U$ is
nonempty, is directed, and still has union $\mathcal H$; in particular every
finite type subideal of $\mathcal H$ is contained in a member of it.

## Proof

The family of finite type subideals is closed under sums, hence directed, and
its union is contained in $\mathcal H$. For the reverse inclusion let $V$ be
an affine open and $a\in\mathcal H(V)$. The ideal $\mathcal J\subseteq \mathcal O_V$ generated by $a$ is of finite type and is the restriction of the
quasi-coherent subideal $\mathcal J^{\max}\cap\mathcal H\subseteq\mathcal H$,
where $\mathcal J^{\max}$ is the largest quasi-coherent ideal restricting to
$\mathcal J$ on $V$; so
[lemma-nagata-finite-type-ideal-extension-exists], applied inside
$\mathcal H$, produces a finite type $\mathcal K\subseteq\mathcal H$ with
$\mathcal K|_V=\mathcal J$, and then $a\in\mathcal K(V)$.

For the second statement, apply
[lemma-nagata-finite-type-ideal-extension-exists] to $\mathcal H$ on the
quasi-compact open $U$, where $\mathcal H|_U=\mathcal O_U$ is of finite type:
it produces a finite type $\mathcal K'\subseteq\mathcal H$ with
$\mathcal K'|_U=\mathcal H|_U=\mathcal O_U$, so the subfamily is nonempty. It
is directed because it is again closed under sums. Finally, for any finite
type $\mathcal K\subseteq\mathcal H$ the sum $\mathcal K+\mathcal K'$ lies in
the subfamily and contains $\mathcal K$, so the subfamily is cofinal in the
family of all finite type subideals and has the same union $\mathcal H$.
