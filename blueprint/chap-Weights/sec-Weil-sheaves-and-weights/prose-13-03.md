Every sheaf defined over $k_0$ is a Weil sheaf. By means of the Weil
isomorphism, Frobenius acts on the stalk at a geometric point over each
closed point $x$, so we can speak of the eigenvalues of
$\operatorname{Frob}_x$. In the description by representations the Weil
isomorphism is transport by $s$, and conversely it determines the action
of $s$.

The definition below contains two kinds of bounds. A bound on the eigenvalues
at each closed point, the condition of being punctually of weights $\le w$, is
what the curve case yields on each stalk of a direct image. It cannot serve as
input for the curve case, which needs pure lisse sheaves. A mixed sheaf, by
contrast, can be treated one pure quotient at a time. For a mixed
sheaf the bound on weights is again a pointwise condition
(Lemma [lemma-bounding-weights-mixed-sheaf]). In
Section [chap-Delignes-theorem-in-general] we use this to turn the stalkwise
bounds into the bound of Theorem [theorem-weights-rf-shriek].
