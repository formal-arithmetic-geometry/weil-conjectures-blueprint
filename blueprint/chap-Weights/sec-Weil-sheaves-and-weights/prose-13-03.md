Every sheaf defined over $k_0$ is a Weil sheaf. By means of the Weil
isomorphism, Frobenius acts on the stalk at a geometric point over each
closed point $x$, so we can speak of the eigenvalues of
$\operatorname{Frob}_x$. In the description by representations the Weil
isomorphism is transport by $s$, and conversely it determines the action
of $s$.

The definition below contains two kinds of bounds. The first, being
punctually of weights $\le w$, bounds the eigenvalues at each closed point;
it is what the curve case yields on each stalk of a direct image. But it
cannot serve as input for the curve case, which needs pure lisse sheaves. The second, mixedness, has the opposite virtue: a mixed sheaf can be treated one pure quotient at a time, and
for a mixed sheaf the bound on weights is again a pointwise condition
(Lemma [lemma-bounding-weights-mixed-sheaf]). In
Chapter [chap-The-Deligne-Laumon-theorem-in-general] we use this to turn the stalkwise
bounds into the bound of Theorem [theorem-weights-rf-shriek].
