The adjunction $Rp_*\dashv p^*(1)[2]$ for
$p:\mathbf P^1_S\to S$ is constructed from two maps: a trace
$Rp_*\Lambda(1)[2]\to\Lambda$, obtained from the cohomology of
$\mathbf P^1$, and the cycle class of the diagonal in
$\mathbf P^1_S\times_S\mathbf P^1_S$, which is a special case of the
cycle class of an effective Cartier divisor. The trace is the counit and the
diagonal class is the unit. Both triangle identities reduce to the
fact that the diagonal meets each fibre of either projection
$\mathbf P^1\times\mathbf P^1\to\mathbf P^1$ in a single point, so that
pushing its class forward along either projection and applying the trace
gives $1$.

The topological picture explains why these are the right two maps. For
a bundle $E\to B$ with fibre $S^2$, integration over the fibre
$H^2(S^2)\to\mathbf Z$ is the counit of Poincaré duality on the fibres,
and the unit is the Thom class of the diagonal of $E\times_BE$, which is
Poincaré dual to the identity correspondence. In étale cohomology the
fundamental class of a point on $\mathbf P^1$ comes from the Kummer
sequence, so both maps are built from line bundles: the trace from
$c_1(\mathcal O(1))$, and the class of the diagonal from the divisor
$x_0y_1-x_1y_0=0$. For every other morphism, $f^!$ will be computed from
this adjunction, from $j_!\dashv j^*$ for open immersions and from
localization.
