All objects of the definition exist: by [det-def-sfb-shriek-base-change-morphism-compactification]
both squares are commutative squares of the setting, the right one with $p$, $p'$ proper and the left
one cartesian with $i$, $i'$ open immersions. So $\mathrm{BC}$ is defined by
[det-def-sfb-base-change-morphism-bc] and $\mathrm{Ex}^j$ by [def-sfb-dc-open-base-change-morphism];
the latter is invertible because the left square is cartesian ([thm-sfb-dc-open-base-change-iso]).
Whiskering a natural transformation by a functor and applying a functor to a natural isomorphism give
natural transformations, resp. natural isomorphisms, so both arrows are natural, and the second is
invertible.
