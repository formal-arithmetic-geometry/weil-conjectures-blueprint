With the theorem of the cube and the rigidity lemma in hand, we derive
the structure of an abelian variety that the rest of the appendix uses.
Rigidity, applied to the commutator map, shows that the group law is
commutative. The theorem of the cube, applied to a sheaf on $A\times A\times A$ built from $\mathcal L$ by pulling back along the partial sums
of the coordinates, shows that $n\mapsto[n]^*\mathcal L$ satisfies a
second order recursion, so that $[n]^*\mathcal L$ depends quadratically
on $n$. Explicitly, $[n]^*\mathcal L$ is
$\mathcal L^{\otimes n(n+1)/2}\otimes([-1]^*\mathcal L)^{\otimes n(n-1)/2}$.
For symmetric $\mathcal L$ this is $\mathcal L^{\otimes n^2}$, and for
$\mathcal L$ with $[-1]^*\mathcal L\simeq\mathcal L^{-1}$, such as an
invertible sheaf of degree zero on an elliptic curve, it is
$\mathcal L^{\otimes n}$. The symmetric case, for an ample sheaf, is
Proposition [sk-prop-av-mult-n-pullback].
Proposition [sk-prop-av-isogeny] then shows that
$[n]$ is finite and flat of degree $n^{2g}$, and étale when $n$ is
invertible in $k$. The last hypothesis cannot be dropped. In
characteristic $p$, the differential of $[p]$ is zero, and an elliptic
curve has at most $p$ points of order dividing $p$ over $\bar k$,
although $E[p]$ has rank $p^2$.
