Since $P$ is proper and geometrically integral, so is $P_t$ over
$\kappa(t)$, and $H^0(P_t,\mathcal O_{P_t})=\kappa(t)$
(Stacks [0BUG](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0BUG)).
For (1), let $s$ and $s'$ be nonzero sections of $\mathcal L_t$ and
$\mathcal L_t^{-1}$. Since $P_t$ is integral, the product $ss'$ is a
nonzero element of $H^0(P_t,\mathcal O)=\kappa(t)$, hence a unit, and
so $s$ vanishes nowhere. The converse is clear. For (2), the functions
$t\mapsto\dim H^0(P_t,\mathcal L_t^{\pm1})$ are upper semicontinuous
(Stacks [0BDN](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0BDN)), so the loci where they are positive are closed, and (1)
identifies their intersection with the set in question.
