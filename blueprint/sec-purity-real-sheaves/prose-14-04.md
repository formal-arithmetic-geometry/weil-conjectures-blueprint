For (1), recall that at every closed point $x$ the eigenvalues of
$\operatorname{Frob}_x$ on the stalk of a mixed sheaf are those on the
stalks of the quotients of its filtration. If the quotients have weights
at most $w$, the sheaf is therefore punctually of weights $\le w$.
Conversely, each nonzero
quotient has a nonzero stalk at some closed point $x$, and the
eigenvalues of $\operatorname{Frob}_x$ there are eigenvalues on the
stalk of the sheaf. So if the sheaf is punctually of weights $\le w$,
the weight of the quotient is at most $w$.

The basic example of a pure sheaf is the Tate sheaf
$\overline{\mathbf Q}_\ell(1)$, which is pure of weight $-2$
(Proposition [sk-r-real-linear]).

The behaviour of weights under the usual operations comes down to the
eigenvalues of an operator on a filtered space, on duals and on tensor
and exterior powers.
