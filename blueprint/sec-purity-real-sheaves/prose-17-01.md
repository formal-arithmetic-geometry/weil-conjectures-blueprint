The main result of this section, Theorem [sk-p-real-purity], says
that every irreducible constituent of a real lisse sheaf on a curve is
punctually pure, of weight equal to its determinant weight. It is used
twice: in Section “Weights of the cohomology of curves”, to show that a twist of
the Fourier transform of a pure sheaf is pure, and in
Section “Weights under direct image with proper support”, where we restrict to curves to show that
real sheaves on any base are mixed. The proof is Rankin's argument
described in Section “The weight bound and why it holds”. The Fourier
argument of the next two sections needs two further facts about pure sheaves on curves, and we
prove them here as well: a bound for the weights of $j_*$ at a boundary
point, and a formula for the weight in terms of the growth of squared
trace sums.

We bound the local eigenvalues of a real sheaf by the eigenvalues of
Frobenius on $H^2_c$ of its even tensor powers
(Proposition [sk-p-real-rankin]). Only $H^2_c$ enters, and by
Poincaré duality it is given by the coinvariants of the geometric
monodromy (Lemma [sk-lem-wrp-hc2]).
