Let $C_0/\mathbf F_Q$ be a smooth projective geometrically connected curve,
put $C=C_0\times_{\mathbf F_Q}\overline{\mathbf F}_Q$, and let $J$ be its
Jacobian, equipped with the canonical semilinear
action of arithmetic Frobenius $\sigma_Q:z\mapsto z^Q$
([lem-wf-geometric-jacobian-galois-action]). If $\ell\nmid Q$, then

$$
(T_\ell J)^{\sigma_Q=1}=0,
$$

and $\sigma_Q-1$ is an automorphism of the
rational Tate module $V_\ell J$.

## Proof

The group $\operatorname{Pic}^0(C_0)$ is finite by
[lem-wf-pic0-finite-field] (the curve $C_0$ is smooth and geometrically
connected, hence geometrically irreducible, so that lemma applies).
Under the canonical identification
$J(\overline{\mathbf F}_Q)=\operatorname{Pic}^0(C)$ of
[lem-wf-geometric-jacobian-galois-action] a point of $J(\overline{\mathbf F}_Q)$
fixed by $\sigma_Q$ is the class of an invertible module on $C$ of degree zero
whose class is fixed by $\sigma_Q$. Such a class descends to $\mathbf F_Q$: by fpqc
descent for invertible modules ([lem-pc-fpqc-descent]) an invertible
module on $C$ whose class is fixed by $\sigma_Q$ is the base change of an
invertible module on $C_0$, after normalizing the descent datum along the
cyclic group generated by $\sigma_Q$, and the degree is preserved because it
is read off on geometric fibres. Hence the fixed subgroup
$J(\overline{\mathbf F}_Q)^{\sigma_Q}$ lies in the finite group
$\operatorname{Pic}^0(C_0)$. The Tate module of [def-wf-abelian-tate-module] is
the Tate module of the abelian group $J(\overline{\mathbf F}_Q)$
([def-tate-module-abelian-group]), so [lem-tate-module-no-fixed], applied to
this group and the automorphism $\sigma_Q$, gives $(T_\ell J)^{\sigma_Q=1}=0$,
proving the first assertion. (This is the special case, for the Frobenius action
on the Jacobian of [lem-wf-geometric-jacobian-galois-action], of the argument of
[prop-av-tate-no-frobenius-fixed].)

By [lem-sc-ntorsion-etale],
$J[\ell^n](\overline{\mathbf F}_Q)\cong (\mathbf Z/\ell^n\mathbf Z)^{2\dim J}$; with the multiplication-by-$\ell$
transition maps this makes $T_\ell J$ finite free ([prop-av-tate-rank]) and $V_\ell J$
finite-dimensional. If a vector of $V_\ell J$ is killed by $\sigma_Q-1$,
multiplying it by a power of $\ell$ puts it in $T_\ell J$, where the first
assertion kills it. Hence $\sigma_Q-1$ is injective on the
finite-dimensional vector space $V_\ell J$, and elementary linear algebra
makes it surjective as well.
