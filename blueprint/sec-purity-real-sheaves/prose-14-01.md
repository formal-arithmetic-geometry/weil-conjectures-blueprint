The trace formula writes the zeta function of $X_0$ as an alternating
product of the characteristic polynomials of Frobenius on
$H^i_c(X,\overline{\mathbf Q}_\ell)$. To prove the Riemann hypothesis we have to bound the
absolute values of the eigenvalues. We do this for all constructible
sheaves, not only for the constant sheaf, because the proof factors a
morphism and the intermediate direct images are not constant. The
main theorem says that for a separated morphism of
finite type $f$, the direct image $Rf_!$ does not increase weights
(Theorem [sk-cor-dl-general-complex]). Applied to the
structure map of a smooth proper scheme and combined with Poincaré
duality, it implies the Weil conjectures
(Theorem [sk-thm-weil-final]).

Weights are defined by the absolute values of the Frobenius
eigenvalues on stalks at closed points, so we first fix how Frobenius
acts on sheaves.
