The main result of this section is that a rank-one lisse Weil sheaf on
a smooth geometrically connected curve has finite geometric monodromy
(Theorem [sk-p-real-rankone]). It follows that such a sheaf is
pure, and that the determinant of every lisse Weil sheaf on the curve
is pure (Corollary [sk-d-real-detweights]). Rankin's argument in
Section “Purity of real sheaves on curves” controls the cohomology of tensor powers
through the determinant weights of their constituents, where the
determinant weight of an irreducible sheaf is the weight of its
determinant divided by its rank. By Corollary [sk-d-real-detweights] this
weight is defined, and Section “Monodromy and determinant weights” computes it for tensor
and exterior powers.

The logarithm argument of the next subsection needs that Frobenius has
no fixed vector on $H^1(U,E)$ for a smooth curve $U_0/k_0$
(Proposition [sk-prop-wrp-h1-no-fixed]). For a proper curve, Kummer theory
and duality identify $H^1$ with the dual of the Tate module of the
Jacobian $J$, that is, of $T_\ell J=\varprojlim_nJ[\ell^n](k)$, on
which Frobenius has no fixed vector. For an open curve,
the boundary contributes in addition a permutation representation
twisted by $(-1)$, which has no fixed vector either. To reduce to the
proper case we first compactify.
