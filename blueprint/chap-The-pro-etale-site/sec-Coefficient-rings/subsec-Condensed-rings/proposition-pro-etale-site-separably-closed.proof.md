Since a finitely presented étale $\Omega$-algebra is a finite product of
copies of $\Omega$, filtered colimits of such algebras correspond,
under $\operatorname{Spec}$, to inverse limits of finite sets. Faithfully
flat covering maps become surjections of profinite sets, and by
quasi-compactness every cover can be refined by a finite one, so the
sites are equivalent. The topoi agree because the pro-étale affines
form a basis and sheaves are determined by their values on them. The
last assertion is the definition of the condensed ring
$\underline R$ of a topological ring $R$ given above, whose sections over a
profinite set $S$ are the continuous maps $S\to R$; for discrete $R$
these are the locally constant functions, which are the sections of
the classical constant sheaf.
