The short exact sequence in
[corollary-finite-quotients-their-transition-maps--1] gives the
quasi-isomorphism from the two-term cone to $B_n[0]$. This cone is
K-flat: it is the cone of a map between copies of the tensor unit in
degree zero, and K-flat objects are closed under cones. Consequently
tensoring with it computes the derived tensor product for an arbitrary
complex $M$. The standard tensor-cone isomorphism identifies this tensor
product with $\operatorname{cone}(\pi_E^n:M\to M)$. Both the augmentation
and this isomorphism are natural in $M$.

The equality $\pi_E^{n+1}=\pi_E^n\pi_E$ gives the map
$(\pi_E,\mathrm{id})$ between the two-term resolutions. Its degree-zero
component followed by reduction is the quotient projection. Naturality
of the augmentation and of the tensor-cone isomorphism gives the claimed
transition identity. The same construction takes the cone inclusion to
the reduction map. Finally, the signed tensor-cone shift comparison
multiplies the shifted source summand by $(-1)^r$, giving the asserted
compatibility with shifts after localization.
