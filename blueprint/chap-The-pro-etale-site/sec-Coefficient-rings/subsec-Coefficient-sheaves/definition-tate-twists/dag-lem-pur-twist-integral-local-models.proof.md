1. Work first on the localized pro-etale sites. `essentiallySmallSelf`, `GrothendieckTopology.WEqualsLocallyBijective.ofEssentiallySmall`, and `hasSheafifyEssentiallySmallSite` give the sheafification and local-bijection instances used below; the locally presentable Grothendieck framework `lem-coef-grothendieck-locally-presentable`, finite-limit preservation of the transfinite plus construction [dag-lem-sfb-transfinite-plus-finite-limits], and its orthogonality to sheaves [dag-lem-sfb-transfinite-plus-sheaf-orthogonality] supply the corresponding sheaf-theoretic infrastructure. For the root sheaf in [dag-def-pur-twist-sheaves], apply `SheafOfModules.isFiniteLocallyFreeOfRank_iff_exists_over_iso_free` (the notion is `def-amb-finite-locally-free-module`) to hL. Pull the resulting local free basis back along the cover and replace its covering sieve by the sigma-type of arrows lying in that sieve. The equality of this `Presieve.ofArrows` with the original sieve gives the required pro-etale cover and local unit bases for the completed root line. (uses [dag-lem-sfb-transfinite-plus-finite-limits], [dag-lem-sfb-transfinite-plus-sheaf-orthogonality], `lem-coef-grothendieck-locally-presentable`)
2. For a test object U, use the concrete coefficient rings of [dag-def-pur-twist-coefficient-rings], modeled by continuous coefficient functions as in `def-weil-pbc-lisse-lattice-coefficients`. The coefficient square and the bijection `purTwistCoefficientTensor` [dag-lem-pur-twist-coefficient-tensor] give a ring equivalence q from the coefficient tensor pushout to the completed coefficient ring. The pure-section maps and their binary coherence are the data of `def-sp-scalar-tensor-comparison-data` and `lem-sp-scalar-extension-binary-coherence`. Applying `TensorProduct.AlgebraTensorModule.cancelBaseChange`, `TensorProduct.comm`, and `TensorProduct.congr`, the map sending s tensor x to d(s) tensor x is an additive equivalence; `pushoutModuleEquiv_tmul` identifies it with `integralRawForward` on pure tensors. Thus `integralRawForward_isIso` follows sectionwise from `ConcreteCategory.isIso_iff_bijective`. Sheafification then uses `restrictionSheafificationComparison_isIso` and `PresheafOfModules.sheafificationHomEquiv` to produce the discrete/completed scalar isomorphism `integralDiscreteIso`. (uses [dag-lem-pur-twist-coefficient-tensor], `def-weil-pbc-lisse-lattice-coefficients`, `def-sp-scalar-tensor-comparison-data`, `lem-sp-scalar-extension-binary-coherence`)
3. The unique prescribed completed action gives `integralForgetIso`; `rootToIntegral` sends x to the pure tensor 1 tensor x, and `integralExtensionReverse` is its scalar-extension lift. Its section formula is `SheafOfModules.scalarExtensionLift_tmul`. To prove that the reverse extension is inverse to `integralDiscreteIso`, check the composite on scalar pure tensors with `SheafOfModules.scalarExtension_hom_ext`, use `integralDiscreteIso_tmul` and `SheafOfModules.scalarExtensionSection_smul`, and then use the uniqueness equation for the completed action. The sheaf-Hom interpretation [dag-def-pur-sheaf-hom] and the tensor-generator evaluation comparison `lem-sp-scalar-extension-dual-evaluation` justify this generator-level Hom check. Cancellation of the two displayed isomorphisms and reflection of isomorphisms by `SheafOfModules.toSheaf` yield `integralExtensionReverse_isIso`. Transporting the rank-one bases from Step 1 along its inverse gives the required completed integral local unit models. (uses [dag-def-pur-sheaf-hom], `lem-sp-scalar-extension-dual-evaluation`)
4. Fix U and n, and let J be the ideal generated by the sheafified coefficient uniformizer. The coefficient tensor calculation [dag-lem-pur-twist-coefficient-tensor] and the continuous-function coefficient model `def-weil-pbc-lisse-lattice-coefficients` supply the sectionwise coefficient maps; the DVR calculation `lem-weil-l-adic-extension-integers-dvr` together with the coefficient-ring description [dag-def-pur-twist-coefficient-rings] gives the positive quotient. `Ideal.mk_ker` and `Ideal.span_singleton_pow`, followed by `lifted_principal_kernel` and `constantSection_principal_quotient`, show that the quotient section map is surjective with kernel J^(n+1) (`integralQuotient_sections`). Applying `completion_positive_iso_normalized` to the quotient tower and its preserved limit (`isLimitOfPreserves`, `Concrete.isLimit_ext`) identifies its inverse limit with the completed coefficient sections; the normalization records every finite projection as `AdicCompletion.evalₐ` followed by the corresponding quotient map (`integralUniformizerCompletion`). Finally `AdicCompletion.pow_smul_top_eq_ker_eval`, `Submodule.fg_span_singleton`, `Ideal.map_map`, and `Ideal.mem_map_iff_of_surjective` give the kernel identity for the completed projection (`completion_eval_kernel` and `integralProjection_kernel`). (uses [dag-lem-pur-twist-coefficient-tensor], `def-weil-pbc-lisse-lattice-coefficients`)
5. Let phi be the map from the discrete integral coefficient ring to the completion, q the discrete quotient, and p the completed projection. The scalar square, sectionwise surjectivity, and the kernel formula from Step 4 imply via `scalarLinear_of_kernel_span` that every q-linear map is p-linear: the kernel is generated by images of ker q, and `Submodule.span_induction` proves it acts by zero. This gives `quotientRestrictionHomEquiv`. Composing it with `PurScalar.tensorAdjunction` produces `quotientTensorAdjunction`; `Adjunction.leftAdjointUniq` then gives `quotientReductionIso`. The pure-tensor comparisons and their naturality are those of `def-sp-scalar-tensor-comparison-data` and `lem-sp-scalar-extension-binary-coherence`, with the module-Hom and evaluation conventions of [dag-def-pur-sheaf-hom] and `lem-sp-scalar-extension-dual-evaluation`. Composing the reduction isomorphism with `PurScalar.tensorFunctor` and `integralForgetIso` yields `integralFiniteIso`, the stated comparison between the completed scalar reduction and `purTwistFiniteOne`. (uses [dag-def-pur-sheaf-hom], `def-sp-scalar-tensor-comparison-data`, `lem-sp-scalar-extension-binary-coherence`, `lem-sp-scalar-extension-dual-evaluation`)
6. Extend the completed local basis through `integralFiniteIso` and `SheafOfModules.extendedBasisIso`; this gives, on the same covering family, a unit model for every finite twist. For nonvanishing, the continuous coefficient realization `def-weil-pbc-lisse-lattice-coefficients`, the DVR facts in `lem-weil-l-adic-extension-integers-dvr`, and `IsDiscreteValuationRing.irreducible_iff_uniformizer` show that the positive uniformizer power is a proper ideal; `Ideal.Quotient.nontrivial_iff` and `discreteCoefficientComparison` therefore show by evaluation at any point that 1 is not zero in the finite coefficient sheaf. If S is nonempty, `ProEt.mem_precoverage_iff` and `Scheme.ofArrows_mem_precoverage_iff` lift a point to some member of the covering family. An assumed `IsZero` object makes its identity map zero; transporting the unit section through the local isomorphism and evaluating at the lifted point contradicts the preceding 1-ne-0 statement. Hence every finite model is nonzero over a nonempty base. Combining this with the completed model from Step 3 and the comparison from Step 5 proves all conjuncts of the theorem, with no derived assertion; the concrete sheaves involved are those fixed in [dag-def-pur-twist-sheaves]. (uses `def-weil-pbc-lisse-lattice-coefficients`)
