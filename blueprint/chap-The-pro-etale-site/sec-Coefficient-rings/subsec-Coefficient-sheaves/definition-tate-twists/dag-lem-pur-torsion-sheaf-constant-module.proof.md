1. Put $A=\mathbf Z/N\mathbf Z$ and let $P$ be the constant presheaf of rings with value $A$ and identity restriction maps. Write $R=A_{\mathcal C}$ and $\eta:P\to R$ for its sheafification map [dag-def-weil-pbc-constant-sheaf]. For any abelian sheaf $H$, the identity $H\to H$ is a sheafification of its underlying abelian presheaf: every presheaf morphism $H\to K$ to a sheaf factors uniquely through that identity [dag-def-weil-pbc-sheafification]. Thus in the module-sheafification theorem we may take the associated abelian sheaf of $\mathcal F$ to be $\mathcal F$ itself, with unit the identity; no comparison of different underlying sheaves is needed. (uses [dag-def-weil-pbc-sheafification])
2. By `lem-weil-pbc-zmod-module-structure-killed-by-n` (1), for each $U$ the hypothesis $N\mathcal F(U)=0$ gives the $A$-module structure $\bar a\cdot x=ax$, for any integer lift $a$. Explicitly, another lift $a+Nk$ gives $(a+Nk)x=ax+k(Nx)=ax$. Each restriction map is additive, and hence $A$-linear by part (4) of that same node. Consequently these sectionwise actions make the underlying abelian presheaf of $\mathcal F$ a presheaf of $P$-modules: restrictions satisfy $(\bar a\cdot x)|_V=\bar a\cdot(x|_V)$ since $P$ has identity restrictions. (uses `lem-weil-pbc-zmod-module-structure-killed-by-n`)
3. Apply `lem-pc-sites-module-sheafification-interface` with $\mathcal O=P$, $\mathcal O^\#=R$, $\mathcal P=\mathcal F$ with its action from step 2, and abelian sheafification $\mathcal F\xrightarrow{\mathrm{id}}\mathcal F$ from step 1. It gives a unique compatible action $R\times\mathcal F\to\mathcal F$ making $\mathcal F$ a sheaf of $R$-modules. Compatibility reads $\eta_U(\bar a)\cdot x=ax$, exactly the normalization in clause (1). Every action required in clause (1) satisfies this compatibility, so the uniqueness assertion of the cited node proves uniqueness on the fixed underlying abelian sheaf, not merely uniqueness up to isomorphism. In Lean, use PresheafOfModules.Sheafify.module with the identity on the underlying abelian presheaf, and use map_smul_eq and map_smul for normalization and restrictions. Given another normalized action, each of its scalar products is an SMulCandidate: restrict it, lift the scalar locally, and choose an integer representative of the lifted ZMod element. Subsingleton SMulCandidate makes both scalar products equal, and Module.ext' gives equality of module structures. The constant ring is placed in the site universe max(uC,vC), so the standard ring sheafification unit is locally bijective; the abelian-group universe remains independent. (uses `lem-pc-sites-module-sheafification-interface`)
4. Let $H$ be any sheaf of $R$-modules, in the sense of `def-weil-sheaf-of-modules-category`. Because $\eta_U:A\to R(U)$ is a unital ring homomorphism, $\eta_U(\bar a)=a\cdot1_{R(U)}$ for every integer $a$. By `lem-weil-pbc-zmod-module-structure-killed-by-n` (3), its action on $H(U)$ is therefore $ax$. In particular $Nx=\eta_U(\bar N)x=\eta_U(0)x=0$, so the underlying abelian sheaf is killed by $N$. Its constant-presheaf action is the one of step 2; step 3 shows that its full $R$-action is the unique extension of that action. This proves the converse assertion about objects in clause (2), including module structures not initially assumed to satisfy the normalization. (uses `lem-weil-pbc-zmod-module-structure-killed-by-n`)
5. Let $h:\mathcal F\to\mathcal G$ be an additive sheaf morphism with normalized module structures from step 3. Transporting any normalized action by an additive sheaf automorphism preserves its integer normalization and semilinear restrictions, since the automorphism and its inverse commute with restrictions and integer multiples. Uniqueness from step 3 makes the transported action equal to the original one, so every such automorphism is linear. Form the direct sum $H$ of the given module sheaves `def-weil-sheaf-of-modules-category`, and forget to its underlying abelian sheaf. Step 4 shows that $H$ is killed by $N$, and its action is normalized because a unital ring homomorphism preserves integer casts. Write $i,p,j,q$ for its first inclusion, first projection, second inclusion and second projection. The additive endomorphism $t=j\circ h\circ p$ has $t^2=0$ since $p\circ j=0$. Hence $1+t$ is an additive sheaf automorphism with inverse $1-t$ and is linear by the preceding argument. The composite $q\circ(1+t)\circ i$ is $h$, since $q\circ i=0$, $p\circ i=1$ and $q\circ j=1$. Inclusions and projections are module morphisms, so $h$ is linear. Conversely, module morphisms are additive by `def-weil-sheaf-of-modules-category`.
