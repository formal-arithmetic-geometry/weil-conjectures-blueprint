1. Let $j:E\hookrightarrow F$ be the given inclusion. By `def-sfb-padic-subfield-condensed-ring`, both absolute values are restrictions of the same value on $\Omega$, so $|j(x)|_F=|x|_E$. By `def-sfb-padic-integers-condensed-ring`, membership in either integer ring is precisely the inequality $|x|\le1$. Thus $r:\mathcal O_E\to\mathcal O_F$, $x\mapsto j(x)$, is well defined and preserves all ring operations. For $x,y\in\mathcal O_E$, $|r(x)-r(y)|_F=|x-y|_E$, so it is continuous in the specified subspace topologies. The ordinary inclusions $a_H:\mathcal O_H\hookrightarrow H$ satisfy $a_F\circ r=j\circ a_E$ by evaluation at each $x$. (uses `def-sfb-padic-subfield-condensed-ring`, `def-sfb-padic-integers-condensed-ring`)
2. Apply `def-sfb-continuous-map-condensed-ring-map` to $r$. This gives a ring-object morphism $b:\underline{\mathcal O_E}\to\underline{\mathcal O_F}$ whose component at a profinite set $S$ sends $f$ to $r\circ f$. The integral inclusion $a_H$ induces the postcomposition map $\underline a_H$ of `def-sfb-integers-to-subfield-point`, and the field map induces $\underline j$ of `def-sfb-qlbar-subextension-map`. Both composites in $\underline a_F\circ b=\underline j\circ\underline a_E$ send $f$ to the identical function $S\to F$, so they are equal as natural transformations. (uses `def-sfb-continuous-map-condensed-ring-map`, `def-sfb-integers-to-subfield-point`, `def-sfb-qlbar-subextension-map`)
3. Use the functor on ring objects of `sk-def-sfb-pulled-back-coefficients` and put $\varphi=p_X^*b$. It preserves unit and multiplication. Functoriality applied to the equality of step 2 gives $p_X^*(\underline a_F)\circ\varphi=p_X^*(\underline j)\circ p_X^*(\underline a_E)$. By `def-sfb-integers-to-subfield`, $p_X^*(\underline a_H)=a_{H,X}$ for $H=E,F$. By `def-sfb-subfield-coefficient-map`, $i_{E,F,X}$ is the coefficient change of $\underline j$, which by `def-sfb-coefficient-map` is $p_X^*(\underline j)$. So these three maps are exactly $a_{F,X}$, $i_{E,F,X}$ and $a_{E,X}$, respectively. This proves existence with the required square and the specified coefficient objects. (uses `sk-def-sfb-pulled-back-coefficients`, `def-sfb-integers-to-subfield`, `def-sfb-subfield-coefficient-map`, `def-sfb-coefficient-map`)
4. The point functor $u:S\mapsto\operatorname{Spec}LC(S,\mathbf Z)$ preserves finite limits and coverings and is continuous by clause 1 of `sk-def-sfb-pullback-from-point`. Profinite sets have the point as terminal object and fibre products with their usual subspace topology (closed subspaces of finite products of compact Hausdorff totally disconnected spaces); hence they have finite limits. Work in a common larger sheaf universe, as in that node. Apply `lem-weil-pbc-left-exact-site-inverse-image-tensor` to $u$: the sheafified Kan-extension functor $p^*$ preserves finite limits on abelian sheaves. The functor $\pi_X^*$ for $\pi_X:X\to\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf Z$ preserves finite limits by [dag-lem-sfb-finite-upper-star-exact]. Clause 4 of the first node identifies $p_X^*=\pi_X^*\circ p^*$. A composite of these two functors therefore preserves finite limits: its comparison maps are composites of the two invertible finite-limit comparisons. (uses [dag-lem-sfb-finite-upper-star-exact], `sk-def-sfb-pullback-from-point`, `lem-weil-pbc-left-exact-site-inverse-image-tensor`)
5. The map $\underline a_F$ of step 2 is a categorical monomorphism. Indeed if $v,w:G\to\underline{\mathcal O_F}$ have equal composites with it, then for every profinite $S$ and $z\in G(S)$ the functions $v_S(z)$ and $w_S(z)$ have the same values in $F$. Injectivity of the subset inclusion $\mathcal O_F\hookrightarrow F$ makes them equal in $\mathcal O_F$, so $v=w$. A map $m:A\to B$ is monic exactly when its square with both parallel sides $m$ and both other sides $\mathrm{id}_A$ is a pullback: the pullback universal property is precisely cancellation of $m$. The finite-limit preservation in step 4 sends this square for $\underline a_F$ to that square for $p_X^*\underline a_F=a_{F,X}$. Thus $a_{F,X}$ is monic as an underlying abelian-sheaf morphism.
6. If $\psi$ is another ring-object morphism satisfying the prescribed square, then $a_{F,X}\circ\psi=i_{E,F,X}\circ a_{E,X}=a_{F,X}\circ\varphi$. By the cancellation proved in step 5, $\psi=\varphi$. This is uniqueness of the underlying morphism, and the property of preserving unit and multiplication contributes no additional choice.
