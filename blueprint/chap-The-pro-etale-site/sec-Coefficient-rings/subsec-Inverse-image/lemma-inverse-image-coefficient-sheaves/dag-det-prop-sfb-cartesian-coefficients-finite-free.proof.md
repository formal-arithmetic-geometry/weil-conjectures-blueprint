1. Fix the algebra and module structures coming from the actual inclusion E ⊆ F and from PadicAlgCl.finite_integersMap. The lower integer ring is a PID, the upper integer ring is finite and integral over it, and the integrally closed upper ring is the integral closure in F. The hypotheses of [dag-lem-sfb-integral-closure-rank] therefore identify the finite rank of this free module with [F:E]. Using IsIntegralClosure.of_isIntegrallyClosed, IsIntegralClosure.module_free, IsIntegralClosure.rank, and Module.finBasisOfFinrankEq, the private integral_basis_exists constructs the required Nonempty (finiteCoefficientIntegralBasis ℓ h), with the scalar towers used by the theorem made explicit. (uses `lem-sfb-integral-closure-rank`)
2. For a chosen integral basis b, the map from the finite product of integer rings to the upper integer ring is the finite sum ∑ a_i b_i. The equality b.equivFun.symm a = ∑ a_i • b_i and continuity of the coefficient inclusion show that this map is continuous; compactness of the source and the closed-map criterion give a homeomorphism, hence continuous coordinate maps. The presheaves of continuous functions and their sheafified ring objects are those supplied by `det-def-sfb-ring-object-presheaf`, in the Grothendieck pro-etale sheaf setting of `lem-sfb-proetale-sheaves-grothendieck`. The module actions used below are transported along lax/strong monoidal functors by `def-sfb-modobj-transport` and `sk-def-sfb-transport`; their relative tensor and symmetry are the coequalizer constructions of `def-sfb-modobj-relative-tensor`, `pur713-def-relative-tensor`, and `lem-sfb-modobj-symmetric-bimodules`, with the abelian, Grothendieck, and finite-limit module infrastructure supplied by `lem-sfb-modobj-abelian`, `lem-sfb-modobj-grothendieck`, and `lem-sfb-modobj-forget-left-exact`. The private continuous_basis_iso then takes the continuous coordinates and basis vectors, forms biproduct lift and desc maps, and proves their inverse identities sectionwise from Module.Basis.sum_repr and coordinate evaluation; its injection formula is precisely finiteCoefficientPointSummand. (uses `det-def-sfb-ring-object-presheaf`, `lem-sfb-proetale-sheaves-grothendieck`, `def-sfb-modobj-transport`, `sk-def-sfb-transport`, `def-sfb-modobj-relative-tensor`, `pur713-def-relative-tensor`, `lem-sfb-modobj-symmetric-bimodules`, `lem-sfb-modobj-abelian`, `lem-sfb-modobj-grothendieck`, `lem-sfb-modobj-forget-left-exact`)
3. The point inverse-image functor is formed from the profinite-to-pro-etale functor of `def-sfb-profinite-to-proetale`, whose continuity is `lem-sfb-profinite-to-proetale-continuous`. Its pushforward description is [dag-def-sfb-finite-lower-star], and the inverse image is left adjoint to it by `def-sfb-finite-upper-lower-star-adjunction`; consequently the inverse image is exact by [dag-lem-sfb-finite-upper-star-exact]. The adjunction therefore preserves the finite coproducts used by the biproduct, by `lem-weil-pbc-left-adjoint-preserves-colimits`, and the resulting functor is additive by `lem-sfb-preserving-colimits-additive`. Apply the private pullback_basis_iso: transport the point isomorphism with `lem-sfb-monoidal-finite-free-transport`, then compose with mapModComapIso and the identity regular-module isomorphism. This yields, for every scheme X, the integral isomorphism and the exact finiteCoefficientIntegralLeg summand formulas. (uses [dag-def-sfb-finite-lower-star], [dag-lem-sfb-finite-upper-star-exact], `def-sfb-profinite-to-proetale`, `lem-sfb-profinite-to-proetale-continuous`, `def-sfb-finite-upper-lower-star-adjunction`, `lem-weil-pbc-left-adjoint-preserves-colimits`, `lem-sfb-preserving-colimits-additive`, `lem-sfb-monoidal-finite-free-transport`)
4. For the same integral basis, use IsIntegralClosure.isLocalization and Mathlib Basis.localizationLocalization to localize the basis from the integer rings to E and F. The resulting rational basis has exactly the same vectors (the private rational_basis proves c i = (b i : F.1)); restriction of each coordinate to Q_ℓ is continuous by finite-dimensional continuity. Applying continuous_basis_iso to the continuousMapRingObj fields and then the same pullback_basis_iso construction, with the transported finite-free module supplied by `lem-sfb-monoidal-finite-free-transport`, gives the rational isomorphism on every X and the prescribed finiteCoefficientRationalLeg formulas. The rank and localization input is the same integral-closure calculation of [dag-lem-sfb-integral-closure-rank]. (uses `lem-sfb-integral-closure-rank`, `lem-sfb-monoidal-finite-free-transport`)
5. The integral-to-rational summand compatibility is checked before applying the point inverse image: both composites reduce, by Functor.map_comp, to the same multiplication-by-b map in the upper field, using the inclusion of integer rings into the localized field. Applying the point pullback preserves this equality. The point inverse-image comparison is the canonical composition isomorphism of [dag-def-sfb-pullback-from-point-comp-iso], whose monoidal/module interpretation is the comparison in [dag-sk-def-sfb-pullback-from-point-comp]. Thus the two displayed summand composites agree for every X and basis index. (uses [dag-def-sfb-pullback-from-point-comp-iso], [dag-sk-def-sfb-pullback-from-point-comp])
6. For inverse-image naturality, rewrite each coefficient-map summand through the corresponding point-basis formula already proved in the integral and rational constructions. The naturality equation for multiplication by a fixed basis vector is exactly the hom component of the point-pullback comparison from [dag-sk-def-sfb-pullback-from-point-comp], with the comparison itself supplied by [dag-def-sfb-pullback-from-point-comp-iso]. The private point_basis_naturality therefore gives both the integral and rational inverse-image squares, and square_of_summand_formulas assembles the stated maps on the coproduct injections. (uses [dag-sk-def-sfb-pullback-from-point-comp], [dag-def-sfb-pullback-from-point-comp-iso])
7. Fix X and an integral isomorphism e from the preceding construction. The private finite_free_flat identifies Mod.extendScalars followed by restriction of scalars with the finite-power functor through Mod.finiteCoordinateIso and its naturality. Finite powers preserve finite limits; the module forgetful functors preserve and reflect the relevant limits using the module-category infrastructure of `lem-sfb-modobj-forget-left-exact` and `lem-sfb-modobj-abelian`. This proves PreservesFiniteLimits for extension along coefficientMap (finiteCoefficientIntegersPointMap ℓ h) at X. For extension along the rational coefficient map, the theorem uses the existing finite-limit-preservation instance recorded by `lem-sfb-subfield-flat`. Combining these clauses for every X completes the asserted flatness conjunction. (uses `lem-sfb-modobj-forget-left-exact`, `lem-sfb-modobj-abelian`, `lem-sfb-subfield-flat`)
