1. Work in the category of module sheaves ringedSiteModules of [dag-sk-def-pbc-conventions]. Write A for the coefficient ring sheaf and F_n = scalarTensorPower A P n. By `def-sp-scalar-tensor-comparison-data` and [dag-def-pur-sheaf-tensor-product], F_0 = A, F_1 = P, and F_{n+2} is the sheafified site tensor F_{n+1} tensor P. The signed-power definition [dag-def-pur-signed-line-scalar-normalization-data] gives P^$[n]$ = F_n and P^[-(n+1)] = Hom_A(F_{n+1}, A), with internal Hom as in [dag-def-pur-sheaf-hom]. Choose the covering family V_i and chart isomorphisms P|_{V_i} ≅ A|_{V_i} from the hypothesis. (uses [dag-def-pur-sheaf-tensor-product], [dag-def-pur-sheaf-hom], `sk-def-pbc-conventions`, `def-sp-scalar-tensor-comparison-data`)
2. Use the canonical comparison siteSheafTensorRightIso of [dag-def-pur-site-sheaf-tensor-presentation-data], supplied by `lem-weil-pbc-independent-sheaf-tensor-colimits`, to identify the explicit sheafified site tensor with the tensor of monoidalCategory `lem-weil-pbc-module-tensor-symmetric-monoidal`, also on each slice. These are comparisons of sheaf tensors and their morphisms: siteTensorSecondMap_naturality `lem-route-site-tensor-second-map-naturality` ensures that the sectionwise maps id tensor g commute with restrictions, and moduleTmul_map `lem-pur-module-tensor-generator-calculus` identifies tensor morphisms on pure sections with applying the two component maps. Thus tensoring chart isomorphisms is compatible with the tensor presentations used below. (uses [dag-def-pur-site-sheaf-tensor-presentation-data], `lem-weil-pbc-independent-sheaf-tensor-colimits`, `lem-weil-pbc-module-tensor-symmetric-monoidal`, `lem-route-site-tensor-second-map-naturality`, `lem-pur-module-tensor-generator-calculus`)
3. On each rank-one chart, tensor restriction and the unit tensor comparison identify every nonnegative power with the coefficient ring. More precisely, fix i and use Mathlib's Nat.twoStepInduction to construct F_n|_{V_i} ≅ A|_{V_i}. At zero this is Iso.refl, since restriction of the unit is the unit; at one it is the given chart. For n+2, `lem-sfb-localized-site-tensor-restriction` first identifies F_{n+2}|_{V_i} with the site tensor of F_{n+1}|_{V_i} and P|_{V_i}. Apply siteSheafTensorRightIso, then Mathlib's MonoidalCategory.tensorIso to the induction isomorphism and the given chart, obtaining the monoidal tensor of two coefficient units. Apply the inverse siteSheafTensorRightIso at the two units and then scalarTensorRightUnitor from `lem-sp-scalar-extension-binary-coherence` to obtain A|_{V_i}. This completes the induction. (uses `lem-sfb-localized-site-tensor-restriction`, `lem-sp-scalar-extension-binary-coherence`)
4. For every n and every object U, take the covering sieve Sieve.ofObjects V U: its arrows W → U are those whose source W admits a map g:W → V_i for some i. It covers by the assumed CoversTop property. Restrict the isomorphism F_n|_{V_i} ≅ A|_{V_i} along g to obtain F_n|_W ≅ A|_W. Concretely, precompose its components and those of its inverse with Over.map g; naturality and the two inverse identities remain valid componentwise. Thus every object has a covering sieve on whose slices F_n is the coefficient unit, exactly the hypothesis required by the evaluation-inverse-pair theorem.
5. Apply LineEvaluation.evaluation_inverse_pair_impl, the evaluation-inverse-pair theorem `lem-pur-sheaf-line-evaluation-inverse-pair`, to F_n and these covering sieves. It gives flatness both of F_n and of its internal dual Hom_A(F_n,A). Indeed, its evaluation isomorphism and the cancellation isomorphisms make tensoring with these two sheaves inverse exact equivalences, and its flatness clauses express this for the site tensor, in the sense of IsFlatOnSite `def-pc-flat-sheaf-modules`. (uses `lem-pur-sheaf-line-evaluation-inverse-pair`)
6. Finally split the integer d into its constructors. If d = ofNat n, then signedLinePower P d is F_n, so take the first flatness clause just obtained. If d = negSucc n, it is Hom_A(F_{n+1},A), so apply the same theorem at n+1 and take its dual-flatness clause. These cases give every signed power, including the degree-zero unit and all internal duals of positive powers.
