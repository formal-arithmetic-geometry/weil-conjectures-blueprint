1. Construct the ordinary unit, product, and evaluation constraints on both sites using signed_line_ordinary_constraints [dag-lem-pur-signed-line-ordinary-constraints]. With the powers of `def-sp-scalar-tensor-comparison-data`, these satisfy OrdinaryLineConstraints [dag-def-pur-signed-line-ordinary-constraint-data]: the unit sends x tensor r to r x, the positive product sends (x tensor y) tensor q to x tensor (y tensor q), and evaluation sends (x tensor phi) tensor y to phi(y)x. Denote these constraints by u_R,p_R,e_R and u_S,p_S,e_S. (uses [dag-def-pur-signed-line-ordinary-constraint-data], [dag-lem-pur-signed-line-ordinary-constraints], `def-sp-scalar-tensor-comparison-data`)
2. Their compatibility with gamma is tested through the module pullback adjunction on pure tensors. Use the adjunction supplied by [dag-lem-pur-ringed-site-module-pullback-exists-universes]; its homEquiv is injective. The pure-section extensionality scalarTensor_hom_ext and the tensor-map formula scalarTensor_first_tmul from `lem-sp-scalar-extension-binary-coherence`, together with tensor_three_ext from the ordinary-constraint construction [dag-lem-pur-signed-line-ordinary-constraints], reduce the three squares to local calculations. Naturality of the adjunction unit gives F(f)(iota(x))=iota(f(x)), and its semilinearity gives iota(r x)=a(r)iota(x). Here iota denotes the pulled section. For the unit square, the two sides on x tensor r are a(r)iota(x), by the degree-zero normalization of T. For the product square, the prescribed formula for gamma turns the two sides on (x tensor y) tensor q into iota(x) tensor T_{n+2}(iota(y tensor q)) and iota(x) tensor (T_{n+1}(iota(y)) tensor T_1(iota(q))); these agree by positive recursion and T_1=t. For the evaluation square, the two sides on (x tensor phi) tensor y are a(phi(y))iota(x) and T_{-(n+1)}(iota(phi))(T_{n+1}(iota(y)))iota(x); the negative evaluation normalization identifies them [dag-def-pur-signed-line-pullback-normalization-data]. Thus the ordinary unit, product, and contraction squares commute. (uses [dag-lem-pur-signed-line-ordinary-constraints], `lem-sp-scalar-extension-binary-coherence`)
3. Write r_d=(- tensor P^$[d]$) and s_d=(- tensor Q^$[d]$). These functors preserve zero morphisms by siteSheafTensorRight_preservesZeroMorphisms `lem-route-site-sheaf-tensor-right-preserves-zero-morphisms`, so they act termwise on unbounded cochain complexes. Lift the ordinary unit and product isomorphisms and gamma with Mathlib's NatIso.mapHomologicalComplex, and the evaluations with NatTrans.mapHomologicalComplex. The three ordinary squares then hold as identities of chain maps: HomologicalComplex.Hom.ext reduces each identity to the corresponding identity on every term. (uses `lem-route-site-sheaf-tensor-right-preserves-zero-morphisms`)
4. Let Q_R,Q_S be the quasi-isomorphism localizations, D^R_d,D^S_d the given derived line functors, and DF=F.mapDerivedCategory. From b_d retain the comparisons c_d: Q_R D^R_d isomorphic to r_d^c Q_R and k_d: Q_S D^S_d isomorphic to s_d^c Q_S, and the same action eta_d=(b_d).2.2; a superscript c means termwise application. Unfolding DerivedAction [dag-def-pur-signed-line-derived-action-data] supplies K-flatness of the single-factor complexes `def-pc-kflat-complex`, flatness of their terms `def-pc-flat-sheaf-modules`, the resolving normalizations of c_d,k_d, and DerivedActionSquare. In particular, with f=F.mapDerivedCategoryFactors, that square identifies eta_d on Q_R(E), after composing with D^S_d(f_E) and k_d(F^c E), with DF(c_d(E)), then f_{r_d^c E}, then Q_S(gamma_d^c(E)). The comparisons are the ones already normalized on K-flat representatives by representativeIso and collapse `def-sf-ringed-kflat-tensor-representative-data`. (uses `def-pc-flat-sheaf-modules`, `def-pc-kflat-complex`)
5. Lift the constraints using Localization.liftNatIso for the units and products and Localization.liftNatTrans for evaluations, conjugating the termwise constraints by c_d on the source and k_d on the target. Localization.liftNatTrans_app and Localization.Lifting.iso give the required application formulas. For example, the source derived unit at Q_R(E) is c_0(E) followed by Q_R(u_R^c(E)); the derived product at Q_R(E), followed by c_{n+2}(E), is D^R_1(c_{n+1}(E)), then c_1(r_{n+1}^c E), then Q_R(p_R^c(E)). The derived evaluation is D^R_{n+1}(c_{-(n+1)}(E)), then c_{n+1}(r_{-(n+1)}^c E), then Q_R(e_R^c(E)). The target formulas use k and Q_S. Together with the ordinary pure-section formulas, these are exactly DerivedLineConstraints for the supplied comparisons [dag-def-pur-signed-line-derived-action-data].
6. To check each derived coherence identity, use Localization.natTrans_ext for Q_R, reducing it to Q_R(E). Substitute the lifted-constraint formulas and DerivedActionSquare from the preceding steps. For the unit and evaluation identities, postcompose with the isomorphism f_E and use cancel_mono; for the product identity, postcompose with D^S_{n+2}(f_E) followed by k_{n+2}(F^c E). Naturality of the target constraint moves f through it; naturality of k, eta, and f, together with Functor.map_comp, then reduces each equality to Q_S applied to the corresponding termwise ordinary square. These squares have already been proved. Localization.natTrans_ext therefore gives the three identities on every derived object. The two families of normalized constraints and these identities assemble by LocalizedLineFamily.mk into DerivedFamilyCoherence(F,P,Q,b) [dag-def-pur-signed-line-derived-action-data], with the original eta family throughout.
