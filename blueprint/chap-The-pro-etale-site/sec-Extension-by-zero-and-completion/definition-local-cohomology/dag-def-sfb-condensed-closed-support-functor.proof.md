1. Fix a condensed ring $\Lambda$ with its monoid-object structure and a scheme $X$. The coefficient morphism package [dag-sk-def-sfb-coefficient-morphisms] supplies the canonical ring map $\alpha_f:f^*\Lambda_Y\to\Lambda_X$ and its mate $\eta_f$, while `def-sfb-alpha` identifies $\alpha_f$ with the component of the pullback-from-the-point comparison. The results [dag-lem-sfb-alpha-iso] and [dag-lem-sfb-alpha-ring-hom] say that this map is an isomorphism of ring objects, so restriction of scalars along $\alpha_f^{-1}$ is available. The module-object transport of `def-sfb-modobj-transport` and `sk-def-sfb-transport` is therefore applicable to the abelian-sheaf functors. (uses [dag-sk-def-sfb-coefficient-morphisms], [dag-lem-sfb-alpha-iso], [dag-lem-sfb-alpha-ring-hom], `def-sfb-alpha`, `def-sfb-modobj-transport`, `sk-def-sfb-transport`)
2. For a morphism $f:X\to Y$, take the abelian-sheaf pullback [dag-def-sfb-finite-upper-star] and apply its module transport `def-sfb-modobj-transport` to the ring object $\Lambda_Y$. Compose with `Mod.comap (asIso (condensedPullbackHom \Lambda f)).inv`; by [dag-lem-sfb-alpha-iso] this is restriction of scalars along $\alpha_f^{-1}$, and [dag-lem-sfb-alpha-ring-hom] makes it a module functor. Thus Lean's `supportPullback` is exactly the canonical module inverse image in the statement. Its additivity and finite-limit behavior come from the transported upper-star functor, using the braided compatibility `lem-sfb-finite-upper-star-braided` and exactness instance [dag-lem-sfb-finite-upper-star-exact]. (uses [dag-def-sfb-finite-upper-star], [dag-lem-sfb-alpha-iso], [dag-lem-sfb-alpha-ring-hom], [dag-lem-sfb-finite-upper-star-exact], `def-sfb-modobj-transport`, `lem-sfb-finite-upper-star-braided`)
3. The direct image used by the unit is the existing module lower-star: `def-sfb-module-lower-star` and `sk-def-sfb-module-pushforward-pullback` describe it as transport of the abelian-sheaf pushforward followed by restriction along $\eta_f$. The direct-image infrastructure recorded in `def-weil-derived-direct-image` has this ordinary lower-star as the input to its right-derived construction in the commutative cases; here only the ordinary lower-star is used, so no derived functor or extra commutativity hypothesis is introduced. The transported upper-star/lower-star adjunction, together with the scalar-transport equivalence supplied by `Mod.comap` and `CategoryTheory.Equivalence.mk`, gives Lean's `supportPullbackAdjunction` via `Adjunction.mapModAdjunction` and the associator isomorphism. (uses `def-sfb-module-lower-star`, `sk-def-sfb-module-pushforward-pullback`, `def-weil-derived-direct-image`)
4. For each $X$, the underlying pro-etale sheaf category is Grothendieck abelian by `lem-sfb-proetale-sheaves-grothendieck`, and the module-object hypotheses in `lem-sfb-modobj-abelian` and `lem-sfb-modobj-grothendieck` make $\operatorname{Mod}_{\Lambda_X}$ an abelian Grothendieck category. Consequently the abbreviation `supportModules \Lambda X := Mod (Sheaf (Scheme.ProEt.topology X) Ab) (condensedRingObj \Lambda X)` has the instance `supportModules_grothendieck`, obtained in Lean by `Mod.isGrothendieckAbelian`. (uses `lem-sfb-proetale-sheaves-grothendieck`)
5. Let $u_j:(\mathord{\mathrm{id}})\to j_*j^*$ be the unit of the module adjunction just constructed. For a module $M$, define `supportKernel \Lambda j` by `kernel ((supportPullbackAdjunction \Lambda j).unit.app M)`, using the module adjunction supplied by `sk-def-sfb-module-pushforward-pullback`. For a map $a:M\to N$, naturality of the unit supplies the commutative square needed by `kernel.map`; hence its map is the unique morphism whose composite with the kernel inclusion is $a$ followed by the inclusion for $M$. The equations for identities and composition are proved by `equalizer.hom_ext`, so this is a functor. The natural inclusion `supportKernelι` is the component `kernel.ι` and its naturality is the corresponding kernel identity. (uses `sk-def-sfb-module-pushforward-pullback`)
6. Define `closedSupport \Lambda i j` as the composite `supportKernel \Lambda j \mathbin{\triangleright} supportPullback \Lambda i`; on objects this is $i^*\ker(M\to j_*j^*M)$. The kernel functor is additive: addition of two induced kernel maps is reduced by `equalizer.hom_ext` to the additivity equation for the original maps, and the inverse-image functor is additive by the transported upper-star construction. Lean closes the composite instance with `inferInstanceAs ((supportKernel \Lambda j \mathbin{\triangleright} supportPullback \Lambda i).Additive)`. Therefore the induced map on every module morphism is exactly the stated $i^*K_j(a)$, and for a closed immersion with complementary open immersion this is the announced closed-support functor. (uses [dag-lem-sfb-finite-upper-star-exact])
