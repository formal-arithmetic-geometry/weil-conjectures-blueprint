1. Using the ring-object and sheafification structure supplied by `det-def-sfb-ring-object-presheaf`, define the natural endomorphism `uniformizerMap` sectionwise by multiplication by `Padic.coefficientExtensionUniformizer l E`, and use `Mod.homMk` together with the tensor-hom extensionality calculation to obtain the regular-module endomorphism `uniformizerEnd`. The section computation is `uniformizerEnd_apply`, so `uniformizer_exists` supplies an endomorphism d with exactly the required pointwise formula. (uses `det-def-sfb-ring-object-presheaf`)
2. For scalar actions on complexes, `action_mul` identifies the action of a product of endomorphisms with the composite actions (in the order used by the Lean definition). Applying `pow_succ` gives `action_succ`: the action of `d ^ (n + 1 + 1)` is the action of d followed by the action of `d ^ (n + 1)`. This is the factorization hypothesis required for the normalized principal-reduction comparison.
3. Apply `PrincipalNormalization.derived_principal_normalization l E X d hd (action_succ l E X d)` from [dag-lem-pe-principal-reduction-derived-normalization]. It produces coherent comm-shift structures `sF` on the canonical reduction functors and isomorphisms κ from the localized scalar-cone functors to those reductions, together with their additivity and `Functor.IsTriangulated` instances, the transition equation, the cone-inclusion/unit equation, and the normalized shift equation. (uses [dag-lem-pe-principal-reduction-derived-normalization])
4. Instantiate the local construction `strict_calculus` with `μ n = DerivedNakayama.scalarOnModules l E X (d ^ (n + 1))`, `a = DerivedNakayama.scalarOnModules l E X d`, the factorization `action_succ`, the reduction functors and the structures from the preceding step. It defines `D n` as the strict cone of the chain action, with object equation by rfl; χ is `principalConeCoordinates`; T is the diagonal cone map induced by `(a, id)`; j is `strictConeUnit` (the cone inclusion); `sD` is `strictConeShift`; and Φ is the four-coordinate `strictInterchange` isomorphism.
5. The coordinate lemmas inside `strict_calculus` compute the cone differential as `(dx + d_n y, -dy)`, identify T with `(id, d)` and j with the first biproduct inclusion, and verify functoriality, tower compatibility, cone inclusions, coaugmentations, and the signed four-coordinate permutation `(b,a,b',a') ↦ (b,b',a,-a')`. The shift structure is built from `CochainComplex.mappingCone.shiftIso` and the sign `r.negOnePow`; `strictConeShift_coordinates` proves its coordinate equation, while the corresponding naturality and addition lemmas prove all coherent shift squares and the boundary equation. The same calculations give Φ's naturality and its compatibility with T and j.
6. For a quasi-isomorphism g, `strictCone_quasiIso` applies `DerivedCategory.isIso_Q_map_iff_quasiIso` and the Mathlib triangle two-out-of-three result `isIso₃_of_isIso₁₂` to the morphism between the two distinguished mapping-cone triangles; the two copies of `DerivedCategory.Q.map g` are isomorphisms, so the cone map is a quasi-isomorphism. This is precisely the triangle argument supplied by `lem-sf-triangle-morphism-iso`. The `strict_calculus` output therefore satisfies the quasi-isomorphism clause as well as every remaining conjunction, and pairing it with d and its section formula proves the stated theorem. (uses `lem-sf-triangle-morphism-iso`)
