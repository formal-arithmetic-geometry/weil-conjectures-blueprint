1. Put $P=\prod_n C_n$ and $Q=\prod_n F(C_n)$, with products formed termwise. This is justified directly by `def-weil-category-product`: in degree $i$ choose the product, define its differential by $\mathrm{pr}_n^{i+1}d_P^i=d_{C_n}^i\mathrm{pr}_n^i$, and check $d_P^{i+1}d_P^i=0$ after every projection. The universal lift of a family of chain maps is a chain map by the same projection test. For each $i$ define $p^i:F(P^i)\to Q^i$ by $\mathrm{pr}'{}^i_n p^i=F(\mathrm{pr}_n^i)$. Applying the same test gives $d_Q^i p^i=p^{i+1}F(d_P^i)$, since both composites with $\mathrm{pr}'{}^{i+1}_n$ are $F(d_{C_n}^i\mathrm{pr}_n^i)$. Thus $p:F(P)\to Q$ is a chain map. This uses no preservation of countable products by $F$. (uses `def-weil-category-product`, `def-weil-pbc-additive-functor`)
2. Let $b=1-s_C$ and $b'=1-s_{FC}$ as in `def-weil-tower-transition-endomorphism`, with tower step $t_n:C_{n+1}\to C_n$. In each degree, the $n$th projection of $pF(b)$ is $F(\mathrm{pr}_n b)=F(\mathrm{pr}_n-t_n\mathrm{pr}_{n+1})=F(\mathrm{pr}_n)-F(t_n)F(\mathrm{pr}_{n+1})$. This is also the $n$th projection of $b'p$, by the defining equations for $b'$ and $p$. Additivity and the product universal property give $pF(b)=b'p$. (uses `def-weil-tower-transition-endomorphism`, `def-weil-category-product`, `def-weil-pbc-additive-functor`)
3. Use [dag-def-sf-strict-roos-functor] and [dag-def-weil-signed-mapping-cocone-functor]. In ordered coordinates $(y,z)=(\operatorname{snd},\operatorname{fst})$, the signed fibre of $b:P\to P$ has degree $i$ object $P^{i-1}\oplus P^i$ and differential $(y,z)\mapsto(-d_Py-bz,d_Pz)$, obtained by shifting the cone matrix by $[-1]$. By `lem-pe-functor-finite-matrix-coordinates`, the canonical binary biproduct comparison identifies $F$ of this object with $F(P^{i-1})\oplus F(P^i)$ and carries its differential to $(-F(d_P)y-F(b)z,F(d_P)z)$. The diagonal map $(p^{i-1},p^i)$ to $Q^{i-1}\oplus Q^i$ is a chain map: its first differential coordinate is $-p^iF(d_P)y-p^iF(b)z=-d_Qp^{i-1}y-b'p^iz$, and its second is $p^{i+1}F(d_P)z=d_Qp^iz$, by steps 1 and 2. Equivalently, first apply the additive comparison to the source cocone and then the signed cocone map of the commuting square $(p,p)$. Denote the composite by $\alpha$. (uses [dag-def-sf-strict-roos-functor], [dag-def-weil-signed-mapping-cocone-functor], `lem-pe-functor-finite-matrix-coordinates`)
4. The two degreewise coordinates of $\alpha$ are $p^iF(\operatorname{fst}^i)$ and $p^{i-1}F(\operatorname{snd}^i)$, respectively. Composing the first with $\mathrm{pr}'{}^i_n$ gives $F(\mathrm{pr}_n^i)F(\operatorname{fst}^i)=F(\mathrm{pr}_n^i\operatorname{fst}^i)$; composing the second with $\mathrm{pr}'{}^{i-1}_n$ gives $F(\mathrm{pr}_n^{i-1}\operatorname{snd}^i)$. The first equations assemble as chain maps, since $\operatorname{fst}$ and the projections are chain maps. The second equations are degree $-1$ cochain coordinate equations only; no assertion that $\operatorname{snd}$ is a chain map is used. These are exactly both prescriptions in the statement. (uses [dag-def-weil-signed-mapping-cocone-functor], `def-weil-category-product`)
5. If $\beta$ satisfies both prescriptions, then in every degree its composites with the unshifted and shifted cocone coordinates agree with those of $\alpha$: apply the uniqueness clause of `def-weil-category-product` separately to $Q^i$ and $Q^{i-1}$. The two binary biproduct projections jointly determine a map: if $u,v:T\to U\oplus V$ have equal projections then $u=\iota_U\pi_Uu+\iota_V\pi_Vu=\iota_U\pi_Uv+\iota_V\pi_Vv=v$. Consequently $\beta^i=\alpha^i$ for every $i$, hence $\beta=\alpha$. Define $\kappa_{F,C}$ to be this unique map (the existing Lean name is scalarRoosMap). Its data are determined by these two coordinate equations. No completion of a triangle is selected. (uses `def-weil-category-product`, `def-sp-abelian`)
