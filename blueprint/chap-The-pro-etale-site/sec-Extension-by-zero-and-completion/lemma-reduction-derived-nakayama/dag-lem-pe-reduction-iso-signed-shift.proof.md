1. Work with the already fixed cochain localization Q and its lax-monoidal derived tensor comparison. The K-flat representative machinery behind this fixed model is the functorial objectwise resolution of `lem-sfb-left-resolution-objectwise-cochain`, its single-row total identification `lem-sfb-left-resolution-single-row-total`, and the comparison of applying an additive functor with totalizing after applying it `lem-sfb-left-resolution-map-total-iso`; the bounded-above double-complex comparison `lem-pc-bounded-above-double-complex-total-comparison` makes the corresponding total quasi-isomorphisms available. The bounded-above flat criterion `lem-sfb-bounded-above-flat-kflat`, together with the flat and K-flat notions `def-pc-flat-sheaf-modules`, `def-pc-kflat-complex`, and `def-pc-module-kflat-complex`, supplies the resolving representatives. Thus the derived tensor and its shifts are the canonical ones, independent of the chosen models by `lem-kun-derived-tensor-well-defined` and `lem-sc-derived-tensor-shift-independent-universe`, with the total-tensor and homotopy constructions governed by `lem-sf-total-bifunctor-homotopy-exact`, `lem-sfb-total-tensor-homotopy`, `lem-sfb-sequential-kflat-derivability`, and `lem-sfb-unary-left-derived-calculus`. We use these in the ambient monoidal, Grothendieck, cochain, and triangulated settings `def-sfb-monoidal-category`, `def-sp-grothendieck-abelian`, `def-weil-abstract-derived-category`, `def-weil-triangulated-category`, with the derived monoidal structure `lem-sfb-derived-monoidal` and the derived tensor/hom conventions `sk-def-sfb-derived-tensor-hom`. 2. At the cochain level expand the inverse of the canonical tensor-scalar-cone comparison (the cone/tensor comparison is the one supplied by `lem-pc-tensor-mapping-cone-iso`). Its composite with the cone inclusion inr is the right-unitor inverse followed by tensoring the coefficient inclusion, while its inl component is the right-unitor inverse followed by the signed total-tensor inclusion ι₁; these are the two component calculations `inv_inr` and `inv_inl`. For the shifted complex M$[t]$, `iota_shift` computes the ι₁ component on a (p,q)-summand. The total-tensor sign changes by (-1)^t; `Int.negOnePow_add` and `Int.units_mul_self` reduce the two signs, and `rightUnitor_inv_mapBifunctorShift₁Iso_hom` together with the shift naturality of the tensor map identifies the unitor terms. Extensionality by `mappingCone.ext_from` therefore proves the chain equation `chain_inv_shift`: the inverse mapping-cone shift followed by the shifted tensor-scalar-cone inverse equals the tensor-scalar-cone inverse for M$[t]$ followed by the signed tensor shift.
3. Let K be the scalar cone. Its K-flatness is the previously established `kFlat_scalarCone`, so the right-tensor counit has the shift compatibility `commShift_tensorRightCounit` (the derived monoidal comparison is the one fixed by `lem-sfb-derived-monoidal`). In the proof of `mu_shift`, factor the natural transformation from tensoring with Q(K) to tensoring with Q of the coefficient object through the homotopy quotient: use `quotientCompQhIso`, the quotient/tensor factorization isomorphism, associators, and `tensorRightCounit`. Call this factorization τ. The two outer factors have `NatTrans.CommShift`, and `commShift_tensorRightCounit` gives the middle factor, hence τ has `NatTrans.CommShift`. Its component is exactly `Functor.LaxMonoidal.μ Q` by the displayed component computation `heq`; applying `NatTrans.shift_app_comm τ t M` gives the shift equation `mu_shift` for μ, with the K-flat derived-tensor interpretation supplied by `sk-def-sfb-derived-tensor-hom`.
4. Apply Q.map to `chain_inv_shift` and expand `tensorScalarConeIso` as the shifted lax-monoidal comparison followed by Q.map of the cochain tensor-scalar-cone isomorphism. Naturality of `Q.commShiftIso`, functoriality of Q, and the equation `mu_shift` move the chain-level square through localization. Since the scalar-cone μ component is invertible by `isIso_μ_scalarCone` (using `kFlat_scalarCone`), cancel its shifted component with `cancel_mono`; `Functor.map_comp`, `IsIso.inv_hom_id`, and the shift naturality identities leave exactly the tensor-level equation `tensor_inv_shift`.
5. Finally use the short exact sequence hypothesis. The cone augmentation `scalarConeDesc w` becomes an isomorphism after Q by `isIso_Q_map_scalarConeDesc w hS`; write a = Q.map(scalarConeDesc w) and let τ be its image under the flipped curried tensor, from tensoring with Q of the scalar cone to tensoring with Q of the coefficient object. Unfold the inverse of `reductionIso`: it is the inverse tensor-scalar-cone comparison followed by whiskering with a. The equation `tensor_inv_shift` rewrites the shifted tensor-scalar-cone part, and `NatTrans.shift_app_comm τ t (Q.obj M)` rewrites the coefficient part. After reassociation, cancel the epimorphism `(tensorScalarConeIso (M⟦t⟧) b).inv` and then the monomorphism given by the target right-tensor shift isomorphism; the remaining equality is `whisker_exchange` (in the symmetric direction). This is precisely the displayed equation for `(reductionIso w hS M).inv⟦t⟧'` and hence proves the claimed signed shift compatibility for every M and t.
