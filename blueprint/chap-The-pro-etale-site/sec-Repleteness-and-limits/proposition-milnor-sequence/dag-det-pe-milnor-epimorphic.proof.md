1. Fix an affine weakly contractible object $W$. By [dag-det-pe-sections-limits-colimits], evaluation $\Gamma(W,-)$ on $\mathcal R$-modules preserves all limits and colimits. In particular $P(W)=\prod_n M_n(W)$ and it preserves cokernels and the zero object. Since $t_n$ is an epimorphism, $\coker t_n=0$, hence $\coker(t_n(W))=(\coker t_n)(W)=0$. A module homomorphism has zero cokernel exactly when its image is its target (its cokernel is the quotient by its image). Thus every $t_n(W)$ is surjective. This uses the module-valued evaluation statement, without assuming that an arbitrary epimorphism of sheaves is surjective on arbitrary objects. (uses [dag-det-pe-sections-limits-colimits])
2. For an arbitrary $a=(a_n)\in\prod_n M_n(W)$, choose, using the surjectivity in step 1, a set-theoretic right inverse $s_n\colon M_n(W)\to M_{n+1}(W)$ of each $t_n(W)$. Define $m_0=0$ and $m_{n+1}=s_n(m_n-a_n)$ recursively. The induction is well-defined: if $m_n\in M_n(W)$, then $m_n-a_n\in M_n(W)$ and $m_{n+1}\in M_{n+1}(W)$. For every $n\geq0$ one has $t_n(W)(m_{n+1})=m_n-a_n$, so $((1-T)(W)(m))_n=m_n-t_n(W)(m_{n+1})=a_n$. Therefore $(1-T)(W)$ is surjective. The choices are made separately for each $W$; no compatible choices with respect to restrictions, and no splitting in the sheaf category, are asserted.
3. For every affine weakly contractible $W$, step 2 says that $P(W)\xrightarrow{(1-T)(W)}P(W)\to0$ is exact at the second term. Apply assertion 1 of [dag-det-pe-sections-conservative] to the sequence $P\xrightarrow{1-T}P\to0$, whose composite is zero. It is exact at the second term as a sequence of module sheaves. Thus the image of $1-T$ is all of $P$, or equivalently its cokernel is zero, and $1-T$ is an epimorphism. (uses [dag-det-pe-sections-conservative])
4. Apply assertion 2 of `det-pe-milnor-sequence` to the given tower of modules concentrated in degree zero. It identifies $R^1\varprojlim_n M_n$ with $\coker(1-T)$, which vanishes by step 3, identifies $H^0(R\varprojlim_n M_n)$ with $\varprojlim_nM_n$, and gives zero cohomology in every degree outside $\{0,1\}$. In particular the derived limit has cohomology only in degree zero. (uses `det-pe-milnor-sequence`)
5. By the sectionwise description in [dag-def-pur-sheaf-inverse-limit], a section of $L=\varprojlim_nM_n$ is a tuple $(m_n)$ with $m_n=t_n(m_{n+1})$, exactly a section of $\ker(1-T)$. The differential of $L[0]$ is zero. Products of the complexes $M_n[0]$ have $P$ in degree zero and zero in other degrees, so the same tuple of projections identifies $L[0]$ with the termwise kernel of $1-T:P[0]\to P[0]$. This morphism is epic in degree zero by step 3 and is the identity of the zero object in every other degree. Apply `lem-weil-degreewise-epi-kernel-cocone-qis` to obtain the specified quasi-isomorphism $\kappa:L[0]\to\operatorname{Cone}(1-T)[-1]$, with degree-$p$ components $(0,\iota^p)$ in the summands $P^{p-1}\oplus P^p$. The cone convention is that of `det-pe-milnor-sequence`. Thus $\kappa$ is precisely the canonical comparison formed from the actual inverse-limit projections and zero in the shifted summand. Localization at quasi-isomorphisms `def-weil-abstract-derived-category` makes this particular comparison an isomorphism. (uses `def-pur-sheaf-inverse-limit`, `lem-weil-degreewise-epi-kernel-cocone-qis`, `det-pe-milnor-sequence`, `def-weil-abstract-derived-category`)
