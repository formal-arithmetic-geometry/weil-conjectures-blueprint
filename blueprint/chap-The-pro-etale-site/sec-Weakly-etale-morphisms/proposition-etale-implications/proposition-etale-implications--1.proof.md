The constant system on $X$ exhibits $f$ as a pro-étale affine over
$Y$. Apply this to $\operatorname{Spec}(B)\to\operatorname{Spec}(R)$
and use [definition-ind-etale-pro-etale-weakly] to obtain the
ring-map assertion.

Now write an ind-étale $R$-algebra as
$B=\varinjlim_\alpha B_\alpha$, with each $B_\alpha$ étale over
$R$. Since the $B_\alpha$ are flat over $R$, their filtered colimit
$B$ is flat over $R$.

Put $I=\ker(B\otimes_RB\to B)$. An element $x\in I$ comes from
some $B_\alpha\otimes_RB_\alpha$. Its product vanishes at a later
stage, so after increasing $\alpha$ it comes from an element of
$I_\alpha=\ker(B_\alpha\otimes_RB_\alpha\to B_\alpha)$.
Since $B_\alpha$ is étale, $I_\alpha$ is finitely generated and
$I_\alpha=I_\alpha^2$, hence is generated by an idempotent. Its
image gives an idempotent $e\in I$ with $xe=x$.

It follows that $I$ is pure. Indeed, for every finitely generated
ideal $J$ of $B\otimes_RB$ and every $x\in I\cap J$, the equality
$x=ex$ gives $x\in IJ$; thus $I\cap J=IJ$. The multiplication map
is surjective, and a surjective ring map with pure kernel is flat.
Together with flatness over $R$, this proves that $R\to B$ is
weakly étale.
