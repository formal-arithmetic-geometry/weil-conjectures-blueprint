For instance, $\mathbf F_q\to\overline{\mathbf F}_q$ is ind-étale, as
the colimit of the finite fields $\mathbf F_{q^m}$, but it is not
étale, because $\overline{\mathbf F}_q$ is not of finite type over
$\mathbf F_q$.

In (2) the flatness of the diagonal takes the place of unramifiedness;
flatness of $R\to B$ alone would not suffice. The converse of the second
implication in (1) fails in general: a weakly étale map need not be a
filtered colimit of étale maps. It does become ind-étale after a
faithfully flat refinement
(Theorem [theorem-pro-etale-refinements-weakly-etale] below, and
[Stacks, [097Z](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/097Z)]). Geometrically, a weakly étale scheme over $X$ is covered,
in the pro-étale topology, by a limit of étale $X$-schemes.
