1. Put m = max(u+1,r,v). The pro-etale site is essentially small in m: its objects are m-small, and its hom types are m-small by UnivLE.trans. Evaluation of module sheaves preserves m-small limits sectionwise, by SheafOfModules.evaluationPreservesLimitsOfSize. In particular it preserves kernels. Evaluation is additive since addition of morphisms is sectionwise. (uses `lem-weil-pbc-module-sheaves-limits-sectionwise`)
2. For an m-small diagram D of module sheaves and a colimit cocone c, form the presheaf colimit P. The sheafification adjunction identifies the sheafification of P with c.pt: sheafification preserves the colimit, and its invertible counit identifies the sheafified underlying diagram with D. The triangle identity and uniqueness on colimit inclusions identify the canonical comparison P -> c.pt.val with the adjunction unit followed by this isomorphism. After forgetting to abelian presheaves, the unit is locally surjective by the WEqualsLocallyBijective sheafification instance. Thus the canonical comparison is locally surjective. This is the private colimit_comparison_locally_surjective lemma. (uses `lem-weil-pbc-presheaf-modules-colimits-sectionwise`, `def-weil-presheaf-modules-sheafification`, `lem-pc-sites-sheafification-underlying-sets`)
3. Express a coproduct as the filtered colimit of its finite subcoproducts, using CoproductsFromFiniteFiltered.isColimitFiniteSubproductsCocone. The comparison of step 2 gives local presheaf representatives of a section over W. Apply the finite-cover-refinement theorem to its image sieve. Presheaf evaluation followed by forget to abelian groups and then types preserves this filtered colimit, so each representative comes from a finite subcoproduct. Take the union of these supports over the finite covering family. The coordinate projection onto this union followed by inclusion fixes the section on each covering member; naturality and separatedness show that it fixes the original section. Thus every section over W comes from a finite subcoproduct. Empty covering families and empty supports are allowed. This is pe_coproduct_section_finite_support. (uses `lem-weil-pbc-proetale-affine-finite-cover-refinement`, `lem-weil-pbc-presheaf-modules-colimits-sectionwise`)
4. Additivity and finite-limit preservation make evaluation preserve finite coproducts. Hence every finite sigmaComparison is invertible. Step 3 then proves surjectivity of the arbitrary sigmaComparison. For injectivity, represent an element of its kernel in a finite module subcoproduct, using the filtered-colimit presentation and joint surjectivity after forgetting through abelian groups. The corresponding finite sheaf subcoproduct has the explicit coordinate projection as a retraction of its inclusion. Apply evaluation of this retraction to the vanishing comparison. Invertibility of the finite comparison forces the finite representative, hence the original element, to be zero. ConcreteCategory.isIso_iff_bijective and PreservesCoproduct.of_iso_comparison give preservation of all m-small coproducts. These are pe_evaluation_coproduct_isIso and pe_evaluation_preserves_coproducts.
5. The forgetful functor from module sheaves to abelian sheaves preserves finite colimits, hence epimorphisms. By the abelian-sheaf epi criterion its image is locally surjective, and forgetting to type-valued sheaves preserves local surjectivity. Apply the defining weak-contractibility hypothesis hW to get surjectivity on W-sections. The module epi criterion shows that evaluation preserves epimorphisms. Together with sectionwise kernels, Functor.preservesHomology_of_preservesEpis_and_kernels proves exactness, and Functor.preservesFiniteColimits_of_preservesHomology gives finite-colimit preservation, including cokernels and coequalizers. No commutativity assumption on the coefficient ring is used. (uses `lem-weil-pbc-site-abelian-sheaf-epi-locally-surjective`, `lem-weil-pbc-module-sheaves-limits-sectionwise`)
6. Apply preservesColimits_of_preservesCoequalizers_and_coproducts to steps 4 and 5. Its coproduct/coequalizer presentation is the blueprint presentation by objects and arrows; its canonical cocone gives preservation of all m-small colimits, including the empty diagram and filtered diagrams.
7. To retain the statement's independent diagram universes, use isGrothendieckAbelianOfUniverses for arbitrary ring sheaves. Shrink only the hom types of the module-sheaf category to m with ShrinkHoms.equivalence, and compose evaluation with its inverse. The resulting functor still preserves m-small limits and colimits. A Grothendieck abelian category has a generator and a cogenerator, is well-powered and co-well-powered, and has small limits and colimits. Mathlib's special adjoint functor theorem therefore gives both adjoints to this functor: apply isRightAdjoint_of_preservesLimits_of_isCoseparating to isCoseparator_coseparator, and isLeftAdjoint_of_preservesColimits_of_isSeparating to isSeparator_separator. All these hypotheses are supplied by the existing Grothendieck-category instances. (uses `lem-weil-pbc-ring-sheaves-grothendieck`)
8. A left adjoint preserves every existing colimit, and a right adjoint preserves every existing limit, of any diagram size. The equivalence functor has both properties too. The equivalence unit, whiskered by evaluation, identifies the composite of the equivalence functor with the transported evaluation with the original evaluation. Apply preservesLimits_of_natIso and preservesColimits_of_natIso to this explicit natural isomorphism. This proves the conjunction at the original, unrestricted diagram universes, without changing the ring universe, module universe, hypothesis, or functor.
