1. For a continuous finite-limit-preserving functor between small sites, restriction to a slice commutes with direct image by the identity on sections. Take its mate under the restriction/star adjunctions. At an object it is the image of the product comparison isomorphism, hence invertible. Iterated mates give invertibility of the pullback/restriction mate. The mate counit identity then reduces invertibility of the restricted global counit to that of the counit on the slice. Apply this directly to the AsSmall coefficient-sized comparison inclusion; its finite-limit preservation is transported from the original inclusion through the two equivalences.
2. Give M the discrete topology. Continuous functions into M form a module presheaf on schemes whose underlying presheaf is an fpqc sheaf, hence a pro-etale sheaf. On both the etale and pro-etale sites the map from the constant presheaf sending a section to its constant function is locally bijective. Local surjectivity follows from the cover by open fibres, indexed by points of the scheme so no coefficient-size bound is needed. For local injectivity, either the two values agree or equality of their constant functions forces the scheme to be empty, where the empty covering applies. Cocontinuity of AsSmall.down and of slice forgetful functors transports these local bijections to the coefficient-sized slice sites. The sheafification maps are locally bijective, so their universal factorizations identify both constant sheaves with the same continuous-function sheaf and prove the canonical constant direct-image map invertible. (uses [dag-def-weil-pbc-constant-sheaf])
3. The adjoint of this constant direct-image map is invertible: compose the constant-sheaf/evaluation adjunction with comparison, identify evaluation at the image of the terminal object, and use uniqueness of left adjoints. The explicit sheafification unit equation identifies the resulting isomorphism with the adjoint of the canonical direct-image map. Since that adjoint is the comparison pullback of the direct-image map followed by the counit, and both the adjoint and the direct-image map are invertible, the counit on constant M is invertible. (uses `lem-weil-pbc-constant-sheaf-maps-global-sections`)
4. Naturality of the slice counit transports its invertibility from constant M across the supplied chart isomorphism. The localization calculation gives the displayed restriction of the global counit. The empty chart and zero module are included throughout.
