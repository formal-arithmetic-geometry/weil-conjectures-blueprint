1. For finite discrete R, a continuous map from a profinite set to R is constant on a finite clopen partition. Thus the sheafified constant ring on profinite sets is the discrete condensed ring, naturally in R; multiplication and units agree pointwise. Pull this ring identification to T using the canonical constant-sheaf pullback comparison and the monoidal ring transport. This identifies p_T^* a with the constant ring map a, so every coefficient in the argument is p_T^* of a condensed coefficient. (uses `def-sfb-constant-sheaf-scheme-pullback-comparison`, `lem-weil-pbc-finite-discrete-scheme-ring-constant`, `det-def-sfb-pulled-back-coefficients`)
2. First work with sheaves. The scalar square for comparison inverse image has identity underlying components: both actions are the pullback of the B-action precomposed with a. This is the common-composite comparison for mapMod. For open extension the projection-formula restriction comparison also has identity underlying components; the coefficient pullback square gives c^p. On the etale site the explicit summand/sheafification comparison gives c^e. Its unit equation, and the same identity summand generators on the pro-etale site, identify these comparisons with the specified open-unit normalization. (uses `def-sfb-comap-mapmod-square`, `def-sfb-projection-formula-comap`, `def-sfb-comap-open-immersion-shriek-iso`, `lem-cbc-ringed-open-scalar-adjunction`, `det-def-sfb-module-lower-shriek`)
3. Construct b_R from the abelian site/open adjunction comparison: both right adjoints evaluate an etale test W over X to the same M(W), so their comparison is the identity. Taking its left-adjoint conjugate gives the canonical b. Naturality for multiplication by r makes this map R-linear, and locally constant scalars follow by sheaf locality. Thus forgetting coefficients sends b_A and b_B to this same abelian comparison. In the displayed equation all other comparisons become identities, so both paths become b on the underlying abelian sheaf of L. Module-sheaf maps are determined by their underlying maps; hence the sheaf equation holds. (uses [dag-lem-cbc-proetale-extension-zero-comparison], `det-def-sfb-module-lower-shriek`, `def-sfb-modobj-restriction`)
