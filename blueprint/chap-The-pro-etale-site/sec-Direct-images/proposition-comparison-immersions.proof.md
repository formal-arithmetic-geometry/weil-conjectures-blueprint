For (1), both functors are left adjoint to restriction followed by
$\nu_{U,*}$, and this also identifies the adjunction maps
[Stacks, [09AD](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/09AD)]. As the functors are exact, the identity passes to derived
categories. In (2), the first identity is
Theorem [theorem-comparison-direct-images], and the second is the
comparison of inverse images. Since $\nu_X^*$ is exact, it carries the
localization triangle to a distinguished triangle. Its first map is
carried to the counit on $X_{\mathrm{pro}\acute{e}t}$ because the isomorphism of (1)
identifies the adjunction maps, and its second map to the unit by
Lemma [lemma-comparison-direct-images-compatibilities](1), applied to
$g=i$ and $L=K$.
Part (3) follows from (1) and Theorem [theorem-comparison-direct-images]
for the proper, hence quasi-compact and quasi-separated, map $\bar f$.
