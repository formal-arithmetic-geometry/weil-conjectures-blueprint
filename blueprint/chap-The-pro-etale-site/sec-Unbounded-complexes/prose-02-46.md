Over $\overline{\mathbf F}_q$, for example, a variety of dimension $n$
has $\ell$-cohomological dimension at most $2n$. The curve $\mathbf P^1$
attains this bound, since $H^2(\mathbf P^1,\mathbf Z/\ell)\neq0$. Over
$\mathbf F_q$ one more degree is possible. For $\ell\neq p$ the
Hochschild–Serre spectral sequence gives
$H^3(\mathbf P^1_{\mathbf F_q},\mu_\ell)\simeq
H^1(\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf F_q,H^2(\mathbf P^1_{\overline{\mathbf F}_q},\mu_\ell))
=H^1(\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf F_q,\mathbf Z/\ell)=\mathbf Z/\ell$.

To extend the comparison theorems to unbounded complexes with these
bounds, we need two facts. First, the bounds hold uniformly over the affine
objects on which we take sections
(Lemma [lemma-uniform-bounds-pro-etale-affines]). Second, on the
pro-étale side a cohomology group of an unbounded complex in a fixed
degree only sees a bounded-below truncation
(Lemma [lemma-truncation-pro-etale-sections]).
