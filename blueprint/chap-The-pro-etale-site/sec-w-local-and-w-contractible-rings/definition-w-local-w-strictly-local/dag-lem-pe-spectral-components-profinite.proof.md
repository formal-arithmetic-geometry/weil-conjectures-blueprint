1. Write $P=\pi_0(T)$ and let $q:T\to P$ send a point to its connected component. It is surjective, and the quotient topology means that $W\subseteq P$ is open exactly when $q^{-1}(W)$ is open. By `def-weil-spectral-space`, $T$ is quasi-compact. For any open cover $(W_i)$ of $P$, the inverse images $q^{-1}(W_i)$ cover $T$. Choose finitely many covering inverse images; surjectivity of $q$ shows that the corresponding $W_i$ cover $P$. Thus $P$ is quasi-compact.
2. Let $a\ne b$ in $P$, and choose representatives $x,y\in T$. Their connected components differ, so $y$ is not in the component of $x$. By `def-weil-spectral-space`, $T$ is quasi-compact, has a basis of quasi-compact open subsets, and intersections of two quasi-compact opens are quasi-compact. Thus all three hypotheses of `lem-weil-pbc-quasicompact-components-clopen-intersection` hold. Its intersection formula therefore gives a clopen subset $U\subseteq T$ with $x\in U$ and $y\notin U$. Every connected component meeting $U$ lies in $U$: otherwise its intersections with $U$ and $T\setminus U$ would be nonempty disjoint relatively open sets covering that connected component, contrary to connectedness. Consequently $U$ is saturated for $q$, so $q^{-1}(q(U))=U$ and $q^{-1}(P\setminus q(U))=T\setminus U$. By the quotient topology, $q(U)$ and its complement are open. They contain $a$ and $b$ respectively and are disjoint. Thus $P$ is Hausdorff; in fact distinct points are separated by complementary clopen subsets. (uses `lem-weil-pbc-quasicompact-components-clopen-intersection`)
3. If a connected subset $A\subseteq P$ contained distinct points $a,b$, the complementary clopen sets separating them in step 2 would intersect $A$ in two nonempty disjoint relatively open subsets covering $A$, a contradiction. Every connected subset therefore has at most one point, so $P$ is totally disconnected. Together with steps 1 and 2, $P$ is quasi-compact, Hausdorff and totally disconnected, which is precisely a compact Hausdorff totally disconnected, or profinite, space.
