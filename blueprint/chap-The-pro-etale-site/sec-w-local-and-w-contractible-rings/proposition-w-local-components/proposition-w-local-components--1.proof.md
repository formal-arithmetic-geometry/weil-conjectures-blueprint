The closed subspace $T^c$ is spectral and $T_1$, hence profinite,
and $\pi_0(T)$ is Hausdorff. The natural map is continuous. It is
surjective because every point has a closed specialization in its
connected component.

For injectivity, separate two distinct closed points by a clopen
subset $U_0$ of $T^c$. Let $U$ and $V$ be the sets of generalizations
of points in $U_0$ and $T^c\setminus U_0$, respectively. In a w-local
space the generalizations of a closed subset form a closed subset,
so $U$ and $V$ are closed. Every point specializes to a closed point,
and this closed point is unique by
[definition-w-local-w-strictly-local--2]. Thus $U$ and $V$ are
disjoint and cover $T$, making them clopen. The two chosen closed
points cannot lie in the same connected component. The natural map
is therefore a continuous bijection from a compact space to a
Hausdorff space, and hence a homeomorphism.
