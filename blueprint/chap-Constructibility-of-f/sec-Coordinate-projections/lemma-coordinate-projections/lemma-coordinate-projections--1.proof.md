The projection $\mathbf A^1_S\to S$ sends $\eta'$ to $\eta$, so the
underlying space of $t^{-1}(\eta')$ is a subspace of $X_{k(S)}$.
The latter is locally of finite type over a field, hence Jacobson.
For each point $x$ of $t^{-1}(\eta')$, choose a closed point $y$ in
its closure in $X_{k(S)}$. The induced morphism
$X_{k(S)}\to\mathbf A^1_{k(S)}$ is locally of finite type and sends
closed points to closed points. Thus $y$ does not lie over $\eta'$:
the generic point of the affine line over $k(S)$ is not closed.

Taking closures in $X_{k(S)}$ gives a strictly increasing map on
irreducible closed subsets of $t^{-1}(\eta')$. Every nonempty chain
of these subsets can be extended by one step at the bottom: choose $x$
in its smallest member, and place the closure of a specialization $y$
as above below its closure in $X_{k(S)}$. This inclusion is strict
because $y$ lies outside $t^{-1}(\eta')$ and the spaces are $T_0$.
Taking the supremum of the lengths of the chains proves the inequality.
If the fibre is empty, it follows from $\bot+1=\bot$.
