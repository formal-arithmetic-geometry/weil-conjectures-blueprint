Part (2) generalizes $j^!=j^*$ from open immersions to étale maps.
For $d=1$, $k$ algebraically closed and $S=\operatorname{Spec}k$, part (1) says that
$\Hom(R\Gamma_c(\mathbf A^1,F),M)=\Hom(F,M_{\mathbf A^1}(1)[2])$. For
$F=\Lambda$ this is consistent with the example above. Since
$R\Gamma_c(\mathbf A^1_{\bar k},\Lambda)\simeq\Lambda(-1)[-2]$, the left
side is $\Hom(\Lambda,M(1)[2])$. Since
$R\Gamma(\mathbf A^1_{\bar k},\Lambda)=\Lambda$, so is the right side.
