The map $c_D$ will be the boundary map of the extension of
$i_*\mathbf Z_D$ by $\mathbf G_m$ formed by meromorphic functions
with poles along $D$, reduced modulo $\ell^r$; this extension is the étale
version of the divisor sequence $0\to\mathcal O_Z\to\mathcal O_Z(D)\to
\mathcal O_Z(D)|_D\to0$.

We construct $c_D$ in $D(Z_{\acute{e}t},\Lambda)$. The functor $\nu_Z^*$, which
commutes with inverse images and with change of coefficients, then
transports it and its compatibilities to $Z_{\mathrm{pro}\acute{e}t}$.

Let $j:Z\setminus D\hookrightarrow Z$ be the open complement. Let
$\mathcal M_D\subset j_*\mathbf G_m$ be the subsheaf which, on an open
where $D$ has a regular equation $t$, consists of the products
$ut^{-m}$ with $u$ a unit and $m$ a locally constant integer on
$D$; equivalently, it is generated by $\mathbf G_m$ and $t^{-1}$.
Since replacing $t$ by a unit multiple gives the same subsheaf,
$\mathcal M_D$ is globally defined. Sending $t^{-1}$ to $1$, we obtain
a short exact sequence of abelian sheaves on $Z_{\acute{e}t}$

$$
\Sigma_D:\ 0\longrightarrow\mathbf G_m\longrightarrow \mathcal M_D
\longrightarrow i_*\mathbf Z_D\longrightarrow0,
\tag{6.1}
$$

whose exactness can be checked on geometric stalks. Away from $D$, the function $t$ is a unit, so there $\mathcal M_D=\mathbf G_m$, and both the
quotient and the stalk of $i_*\mathbf Z_D$ are zero. At a point of $D$, on the other hand, no
nonzero power of a regular nonunit $t$ is a unit, and therefore the
exponent $m$ in $ut^{-m}$ is well defined. The kernel of the map $ut^{-m}\mapsto m$ is then $\mathbf G_m$, and the map is surjective because exponents lift locally to
powers of $t^{-1}$. For example, for
$D=\{0\}\subset Z=\mathbf A^1=\operatorname{Spec}k[t]$, the sections of
$\mathcal M_D$ near the origin are the functions $ut^{-m}$, that is, the
rational functions whose only zero or pole is at $0$, and the map
records the order of the pole, $m=-\operatorname{ord}_0$.

The boundary of $\Sigma_D$ is a morphism $i_*\mathbf Z_D\to\mathbf G_m[1]$
in $D(Z_{\acute{e}t},\mathbf Z)$, to which
we apply $-\otimes^{\mathbf L}_{\mathbf Z}\mathbf Z/\ell^r\mathbf Z$. The
source becomes $i_*\mathbf Z/\ell^r\mathbf Z$. For the target, the
Kummer sequence [Stacks, [03PL](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/03PL)] shows that the cone of multiplication by
$\ell^r$ on $\mathbf G_m$ has the single cohomology sheaf
$\mu_{\ell^r}$ in degree $-1$, and hence the target becomes
$\mu_{\ell^r}[2]$. This is $c_D$ for $\mathbf Z/\ell^r\mathbf Z$.
For a finite $\mathbf Z/\ell^r\mathbf Z$-algebra $\Lambda$, we apply
$-\otimes^{\mathbf L}_{\mathbf Z/\ell^r}\Lambda$. Since
$\mu_{\ell^r}$ is locally free of rank one over
$\mathbf Z/\ell^r\mathbf Z$, the target becomes $\Lambda_Z(1)[2]$.

To identify the composite with $c_1$, we cover $Z$ by opens with local
equations $t_\alpha$. The local lifts of the section $1$ of
$i_*\mathbf Z_D$ through $\Sigma_D$ are the $t_\alpha^{-1}$. Their
ratios are the transition functions of $\mathcal O_Z(D)$; this fixes the
sign convention. Hence the boundary of $\Sigma_D$ followed by the
Kummer boundary is the class of $\mathcal O_Z(D)$. Changing a local equation
multiplies a lift by a unit, and this changes the cocycle by a coboundary.

Naturality holds because the local equations, the units and the
sequence $\Sigma_D$ pull back, compatibly with the boundary maps. On the
source we use the identity $g^*i_*=i'_*g_D^*$ for a base change
$g:Z'\to Z$ with $i':D'\hookrightarrow Z'$ and $g_D:D'\to D$;
it can be checked on geometric stalks.
