Since the Kummer cover $T^n=a$ is étale only when $n$ is invertible,
$p$-torsion in characteristic $p$ needs a different sequence.
In the Artin–Schreier sequence the role of $\mathbf G_m$ is played by
the additive group, whose étale cohomology is the coherent cohomology
of the structure sheaf. Coherent cohomology of a curve vanishes
above degree one. This gives $H^q(C,\mathbf F_p)=0$ for $q\geq3$. For
$q=2$ we also need that $\varphi-1$ is surjective on $H^1(C,\mathcal O_C)$,
which holds over an algebraically closed field. So $H^2(C,\mathbf F_p)=0$
even on a proper curve, and there is no analogue of the class of a point
in $H^2$.

On a proper curve, $H^1(C,\mathbf F_p)$ is the fixed space of the
Frobenius-linear map $\varphi$ on the $g$-dimensional space
$H^1(C,\mathcal O_C)$. Hence it has dimension at most $g$
(Proposition [proposition-p-torsion-smooth-proper-curve]). The
bound can be strict. For an elliptic curve, $\varphi$ on the line
$H^1(E,\mathcal O_E)$ is bijective when $E$ is ordinary, and zero when
$E$ is supersingular, as is $y^2=x^3-x$ over $\overline{\mathbf F}_3$.
Accordingly $H^1(E,\mathbf F_p)$ has dimension one or zero. On an affine
curve the cokernel of $\varphi-1$ on the ring of functions contributes to
$H^1$, and this group is infinite
(Example [example-affine-line-characteristic-p]).
