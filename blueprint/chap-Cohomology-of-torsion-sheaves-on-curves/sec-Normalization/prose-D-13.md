The normalization sequence reduces constant coefficients on a reduced
curve to the smooth case. By
Lemma [lemma-sheaves-finite-support-finite-morphisms], its cokernel term has finite
sections, unchanged under field extension, and no positive cohomology.
Moreover, by Lemma [lemma-finite-morphisms-cohomology], $H^q(C,\pi_*\underline M)=H^q(C^{\mathrm{nor}},\underline M)$.
The long exact sequence therefore yields isomorphisms
$H^q(C,\underline M)\simeq H^q(C^{\mathrm{nor}},\underline M)$
for $q\geq2$, while in degrees zero and one it gives a natural exact sequence
relating $C$ and $C^{\mathrm{nor}}$, whose remaining term is a finite
group. From these sequences, vanishing and finiteness pass
from $C^{\mathrm{nor}}$ to $C$, and by the five lemma so does invariance
under field extension.

The normalization of an affine or proper curve is again affine or
proper. Hence all assertions of
Theorem [theorem-cohomology-curves] for constant $\mathbf F_\ell$ follow from
the calculations on smooth curves. For $(C_8)$, we apply this comparison
to $C$ and to the inverse image of an open subset. The commutative
square of restriction maps then reduces surjectivity to the smooth components.
