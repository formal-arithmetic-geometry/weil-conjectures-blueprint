We reduce a local system to constant sheaves by pulling it back along
a cover of degree prime to $\ell$, on which it becomes unipotent. To find such a cover, note that a local
system of finite $\mathbf F_\ell$-vector spaces has finite
monodromy: the fundamental group acts on a fibre through a
finite quotient. The local system then becomes a successive extension of
constant sheaves on the cover defined by an $\ell$-Sylow subgroup of the
monodromy group.

On the full cover of its monodromy the local system even becomes
constant. But that cover can have degree divisible by $\ell$, and then
the local system need not be a direct summand of the pushforward of its
pullback. For example, let $v:y^2=x$ be the double cover of $\mathbf G_m$,
in characteristic different from two, and let $\ell=2$. The composite of
the unit $\mathbf F_2\to v_*\mathbf F_2$ and the trace is multiplication by
$2$, which is zero. In fact $\mathbf F_2$ is not a direct summand of
$v_*\mathbf F_2$ at all. The monodromy representation of
$v_*\mathbf F_2$ is the regular representation $\mathbf F_2[\mathbf Z/2]$,
which is indecomposable.

For a cover of degree prime to $\ell$, on the
other hand, Lemma [lemma-reduction-unipotent-local-system] shows that the
trace divided by the degree splits the unit. It therefore suffices to
prove the vanishing, finiteness and invariance statements for the
pullback. The pullback is unipotent, that is, built from constant sheaves
by extensions.
