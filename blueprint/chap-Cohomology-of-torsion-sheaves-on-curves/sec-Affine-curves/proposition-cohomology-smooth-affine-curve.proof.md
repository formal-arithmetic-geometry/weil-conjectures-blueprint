By the Kummer sequence, the vanishing above degree one follows from the
divisibility of $\operatorname{Pic}(U)$ and the vanishing of
$H^q(U,\mathbf G_m)$ for $q\geq2$ (Lemma [lemma-cohomology-multiplicative-group]). For $H^1$ we describe
$\mu_n$-torsors as line bundles $L$ with a trivialization of
$L^{\otimes n}$. After extending $L$ to $\overline C$, the orders at the
punctures measure the failure of the torsor to extend. These orders give
the residue map $\partial$. We prove exactness at each place, and then deduce the rank
and the invariance under field extension.

Since $\overline C$ is regular, we may use divisors to see that

$$
\operatorname{Pic}(U)=\operatorname{Pic}(\overline C)/
\langle\mathcal O_{\overline C}(x):x\in D\rangle.
$$

One of these line bundles has degree one. Every class in
$\operatorname{Pic}(\overline C)$ is therefore congruent, modulo the
subgroup generated by it, to a class of degree zero. Hence
$J(\overline C)(k)$ surjects onto $\operatorname{Pic}(U)$. As $J(\overline C)(k)$ is
$n$-divisible (Corollary [corollary-torsion-jacobian-field-extension]), so is
$\operatorname{Pic}(U)$.
By the Kummer sequence and Lemma [lemma-cohomology-multiplicative-group], we have
$H^2(U,\mu_n)=\operatorname{Pic}(U)/n=0$ and $H^q(U,\mu_n)=0$ for
$q\geq3$.

We construct the sequence (D.1) using line
bundles.  Recall that a $\mu_n$-torsor on $U$ is given by a pair
$(L,\alpha:L^{\otimes n}\simeq\mathcal O_U)$ of a line bundle and a
trivialization of its $n$th power.  To define its residues, we extend $L$ to a line
bundle $\overline L$ on $\overline C$ and let $s=\alpha^{-1}(1)$ be the
corresponding rational section of $\overline L^{\otimes n}$.  The
divisor of $s$ is supported on $D$, since $s$ generates on $U$. Its
orders modulo $n$ do not depend on the extension. Indeed, two
extensions differ by $\mathcal O(E')$ with $E'$ supported on $D$, and
this changes the divisor of $s$ by $nE'$. These orders define
$\partial(L,\alpha)$. Their sum is zero modulo $n$, since the divisor of
$s$ has degree $\deg\overline L^{\otimes n}=n\deg\overline L$.

Suppose all orders are divisible by $n$, and write
$\operatorname{div}(s)=nE$ with $E$ supported on $D$. Then $s$ is a
nowhere vanishing section of
$(\overline L(-E))^{\otimes n}=\overline L^{\otimes n}(-nE)$. So the
trivialization extends to a torsor with line bundle $\overline L(-E)$ on
$\overline C$, and this torsor restricts to $(L,\alpha)$ on $U$. This
proves exactness at $H^1(U,\mu_n)$.

To prove
injectivity, note that a trivialization on $U$ of a torsor on $\overline C$
gives a rational section of its line bundle whose $n$th power has no
zero or pole on $\overline C$.  Therefore the divisor of the section is
zero, and the section trivializes the torsor on $\overline C$.

To see that $\partial$ maps onto the kernel of $\sum$, we lift a
residue vector with sum zero modulo $n$ to an integral divisor $B$
supported on $D$. Its degree is divisible by $n$ because the residues sum
to zero. We claim that, as a consequence, there is a line bundle $\overline L$ with
$\overline L^{\otimes n}\simeq\mathcal O_{\overline C}(B)$. To find it, choose
$P\in\overline C(k)$ and put $L_1=\mathcal O_{\overline C}((\deg B/n)P)$, so that the
line bundle $\mathcal O_{\overline C}(B)\otimes L_1^{\otimes-n}$ has degree
zero. By divisibility of $J(\overline C)(k)$ it is $N^{\otimes n}$
for some $N$ of degree zero, and $\overline L=L_1\otimes N$ works. It remains to
produce the torsor. The
canonical section of $\mathcal O_{\overline C}(B)$ has divisor $B$ and is
nowhere zero on $U$. It therefore defines a trivialization of
$\overline L^{\otimes n}|_U$, that is, a Kummer torsor on $U$ whose
residues are the orders of $B$. Finally, the
summation map is surjective since $D$ is nonempty. This proves that the
sequence is exact (see also [Stacks, [03RR](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/03RR)]).

The left term and the kernel of summation are free $\mathbf Z/n$-modules of
ranks $2g$ and $\#D-1$. Hence the resulting short exact sequence
splits noncanonically. For naturality, note that the formation of the
compactification, the boundary points, the line bundles and the orders
of vanishing commutes with base change. The
leftmost pullback map is an isomorphism by
Proposition [proposition-smooth-proper-curves-torsion-prime], and the residue groups and their
summation maps are unchanged. By the five lemma applied to the two
exact sequences, the middle pullback map is an isomorphism.
