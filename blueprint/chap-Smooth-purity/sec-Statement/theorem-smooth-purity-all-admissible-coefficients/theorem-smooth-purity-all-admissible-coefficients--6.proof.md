The existence proof in [theorem-smooth-purity-all-admissible-coefficients--2] chooses compatible finite-level isomorphisms and applies derived Nakayama. It does not prove that the chosen reductions are the normalized finite orientations. Thus reducing the desired equality to the finite normalization does not identify this chosen integral orientation with the affine-line trace.
