For (1), the objects $K$ for which $\mu_{f,K}$ is an isomorphism form a
triangulated subcategory, since the map is a natural transformation of
exact functors compatible with shifts
(`isIso_upperShriekUnitTensorMap_of_triangEnvelope`). Every bounded
constructible complex lies in the triangulated subcategory generated by
the objects $Rg_*Rj_*L$, where $g:Z\hookrightarrow Y$ is a closed immersion,
$j:U\hookrightarrow Z$ is an open immersion with $U$ affine, and $L$ is
dualizable (`triangEnvelope_lisseLowerStarGenerator`). It therefore
suffices to treat these objects.

For a dualizable $L$, the projection formula and the evaluation and
coevaluation maps give the assertion for any morphism
(`isIso_upperShriekUnitTensorMap_of_isDualizable`). Apply this to the
base change $f_U$ of $f$ to $U$. To pass first through $Rj_*$ and then
through $Rg_*$, use `isIso_upperShriekUnitTensorMap_lowerStar`. The
ordinary base change isomorphism is smooth base change in the open step
(Theorem [theorem-smooth-base-change-all-admissible]) and proper base
change in the closed step, where $Rg_!=Rg_*$
(Proposition [proposition-composition-base-change-rf-shriek]).

The passage through either immersion uses the compatibility of
$\mu$ with the exceptional base change map of
Definition [definition-exceptional-base-change-map], namely
`map_upperShriekUnitTensorMap_comp_upperShriekBaseChange` from
Proposition [proposition-compatibility-purity-base-change]. The exceptional
base change map is invertible on the direct image in question by the
adjunctions for the immersion. It is also invertible at $\Lambda_Y$
(`isIso_upperShriekBaseChange_unit_of_smooth`), and $\omega_f$ is dualizable
(`isDualizable_upperShriek_unit_of_smooth`). Thus the pullback of
$\mu_{f,Rg_*N}$ is an isomorphism. Its source and target lie in the
essential image of the fully faithful direct image along the pulled-back
immersion; pullback is conservative on that image. This proves the two
immersion steps, and dévissage gives (1).

For these properties of $\omega_f$, Lean works locally in pure relative
dimension. Dualizability follows by extending scalars from the integral
relative dualizing complex, which is isomorphic to a shifted integral Tate
twist. That integral isomorphism is obtained by lifting isomorphisms at
finite levels, using finite smooth purity
(Proposition [proposition-smooth-purity-finite-coefficients]); extension
of scalars commutes with $f^!$
(Lemma [lemma-change-coefficients-exceptional-inverse-image]). Base change
at the unit follows from `smoothOrientation_baseChange`, which supplies
an isomorphism between the pulled-back shifted Tate twist and the shifted
Tate twist for the base change. This is an existence statement about that
comparison, rather than an identification with a prescribed comparison.

For (2), the adjunction identity identifies the adjoint of the chosen
smooth trace with the chosen orientation
(`smoothPurityMap_eq_smoothOrientation`). This orientation is an
isomorphism (`isIso_smoothOrientation`): it is obtained by factoring $f$
through its smooth image and transporting an orientation along an open
immersion. On an open immersion the orientation is the usual
identification. Otherwise `smoothOrientationOfSmooth` chooses an
isomorphism lifting the finite orientations when one with this property
exists, and uses an isomorphism obtained from the integral relative
dualizing complex otherwise. The proof uses only invertibility; it does
not establish the existence of a compatible lift of the canonical finite
orientations. Rewriting by this adjunction identity proves (2).
