Every identification of $f^!$ in this section is ultimately an
adjunction whose counit is integration along affine lines, so we first make
that integration explicit. The affine line is not proper, so we cannot
integrate on it directly; instead we integrate on $\mathbf P^1$. There, on a
geometric fibre $H^2(\mathbf P^1,\mathbf Z/\ell^n(1))\simeq\mathbf Z/\ell^n$
is generated by $c_1(\mathcal O(1))$, the class of a point, and the trace
map of $\mathbf P^1$ reads off the coefficient of this class. The trace map
of $\mathbf A^1$ is then its composite with the map
$H^2_c(\mathbf A^1,\mathbf Z/\ell^n(1))\to H^2(\mathbf P^1,\mathbf Z/\ell^n(1))$
given by extension by zero. This construction works over an arbitrary base
$S$ and commutes with base change, and iterating it gives the trace map
(9.11) of $\mathbf A^d$, which is the counit
of the adjunction on a chart.

For a scheme $T$, Chern classes are defined by the Kummer sequence on $T_{\acute{e}t}$,

$$
1\longrightarrow\mu_{\ell^n}\longrightarrow\mathbf G_m
\xrightarrow{\ (\cdot)^{\ell^n}\ }\mathbf G_m
\longrightarrow1,
\tag{9.7}
$$

whose boundary $H^1(T_{\acute{e}t},\mathbf G_m)\to
H^2(T_{\acute{e}t},(\mathbf Z/\ell^n\mathbf Z)_T(1))$ sends a line bundle $L$
to $c_1(L)$. For a scheme $S$, let $q:\mathbf P^1_S\to S$ be the projection. By
Lemma [lemma-cohomology-projective-line-trace], the unit and
$c_1(\mathcal O_{\mathbf P^1_S}(1))$ give an isomorphism, compatible with base change,

$$
Rq_*(\mathbf Z/\ell^n\mathbf Z)_{\mathbf P^1_S}
\simeq(\mathbf Z/\ell^n\mathbf Z)_{S}\oplus(\mathbf Z/\ell^n\mathbf Z)_{S}(-1)[-2],
\tag{9.8}
$$

and the *trace map*
$\operatorname{Tr}_q:Rq_*(\mathbf Z/\ell^n\mathbf Z)_{\mathbf P^1_S}(1)[2]
\to(\mathbf Z/\ell^n\mathbf Z)_{S}$ is the projection onto the second
summand, after twisting. It sends $c_1(\mathcal O_{\mathbf P^1_{\bar s}}(1))$ on
each geometric fibre to $1$.

Let $p_1,p_2$ be the projections of
$\mathbf P^1_S\times_S\mathbf P^1_S$, and let $x_0,x_1$ and $y_0,y_1$ be the
pullbacks along $p_1$ and $p_2$ of the homogeneous coordinates of
$\mathbf P^1_S$. The diagonal $\Delta$ is the zero locus of the section
$x_0y_1-x_1y_0$ of $p_1^*\mathcal O(1)\otimes p_2^*\mathcal O(1)$, so that
$\mathcal O(\Delta)$ is this line bundle and $x_0y_1-x_1y_0$ is its
canonical section. Lemma [lemma-cycle-class-cartier-divisor] provides the cycle
class of $\Delta$,

$$
\delta:\Delta_*(\mathbf Z/\ell^n\mathbf Z)_{\mathbf P^1_S}
\longrightarrow(\mathbf Z/\ell^n\mathbf Z)_{\mathbf P^1_S\times_S\mathbf P^1_S}(1)[2],
$$

the Kummer boundary of $\mathcal O(\Delta)$ with its canonical section,
compatible with every base change on $S$. Let
$\operatorname{Tr}_{p_i}$ be the pullback of $\operatorname{Tr}_q$. Then the
diagonal integrates to $1$ along either factor, that is,

$$
\operatorname{Tr}_{p_i}\circ Rp_{i*}\delta
=\operatorname{id}_{(\mathbf Z/\ell^n\mathbf Z)_{\mathbf P^1_S}}
\qquad(i=1,2).
\tag{9.9}
$$

This is (6.4) of Proposition [proposition-adjunction-projective-line]. To see it directly,
note that both sides are endomorphisms of a constant sheaf, so it suffices to check
(9.9) on geometric fibres of $p_i$, where
the diagonal is a single rational point with line bundle $\mathcal O(1)$,
and $\delta$ restricts to $c_1(\mathcal O(1))$, which $\operatorname{Tr}_q$
sends to $1$.

For $\Lambda=\mathbf Z/\ell^n\mathbf Z$,
Proposition [proposition-adjunction-projective-line] gives the adjunction
$Rq_*\dashv q^*(-)(1)[2]$, and its unit and counit, (6.5) and
(6.6) there, take the following form. The counit is integration
along the fibres,

$$
\varepsilon_K:Rq_*\bigl(q^{*}K(1)[2]\bigr)
\simeq K\otimes^{\mathbf L} Rq_*(\mathbf Z/\ell^n\mathbf Z)_{\mathbf P^1_S}(1)[2]
\xrightarrow{\ \operatorname{id}_K\otimes\operatorname{Tr}_q\ } K,
\tag{9.10}
$$

and the unit is the class of the diagonal,

$$
\eta_M:\ M\simeq Rp_{2*}\Delta_*M
\xrightarrow{\ Rp_{2*}(\delta\otimes p_1^*M)\ } Rp_{2*}p_{1}^{*}M(1)[2]
\simeq q^{*}Rq_*M(1)[2],
$$

using the projection formulas and proper base change. Both commute with
base change on $S$, and the triangle identities reduce to
(9.9).

Let $j:\mathbf A^1_S\hookrightarrow\mathbf P^1_S$ be the complement of
the section at infinity, and let $p=q\circ j$. Composing
this adjunction with $j_!\dashv j^*$ we obtain an adjunction
$Rp_!\dashv p^*(-)(1)[2]$ between
$D^b_{\mathrm{ctf}}(\mathbf A^1_{S,\mathrm{pro}\acute{e}t},\mathbf Z/\ell^n\mathbf Z)$ and
$D^b_{\mathrm{ctf}}(S_{\mathrm{pro}\acute{e}t},\mathbf Z/\ell^n\mathbf Z)$, whose counit is the counit for the
open immersion followed by $\varepsilon_K$. At the constant sheaf this
counit is the trace map of the affine line
$\operatorname{Tr}_p:Rp_!(\mathbf Z/\ell^n\mathbf Z)_{\mathbf A^1_S}(1)[2]
\to(\mathbf Z/\ell^n\mathbf Z)_S$ of (9.1),
and it is an isomorphism. Indeed, we apply $Rq_*$ to the localization
triangle for the section at infinity. In the resulting triangle, restriction to infinity is the
identity on the first summand of (9.8), and it kills
the second summand, because the pullback of $\mathcal O(1)$ along the
section at infinity is the trivial line bundle on $S$, and so the
restriction of $c_1(\mathcal O(1))$ is $0$. Hence
$Rp_!(\mathbf Z/\ell^n\mathbf Z)_{\mathbf A^1_S}\simeq
(\mathbf Z/\ell^n\mathbf Z)_S(-1)[-2]$ is the second summand. Over a geometric
point this is $H^2_c(\mathbf A^1,(\mathbf Z/\ell^n\mathbf Z)(1))\simeq
\mathbf Z/\ell^n\mathbf Z$, generated by the class $\kappa$ that maps to
the class of a point in $H^2(\mathbf P^1,(\mathbf Z/\ell^n\mathbf Z)(1))$.

For affine space, let $p_j:\mathbf A^j_S\to S$ be the projection and
let $r_j:\mathbf A^j_S\to\mathbf A^{j-1}_S$ be the map forgetting the last
coordinate. We define $\operatorname{Tr}_{p_j}$ inductively as the composite

$$
\begin{aligned}
Rp_{j!}(\mathbf Z/\ell^n\mathbf Z)_{\mathbf A^j_S}(j)[2j]
&\simeq Rp_{j-1!}\bigl(Rr_{j!}(\mathbf Z/\ell^n\mathbf Z)_{\mathbf A^j_S}(1)[2]\bigr)(j-1)[2j-2]\\
&\xrightarrow{\ \operatorname{Tr}_{p_{j-1}}\circ Rp_{j-1!}(\operatorname{Tr}_{r_j})\ }
(\mathbf Z/\ell^n\mathbf Z)_S,
\end{aligned}
$$

using $Rp_{j!}=Rp_{j-1!}Rr_{j!}$ and the projection formula. For $j=d$
this is the *trace map of affine space*, an isomorphism (the identity when $d=0$)

$$
\operatorname{Tr}_{p_d}:Rp_{d!}(\mathbf Z/\ell^n\mathbf Z)_{\mathbf A^d_S}(d)[2d]
\xrightarrow{\sim}(\mathbf Z/\ell^n\mathbf Z)_{S}.
\tag{9.11}
$$

By Lemma [lemma-affine-space-etale-localization](1), $Rp_{d!}\dashv
p_d^*(-)(d)[2d]$, and the counit is the composite of the $d$ counits for the
affine lines, which at the constant sheaf is
(9.11). Like those of the affine line, the
unit and counit commute with base change. On a geometric fibre, the external product
$\kappa\boxtimes\cdots\boxtimes\kappa\in
H_c^{2d}(\mathbf A^d_{\bar s},(\mathbf Z/\ell^n\mathbf Z)(d))$
integrates to $1$, since by the projection formula, integrating along the
last coordinate replaces the last factor by its integral, which is $1$.

Objects of a derived category do not glue in general. For example, suppose
$X=U\cup V$ and we know objects on $U$ and on $V$ isomorphic to $f^!K|_U$ and
$f^!K|_V$. Then the two isomorphisms restricted to $U\cap V$ may differ by an
automorphism, and nothing tells us which identification to glue along. On a
chart $U\to\mathbf A^d_Y$ we have an explicit candidate for $f^!K|_U$,
namely $e^*p_d^*K(d)[2d]$, and we want to compare such candidates with
$f^!K$ and later, for towers, to glue $f^!$ from them. For this we keep the
counit together with the object. The point is that a pair consisting of an
object and a counit that satisfies a universal property is unique up to a
unique isomorphism compatible with the counit. Hence local pairs can be compared on overlaps
without any choice of transition maps. We use this for the comparison following
Definition [definition-representing-objects] and in the construction of
the right adjoint for towers (Proposition [proposition-sp-tower-geometric]).
