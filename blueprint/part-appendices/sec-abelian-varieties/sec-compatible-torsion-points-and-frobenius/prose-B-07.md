Fix a prime $\ell$ invertible in $k$. Compatible systems of
$\ell$-power torsion points of $A$ form the Tate module $T_\ell A$, a
$\Zl$-module with an action of $\operatorname{Gal}(\bar k/k)$. For a
complex torus $\mathbf C^g/\Lambda$ it is $\Lambda\otimes\Zl$, the first
homology with $\ell$-adic coefficients. The count of
Proposition [sk-prop-av-isogeny] gives the orders of
the groups $A[\ell^n](\bar k)$, and these orders, known for all $n$,
force them to be free over $\mathbf Z/\ell^n$ of rank $2g$;
divisibility of $A(\bar k)$ makes the transition maps surjective, and
$T_\ell A$ is free of rank $2g$ over $\Zl$
(Proposition [sk-prop-av-tate-rank]).

Frobenius is better behaved on the limit than on the finite levels. For
the elliptic curve $E_0:y^2=x^3-x$ over $\mathbf F_3$, the three roots
of $x^3-x$ lie in $\mathbf F_3$, so all of $E[2]$ is rational and
Frobenius acts trivially on it. On $T_2E$, however, no power of
Frobenius has a nonzero fixed vector, because a fixed vector would be an
unbounded compatible system of $2$-power torsion points in the finite
group $E_0(\mathbf F_{3^m})$ (Proposition [sk-p-tate]).
Through the Jacobian, this is the statement that Frobenius has no fixed
class in $H^1$ of a curve (Proposition [sk-prop-wrp-h1-no-fixed]).
