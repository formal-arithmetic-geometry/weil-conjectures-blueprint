Every modification in the proof of Nagata's theorem is a blow-up whose
center avoids a fixed open, so that this open is not changed.
The open $U$ is the scheme being compactified, or a piece of it, and
only the boundary may be modified. For instance, the coordinate axes
minus the origin are disjoint closed subsets of
$U=\mathbf A^2\setminus\{0\}$, but their closures in $\mathbf A^2$ meet at
the origin; in the blow-up of the origin, which is an isomorphism over
$U$, their strict transforms meet the exceptional line in the two
distinct points of slope $0$ and $\infty$, and so become disjoint. This
is the mechanism of Lemma [sk-lem-nagata-sketch-boundary]. We use blow-ups
rather than arbitrary proper modifications that are isomorphisms over
$U$, because a blow-up is given by an ideal: two of them are dominated
by the blow-up of the product ideal, and a composite is again one
(Lemma [sk-lem-nagata-sketch-blowups]). The ideal is required to be
of finite type, so that the blow-up is proper.
