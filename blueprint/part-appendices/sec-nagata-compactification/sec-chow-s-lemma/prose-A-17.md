Nagata's theorem embeds $X$ as an open subscheme of a proper scheme and
provides the compactifications used to define $Rf_!$. Chow's lemma
plays a different role: it replaces a proper $X$ by a projective scheme
mapping properly and surjectively onto it, and in this way it supplies
the projective morphisms used in the reduction of proper base change.
In Section [sec-pbc-reduction] proper base change is first proved for
projective morphisms, and it descends along a proper surjection
$\pi:\widetilde X\to X$ as soon as it holds for $\pi$ and for the
composite $f\pi$ (Lemma [sk-lem-pbc-composition-cancellation]). Chow's lemma
is the statement that such a $\pi$ exists with both $\pi$ and $f\pi$
projective. Some modification is unavoidable, since there are proper
varieties that are not projective; Hironaka gave smooth examples in
dimension three. We deduce the theorem from the form in the Stacks
Project, which gives an immersion of $\widetilde X$ into projective space
over $S$; properness then makes this immersion closed.
