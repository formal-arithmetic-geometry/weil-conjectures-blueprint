The chart of the Picard scheme, in degree $g$, is cut out by one
numerical condition, $h^1(\mathcal L)=0$, equivalently by
Riemann–Roch $h^0(\mathcal L)=1$, and two tools control it.
Riemann–Roch relates $h^0$ and $h^1$ to the degree, and in particular
shows that an invertible sheaf of degree $g$ with $h^1=0$ has exactly one
section up to scalars. Coherent base change shows that in a family over
$T$ the condition “$Rf_{T,*}\mathcal L$ is an invertible sheaf in degree
zero” is open on $T$ and compatible with base change, so that it
defines an open subfunctor of the Picard functor. The condition is not
closed, because $h^0$ can jump up under specialization. For a curve of
genus two, a divisor $D$ of degree two has $h^0(\mathcal O(D))=1$ unless
$\mathcal O(D)\simeq\omega_{C/k}$, where $h^0=2$; so the chart misses the
line of canonical divisors in $H_2$, and the Picard scheme in degree two
is covered by the chart together with its translates. Both tools must commute
with extension of the ground field, because the Picard scheme is built
over a separably closed field and its points are tested over larger
fields.

Fix a smooth proper geometrically connected curve $C$ over $k$, with
structure morphism $f:C\to\operatorname{Spec}k$, and set
$g=\dim_kH^1(C,\mathcal O_C)$. Such a curve is projective
[Stacks, Tag 0A26](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0A26).
