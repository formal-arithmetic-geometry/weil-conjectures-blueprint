We now prove the decomposition
$\mathfrak l=Z(\mathfrak l)+[\mathfrak l,\mathfrak l]$ for a Lie algebra with a
faithful semisimple representation
(Proposition [sk-p-lie]), which is the input of
Lemma [sk-p-product]. The proof has two halves. First, the
radical acts by scalars on each simple summand. Lie's theorem gives a
common eigenvector, and a trace computation, in which we divide by the
dimension of a subspace, shows that the eigenspace is stable under all
of $\mathfrak l$. Faithfulness then puts the radical in the center.
Second, the quotient by the center has zero radical, and for such a Lie
algebra Cartan's criterion makes the Killing form nondegenerate, which
forces the algebra to equal its derived algebra
(Lemma [sk-lem-walg-killing]). For $\mathfrak{gl}_n$ the decomposition is
the familiar one into scalars and trace-zero matrices,
$\mathfrak{gl}_n=\Omega\cdot1\oplus\mathfrak{sl}_n$; the upper triangular
algebra $\mathfrak b$ of the previous subsection shows that the
hypothesis of semisimplicity cannot be dropped.
