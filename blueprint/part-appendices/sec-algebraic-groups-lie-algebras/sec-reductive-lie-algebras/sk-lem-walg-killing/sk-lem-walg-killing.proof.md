By invariance of $B$, the kernel $\mathfrak k$ of $B$
is an ideal. For $x\in[\mathfrak k,\mathfrak k]$ and $y\in\mathfrak k$,
the operators $\operatorname{ad}x$ and $\operatorname{ad}y$ map
$\mathfrak l$ into $\mathfrak k$, so the trace of
$\operatorname{ad}x\operatorname{ad}y$ may be computed on $\mathfrak k$.
Since $x\in\mathfrak k$, we obtain

$$
\operatorname{tr}_{\mathfrak k}
(\operatorname{ad}(x)|_{\mathfrak k}
\operatorname{ad}(y)|_{\mathfrak k})=B(x,y)=0.
$$

By Cartan's criterion (Lemma [sk-lem-walg-trace-criterion]), the
image of $\mathfrak k$ in $\operatorname{End}(\mathfrak k)$ is
solvable. The kernel of $\mathfrak k\to\operatorname{End}(\mathfrak k)$
is the center of $\mathfrak k$, which is abelian. Hence $\mathfrak k$
is a solvable ideal of $\mathfrak l$, and therefore $\mathfrak k=0$.
This proves that $B$ is nondegenerate. By invariance and
nondegeneracy, we have

$$
[\mathfrak l,\mathfrak l]^\perp=Z(\mathfrak l).
$$

Since the center is a solvable ideal, it is zero. Thus the derived
algebra has zero orthogonal complement, and hence it is all of
$\mathfrak l$.
