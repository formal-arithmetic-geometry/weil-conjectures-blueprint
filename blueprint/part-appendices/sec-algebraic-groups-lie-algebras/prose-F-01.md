The determinant weights of tensor and exterior powers
(Proposition [sk-prop-walg-determinant-weights]) are computed from a central
element of the arithmetic monodromy group $G$, and its construction rests
on Lemma [sk-p-product]: for a semisimple Weil
representation $V$, the identity component $G^\circ$ is generated by
$Z(G^\circ)^\circ$ and the identity component $H^\circ$ of the geometric
monodromy group. This is the algebraic form of a familiar fact about
compact Lie groups, that a connected compact group is the product of the
identity component of its center and its derived group. For algebraic
groups over $\overline{\mathbf Q}_\ell$ the argument passes through the Lie algebra
$\mathfrak g$ of $G^\circ$. A faithful semisimple representation forces
$\mathfrak g$ to be reductive,

$$
\mathfrak g=Z(\mathfrak g)+[\mathfrak g,\mathfrak g]
$$

(Proposition [sk-p-lie]); the derived algebra lies in
the Lie algebra of $H$ because commutators of $G$ lie in $H$; and a closed
connected subgroup with the full Lie algebra is everything.

Three facts make this work, and we prove them here. The group $G^\circ$
and its Lie algebra have the same stable subspaces, and the center of
$\mathfrak g$ is the Lie algebra of the center
(Lemma [sk-p-comparison]). Both follow from Cartier's theorem that
algebraic groups in characteristic zero are smooth
(Proposition [sk-i-cartier]), which lets us read off a closed
subgroup from its Lie algebra (Lemma [sk-p-geometry]). The
third fact, the decomposition of $\mathfrak g$ displayed above, follows
from Cartan's criterion for solvability. All three use
characteristic zero. In characteristic $p$ the group scheme $\mu_p$ is
not reduced, and it is a proper closed subgroup of $\mathbf G_m$ with the
same Lie algebra. Cartan's criterion uses that the prime field is
$\mathbf Q$: for $p>3$ the Witt algebra $W(1)$ over $\mathbf F_p$, the
derivations of $\mathbf F_p[x]/(x^p)$, is simple, and yet its trace form
$\operatorname{tr}(\operatorname{ad}x\operatorname{ad}y)$ is identically
zero. The proof of Proposition [sk-p-lie]
divides by the dimension of a vector space. Since the monodromy groups are defined over $\overline{\mathbf Q}_\ell$, this
is no restriction. For the classical theorems of Lie, Engel and Cartan we
refer to Humphreys (Hum) and sketch only the step where the
characteristic matters.

Throughout, $\Omega$ is an algebraically closed
field of characteristic zero, and all Lie algebras and representations
are finite-dimensional. An algebraic group is a group scheme of finite
type over $\Omega$. Stabilizers and centralizers are taken as subgroup
schemes; by Cartier's theorem below, they are smooth. The Lie algebra
$\operatorname{Lie}(G)$ of an algebraic group $G$ is its tangent space
at the identity; for a closed subgroup of $\GL(V)$ it is described by
dual numbers in Section [sec-app-walg-lie].
