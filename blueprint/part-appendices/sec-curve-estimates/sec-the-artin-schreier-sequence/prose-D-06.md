The Kummer cover $T^n=a$ is étale only when $n$ is invertible, and
hence for $p$-torsion in characteristic $p$ we need a different sequence.
The Artin–Schreier sequence replaces $\mathbf G_m$ by the additive
group, whose étale cohomology is the coherent cohomology of the
structure sheaf. Coherent cohomology of a curve vanishes above degree
one, so $H^q(C,\mathbf F_p)=0$ for $q\geq3$; for $q=2$ we also need
that $\varphi-1$ is surjective on $H^1(C,\mathcal O_C)$, which holds
over an algebraically closed field. So $H^2(C,\mathbf F_p)=0$ even on a
proper curve, and there is no analogue of the class of a point in $H^2$.
On a proper curve,
$H^1(C,\mathbf F_p)$ is the fixed space of the Frobenius-linear map
$\varphi$ on the $g$-dimensional space $H^1(C,\mathcal O_C)$, and so has
dimension at most $g$ (Proposition [sk-prop-curve-artin-schreier]). The
bound can be strict. For an elliptic curve, $\varphi$ on the line
$H^1(E,\mathcal O_E)$ is bijective when $E$ is ordinary, and zero when
$E$ is supersingular, as is $y^2=x^3-x$ over $\overline{\mathbf F}_3$;
then $H^1(E,\mathbf F_p)$ has dimension one or zero, matching the number
$p$ or $1$ of points of $E[p](\bar k)$. On an affine curve the
cokernel of $\varphi-1$ on the ring of functions contributes to $H^1$,
and this group is infinite (Example [sk-rem-curve-wild-example]).
