By [lem-sc-compactification] there is a proper smooth curve
$\overline C$ with $C=\overline C\setminus S$, and $S$ is
finite ([lem-sc-dim0-finite]) and nonempty: if $S=\emptyset$
then $C=\overline C$ would be proper and affine, hence finite of
dimension zero by [lem-sc-proper-affine-finite],
contradicting $\dim C=1$. By [lem-sc-divisor-class-curve],
$\operatorname{Pic}(\overline C)=\operatorname{Cl}(\overline C)$
and $\operatorname{Pic}(C)=\operatorname{Cl}(C)$, and the
restriction map

$$
\operatorname{Cl}(\overline C)\to\operatorname{Cl}(C),\qquad
D\mapsto D|_C,
$$

is surjective (a divisor of $C$ is a divisor of $\overline C$),
with kernel exactly the subgroup generated by the classes
$[P]$, $P\in S$: indeed $D|_C$ is principal iff
$D|_C=\operatorname{div}(f)$ for $f\in k(C)^*=k(\overline C)^*$,
iff $D-\operatorname{div}(f)$ is supported on $S$. Hence

$$
\bigoplus_{P\in S}\mathbf Z[P]
\longrightarrow\operatorname{Pic}(\overline C)
\longrightarrow\operatorname{Pic}(C)\longrightarrow0
$$

is exact. By [lem-sc-pic-proper-decomposition],
$\operatorname{Pic}(\overline C)=\mathbf Z\oplus\operatorname{Pic}^0(\overline C)$
with the $\mathbf Z$-factor generated by $[P_0]$; under this
split, $[P]\mapsto(1,[P]-[P_0])$, and since $P_0\in S$ the
subgroup $\langle[P]:P\in S\rangle$ contains $(1,0)$, so the
quotient is

$$
\operatorname{Pic}(C)\cong J_{\overline C}(k)/\Lambda,\qquad
\Lambda=\langle[P]-[P_0]:P\in S\rangle
$$

([def-sc-pic0], [def-sc-jacobian],
[thm-sc-jacobian-representability]). The group
$J_{\overline C}(k)$ is
$n$-divisible ([thm-av-final](d)), hence so is its
quotient $\operatorname{Pic}(C)$. Apply
[lem-sc-divisible-quotient-torsion] with
$G=J_{\overline C}(k)$. The subgroup $\Lambda$ is generated
by the finite set $\{[P]-[P_0]:P\in S\}$, so $\Lambda/n\Lambda$
is finite: it is a quotient of $(\mathbf Z/n\mathbf Z)^{|S|}$.
Also $G[n]=J_{\overline C}[n](k)$ by [def-sc-mult-n], and
this is finite by [thm-av-final](c). The exact sequence
therefore expresses $\operatorname{Pic}(C)[n]$ as an extension of
two finite groups, proving finiteness.

For the units: a unit $u\in\mathcal O_C(C)^\times$ is a rational function
on $\overline C$ without zeros or poles on $C$, so $\operatorname{div}(u)$
is supported on $S$ and $D$ is defined. Since $\operatorname{div}(u)$ is
principal, $a(D(u))=0$. Conversely, if $\sum_Pm_PP=\operatorname{div}(f)$
is principal, then $f$ has no zeros or poles on $C$, so
$f\in\mathcal O_C(C)^\times$ and $D(f)=(m_P)$; thus
$\operatorname{im}D=\ker a$. A unit with $D(u)=0$ has trivial divisor on
$\overline C$, hence is a global function on $\overline C$, i.e. a constant
in $k^\times$ ([lem-sc-global-functions]); as $k$ is algebraically closed,
it is an $n$-th power for every $n\geq1$. The isomorphism
$\operatorname{Pic}(C)\cong\operatorname{Pic}(\overline C)/\operatorname{im}a$
is the exact sequence displayed above.
