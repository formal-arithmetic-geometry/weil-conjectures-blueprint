Using the normalization sequence, we reduce the case of constant
coefficients on a reduced curve to the smooth case. By
Lemma [sk-lem-curve-finite-support], the cokernel term has finite
sections, unchanged under field extension, and no positive cohomology,
while $H^q(C,\pi_*\underline M)=H^q(C^{\mathrm{nor}},\underline M)$.
Therefore the long exact sequence yields isomorphisms
$H^q(C,\underline M)\simeq H^q(C^{\mathrm{nor}},\underline M)$
for $q\geq2$, and a natural exact sequence relating degrees zero and
one on $C$ and on $C^{\mathrm{nor}}$ whose remaining term is a finite
group. Along these sequences, vanishing and finiteness pass from $C^{\mathrm{nor}}$ to $C$,
and by the five lemma so does invariance under field extension.
As the normalization of an affine or
proper curve is again affine or proper, we conclude that all assertions of
Theorem [sk-thm-curve-package] for constant $\mathbf F_\ell$ follow from
the calculations on smooth curves. For $(C_8)$, we apply this comparison
to $C$ and to the inverse image of an open subset, and the commutative
square of restriction maps reduces surjectivity to the smooth components.

A constructible sheaf on a curve is locally constant on a dense open
subset. Removing the finitely many remaining points replaces the sheaf
by its extension by zero from this open, and changes cohomology only in
degrees zero and one.
