Let $C$ be a smooth curve over $k$ (proper or not; for reducible $C$ the
assertion holds componentwise). The map
$$
 \operatorname{Cl}(C)\to\operatorname{Pic}(C),\qquad
 D\mapsto[\mathcal O_C(D)],
$$
is an isomorphism of abelian groups.

## Proof

The map is well defined: for $f\in k(C)^*$,
$\mathcal O_C(\operatorname{div}f)=f^{-1}\mathcal O_C\cong\mathcal O_C$,
and the class of $\mathcal O_C(D)$ is independent of the representative of
$D$ modulo principal divisors. It is a homomorphism: the multiplication map
$\mathcal O_C(D)\otimes\mathcal O_C(D')\to\mathcal O_C(D+D')$,
$f\otimes g\mapsto fg$, is an isomorphism (locally both sides are
invertible modules generated by the same element of $k(C)$).
Surjectivity: by [lem-sc-line-bundle-rational-section] every invertible
module is $\mathcal O_C(D)$ for $D=\operatorname{div}(s)$ a rational
section. Injectivity: if $\mathcal O_C(D)\cong\mathcal O_C$, let $f$ be the
image of $1$ under an isomorphism $\mathcal O_C\to\mathcal O_C(D)$; then
$f\in k(C)^*$ and $\mathcal O_C(D)=f\cdot\mathcal O_C$, whence
$D=\operatorname{div}(f)$, so $D$ is principal.
