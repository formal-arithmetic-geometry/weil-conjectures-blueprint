Fix a prime $\ell\nmid q$. By [sk-input-weights], the reciprocal roots of
$P_{i,\ell}$ have $\iota$-modulus $q^{i/2}$. Since distinct degrees
give distinct moduli, the polynomials $P_{i,\ell}$ are pairwise
coprime. In particular, the products over odd and over even degrees

$$
 N_\ell=\prod_{i\ \mathrm{odd}}P_{i,\ell},\qquad
 D_\ell=\prod_{i\ \mathrm{even}}P_{i,\ell}
$$

form a reduced fraction $N_\ell/D_\ell$ with constant coefficients one.
Let $A/B$ be the normalized integral reduced fraction of the zeta
function ([sk-lem-wweil-integer-rationality]). By the determinant identity of
[sk-cor-tf-point-counting] and the uniqueness of reduced fractions over
$\mathbf Q_\ell$, we get

$$
 A=N_\ell,\qquad B=D_\ell
 \quad\hbox{in }\mathbf Q_\ell[T].
$$

The reciprocal polynomials
$A^\#(U)=U^{\deg A}A(U^{-1})$ and
$B^\#(U)=U^{\deg B}B(U^{-1})$ are monic integral polynomials, so
their roots are nonzero algebraic integers. Choose an embedding
$j_\ell:\overline{\mathbf Q}\hookrightarrow\overline{\mathbf Q}_\ell$. By the
last display, every such root $a$ corresponds to an eigenvalue on exactly one
$H^i_\ell$.

Let $\tau:\mathbf Q(a)\hookrightarrow\mathbf C$ be any embedding. We
claim that $\tau\circ j_\ell^{-1}$, defined on $j_\ell(\mathbf Q(a))$,
extends to a field isomorphism
$\iota':\overline{\mathbf Q}_\ell\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathbf C$.
To see this, first extend it to the algebraic closures of $\mathbf Q$
in the two fields. Both fields have transcendence degree $|\mathbf R|$
over $\overline{\mathbf Q}$, because an uncountable field that is
algebraic over the field generated by a transcendence basis over a
countable field has the cardinality of that basis. A bijection between
the two bases then extends to an isomorphism of the algebraic
closures. Applying [sk-input-weights] with this
$\iota'$ in the same degree $i$, we find

$$
 |\tau(a)|=q^{i/2}.
$$

Thus $a$ has absolute value $q^{i/2}$ under every complex embedding,
and since $q>1$ this determines $i$.

For odd $i$, let $R_i$ be the submultiset of the roots of $A^\#$
whose absolute value under every complex embedding is $q^{i/2}$, and
for even $i$ define $R_i$ in the same way using the roots of $B^\#$.
The root multisets of $A^\#$ and $B^\#$ are Galois-stable, with
multiplicities, and the condition on all complex embeddings is
Galois-invariant, so each $R_i$ is Galois-stable. Put

$$
 P_i(T)=\prod_{a\in R_i}(1-aT).
$$

The coefficients of $P_i$ are rational by Galois invariance, and they
are algebraic integers because the roots satisfy monic integral
equations. Hence they are integers. For the chosen $\ell$, the root
multiset of $P_i$ is exactly that of $P_{i,\ell}$, and since both have
constant coefficient one, $P_i=P_{i,\ell}$. For any other prime, the
same argument identifies the factor in degree $i$ with the same
submultiset of the roots of $A^\#$ or $B^\#$, so $P_i$ does not depend
on the prime. Comparing with a single $P_{i,\ell}$ also proves
uniqueness.

The complement follows by substituting $P_i=P_{i,\ell}$ into the
determinant identity, and from $A=N_\ell$, $B=D_\ell$.
