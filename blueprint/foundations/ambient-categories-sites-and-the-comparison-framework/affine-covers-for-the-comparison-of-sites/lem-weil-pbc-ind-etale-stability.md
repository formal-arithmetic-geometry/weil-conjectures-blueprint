The classes of ind-etale [def-weil-pbc-ind-etale-algebra] and ind-Zariski
maps are stable under base change, composition, and filtered colimits. An
ind-etale map is weakly etale. If $B,C$ are ind-etale over $A$, every
$A$-algebra map $B\to C$ is ind-etale. Weakly etale maps are stable under
composition and base change.

## Proof

Base change preserves finite products of localizations and etale
finite-presentation algebras. A finite presentation and its relations descend
through a filtered colimit. In a composite of ind-etale maps, descend each
finite-presentation algebra at the second step to a sufficiently late stage
at the first step, using [lem-weil-pbc-etale-finite-data-descent] on affines.
Taking all such stages gives the presentation required by
[def-weil-pbc-ind-etale-algebra]. For ind-Zariski maps the same calculation
just descends the finitely many elements being inverted and the finitely many
product idempotents. It also proves the filtered-colimit assertion.

For a finite-presentation etale algebra $E/A$, its diagonal is both open and
closed in $\operatorname{Spec}(E\otimes_AE)$: it is open by the etale
condition and closed because the map is affine. Thus the kernel of
multiplication is generated by an idempotent. If $B=\varinjlim E_i$, an
element in the kernel of $B\otimes_AB\to B$ descends to an element whose
multiplication is zero at a sufficiently late stage. The kernel is therefore
generated by idempotents. The quotient by this kernel is the localization
inverting their complements. Consequently $B\otimes_AB\to B$ is ind-Zariski
and flat. Also $B/A$ is flat, as a filtered colimit of flat algebras. This
proves weak etaleness.

Given $B\to C$ as in the statement, factor it as $B\to B\otimes_AC\to C$. The
first map is ind-etale by base change. The second is the base change of the
just computed diagonal of $B/A$, hence ind-Zariski. This proves the relative
assertion. Finally, for a composite of weakly etale maps $A\to B\to C$, the
factorization

$$
 C\otimes_AC\longrightarrow C\otimes_BC\longrightarrow C
$$

has first arrow a base change of the multiplication of $B/A$ and second arrow
the multiplication of $C/B$. Both are flat. Together with transitivity of
flatness this proves the assertion about composites; the base-change
assertion follows directly from the two flatness conditions.
