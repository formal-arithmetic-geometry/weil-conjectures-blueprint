Let $A$ be an integrally closed domain with fraction field $K$, and let $B/A$
be weakly etale [def-weil-pbc-ind-etale-algebra]. Then $B$ is integrally
closed in $B\otimes_AK$.

## Proof

We give the reduction to elementary flat-module algebra. For each $x\in
K\setminus A$, choose a valuation overring $V_x\subset K$ containing $A$ but
not $x$. This is the valuation criterion for integral dependence. Set
$V=\prod_xV_x$, $L=\prod_xK$. The diagonal map of $K$ makes $A=K\times_LV$.
The map $V\to L$ is localization at tuples with all coordinates nonzero:
denominators can be chosen coordinatewise. Every finitely generated ideal of
$V$ is generated by one tuple, by choosing in each valuation ring a generator
of smallest valuation among the given finite list. Its annihilator is
generated by the idempotent of the coordinates where that tuple is zero. Thus
each such ideal is projective; every ideal is flat by filtered colimits.
Submodules of finite free modules are flat by induction on the number of
coordinates, using the image ideal in the last coordinate; arbitrary free
modules follow by filtered colimits. Hence all modules over $V$ have flat
resolutions of length at most one.

Flatness of $B/A$ preserves the exact equalizer sequence defining the
pullback square, and gives

$$
 B=(B\otimes_AK)\times_{B\otimes_AL}(B\otimes_AV).
$$

Put $C=B\otimes_AV$. It is weakly etale over $V$ by
[lem-weil-pbc-ind-etale-stability], so [lem-weil-pbc-weak-etale-tor] bounds
its flat dimensions by one. Its localization $C'=B\otimes_AL$ is injective,
since $B/A$ is flat and $V\to L$ is injective.

If $z\in C'$ is integral over $C$, the subalgebra $D=C[z]$ is finite over
$C$. It is flat: from $D\subset C'$, the Tor exact sequence identifies
$\operatorname{Tor}_1^C(D,M)$ with a subquotient of
$\operatorname{Tor}_2^C(C'/D,M)=0$. Flatness of $D$ and $C'$ makes the
composite

$$
 D\otimes_CD\longrightarrow C'\otimes_CD
 \longrightarrow C'\otimes_CC'=C'
$$

injective. Its image is $D$, so multiplication on $D\otimes_CD$ is an
isomorphism. Also $C\to D$ is faithfully flat: it is flat and injective
integral, and lying over gives surjectivity on spectra. The faithfully flat
equalizer $C\to D\rightrightarrows D\otimes_CD$ then gives $C=D$. Thus $C$ is
integrally closed in $C'$. An element of $B\otimes_AK$ integral over $B$
consequently lies in $C$, hence in $B$ by the displayed pullback. If $A=K$,
the assertion is immediate and the empty product construction is
unnecessary. This expands the reductions in
[Stacks Project, Tag 092W](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/092W) and
[Tag 092V](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/092V).
