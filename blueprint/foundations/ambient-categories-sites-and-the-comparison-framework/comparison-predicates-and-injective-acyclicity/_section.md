The two predicates the proper-base-change chapter argues with:
$\mathrm{PBC}(f)$, that the named derived exchange morphism is an isomorphism
after every base change and for every torsion sheaf
[def-weil-pbc-property], and $\mathrm{IA}_r(f)$, that injective
$\mathbf F_r$-module sheaves stay acyclic after every base change
[def-weil-pbc-injective-acyclicity-property]. Their equivalence is proved later
[lemma-weil-pbc-property-iff-acyclicity]; the point of $\mathrm{IA}$ is that
it is the form stable under composition and cancellation. The section also
records the finite refinement of a surjective etale morphism
[lemma-weil-pbc-finite-refinement-etale-cover], the spectral restriction lemma
[lemma-weil-pbc-spectral-restriction] with its counterexample for sheaves that
are not locally constant, and the Stein factorization
[def-weil-pbc-stein-factorization] with the two classical inputs the
blueprint does not discharge [thm-weil-pbc-stein-inputs].
