For $C=\mathbf P^1_k$ and $\mathcal L=\mathcal O(d)$ with $d\geq0$, the
sections are the binary forms of degree $d$, a space of dimension
$d+1=\deg\mathcal L+1-0$, and $\omega=\mathcal O(-2)$ has degree $-2=2g-2$.

We quote the theorem from [Stacks, [0B5B](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0B5B)], where it is proved for smooth
curves and the duality is given by the residue trace on differentials.
We prove its compatibility with field extension in
Corollary [corollary-serre-duality-field-extension] below. For the displayed vanishing, recall that a nonzero
section of an invertible sheaf on an integral curve has an effective
divisor of zeros. Hence an invertible sheaf of negative degree has no
nonzero section. If $\deg\mathcal L>2g-2$, then
$\omega_{C/k}\otimes\mathcal L^{-1}$ has negative degree, and
$H^1(C,\mathcal L)=0$ by duality. The dimension formula then follows from
Riemann–Roch.

The Picard scheme is constructed over a separably closed field, but its
points are tested over arbitrary extensions. Hence Riemann–Roch and
coherent base change must be compatible with extension of the ground
field, and for duality we need this for the pairing itself, not only
for the dimensions.
