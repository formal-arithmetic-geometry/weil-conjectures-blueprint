We now read off determinant weights from the scalars by which the
central element acts. The definition repeats that of
Corollary [sk-d-real-detweights] for representations. The new notation
$\operatorname{DW}(V)$ records the determinant weights of all simple
factors of $V$, because, as the example $\overline{\mathbf Q}_\ell\oplus \overline{\mathbf Q}_\ell(-1)$ before that
corollary shows, the determinant weight of $V$ itself is only their
average.

For that example, $V=\overline{\mathbf Q}_\ell\oplus \overline{\mathbf Q}_\ell(-1)$ with $\operatorname{DW}(V)=\{0,2\}$,
the formulas of Proposition [sk-prop-walg-determinant-weights] give
$\operatorname{DW}(V\otimes V)=\{0,2,4\}$ and
$\operatorname{DW}(\bigwedge^2V)=\{2\}$, as one checks directly, since
$V\otimes V$ is the sum of $\overline{\mathbf Q}_\ell$, two copies of $\overline{\mathbf Q}_\ell(-1)$ and
$\overline{\mathbf Q}_\ell(-2)$, and $\bigwedge^2V=\overline{\mathbf Q}_\ell(-1)$. In general the factors are
not visible, and the proposition reads each determinant weight off
the central element of the previous subsection, after passing to
semisimplifications by Lemma [sk-r-linear-library].
