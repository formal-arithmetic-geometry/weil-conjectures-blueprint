Let $j$ be the inclusion of a dense open in a smooth curve, and let
$\mathcal F$ be a lisse sheaf on the open. The Fourier argument needs an
extension of $\mathcal F$ to the whole curve that loses nothing at the
boundary points. Extension by zero does lose something: for the constant
sheaf on $\mathbf G_m\subset\mathbf A^1$, $j_!\overline{\mathbf Q}_\ell$ has stalk $0$ at the
origin, while the constant sheaf on $\mathbf A^1$ is the natural
extension. The ordinary direct image $j_*\mathcal F$ recovers a lisse
extension whenever one exists, and its stalk at a boundary point is the
space of inertia invariants (Lemma [sk-i-real-boundary]). Shifted by
one it is a simple perverse sheaf when $\mathcal F$ is irreducible
(Lemma [sk-lem-wft-curve-simples]), whereas $j_!\mathcal F[1]$ has the
punctual subobject $j_*\mathcal F/j_!\mathcal F$, which is nonzero as soon
as some inertia group has invariants. Simplicity is what makes the
Fourier transform of $(j_*M)[1]$ again of this form
(Proposition [sk-p-dl-a1-middle]), and the stalk at the origin of
the transform is then a stalk of a middle extension
(Corollary [sk-c-dl-a1-hc1]).
We call $j_*\mathcal F$ the *middle extension* of $\mathcal F$.
We show in Proposition [sk-p-real-semi] that if $\mathcal F$ is
punctually of weights $\le a$, then so is $j_*\mathcal F$, also at the
points of the complement (the boundary points).
In Section [sec-deligne-laumon-a1] we use it to bound the weights of
the boundary terms in the Fourier transform of a middle extension.
