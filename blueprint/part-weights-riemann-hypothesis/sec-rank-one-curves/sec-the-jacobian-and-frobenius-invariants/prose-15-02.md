The logarithm argument of the next subsection needs that Frobenius has
no fixed vector on $H^1(U,E)$ for a smooth curve $U_0/k_0$
(Proposition [sk-prop-wrp-h1-no-fixed]). For a proper curve, Kummer theory
and duality identify $H^1$ with the dual of the Tate module of the
Jacobian $J$, that is, of $T_\ell J=\varprojlim_nJ[\ell^n](k)$, on
which Frobenius has no fixed vector. For an open curve,
the boundary contributes in addition a permutation representation
twisted by $(-1)$, which has no fixed vector either. To reduce to the
proper case we first compactify.

Over $\mathbf C$ the corresponding statement is classical: the
Jacobian is a torus $V/\Lambda$ with $\Lambda=H_1(C,\mathbf Z)$, and
$H^1(C,\mathbf Z)=\Hom(\Lambda,\mathbf Z)$.
Proposition [sk-d-real-jacobian] is the $\ell$-adic version, with
$T_\ell J$ in place of $\Lambda\otimes\Zl$. Since the link with the
Jacobian is Kummer theory, a statement about $\mu_{\ell^n}$ and line
bundles, we work with the coefficients $\mathbf Z/\ell^n$ and pass to
$E$ only at the end. The arithmetic input is merely that $J_0(k_0)$ is
finite, which holds because $J_0$ is of finite type over a finite
field. For the elliptic curve $C_0:y^2=x^3-x$ over $\mathbf F_3$ of the
introduction, which is its own Jacobian, arithmetic Frobenius $B$ acts
on $T_\ell J$ with eigenvalues $\pm i\sqrt3$, and
$\det(1-B)=(1-i\sqrt3)(1+i\sqrt3)=4$ is the order of $J_0(\mathbf F_3)$,
which consists of the three points with $y=0$ and the point at
infinity.
