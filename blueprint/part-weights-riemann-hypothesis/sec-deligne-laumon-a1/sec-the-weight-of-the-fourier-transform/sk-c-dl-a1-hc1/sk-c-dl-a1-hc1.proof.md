At the rational origin $0'$ of the dual line, the kernel is
canonically trivial. Hence the stalk formula of
Proposition [sk-thm-inversion], which is compatible with
Frobenius, gives

$$
\bigl(\operatorname{FT}_\psi((j_*M)[1])\bigr)_{0'}
=R\Gamma_c(\mathbf A^1,j_*M)[2].
$$

We compare this with the stalk of $(j'_*M')[1]$, which is concentrated
in degree $-1$. In degree $0$ we see that
$H_c^2(\mathbf A^1,j_*M)=0$, and in degree $-1$ we obtain

$$
H_c^1(\mathbf A^1,j_*M)
\simeq(j'_*M')_{0'}
=(M'_{\bar\eta})^{I_{0'}},
$$

where $I_{0'}$ is the inertia group at the origin $0'$ of the dual line
acting on the generic fibre of $M'$. The last equality is the formula
for the stalk of a middle extension (Lemma [sk-i-real-boundary]). By
Proposition [sk-p-real-semi], the weights of this stalk are at most
the weight $w+1$ of $M'$ (Theorem [sk-p-dl-a1-fourier-weight]).
