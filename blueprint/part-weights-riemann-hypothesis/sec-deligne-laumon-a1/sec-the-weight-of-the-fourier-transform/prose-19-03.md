The Plancherel formula identifies the weight $v$, because for a pure
sheaf the number $\nu$ of Definition [sk-def-wrp-l2-definition] is its
weight. This is the order in which the Gauss sum was computed at the
start of Section [sec-fourier-transform]: first one knows that
$|\hat\chi(y)|$ is the same for all $y\ne0$, then Plancherel fixes the
common value. Here Proposition [sk-p-dl-a1-some-weight] plays the
role of the first step, and Theorem [sk-thm-wrp-l2-dictionary] turns the
count of squared traces into a weight on both sides. That
theorem is proved for pure sheaves, which is why the purity of $M'$ had
to come first. The
consequence for cohomology is Corollary [sk-c-dl-a1-hc1], which
reads off $H^1_c(\mathbf A^1,j_*M)$ as the stalk of $j'_*M'$ at the
origin of the dual line and so bounds its weights by $w+1$.
