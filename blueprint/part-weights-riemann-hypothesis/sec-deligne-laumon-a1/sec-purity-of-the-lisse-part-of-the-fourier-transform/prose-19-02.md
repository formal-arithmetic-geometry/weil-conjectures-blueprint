Throughout this subsection and the next, $j:U_0\hookrightarrow\mathbf A^1_{k_0}$
is a dense open and $M$ is a nonzero irreducible lisse Weil sheaf
on $U_0$, pure of weight $w$. We assume that no geometric irreducible
constituent of $M$ is the restriction of $\mathcal L_{\psi(ax)}$ for
some $a\in\bar k_0$, and we let $M'$ be the lisse Weil sheaf on a dense
open $j':U'_0\hookrightarrow\mathbf A^{1\vee}_{k_0}$ with
$\operatorname{FT}_\psi((j_*M)[1])\simeq(j'_*M')[1]$
(Proposition [sk-p-dl-a1-middle]).

The real sheaf that we construct below is built from extensions by
zero, because these have the simplest Fourier transforms. We first
compare the transform of $j_!M$ with that of the middle extension
$j_*M$; they differ by a lisse sheaf of weights at most $w$.

Extension by zero is also what makes reality checkable. At a point of
$U_0$ the eigenvalues of $M^0$ and of its dual are complex conjugate
because they have absolute value one, but at a boundary point the stalk
of $j_*M^0$ is the space of inertia invariants, and the stalk of
$j_*(M^0)^\vee$ is the invariants of the dual representation; nothing
we know at this stage makes their Frobenius eigenvalues conjugate. With
$j_!$ both stalks vanish there, and the trace function of the sum of
the two transforms is visibly real. The price is that $j_!M$ is not a
simple perverse sheaf, so its transform $G$ is not $j'_*M'$ but an
extension of it by the transform $B'$ of the boundary
(Lemma [sk-p-dl-a1-correction]). What the proof of Proposition [sk-p-dl-a1-some-weight] uses
is that $G$ is a sheaf and that it surjects onto $j'_*M'$, so that a
twist of $M'$ becomes a quotient of a real sheaf.
