For a proper map we have $Rf_!=Rf_*$, so the upper bound of
Theorem [theorem-direct-image-mixed-sheaf] applies to ordinary
cohomology. If the map is also smooth, Poincaré duality on each fibre
pairs $H^i(X_{\bar y},F)$ perfectly with
$H^{2d-i}(X_{\bar y},F^\vee(d))$. The eigenvalues of the second group
are the inverses of those of the first. The second group has weights at
most $(-w-2d)+(2d-i)=-w-i$, and this upper bound becomes the lower
bound $w+i$ for the first.

Both hypotheses are needed. Without properness, $H^1_c$ of
$\mathbf G_m$ has weight $0$ instead of $1$. Without smoothness duality
fails. For example, the nodal cubic is proper, and its $H^1$ is a line
on which Frobenius acts by $\pm1$, of weight $0$. The relative
statement is no harder than the absolute one, because we check the
bound at one closed point $y$ at a time, on the smooth proper fibre over
$\kappa(y)$. In the proof of
Theorem [theorem-weil-conjectures-smooth-proper-schemes] we use
only the case of a point as base and the constant sheaf
(Corollary [corollary-purity-cohomology-smooth-proper-variety]).
