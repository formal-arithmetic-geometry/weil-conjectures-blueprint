A quasi-isomorphism $s\colon I\to J$ between K-injective complexes is a homotopy
equivalence: its cone is acyclic and K-injective, hence its identity is null-homotopic.
Moreover $\Hom_{K(\mathcal C)}(K,I)=\Hom_{D(\mathcal C)}(K,I)$ for $I$ K-injective. So
the K-injective complexes, with the homotopy equivalences, form a right derivability
structure for the quasi-isomorphisms: every complex has a K-injective resolution, and a
morphism of complexes lifts, uniquely up to homotopy, to a morphism of chosen
resolutions. Therefore $K\mapsto Q(F(I_K))$ is a well-defined functor on $D(\mathcal C)$.
Composing the resolution maps $K\to I_K$ with $F$ gives a natural transformation
$Q\circ F\Rightarrow RF\circ Q$. It is universal among natural transformations from
$Q\circ F$ to functors factoring through $D(\mathcal C)$, because every such functor
inverts $K\to I_K$. This is the universal property of the total right derived functor,
and the unit is the identity on K-injective complexes.
