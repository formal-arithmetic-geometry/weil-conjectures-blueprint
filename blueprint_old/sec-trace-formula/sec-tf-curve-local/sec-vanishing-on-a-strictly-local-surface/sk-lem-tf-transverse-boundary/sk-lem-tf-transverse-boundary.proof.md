The idea is that, since the monodromy is an $\ell$-group, we may filter
the coefficients until they become the constant sheaf $\mathbf F_\ell$.
For constant coefficients, $M_z=0$ says that on the strict localization
of $X$ at $z$, restriction from the complement of $D_2$ to the punctured
curve $D_1-z$ is an isomorphism on cohomology, and this holds because a
local equation of $D_2$ gives the generator of $H^1$ on both sides. The vanishing of the restriction map then follows from
$M_z=0$ by specialization along $D_2$.

For the assertion at $z$, pass to the strict localization $S$ of
$X$ at $z$ and write $D_{1,S},D_{2,S}$ for the two transverse divisors.
By the formula for stalks of a direct image along an open immersion
[Stacks, Tag 03Q7](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/03Q7) and localization we have

$$
M_z=R\Gamma(S-D_{2,S},j'_!N).
$$

To prove that this object is zero we may forget the $A$-action,
since the exact forgetful functor is conservative and the cohomology of
sheaves of modules agrees with the cohomology of the underlying abelian sheaves
[Stacks, Tag 03FD](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/03FD). A finite abelian $\ell$-group with an action of a
finite $\ell$-group has a finite filtration whose quotients are
$\mathbf F_\ell$ with trivial action: we first filter by powers of $\ell$ and then use
the nilpotence of the augmentation ideal of the group algebra over
$\mathbf F_\ell$. Since $j'_!$ and $R\Gamma(S-D_{2,S},-)$ are exact, the
class of $N$ for which $R\Gamma(S-D_{2,S},j'_!N)=0$ is closed under
extensions, so using truncations and these composition series we reduce
the assertion to $N=\mathbf F_\ell$ on $S-(D_{1,S}\cup D_{2,S})$.

For these constant coefficients, the localization sequence for $D_{1,S}-z$
and its complement in $S-D_{2,S}$ identifies the object we want to show is
zero with the cone, shifted by $-1$, of

$$
R\Gamma(S-D_{2,S},\mathbf F_\ell)
\longrightarrow R\Gamma(D_{1,S}-z,\mathbf F_\ell).
$$

Both complexes have cohomology $\mathbf F_\ell$ in degree zero,
$\mathbf F_\ell(-1)$ in degree one, and zero in all other degrees.
For the surface this follows from localization, the acyclicity of the
strictly local schemes $S,D_{2,S}$, and
$i_{D_{2,S}}^!\mathbf F_\ell=\mathbf F_\ell(-1)[-2]$. This purity
identity follows by transitivity from the dualizing complexes of the
smooth schemes $S$ and $D_{2,S}$. For the punctured trait, vanishing in degrees
$\geq2$ follows from Proposition [sk-prop-curve-affine-kummer] by
passing to the filtered limit of punctured neighbourhoods that are curves,
and the Kummer sequence shows that
$H^1(-,\mu_\ell)=k'^*/k'^{*\ell}\simeq\mathbf Z/\ell$, where $k'$ is the
fraction field. For the last isomorphism, the units of the strictly
henselian trait are $\ell$-divisible, so the valuation induces
$k'^*/k'^{*\ell}\simeq\mathbf Z/\ell$, and the class of a uniformizer is a
generator.

The restriction is the identity in
degree zero. In degree one, let $g$ be a local equation of $D_{2,S}$. Since
the regular local ring of $S$ is factorial, $\operatorname{Pic}(S-D_{2,S})=0$
and the units on $S-D_{2,S}$ are the units of $S$ times the powers of $g$.
The units of $S$ are $\ell$-divisible, so the Kummer sequence shows that
$H^1(S-D_{2,S},\mu_\ell)\simeq\mathbf Z/\ell$, generated by the class of $g$.
Since $D_{1,S}$ and $D_{2,S}$ meet transversely, $g$ restricts to a uniformizer
of $D_{1,S}$, whose class generates $H^1(D_{1,S}-z,\mu_\ell)$. Hence the
restriction is an isomorphism in degree one as well. Since both
complexes vanish in the other degrees, the restriction is an
isomorphism, its cone is zero, and $M_z=0$ in $D(A)$ by conservativity.

Away from $z$, only the divisor $D_2$ remains. Since $D_1$ meets $D_2$ only at
$z$, we have $j'_!N=N$ near $D_2-z$, so $M=Rj_*N$ there. Abhyankar's lemma along
the regular divisor $D_2$ [Stacks, Tag 0EYG](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0EYG) says that, étale locally on $D_2$, a finite
tame local system is trivialized by a Kummer cover of a local
parameter of $D_2$ and an unramified cover. Our finite $\ell$-group
monodromy is tame, because wild inertia is pro-$p$. Consequently, the
stalks of $Rj_*N$ are the continuous cohomology of the same
representation of tame inertia, and their specialization maps along $D_2-z$
are isomorphisms. Explicitly, the pro-$\ell$ part of tame inertia is
$\mathbf Z_\ell(1)$, and its cohomology with coefficients in a finite $\ell$-primary
module is computed by the two-term complex given by a topological
generator minus one. On these coefficients, taking invariants under
the remaining factors of tame inertia, whose finite quotients have order
prime to $\ell$, is exact. Since we may choose the parameter and the
unramified cover on an étale neighbourhood, this
description, including the specialization maps, is locally constant.
Applying the argument to the cohomology sheaves of $N$ and using the
bounded spectral sequence proves the assertion for $N$.

For the last assertion, choose a geometric generic point $\bar\eta$
of the connected regular curve $D_2$, together with specialization maps
from $\bar\eta$ to $z$ and to $w$. By naturality of restriction we get a
commutative square

$$
\begin{CD}
R\Gamma(D_2,M|_{D_2}) @>>> M_w\\
@VVV @VV{\sim}V\\
M_z=0 @>>> M_{\bar\eta}.
\end{CD}
$$

The right arrow is an isomorphism by the local constancy just
proved, so the upper arrow is zero.
