**Step 1.** *(1) The constant ring.* The final object of $V_{\Et}$ is $\mathrm{id}:V\to V$ and that of $U_{\Et}$ is $\mathrm{id}:U\to U$ [def-weil-pbc-etale-site], and $U\times_VV=U$, so for a sheaf $F$ on $U_{\Et}$ we have $\Gamma(V_{\Et},u_*F)=(u_*F)(V)=F(U)=\Gamma(U_{\Et},F)$, naturally in $F$. Let $F$ be any sheaf of commutative rings on $U_{\Et}$. By [lem-weil-pbc-etale-inverse-image-algebraic-structures] (2) (adjunction $u^{-1}\dashv u_*$ on sheaves of rings) and by [lem-weil-pbc-constant-sheaf-maps-global-sections] (for rings) applied on $V_{\Et}$ and on $U_{\Et}$ we get bijections, each natural in $F$: $\operatorname{Hom}(u^{-1}R_V,F)\cong\operatorname{Hom}(R_V,u_*F)\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathrm{Ring}}(R,(u_*F)(V))=\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathrm{Ring}}(R,F(U))\cong\operatorname{Hom}(R_U,F)$. By the Yoneda lemma (in the category of sheaves of commutative rings on $U_{\Et}$) there is a unique isomorphism of sheaves of rings $c:u^{-1}R_V\to R_U$ such that this composite bijection is $\psi\mapsto\psi\circ c$. To identify $c$, take $F=R_U$ and $\psi=\mathrm{id}$: the composite sends $\mathrm{id}_{R_U}$ to the ring map $c_R:R\to R_U(U)$, $r\mapsto$ the constant section $r$, which corresponds to the morphism $\gamma:R_V\to u_*R_U$ with $\gamma_V\circ c^V_R=c_R$; by naturality of the bijection of [lem-weil-pbc-constant-sheaf-maps-global-sections] under restriction, $\gamma_W$ sends the image of the constant section $r$ to the constant section $r$ of $R_U(U\times_VW)$, i.e. $\gamma$ is the map induced by the universal property of sheafification [def-weil-pbc-sheafification] from the constant presheaf maps $R\to R_U(U\times_VW)$; and $c$ is the adjoint of $\gamma$, i.e. $c=\varepsilon_{R_U}\circ u^{-1}\gamma$. So the morphism $c$ of the statement is an isomorphism of sheaves of rings; by [lem-weil-pbc-etale-inverse-image-algebraic-structures] (2), (3) it is also an isomorphism of the underlying abelian sheaves, $u^{-1}R_V$ being computed by the abelian inverse image.

**Step 2.** *(2) The module structure.* Let $G$ be a sheaf of $R_V$-modules [def-weil-sheaf-of-modules-category], with action $\mu:R_V\times G\to G$, a morphism of sheaves of sets. By [lem-weil-pbc-etale-inverse-image-algebraic-structures] (1) $u^{-1}$ commutes with finite products, so $u^{-1}\mu$ is a morphism $u^{-1}R_V\times u^{-1}G=u^{-1}(R_V\times G)\to u^{-1}G$; by (2) of that lemma the ring and group structures of $u^{-1}R_V$ and $u^{-1}G$ are $u^{-1}$ of those of $R_V$ and $G$. The module axioms (associativity $\mu\circ(m\times\mathrm{id})=\mu\circ(\mathrm{id}\times\mu)$, unit, the two distributivity laws) are equalities between morphisms out of finite products built from $\mu$ and the structure maps; applying the functor $u^{-1}$, which preserves finite products and projections, gives the same equalities for $u^{-1}\mu$. So $u^{-1}G$ is a sheaf of $u^{-1}R_V$-modules with action $u^{-1}\mu$, and this structure is unique with that property since the action map is prescribed. Transporting along the ring isomorphism $c$ of step 1, $R_U$ acts by $u^{-1}\mu\circ(c^{-1}\times\mathrm{id})$. If $\varphi:G\to G'$ is $R_V$-linear, i.e. $\mu'\circ(\mathrm{id}\times\varphi)=\varphi\circ\mu$, applying $u^{-1}$ shows that $u^{-1}\varphi$ is $u^{-1}R_V$-linear, hence $R_U$-linear; so $u^{-1}$ is a functor $\Mod(V_{\Et},R)\to\Mod(U_{\Et},R)$. Its underlying abelian sheaf is the abelian inverse image of the underlying abelian sheaf of $G$, and on morphisms it is the abelian $u^{-1}$, by [lem-weil-pbc-etale-inverse-image-algebraic-structures] (2), (3): $\mathrm{forget}\circ u^{-1}=u^{-1}\circ\mathrm{forget}$ (up to the canonical isomorphism of (3)).

**Step 3.** *(2) Equality with the module pullback.* The module pullback of $(u,c)$ is $u^*G=u^{-1}G\otimes_{u^{-1}R_V}R_U$, the change of rings along $c$ [def-pur-sheaf-tensor-product], i.e. the sheafification of the presheaf $W\mapsto u^{-1}G(W)\otimes_{u^{-1}R_V(W)}R_U(W)$. Since each $c_W:u^{-1}R_V(W)\to R_U(W)$ is a ring isomorphism, the map $u^{-1}G(W)\otimes_{u^{-1}R_V(W)}R_U(W)\to u^{-1}G(W)$, $x\otimes a\mapsto c_W^{-1}(a)x$, is an isomorphism of $R_U(W)$-modules with inverse $x\mapsto x\otimes1$ (for an $A$-module $M$ and a ring isomorphism $A\to A'$, $M\otimes_AA'\cong M$), compatible with the restriction maps and natural in $G$. So the presheaf tensor product is isomorphic to the sheaf $u^{-1}G$ with the $R_U$-module structure of step 2, and the sheafification of a presheaf isomorphic to a sheaf is that sheaf (the isomorphism satisfies the universal property of [def-weil-pbc-sheafification]). Hence $u^*G\cong u^{-1}G$ as sheaves of $R_U$-modules, naturally in $G$.

**Step 4.** *(3) Additivity and exactness.* By [lem-weil-pbc-module-sheaves-colimits-forgetful] (1) the forgetful functor $\mathrm{forget}:\Mod(R_T)\to\Ab(T_{\Et})$ is faithful and preserves kernels and cokernels; the addition of morphisms in $\Mod(R_T)$ is the sectionwise addition, that of $\Ab(T_{\Et})$ [def-weil-sheaf-of-modules-category]. By [prop-weil-pbc-inverse-image-exact] the abelian $u^{-1}$ is additive and preserves kernels and cokernels. Additivity: $\mathrm{forget}(u^{-1}(\varphi+\psi))=u^{-1}(\mathrm{forget}\varphi+\mathrm{forget}\psi)=\mathrm{forget}(u^{-1}\varphi)+\mathrm{forget}(u^{-1}\psi)$ by step 2, and $\mathrm{forget}$ is faithful and additive. Kernels: let $\varphi:G\to G'$ be a morphism of $\Mod(V_{\Et},R)$ with kernel $k:K\to G$. Then $\mathrm{forget}(k)$ is a kernel of $\mathrm{forget}(\varphi)$, so $u^{-1}\mathrm{forget}(k)=\mathrm{forget}(u^{-1}k)$ is a kernel of $\mathrm{forget}(u^{-1}\varphi)$; the comparison morphism $\kappa:u^{-1}K\to\ker(u^{-1}\varphi)$ in $\Mod(U_{\Et},R)$ becomes, after $\mathrm{forget}$ (which preserves the kernel on the right), a morphism between two kernels of the same map compatible with the inclusions, hence an isomorphism of abelian sheaves. A morphism of sheaves of $R_U$-modules whose components are bijective has $R_U(W)$-linear inverse components (the inverse of a bijective linear map is linear), so $\kappa$ is an isomorphism in $\Mod(U_{\Et},R)$: $u^{-1}$ preserves kernels. The same argument with cokernels (or step 5 below and [lem-weil-pbc-left-adjoint-preserves-colimits]) shows that $u^{-1}$ preserves cokernels. An additive functor preserves the zero object and finite direct sums (= finite products and coproducts), and preserving kernels (resp. cokernels) it preserves equalizers $\ker(f-g)$ (resp. coequalizers $\operatorname{coker}(f-g)$); as every finite limit (colimit) is built from a final (initial) object, binary products (coproducts) and equalizers (coequalizers), $u^{-1}:\Mod(V_{\Et},R)\to\Mod(U_{\Et},R)$ commutes with finite limits and finite colimits, i.e. is exact.

**Step 5.** *(3) The adjunction.* Apply [lem-weil-pbc-inverse-image-module-adjunction] with $\mathcal O=R_V$: for a sheaf of $R_V$-modules $G$ and a sheaf of $u^{-1}R_V$-modules $F$, a morphism of abelian sheaves $u^{-1}G\to F$ is $u^{-1}R_V$-linear iff its adjoint $G\to u_*F$ is $R_V$-linear, where $R_V$ acts on $u_*F$ through $R_V\xrightarrow{\eta}u_*u^{-1}R_V$. Let now $F$ be a sheaf of $R_U$-modules, regarded as a $u^{-1}R_V$-module through the isomorphism $c$ of step 1. A morphism $u^{-1}G\to F$ is $R_U$-linear iff it is $u^{-1}R_V$-linear, both module structures being transported along $c$ (step 2). The resulting $R_V$-module structure on $u_*F$ is restriction along $u_*c\circ\eta_{R_V}:R_V\to u_*R_U$, which is the adjoint of $c$, i.e. $\gamma$ (step 1): so $R_V(W)$ acts on $(u_*F)(W)=F(U\times_VW)$ through $\gamma_W$, the image of the constant section $r$ acting as the constant section $r$ of $R_U(U\times_VW)$, which is the $R_V$-module structure of the direct image $u_*F$ of [def-weil-pbc-direct-image]. Hence the adjunction bijection $\operatorname{Hom}_{\Ab}(u^{-1}G,F)\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{\Ab}(G,u_*F)$ of [def-weil-pbc-inverse-image] restricts to a bijection $\operatorname{Hom}_{R_U}(u^{-1}G,F)\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{R_V}(G,u_*F)$, natural in $G$ and $F$ (naturality is inherited from the abelian adjunction, the forgetful functors being faithful), whose unit and counit are the abelian ones (linear by the same lemma). So $u^{-1}\dashv u_*$ on $\Mod(-,R)$; with steps 3 and 4 this is the module pullback $u^*$ of the morphism of ringed sites $(u,c)$, a flat one since $c$ is an isomorphism.
