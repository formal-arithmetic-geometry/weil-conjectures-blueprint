Part (1). Since $\kappa(\mathfrak p)=A_{\mathfrak p}/\mathfrak pA_{\mathfrak p}$, the fibre ring is $C\cong B_{\mathfrak p}/\mathfrak pB_{\mathfrak p}$, where $B_{\mathfrak p}$ is the localization of $B$ at $\varphi(A\setminus\mathfrak p)$, and $j$ corresponds to $B\to B_{\mathfrak p}/\mathfrak pB_{\mathfrak p}$. Primes of a localization correspond to the primes of $B$ disjoint from $\varphi(A\setminus\mathfrak p)$, and primes of its quotient by $\mathfrak pB_{\mathfrak p}$ to those containing $\varphi(\mathfrak p)$; so $\mathfrak Q\mapsto j^{-1}(\mathfrak Q)$ is an inclusion-preserving and inclusion-reflecting bijection from the primes of $C$ onto the primes $\mathfrak q\subset B$ with $\varphi^{-1}(\mathfrak q)=\mathfrak p$. For such $\mathfrak q$, the kernel of $\psi_{\mathfrak q}:C\to\kappa(\mathfrak q)$ is a prime ideal (a ring map to a field has prime kernel), and $j^{-1}(\ker\psi_{\mathfrak q})$ is the kernel of $B\to\kappa(\mathfrak q)$, which is $\mathfrak q$. Hence the inverse bijection is $\mathfrak q\mapsto\ker\psi_{\mathfrak q}$.

Part (2), the residue-field isomorphism. Fix $\mathfrak q$ and put $\mathfrak Q=\ker\psi_{\mathfrak q}$, so that $j^{-1}(\mathfrak Q)=\mathfrak q$. Then $j$ induces a homomorphism of residue fields $\iota:\kappa(\mathfrak q)\to\kappa(\mathfrak Q)$. The map $A\to\kappa(\mathfrak p)$ is surjective on stalks ($A_{\mathfrak p}\to\kappa(\mathfrak p)$ is surjective), this property is stable under base change, and $j$ is the base change of $A\to\kappa(\mathfrak p)$ along $A\to B$; hence the induced map of residue fields $\iota$ is surjective. Being a homomorphism of fields it is injective, so it is an isomorphism; let $\theta:\kappa(\mathfrak Q)\to\kappa(\mathfrak q)$ be its inverse.

On the factor $B$. For $b\in B$, $\iota(\bar b)$ is the image of $j(b)$ in $\kappa(\mathfrak Q)$, so $\theta$ sends the image of $j(b)$ to $\bar b=\psi_{\mathfrak q}(j(b))$.

On the factor $\kappa(\mathfrak p)$. Two ring maps out of $\kappa(\mathfrak p)$ agree once they agree on the image of $A$, since $\kappa(\mathfrak p)$ is the fraction field of $A/\mathfrak p$. For $a\in A$, tensor balancing gives $1\otimes\bar a=\varphi(a)\otimes1=j(\varphi(a))$ in $C$, so by the previous step $\theta\circ(C\to\kappa(\mathfrak Q))$ and $\psi_{\mathfrak q}$ agree on $1\otimes\bar a$. Hence they agree on $1\otimes\kappa(\mathfrak p)$.

Two ring maps out of the tensor product $C=B\otimes_A\kappa(\mathfrak p)$ that agree on $B\otimes1$ and on $1\otimes\kappa(\mathfrak p)$ are equal. By the last two steps, $\psi_{\mathfrak q}$ equals the composite of $C\to\kappa(\mathfrak Q)$ with the isomorphism $\theta:\kappa(\mathfrak Q)\xrightarrow{\sim}\kappa(\mathfrak q)$, which is the induced isomorphism of (2).
